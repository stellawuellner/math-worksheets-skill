Here are the three PDFs for a Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra error-analysis set on
**linear equations, focused on diagnosing confusion between the slope and the
$y$-intercept**.

**Skills Summary (study guide)** — `ss_mbmixup_curr224.pdf`, 2 pages.
An opening box states the rule the whole sheet turns on: in $y = mx + b$ it is
*position*, not size, that decides which number is which, and substituting
$x = 0$ is the quick test for the intercept. Three sections follow — reading
both numbers out of an equation, building the equation from two points, and
building it from a table — each with a rule box, a two-step worked example, and
a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box. A watch-out box shows
$y = 5x + 2$ against $y = 2x + 5$ side by side.

**Worksheet** — `ws_mbmixup_curr224.pdf`, 5 pages, 8 problems.
Problem 1 identifies $m$ and $b$ in a plain equation. Problems 2–8 are all
diagnostic: five find-and-fix items (well past the two requested) in which a
named student's swapped equation is printed and the student must compute the
correct value and name the error, one table item, one side-by-side comparison
of $y = 5x + 2$ with $y = 2x + 5$, and a closing item asking how the swap could
be spotted by estimation alone. Every part has its own answer blank, with about
1.8 cm of writing room each.

**Answer key** — `ak_mbmixup_curr224.pdf`, 3 pages.
Quick Answers bank labelled by response, a "Common wrong answers" line for each
declared trap, and a worked solution per problem. Curriculum block: 8.EE.B.6
and 8.F.B, difficulty 1–4.

**What is verified, honestly.** Twenty-two checks were declared across the eight
problems. **Fifteen are machine-checked with SymPy**: every slope, every
intercept, every substitution into the correct equation, the table reading, and
both comparisons (verified as relations, so the key cannot print `>` where `<`
belongs). **Four misconception traps are also machine-checked** — each planted
swapped equation was recomputed and proved to give a value the correct answer
rejects (11 against 10, 204 against 57, $-77$ against 8, 609 against 150), so
none of the planted errors is one the problem is blind to. Worksheet prose
numbers match the verification data 36 out of 36.

**Seven responses are instructor-judged, not machine-checked**: the written
"name the error" or "explain" part of problems 2–8. Because every problem from 2
on carries one, the key's generated note reads "1 of 8 problems fully
machine-checked" and names problems 2–8 — that is the honest reading, not a
failure: all the arithmetic is checked, and the diagnoses are the part a person
has to read. The Quick Answers bank prints `---` for each, and every worked
solution states what a correct diagnosis must say and what does not earn credit.
