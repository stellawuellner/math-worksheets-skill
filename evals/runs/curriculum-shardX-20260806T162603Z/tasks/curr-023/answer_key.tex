\problem{Leo has 3 crayons. Ana gives him 2 more crayons. Draw a circle for
each crayon. How many crayons does Leo have now?}

\textbf{Solution:} Act the story out: draw 3 circles for Leo's crayons, then
2 more circles for the ones Ana gives him. Count them all: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
So $3 + 2 = 5$. \ans{5\ \text{crayons}}

\emph{Drawing (instructor judges):} a good drawing shows 3 circles plus 2
more circles --- 5 in all --- matching the story.

\problem{There are 5 frogs on a log. 2 more frogs hop up onto it. Write the
story numbers in the equation, then write how many frogs there are in all.}

\textbf{Solution:} The story starts with 5 frogs and 2 more come, so the
equation uses the story numbers 5 and 2: \ $5 + 2 = {}$? Count on from 5:
6, 7. So $5 + 2 = 7$. \ans{7}

\problem{Maya has 6 shells in her pail. She finds 3 more shells on the beach.
Count on from 6 to find how many shells she has now.}

\textbf{Solution:} Start at 6 and count on 3 more: 7, 8, 9. So $6 + 3 = 9$.
\ans{9\ \text{shells}}

\problem{There are 4 books in the bin. Dad puts in 4 more books. Write the
story numbers in the equation, then write how many books are in the bin now.}

\textbf{Solution:} Both story numbers are 4, so the equation is
$4 + 4 = {}$? That is the double of 4: \ $4 + 4 = 8$. \ans{8}

\problem{The ten-frame shows Pia's 5 buttons. She gets 3 more buttons. Draw
the 3 new buttons in the frame. How many buttons does Pia have in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} The frame's top row holds the 5 buttons. Drawing the 3 new
buttons fills three boxes of the bottom row. Count all the dots: $5 + 3 = 8$.
\ans{8\ \text{buttons}}

\emph{Drawing (instructor judges):} the student draws 3 more dots in the
frame, so it shows 8 dots in all.

\problem{Nia read 3 pages of her book in the morning and 7 pages at night.
Count on from the bigger number to find how many pages she read in all.}

\textbf{Solution:} Start big: begin at 7 and count on 3 more: 8, 9, 10.
Addition works in any order, so $3 + 7 = 7 + 3 = 10$. \ans{10\ \text{pages}}

\problem{Milo sees 8 ants on the sidewalk. 2 more ants join them. Write the
story numbers in the equation, then write how many ants there are now.}

\textbf{Solution:} The story numbers are 8 and 2: \ $8 + 2 = {}$? Count on
from 8: 9, 10. So $8 + 2 = 10$. \ans{10}

\problem{Challenge! Tara has 2 stamps. She gets 4 stamps from Gram and 3
stamps from Papa. How many stamps does Tara have now? Tell how you found it.}

\textbf{Solution:} Add all three story numbers: $2 + 4 + 3$. One easy way:
add $4 + 3 = 7$ first, then count on from 7: 8, 9. So $2 + 4 + 3 = 9$.
\ans{9\ \text{stamps}}

\emph{Tell how you found it (instructor judges):} any correct plan counts ---
for example grouping $4+3$ first and then adding 2, or starting at the
biggest number 4 and counting on 3, then 2.
