\problem{The bar is cut into sixths; the dark part shows $\tfrac{2}{6}$ and
the light part shows $\tfrac{3}{6}$. Add: $\tfrac{2}{6} + \tfrac{3}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The pieces are all sixths --- the same size --- so we can
just count them: 2 dark pieces plus 3 light pieces makes 5 shaded pieces out
of 6. The denominator stays 6.
$\tfrac{2}{6} + \tfrac{3}{6} = \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}}$

\problem{Fill in the two missing unit fractions:
$\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{1}{4} + \square + \square$}

\textbf{Solution:} Three fourths is three copies of one fourth:
$\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{4}$.
Each missing addend is $\ans{\tfrac{1}{4}}$ and again $\ans{\tfrac{1}{4}}$

\problem{Subtract: $\tfrac{5}{8} - \tfrac{2}{8}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Same-size pieces (eighths), so subtract the counts:
$5 - 2 = 3$ pieces, each an eighth.
$\tfrac{5}{8} - \tfrac{2}{8} = \ans{\tfrac{3}{8}}$

\problem{Ella walks $\tfrac{3}{10}$ mi to the park, then $\tfrac{4}{10}$ mi
more to school. How far in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} Both distances are in tenths of a mile, so add the counts
of tenths: $3 + 4 = 7$ tenths.
$\tfrac{3}{10} + \tfrac{4}{10} = \tfrac{7}{10}$. Total distance:
$\ans{\tfrac{7}{10}\ \text{mi}}$

\problem{The bar shows $\tfrac{7}{8}$ shaded. Cross out $\tfrac{3}{8}$, then
subtract: $\tfrac{7}{8} - \tfrac{3}{8}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Crossing out 3 of the 7 shaded eighths leaves $7 - 3 = 4$
shaded eighths: $\tfrac{7}{8} - \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{4}{8}$. Four eighths is
half the bar, so simplify by dividing top and bottom by 4:
$\tfrac{4}{8} = \ans{\tfrac{1}{2}}$

\problem{Find the missing fraction: $\square + \tfrac{4}{10} = \tfrac{7}{10}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Think of it as a missing-addend problem: how many tenths
must be added to 4 tenths to reach 7 tenths? Since $7 - 4 = 3$, the missing
fraction is $\ans{\tfrac{3}{10}}$

\problem{Fill in the missing numerator:
$\tfrac{5}{6} = \tfrac{3}{6} + \tfrac{\square}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Decompose 5 sixths as 3 sixths plus the rest:
$5 - 3 = 2$ sixths are missing. The missing numerator is \ans{2}

\problem{Ravi mixes $\tfrac{3}{8}$ L of red, $\tfrac{3}{8}$ L of blue, and
$\tfrac{1}{8}$ L of white paint. How many liters in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} All three amounts are eighths of a liter, so add the
numerators: $3 + 3 + 1 = 7$ eighths.
$\tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{1}{8} = \tfrac{7}{8}$. Total paint:
$\ans{\tfrac{7}{8}\ \text{L}}$

\problem{A garden has 12 equal sections; $\tfrac{9}{12}$ is planted and beans
fill $\tfrac{5}{12}$. What fraction is planted with something other than
beans? Explain why the denominator stays 12.}

\textbf{Solution:} Planted minus beans:
$\tfrac{9}{12} - \tfrac{5}{12} = \tfrac{4}{12}$. Simplify by dividing top and
bottom by 4: $\tfrac{4}{12} = \tfrac{1}{3}$. Fraction planted with something
other than beans: $\ans{\tfrac{1}{3}}$

\emph{Explanation (instructor judges):} a correct explanation says the garden
is still cut into twelfths --- subtracting removes pieces but does not change
the size of each piece, so only the numerator (the count of pieces) changes.

\problem{Maya drinks $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of her bottle in the morning and
$\tfrac{5}{8}$ at lunch. (a) What fraction has she drunk? (b) What fraction is
left?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Add the two drinks:
$\tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{5}{8} = \tfrac{6}{8}$, and $\tfrac{6}{8}$ simplifies
(divide top and bottom by 2) to $\ans{\tfrac{3}{4}}$

(b) The whole bottle is $\tfrac{8}{8}$. Left over:
$\tfrac{8}{8} - \tfrac{6}{8} = \tfrac{2}{8}$, which simplifies to
$\ans{\tfrac{1}{4}}$
