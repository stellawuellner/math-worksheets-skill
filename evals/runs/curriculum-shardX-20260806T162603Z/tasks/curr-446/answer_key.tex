\noindent\textbf{Class A} (minutes): $20, 25, 30, 30, 35, 40, 40, 45, 50, 85$ \\
\textbf{Class B} (minutes): $26, 30, 34, 38, 42, 46, 50$

\problem{Find the mean daily reading time for Class A.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The ten values total $400$, so the mean is $\dfrac{400}{10}$.
\ans{40 \text{ minutes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Find the median daily reading time for Class A.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Ten values, so the median is the average of the 5th and 6th:
$\dfrac{35 + 40}{2}$. \ans{37.5 \text{ minutes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Find the range of the Class A times.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Largest minus smallest: $85 - 20$. \ans{65 \text{ minutes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Library borrowings Mon--Fri: $12, 19, 15, 22, 27$. \ (a) week total.
\ (b) busiest day's count.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $12 + 19 + 15 + 22 + 27$. \ans{95}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad The largest count is Friday's. \ans{27}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Class A quartiles. \ (a) $Q_1$. \ (b) $Q_3$. \ (c) IQR.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Split at the median: the lower half is $20, 25, 30, 30, 35$, whose
middle value is the third. \ans{Q_1 = 30 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad The upper half is $40, 40, 45, 50, 85$, whose middle value is the
third. \ans{Q_3 = 45 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \quad $45 - 30$. \ans{\text{IQR} = 15 \text{ minutes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Outliers for Class A. \ (a) upper fence $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$.
\ (b) name the outlier and describe the shape.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $45 + 1.5(15) = 45 + 22.5$. \ans{67.5 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Only $85$ exceeds $67.5$, so $85$ is the
single high outlier. (The lower fence, $30 - 22.5 = 7.5$, is below every value, so there is no
low outlier.) One long right tail pulls the mean of $40$ above the median of $37.5$: the
distribution is right-skewed, with center near $37.5$--$40$ and a middle half only $15$ wide.
Full credit requires naming $85$ as the outlier and calling the shape right-skewed with the
mean-above-median reason.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Class B. \ (a) mean. \ (b) median. \ (c) what their agreement says about shape.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The seven values total $266$, and $\dfrac{266}{7} = 38$.
\ans{38 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Seven values, so the median is the 4th. \ans{38 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Mean and median are both $38$. When the two
agree, no tail is pulling the balance point away from the middle position, so the distribution
is roughly symmetric --- and indeed the values are evenly spaced about $38$. Class A is the
contrast: there the mean ($40$) sits above the median ($37.5$) because of the outlier at $85$.
Full credit requires the symmetry conclusion and the comparison with Class A.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Find the population standard deviation of the Class B times.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Deviations from the mean of $38$ are
$-12, -8, -4, 0, 4, 8, 12$; their squares total $448$; the variance is
$\dfrac{448}{7} = 64$, and $\sqrt{64} = 8$. \ans{8 \text{ minutes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{One of the ten Class A students is chosen at random. Find $P(\text{read more than }
45 \text{ minutes})$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Two of the ten values exceed $45$ (namely $50$ and $85$), so
$P = \dfrac{2}{10}$. \ans{P = \tfrac{1}{5}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis.} The $85$ is corrected to $45$, giving
$20, 25, 30, 30, 35, 40, 40, 45, 45, 50$. \ (a) new mean. \ (b) new median. \ (c) why one moved
and the other did not.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The corrected values total $360$, so the mean is
$\dfrac{360}{10}$. \ans{36 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad The 5th and 6th values are still $35$ and $40$, so the median is
$\dfrac{35+40}{2}$. \ans{37.5 \text{ minutes}}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} \quad The mean fell from $40$ to $36$; the median
did not move at all. The mean is built from the total, so lowering one value by $40$ lowers the
total by $40$ and the mean by $40/10 = 4$. The median depends only on which values sit in the
middle two positions, and correcting the largest value left that pair untouched --- the median
is \emph{resistant} to extreme values. This is why a skewed distribution is usually summarized
by the median and IQR rather than the mean and standard deviation. Full credit requires the
observed movement of each measure and the resistance argument.
