\problem{\textbf{Two 4s in 4400.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``How many times as much'' is a comparison of two \emph{values},
not two digits, so write each digit's value first: thousands 4 $\rightarrow 4000$,
hundreds 4 $\rightarrow 400$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Compare by dividing the larger value by the smaller:
$4000 \div 400 = 10$.
\ans{10}

\textit{Why:} thousands and hundreds are neighbouring places, and every place is
worth ten times the place to its right.}

\problem{\textbf{The meaning of $10^3$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The exponent counts factors of ten, so
$10^3 = 10 \times 10 \times 10$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply: $10 \times 10 = 100$, and $100 \times 10 = 1000$.
\ans{1000}

\textit{Pattern to notice:} the exponent also counts the zeros --- $10^3$ has
three zeros, and it names the thousands place.}

\problem{\textbf{$47 \times 100$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $100 = 10^2$, so this multiplication makes every digit worth
$10^2$ times as much --- a shift of \emph{two places to the left}.

\textbf{Step 2.} The 4 moves from tens to thousands and the 7 moves from ones to
hundreds; two empty places are filled with zeros as placeholders:
$47 \rightarrow 4700$.
\ans{4700}

\textit{Language that matters:} the digits move, the decimal point does not
``gain zeros''. Zeros appear because the ones and tens places are now empty.}

\problem{\textbf{Two 8s in 0.88.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Write each digit's value: tenths 8 $\rightarrow 0.8$,
hundredths 8 $\rightarrow 0.08$. The question compares the smaller with the
larger, so divide in that order.

\textbf{Step 2.} $0.08 \div 0.8 = 0.1$, which is one tenth.
\ans{0.1}

\textit{Why:} moving one place to the \emph{right} divides a digit's value by
ten. This is the same relationship as problem 1, read in the other direction.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $4780 + 3260$ to the nearest thousand.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Rounding to the nearest $10^3$ means looking at the hundreds
digit of each number: $4780$ has $7$ hundreds, so it rounds up to $5000$;
$3260$ has $2$ hundreds, so it rounds down to $3000$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Add the rounded numbers: $5000 + 3000 = 8000$.
\ans{8000}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $8100$ --- rounded to the nearest hundred
($4800 + 3300$) instead of the nearest thousand.}

\problem{\textbf{$60000$ as a digit times a power of ten.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The leading digit is $6$ and it sits in the ten-thousands
place, and ten-thousands is $10^4$, so the exponent is $4$:
$60000 = 6 \times 10^4$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Check by multiplying back:
$6 \times 10^4 = 6 \times 10000 = 60000$.
\ans{60000}

\textit{Check that transfers:} count the zeros. Four zeros after the 6 means
$10^4$, every time.}

\problem{\textbf{$3.6 \times 1000$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $1000 = 10^3$, so every digit shifts three places to the left.

\textbf{Step 2.} The 3 moves from ones to thousands and the 6 moves from tenths
to hundreds. The tens and ones places are now empty, so they take zeros:
$3.6 \rightarrow 3600$.
\ans{3600}

\textit{Watch for:} students who ``add three zeros'' write $3.6000$. Shifting
digits is the reliable description; adding zeros is not.}

\problem{\textbf{Two 5s in 55500, two places apart.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Values first: ten-thousands 5 $\rightarrow 50000$, hundreds 5
$\rightarrow 500$. The places are ten-thousands, thousands, hundreds --- so the
digits are \emph{two} places apart, not one.

\textbf{Step 2.} Two places means two factors of ten:
$50000 \div 500 = 100 = 10^2$.
\ans{100}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $10$ --- the ``one place left is ten times'' rule
applied without counting how many places apart the digits actually are.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $6238 - 2905$ to the nearest hundred.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Rounding to the nearest $10^2$ means looking at the tens digit:
$6238$ has $3$ tens, so it rounds down to $6200$; $2905$ has $0$ tens, so it
rounds down to $2900$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Subtract the rounded numbers: $6200 - 2900 = 3300$.
\ans{3300}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $3200$ --- $2905$ rounded up to $3000$ because the
$5$ in the ones place was mistaken for a tens digit. Round by the place you were
asked for, and read the digit to its right.}

\problem{\textbf{$4500 \div 100$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $100 = 10^2$, and dividing makes each digit worth one hundredth
as much, so the digits shift \emph{two places to the right}.

\textbf{Step 2.} The 4 moves from thousands to tens and the 5 moves from
hundreds to ones: $4500 \rightarrow 45$.
\ans{45}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $450000$ --- multiplied by $100$ instead of
dividing. A quotient smaller than the dividend is the check.}

\problem{\textbf{Expanded form with powers of ten.}

\textbf{(a)} Term by term: $3 \times 10^4 = 30000$, \; $7 \times 10^2 = 700$,
\; and the last term is $5 \times 10^0 = 5$.

\textbf{(b)} Add them, keeping each digit in its own place:
$30000 + 700 + 5$. There is no thousands term and no tens term, so those places
are empty and take $0$ as a placeholder.
\ans{30705}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $375$ --- the digits written side by side with the
empty places dropped. Placeholders are what keep every other digit in its
place.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $8120 \div 39$.}

\textbf{(a)} $39$ rounds to $40$, because $9$ ones is more than half of ten. The
useful part is that $40 = 4 \times 10$, so the division becomes a fact plus a
shift.

\textbf{(b)} $8120 \div 40$: divide by $10$ first (shift one place right) to get
$812$, then divide by $4$ to get $203$.
\ans{203}

\textbf{(c)} The exact quotient is about $208$, so the estimate is
\emph{smaller} than the exact answer. Rounding the divisor \emph{up} means
sharing into bigger groups, and bigger groups give a smaller quotient.
(Accept any answer that says the estimate is smaller and links it to the larger
divisor.)

\textit{Common wrong answer:} about $271$ --- $39$ rounded down to $30$ rather
than to the nearest ten.}
