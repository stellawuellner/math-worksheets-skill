\wstitleblock{Subtracting Across a Zero}{Grades 2--3 \quad Number and Operations in Base Ten}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Regrouping across one zero, regrouping across two zeros,
estimating the difference, and checking by adding}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{How to trade when there is a zero.} \small You cannot take ones
from a zero, and you cannot take tens from a zero. Go left until you find a digit
you \emph{can} trade, then trade back one place at a time.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked model:} \quad $305 - 168$
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{4.6cm}p{4.6cm}p{4.4cm}}
\textbf{1. Look at the tens.} & \textbf{2. Trade a hundred.} & \textbf{3. Trade a ten.}\\
There are 0 tens, so the tens cannot give the ones a group. Go left. &
$305$ is 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones. Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens:
2 hundreds, 10 tens, 5 ones. &
Now the tens can trade: 2 hundreds, 9 tens, 15 ones. Subtract:
$15-8=7$, $9-6=3$, $2-1=1$.\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent So $305 - 168 = 137$. Check it: $137 + 168 = 305$. \normalsize
