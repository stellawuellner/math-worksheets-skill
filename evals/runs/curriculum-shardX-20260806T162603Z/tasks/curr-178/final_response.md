Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 7 "write the equation" workshop.

- **Worksheet** (`ws_eqn_from_words_curr178.pdf`) — 10 situations, each one
  naming its unknown with a letter and asking for the equation *and* its
  solution. The set moves through the three shapes a student needs to tell
  apart: situations that add or take away (stickers gained, water drained, a
  temperature falling past zero), situations that share or scale (marbles split
  six ways, a rope cut into eight pieces, flour three times the sugar), and
  two-step situations where one charge is paid once and another repeats (taxi
  start fee plus a per-kilometre rate, joining fee plus monthly fee, six equal
  rows plus four potted plants, adult tickets on top of a known child-ticket
  total). Units are stated in every stem and no story detail goes unused.
- **Answer key** (`ak_eqn_from_words_curr178.pdf`) — Quick Answers bank,
  generated Curriculum section (6.EE.B.7 and 7.EE.B.4, difficulty 1–5), and
  solutions that write the equation first, say *why* it says what the story
  says, and only then solve. The three items with a graded equation carry the
  acceptable equivalent forms and the specific wrong forms to reject — for
  example 6(p + 4) = 58, which puts the four potted plants in every row.
- **Study guide** (`ss_eqn_from_words_curr178.pdf`) — one section per shape,
  each with the signal words that point to it, a two-step worked example, and a
  try-it with the answer upside down inside the box; plus a watch-out on
  substituting your answer back to test the *equation* rather than the
  arithmetic.

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 14 responses. **10 are machine-checked**
with SymPy: every solved value is recomputed from the equation itself, so a
correct answer to a mis-written equation cannot pass. **4 are
instructor-judged** — the three "write the equation" responses on problems 4, 8
and 10, and the explanation of what each part of the equation stands for on
problem 10(c). Those are genuinely open: several equivalent equations are
correct, and no checker can read a student's arrangement of them. The Quick
Answers bank marks each with `---`, and each worked solution lists the forms
that earn full credit and the ones that do not. All six study-guide checks
(three examples, three try-its) are machine-verified.
