\wstitleblock{Modeling with Systems and Matrices}{Algebra 2}{}

\begin{center}
\small\textit{Modeling with two- and three-variable systems, writing them as matrix equations, and finding where a line meets a curve}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Define your variables in words before you write any equation, and say what unit each one carries.
  \item The method is yours to choose: substitution, elimination, or a matrix equation. Pick the one the numbers make easiest, and say which you used.
  \item A system with a squared term can have two solutions. Find both, then decide which ones the situation allows.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.1cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

\problem{A theatre sells adult tickets at $x$ dollars each and child tickets at $y$ dollars each. One family buys $3$ adult and $2$ child tickets for $61$ dollars. Another buys $1$ adult and $4$ child tickets for $47$ dollars. Find $x$ and $y$.
\par\vspace*{5.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A farm stand ships crates and boxes. Four crates and three boxes together weigh $47$ kilograms. Two crates weigh $9$ kilograms less than five boxes. Let $x$ be the mass of one crate and $y$ the mass of one box, both in kilograms. Find $x$ and $y$.
\par\vspace*{5.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A roaster blends two coffees to make $20$ kilograms of a house mix. The first coffee costs $12$ dollars per kilogram and the second costs $18$ dollars per kilogram; the mix must cost $288$ dollars in all. Let $x$ and $y$ be the kilograms of each coffee. Write the situation as a matrix equation, then find $x$ and $y$.
\par\vspace*{5.8cm}\ansline}

\problem{A water jet from a fountain follows the path $y = -x^2 + 8x + 9$, and a straight support wire follows $y = 3x + 15$, with $x$ the horizontal distance and $y$ the height, both in metres. Find every point where the jet meets the wire.
\par\vspace*{5.8cm}\ansline}

\problem{A workshop made $16$ items last week: chairs, tables and stools. Cutting takes $2$ hours per chair, $3$ hours per table and $1$ hour per stool, and used $34$ hours in all. Finishing takes $3$ hours per chair, $1$ hour per table and $2$ hours per stool, and used $29$ hours in all. Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ be the number of chairs, tables and stools. Find $x$, $y$ and $z$.
\par\vspace*{6.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A juice bar mixes orange juice at $3$ dollars per litre with mango juice at $5$ dollars per litre. The batch is $24$ litres and costs $88$ dollars. Let $x$ and $y$ be the litres of orange and mango juice.
\par\smallskip
(a)~Write the situation as a matrix equation and find $x$ and $y$.
\par\vspace*{5cm}\ansline
\par\smallskip
(b)~Explain what each \emph{row} of your coefficient matrix records about the batch.
\par\vspace*{2.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A circular pond has edge $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, where $x$ and $y$ are distances in metres from the centre of the pond. A straight gravel path runs along $y = x + 1$. Find both points where the path crosses the pond edge.
\par\vspace*{6.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A workshop rents a floor sander two ways. Plan A charges $40$ dollars plus $6$ dollars per hour; Plan B charges $25$ dollars plus $9$ dollars per hour. Let $x$ be the hours rented and $y$ the total cost in dollars.
\par\smallskip
(a)~Find the number of hours at which the two plans cost the same, and that shared cost.
\par\vspace*{5cm}\ansline
\par\smallskip
(b)~Explain which plan is cheaper for a $10$-hour rental, and why.
\par\vspace*{2.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A cafe sold $20$ drinks in one hour: small, medium and large. Smalls cost $3$ dollars, mediums $4$ dollars and larges $5$ dollars, and the hour brought in $79$ dollars. The cafe sold $3$ more mediums than smalls. Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ be the numbers of small, medium and large drinks. Find $x$, $y$ and $z$.
\par\vspace*{6.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A rectangular garden has a perimeter of $34$ metres and an area of $60$ square metres. Let $x$ and $y$ be its two side lengths in metres.
\par\smallskip
(a)~Find every pair $(x, y)$ that satisfies both conditions.
\par\vspace*{5cm}\ansline
\par\smallskip
(b)~Explain why the two pairs you found describe the same garden.
\par\vspace*{2.4cm}\ansline}

\problem{A feed mill blends three grains into a $100$ kilogram mix. Per kilogram, the grains supply $2$, $1$ and $3$ units of protein, and the mix must supply $220$ units. Per kilogram they supply $3$, $2$ and $1$ units of fibre, and the mix must supply $170$ units. Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ be the kilograms of each grain. Write the blend as a matrix equation and find $x$, $y$ and $z$.
\par\vspace*{6.8cm}\ansline}

\problem{A rectangular field is fenced on three sides, with a river forming the fourth. The fencing available is $120$ metres and the enclosed area must be $1600$ square metres. Let $x$ be the side parallel to the river and $y$ each of the two sides running back from it, both in metres.
\par\smallskip
(a)~Find every pair $(x, y)$ that uses all the fencing and encloses the required area.
\par\vspace*{5.4cm}\ansline
\par\smallskip
(b)~Which pair gives the wider river frontage? Explain, and say whether both pairs are genuinely usable designs.
\par\vspace*{2.6cm}\ansline}
