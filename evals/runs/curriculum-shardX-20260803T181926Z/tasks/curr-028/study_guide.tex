\sstitleblock{Take-From and Comparison Stories Within Ten}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Every story below is one subtraction.} Read the story, decide which
picture it is, then write the equation with the unit.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Take from} --- something leaves the group:
      start $-$ went away $=$ left.
\item \textbf{Compare} --- two groups, nothing leaves:
      bigger $-$ smaller $=$ difference.
\item \textbf{Missing part} --- the story hides a number:
      draw the whole group, then find what fills the gap.
\end{itemize}
\end{formulabox}

\skillheading{1. Take-from stories}

\begin{formulabox}
Start with the whole group. Cross out the things that go away. Count only the
things with no cross on them. Say the unit with the answer.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} 7 birds sit on a fence. 3 birds fly away. How many birds are
left?
\step{Birds leave the fence, so this is take-from and the count goes down.}
\step{Draw 7 dots, cross out 3, and count the dots with no cross: $7 - 3$.}
\[ \ans{4 \text{ birds}} \]
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} 8 muffins are on a tray. 5 muffins are eaten. How many muffins
are left?

\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{3 \text{ muffins}}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Comparison stories: how many more, how many fewer}

\begin{formulabox}
Nothing goes away in a comparison. Line the two groups up, one under the other,
and match them. The extras in the longer row are the answer. ``How many more''
and ``how many fewer'' ask for the \emph{same} gap.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} There are 6 cats and 2 dogs. How many more cats than dogs?
\step{Both groups stay, so match them up instead of taking any away.}
\step{Two cats pair with the 2 dogs. The unmatched cats are the answer:
$6 - 2$.}
\[ \ans{4 \text{ more cats}} \]
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A shelf has 7 blue books and 2 red books. How many fewer red
books than blue books are there?

\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{5 \text{ fewer red books}}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Stories with a missing part}

\begin{formulabox}
Sometimes the story tells you the start and the end, but not the change. Write
the equation with a box for the number you do not know, then count back from the
start to the end. Putting the part back is the check: it must return the start.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A basket has 8 eggs. Some eggs are used. Now 5 eggs are left.
How many eggs were used?
\step{The start and the ending amount are known and the change is missing, so
write $8 - \square = 5$.}
\step{Count back from 8 down to 5: 7, 6, 5 --- that is 3 counts.}
\[ \ans{3 \text{ eggs}} \]
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} 9 ants are on a log. Some ants walk away. Now 2 ants are left.
How many ants walked away?

\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{7 \text{ ants}}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{Watch out.} ``More'' does not mean add. In ``Sam has 3 more shells than
Tia'', the word more describes Sam, and finding Tia's smaller number is a
subtraction. Read the question again before writing the equation, and check that
the answer is smaller than the group you started with.
\end{watchoutbox}
