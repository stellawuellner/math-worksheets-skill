# Composite Area and Perimeter Challenges (Grade 3) — what you received

Three PDFs:

- **Student worksheet** (`ws_composite_curr095.pdf`) — 12 problems on
  3.MD.C.7 / 3.MD.D.8. A short rectangle warm-up, then nine composite
  rectilinear challenges: L-shapes to split for area, labeled shapes to walk
  around for perimeter, a grid L-shape to count, a missing-sides puzzle, a
  staircase shape, and a final challenge that asks for area, perimeter, and a
  comparison on one garden. The four subskills (rectangle area, rectangle
  perimeter, composite area, composite perimeter) are interleaved after the
  warm-up so your child must choose the method each time. Every shape is drawn
  to scale with labeled sides, and every answer line names its unit.
- **Step-by-step answer key** (`ak_composite_curr095.pdf`) — worked reasoning
  for every part (including how to split each shape and how to find missing
  sides), a quick-answers bank, and "Common wrong answers" notes from the
  three declared area-vs-perimeter traps.
- **Study guide** (`ss_composite_curr095.pdf`) — four sections matching the
  four subskills, each with the rule, a worked example, and a fresh try-it
  with the answer printed upside down.

**What is verified:** all 15 responses across the 12 problems are
machine-checked — SymPy recomputed every rectangle area/perimeter, every
composite area (from the shape's actual vertices via the shoelace formula, so
the drawing and the answer cannot disagree), both missing sides, every
perimeter sum, and the final comparison. The three declared traps (perimeter
given instead of area, and area instead of perimeter twice) were
machine-checked to be distinguishably wrong. Nothing on this sheet needs
manual review, and the key's "What is verified" note is silent because every
response is covered. All 8 study-guide examples and try-its are
machine-verified too.
