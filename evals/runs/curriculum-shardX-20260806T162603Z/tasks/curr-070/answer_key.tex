\problem{Fill in: $4 + 4 + 4 = \underline{\ \ } \times 4 = \underline{\ \ }$}

\textbf{Solution:} The 4 repeats 3 times, so the fact is $3 \times 4$. Adding gives $4 + 4 + 4 = 12$.
\[ \boxed{4 + 4 + 4 = 3 \times 4 = 12} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Fill in: $6 + 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = \underline{\ \ } \times 6 = \underline{\ \ }$}

\textbf{Solution:} The 6 repeats 5 times, so the fact is $5 \times 6$. Skip counting by 6: $6, 12, 18, 24, 30$.
\[ \boxed{6 + 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 5 \times 6 = 30} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Mia reads 8 pages each day for 3 days. Multiplication fact and total?}

\textbf{Solution:} Three days of 8 pages is 3 equal groups of 8, so the fact is $3 \times 8 = 24$ (or $8 \times 3 = 24$). The total is $8 + 8 + 8 = 24$ pages. \ans{24}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Array with 3 rows of 6 dots: multiplication fact and total.}

\textbf{Solution:} 3 rows of 6 gives the fact $3 \times 6 = 18$ (or $6 \times 3 = 18$). Counting all dots: $6 + 6 + 6 = 18$. \ans{18}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $4 \times 7$ as repeated addition, then find the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} $4 \times 7$ means 4 groups of 7: $7 + 7 + 7 + 7$. (Seven 4s added is also correct --- it gives the same total.) Adding: $7 + 7 = 14$, $14 + 7 = 21$, $21 + 7 = 28$. \ans{28}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ari's array: 5 rows with 4 dots in each row. Repeated addition and total.}

\textbf{Solution:} One 4 for each of the 5 rows: $4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4$. (By columns, $5 + 5 + 5 + 5$ also shows the array.) Either way the total is $5 \times 4 = 20$ dots. \ans{20}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Fill in: $9 + 9 = \underline{\ \ } \times 9 = \underline{\ \ }$}

\textbf{Solution:} The 9 repeats 2 times, so $9 + 9 = 2 \times 9 = 18$.
\[ \boxed{9 + 9 = 2 \times 9 = 18} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $6 \times 5$ as repeated addition, then find the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} $6 \times 5$ means 6 groups of 5: $5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5$. (Five 6s added is also correct.) Skip counting by 5 six times: $5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30$. \ans{30}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Star array: 7 rows with 5 stars in each row. Multiplication fact and total.}

\textbf{Solution:} 7 rows of 5 gives $7 \times 5 = 35$ (or $5 \times 7 = 35$). Skip counting by 5 seven times lands on 35. \ans{35}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Fill in: $10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = \underline{\ \ } \times 10 = \underline{\ \ }$}

\textbf{Solution:} The 10 repeats 4 times, so $10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 4 \times 10 = 40$.
\[ \boxed{10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 4 \times 10 = 40} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{How many 8s make 40? \quad $8 \times n = 40$}

\textbf{Solution:} Skip count by 8 until you reach 40: $8, 16, 24, 32, 40$ --- that is five 8s. So $8 \times 5 = 40$. \ans{n = 5}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Challenge: 6 rows of chairs with 9 chairs in each row.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The array fact is $6 \times 9 = 54$ (or $9 \times 6 = 54$).

\textbf{Solution (b):} One 9 for each row: $9 + 9 + 9 + 9 + 9 + 9$ (six 9s; adding 6 nine times also works).

\textbf{Solution (c):} The total is $6 \times 9 = 54$ chairs --- not $6 + 9 = 15$, which only adds the row count to one row's chairs. \ans{54}
