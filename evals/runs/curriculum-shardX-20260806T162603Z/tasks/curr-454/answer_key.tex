\problem{(a) Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2+2x}{x+1}$.
(b) How can you tell in advance that substitution is safe?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The denominator at $x = 3$ is $3 + 1 = 4 \neq 0$, so the
quotient is defined at $3$ and the function is continuous there. Substitution is
therefore legal:
\[
  \frac{3^2 + 2(3)}{3 + 1} = \frac{9 + 6}{4} = \frac{15}{4}. \qquad \ans{\tfrac{15}{4}}
\]

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} A correct answer
says the denominator is not zero at the target: $x + 1 = 4$ at $x = 3$, so the
expression is defined there and the limit equals the value. Full credit names
the non-zero denominator explicitly. Do not accept ``because it is a
polynomial'' on its own --- the expression is a quotient, and checking the bottom
is exactly the habit this problem is building.

\problem{Find and fix. The student wrote
$\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \frac{0}{0} = 0$. (a) Correct value.
(b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Substitution genuinely does give $\frac{0}{0}$, which
means it has failed --- not that the answer is $0$. Factor and cancel instead:
\[
  \frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = x + 2 \qquad (x \neq 2),
\]
so the limit is $2 + 2$. \ans{4}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} The diagnosis
must say that $\frac{0}{0}$ is an \emph{indeterminate form}, not a value: it
carries no information about the limit and simply signals that the expression
must be rewritten first. Full credit also names the skipped step --- factor the
numerator as $(x-2)(x+2)$ and cancel the common factor. Accept ``you cannot
divide zero by zero'' only when it is paired with what to do instead.

\problem{$f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x-3}$. Find (a) $\lim_{x\to3^-}f(x)$ and
(b) $\lim_{x\to3^+}f(x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The numerator is the constant $1 > 0$, so the direction is
decided entirely by the sign of $x - 3$.

For $x$ just below $3$, $x - 3$ is a small \emph{negative}, so $1$ divided by it
is a large negative number. \ans{-\infty}

For $x$ just above $3$, $x - 3$ is a small \emph{positive}, so the quotient is a
large positive number. \ans{+\infty}

Because the two sides run in opposite directions, the two-sided limit does not
exist.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-1}\frac{x^2+3x+2}{x^2-1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} At $x = -1$ both parts are zero, so factor both completely:
\[
  \frac{x^2+3x+2}{x^2-1} = \frac{(x+1)(x+2)}{(x+1)(x-1)} = \frac{x+2}{x-1}
  \qquad (x \neq -1).
\]
Substituting $x = -1$ into the reduced form gives
$\frac{-1+2}{-1-1} = \frac{1}{-2}$. \ans{-\tfrac{1}{2}}

Cancelling the $x^2$ terms instead --- a subtraction is not a factor --- would
have produced $1$, which is the trap this problem is built around.

\problem{Find and fix. The student answered $+\infty$ for
$\lim_{x\to2^-}\frac{x+4}{x-2}$. (a) Correct value. (b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Approaching $2$ from the left means $x < 2$, so $x - 2$ is
a small \emph{negative} number, while the numerator is near $+6$. A positive
divided by a small negative is a large negative:
\ans{-\infty}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} The student
checked the numerator's sign and stopped. The diagnosis must say that the
\emph{denominator's} sign was never checked: to the left of $2$ the quantity
$x - 2$ is negative, so positive over negative gives $-\infty$. Full credit
states that the numerator's sign alone cannot decide the direction. Do not
accept ``they forgot a minus sign'' with no reference to which quantity is
negative.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$g(x) = \dfrac{2-x}{(x-5)^2}$. Find (a) $\lim_{x\to5^-}g(x)$ and
(b) $\lim_{x\to5^+}g(x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A squared denominator is positive on \emph{both} sides ---
that is what makes this different from problem 3. The numerator tends to
$2 - 5 = -3 < 0$.

From the left: negative over a small positive, so \ans{-\infty}

From the right: the same negative over the same small positive, so
\ans{-\infty}

Here the two sides agree, so it is correct to write
$\lim_{x\to5} g(x) = -\infty$.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Find and fix. The student answered $-\infty$ for
$\lim_{x\to-2^-}\frac{x-6}{x+2}$. (a) Correct value. (b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Near $x = -2$ the numerator is close to $-8$, so it is
negative --- that much the student had right. But approaching from the left
means $x < -2$, so $x + 2$ is a small \emph{negative} too. A negative divided by
a negative is positive:
\ans{+\infty}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} The diagnosis
must say that both signs matter, and that the denominator's sign was not
checked: on the left of $-2$ the quantity $x + 2$ is negative, so
negative-over-negative gives $+\infty$. Full credit states the
negative-over-negative reasoning. Award partial credit for correcting the answer
with no reason given.

\problem{Challenge --- find and fix. The student cancelled $|x-1|$ as $x-1$ and
answered $2$ for $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{|x-1|}$. (a) Both one-sided limits.
(b) Name the two faults.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Split the absolute value by side.

For $x < 1$: $|x-1| = -(x-1)$, so
$\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{-(x-1)} = -(x+1) \to -(1+1)$, giving
\ans{-2}

For $x > 1$: $|x-1| = x-1$, so $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1} = x+1 \to 1+1$, giving
\ans{2}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} Two faults must
be named. \textbf{First}, the absolute value was dropped: for $x < 1$ the
denominator is $1 - x$, not $x - 1$, so the cancellation as written is valid only
on the right-hand side. \textbf{Second}, the work never separated the two sides
at all --- and since the one-sided values are $-2$ and $2$, the two-sided limit
does not exist, so reporting any single number is wrong in form as well as in
value. Full credit names both faults; award partial credit for either one alone.
