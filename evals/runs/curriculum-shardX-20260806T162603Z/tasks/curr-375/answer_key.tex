\problem{$f(x) = \sqrt{x-5}$. (a) Domain. (b) $\sqrt{4(x-5)}$ as $k\sqrt{x-5}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The radicand must be non-negative: $x - 5 \ge 0$.
\ans{x \ge 5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $4$ is a perfect square and it multiplies the radicand,
so the root does come apart over that product:
$\sqrt{4(x-5)} = \sqrt{4}\,\sqrt{x-5}$. \ans{2\sqrt{x-5}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x-2} = 6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square both sides: $x - 2 = 36$. Check:
$\sqrt{38-2} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 38}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Pendulum period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/9.8}$ seconds, $L = 2$ metres.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute and evaluate inside out:
\[ T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{9.8}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.204081\ldots}
     = 2\pi(0.451754\ldots) = 2.83845\ldots \]
Rounded to two decimal places, \ans{2.84 \text{ s}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x+2} = x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square: $x + 2 = x^2$, so $x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ and
$(x-2)(x+1) = 0$. Candidates $x = 2$ and $x = -1$. Test in the original:
$\sqrt{4} = 2$ $\checkmark$; $\sqrt{1} = 1 \ne -1$ $\times$ (a square root is
never negative). \ans{x = 2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Domain of $g(x) = \sqrt{7-2x}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Require $7 - 2x \ge 0$, so $7 \ge 2x$. Dividing by the
positive $2$ keeps the direction of the inequality. \ans{x \le \tfrac{7}{2}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{5x-4} = \sqrt{2x+8}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Both sides are non-negative wherever they are defined, so
squaring introduces nothing: $5x - 4 = 2x + 8$, so $3x = 12$. Check:
$\sqrt{16} = \sqrt{16}$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 4}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{2x-1} = 4.6$ to two decimal places.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square both sides, then isolate $x$:
\[ 2x - 1 = 4.6^2 = 21.16 , \qquad 2x = 22.16 , \qquad x = 11.08 . \]
Check: $\sqrt{2(11.08)-1} = \sqrt{21.16} = 4.6$ $\checkmark$ \ans{11.08}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{5x+6} = x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square: $5x + 6 = x^2$, so $x^2 - 5x - 6 = 0$ and
$(x-6)(x+1) = 0$. Candidates $x = 6$ and $x = -1$. Test:
$\sqrt{36} = 6$ $\checkmark$; $\sqrt{1} = 1 \ne -1$ $\times$. \ans{x = 6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Domain of $p(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x-4}}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The radicand must be non-negative, and the radical sits in a
denominator so it must not be zero either. Both conditions together give
$x - 4 > 0$ --- a strict inequality, unlike problem 1. \ans{x > 4}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$L = 9.8\left(\dfrac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2$ metres, $T = 2$ seconds.}

\textbf{Solution:} Evaluate the bracket first, then square:
\[ L = 9.8\left(\frac{2}{2\pi}\right)^2 = 9.8(0.318309\ldots)^2
     = 9.8(0.101321\ldots) = 0.99295\ldots \]
Rounded to two decimal places, \ans{0.99 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x+6} = \sqrt{x} + 2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square both sides, keeping the cross term:
\begin{align*}
  x + 6 &= x + 4\sqrt{x} + 4 \\
  2 &= 4\sqrt{x} \\
  \sqrt{x} &= \tfrac{1}{2} \\
  x &= \tfrac{1}{4} .
\end{align*}
Check: $\sqrt{6.25} = 2.5$ and $\sqrt{0.25} + 2 = 0.5 + 2 = 2.5$ $\checkmark$
\ans{x = \tfrac{1}{4}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Synthesis: $\sqrt{2x+7} = x+2$. (a) Domain. (b) Solve.
(c) Left side at $x=-3$. (d) Why $x=-3$ fails.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $2x + 7 \ge 0$ gives $2x \ge -7$. \ans{x \ge -\tfrac{7}{2}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Square: $2x + 7 = x^2 + 4x + 4$, so $x^2 + 2x - 3 = 0$ and
$(x+3)(x-1) = 0$. Candidates $x = 1$ and $x = -3$. Test $x = 1$:
$\sqrt{9} = 3 = 1 + 2$ $\checkmark$. Test $x = -3$: $\sqrt{1} = 1$ but
$-3 + 2 = -1$ $\times$. \ans{x = 1}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $\sqrt{2(-3)+7} = \sqrt{1}$. \ans{1}

\textbf{Solution (d):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} There are two separate tests and
$x = -3$ passes only the first. It is inside the domain, since
$-3 \ge -\tfrac{7}{2}$, so the left side is perfectly well defined and equals
$1$. It fails the equation because the right side $x + 2$ is $-1$ there, and a
square root can never equal a negative number. Squaring hid that sign
difference, which is why the candidate appeared at all. Full credit needs the
distinction between being in the domain and satisfying the equation.
