\problem{Find the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a hexagon ($6$ sides).}

\textbf{Solution:} A polygon with $n$ sides splits into $n-2$ triangles from one vertex, so the interior angles add to $(n-2)\cdot 180^\circ$. With $n = 6$: $(6-2)\cdot 180^\circ = 4 \cdot 180^\circ$. \ans{720^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a polygon with $11$ sides.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same rule with $n = 11$: $(11-2)\cdot 180^\circ = 9 \cdot 180^\circ$. \ans{1620^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A regular polygon has $9$ sides. Find the measure of each exterior angle.}

\textbf{Solution:} One exterior angle at each vertex, taken in the same rotational direction, adds to one full turn: the exterior angles of any convex polygon sum to $360^\circ$. In a regular polygon they are all equal, so each is $360^\circ / 9$. \ans{40^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Three of the angles of a quadrilateral measure $112^\circ$, $87^\circ$ and $63^\circ$. Find the fourth.}

\textbf{Solution:} A quadrilateral has $n = 4$, so its angles add to $(4-2)\cdot 180^\circ = 360^\circ$.
\begin{align*}
  112 + 87 + 63 + x &= 360 \\
  262 + x &= 360 \\
  x &= 98
\end{align*}
The fourth angle is \ans{98^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Each exterior angle of a regular polygon measures $24^\circ$. How many sides does it have?}

\textbf{Solution:} The $n$ equal exterior angles fill $360^\circ$, so $360/n = 24$, giving $n = 360/24$. \ans{n = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Four of the angles of a pentagon measure $102^\circ$, $118^\circ$, $96^\circ$ and $131^\circ$. Find the fifth.}

\textbf{Solution:} A pentagon has $n = 5$, so its angles add to $(5-2)\cdot 180^\circ = 540^\circ$.
\begin{align*}
  102 + 118 + 96 + 131 + x &= 540 \\
  447 + x &= 540 \\
  x &= 93
\end{align*}
The fifth angle is \ans{93^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Each interior angle of a regular polygon measures $156^\circ$. (a) Each exterior angle? (b) How many sides?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} An interior angle and the exterior angle at the same vertex form a straight line, so they are supplementary: $180^\circ - 156^\circ$. \ans{24^\circ}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The equal exterior angles fill $360^\circ$, so $n = 360^\circ / 24^\circ$. \ans{n = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{The interior angles of a polygon add up to $1800^\circ$. How many sides does the polygon have?}

\textbf{Solution:} Read the interior-angle-sum rule backwards.
\begin{align*}
  (n-2)\cdot 180 &= 1800 \\
  n-2 &= 10 \\
  n &= 12
\end{align*}
The polygon has \ans{n = 12}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Marcus writes $\text{sum} = 5 \cdot 180^\circ = 900^\circ$ for a pentagon. (a) Correct sum? (b) Explain his error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} A pentagon has $n = 5$, and the rule subtracts two before multiplying: $(5-2)\cdot 180^\circ = 3 \cdot 180^\circ$. \ans{540^\circ}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: drawing the diagonals from one vertex of a pentagon cuts it into $3$ triangles, not $5$, because the two sides that meet at the chosen vertex do not produce triangles of their own. Marcus multiplied the number of sides by $180^\circ$; he should have multiplied \emph{two fewer than} the number of sides, that is $(5-2)\cdot 180^\circ$.

\emph{Full credit:} names the correct set-up $(5-2)\cdot 180^\circ$ and says why the count drops by two (three triangles, not five). \emph{Half credit:} says $900^\circ$ is too large without identifying which factor is wrong. \emph{No credit:} restates the correct sum with no account of the error.

\problem{In a regular polygon each interior angle is $5$ times each exterior angle. (a) Each exterior angle? (b) Sides?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Let the exterior angle be $x$. The interior angle is then $5x$, and the two are supplementary.
\begin{align*}
  5x + x &= 180 \\
  6x &= 180 \\
  x &= 30
\end{align*}
Each exterior angle measures \ans{30^\circ}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The equal exterior angles fill $360^\circ$, so $n = 360^\circ / 30^\circ$. \ans{n = 12}
