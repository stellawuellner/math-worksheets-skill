\problem{\textbf{Factor $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 2x - 15}$ completely.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Numerator: $x^2 - 9$ is a difference of squares, so it factors
as $(x-3)(x+3)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Denominator: find two numbers with product $-15$ and sum $+2$.
Those are $+5$ and $-3$, so $x^2 + 2x - 15 = (x+5)(x-3)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Write the fraction with both factorisations in place.

\ans{f(x) = \dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x+5)(x-3)}}

\textit{Why this matters:} the shared factor $(x-3)$ is already visible, and it
is the reason $x = 3$ will be a hole rather than an asymptote in later
problems.}

\problem{\textbf{$g(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 4}$: lowest terms, and the
hole.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factor both parts:
$\dfrac{(x-3)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The factor $(x+2)$ appears top and bottom, so it cancels for
every $x$ except $x = -2$, where the original $g$ is undefined.

\ans{g(x) = \dfrac{x-3}{x-2}}

\textbf{Step 3.} The height of the hole is the value the simplified form takes
at $x = -2$: $\dfrac{-2-3}{-2-2} = \dfrac{-5}{-4} = \dfrac{5}{4}$.

\ans{\text{hole at } \left(-2,\ \tfrac{5}{4}\right)}

\textit{Grading note:} the simplified formula alone is only half the answer ---
the excluded value $x = -2$ must be stated, because $g$ and $\frac{x-3}{x-2}$
are not the same function.}

\problem{\textbf{Vertical asymptotes of $h(x) = \dfrac{x+1}{x^2 - 3x - 10}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factor the denominator: two numbers with product $-10$ and sum
$-3$ are $-5$ and $+2$, so $x^2 - 3x - 10 = (x-5)(x+2)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The numerator $x+1$ is not $(x-5)$ or $(x+2)$, so nothing
cancels --- every zero of the denominator survives as a vertical asymptote.

\textbf{Step 3.} Set each surviving factor to zero: $x - 5 = 0$ and
$x + 2 = 0$.

\ans{x = 5}

\ans{x = -2}

\textit{Listen for:} a student who names $x = -1$ as an asymptote. That is the
zero of the NUMERATOR, which is an $x$-intercept, not an asymptote.}

\problem{\textbf{Intercepts of $p(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 8}{x^2 + 4x + 3}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $x$-intercepts come from the numerator: factor
$x^2 - 2x - 8 = (x-4)(x+2)$, so the numerator is zero at $x = 4$ and $x = -2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Check that neither value breaks the denominator:
$x^2 + 4x + 3 = (x+1)(x+3)$ is zero only at $x = -1$ and $x = -3$. Both
candidates survive.

\ans{x = 4}

\ans{x = -2}

\textbf{Step 3.} The $y$-intercept is $p(0) = \dfrac{0 - 0 - 8}{0 + 0 + 3}
= \dfrac{-8}{3}$.

\ans{\left(0,\ -\tfrac{8}{3}\right)}

\textit{Grading note:} $-\frac{8}{3} \approx -2.67$; a decimal answer is fine
if the fraction is shown first.}

\problem{\textbf{Horizontal asymptote of $q(x) = \dfrac{3x^2 - 5}{x^2 + 4}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Numerator and denominator both have degree $2$, so neither
grows faster --- the graph settles onto the ratio of the leading
coefficients rather than onto $y = 0$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Divide top and bottom by $x^2$:
$\dfrac{3 - 5/x^2}{1 + 4/x^2}$. As $x$ grows without bound, $5/x^2 \to 0$ and
$4/x^2 \to 0$, leaving $\dfrac{3}{1}$.

\ans{y = 3}

\textit{Teaching note:} the graph is allowed to sit below the line for small
$x$ --- a horizontal asymptote describes the far left and far right only.}

\problem{\textbf{Dana's intercepts of $r(x) = \dfrac{2x-6}{x^2 - x - 12}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Recompute $r(0)$ carefully. Numerator at $x = 0$: $-6$.
Denominator at $x = 0$: $-12$. The quotient of two negatives is positive.

\textbf{Step 2.} $r(0) = \dfrac{-6}{-12} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so Dana's mistake was
dropping the denominator's minus sign.

\ans{\left(0,\ \tfrac{1}{2}\right)}

\textbf{Step 3.} For the $x$-intercept, set the numerator to zero:
$2x - 6 = 0$, so $x = 3$. The denominator at $x = 3$ is
$9 - 3 - 12 = -6 \neq 0$, so the point really is on the graph.

\ans{(3,\ 0)}

\textit{What Dana did wrong:} she divided $-6$ by $12$ instead of by $-12$,
which flipped the sign of a correct-looking answer.}

\problem{\textbf{$s(x) = \dfrac{x+4}{x^2 - 16}$: asymptote or hole?}

\textbf{Step 1.} $s$ is undefined wherever the denominator is zero:
$x^2 - 16 = (x-4)(x+4) = 0$ gives $x = 4$ and $x = -4$.

\ans{x = 4}

\ans{x = -4}

\textbf{Step 2.} Cancel: $\dfrac{x+4}{(x-4)(x+4)} = \dfrac{1}{x-4}$ for
$x \neq -4$. The factor $(x-4)$ is still in the denominator, so $x = 4$ is the
vertical asymptote; $(x+4)$ cancelled, so $x = -4$ is a hole.

\textbf{Step 3.} The height of the hole is the simplified value at $x = -4$:
$\dfrac{1}{-4-4} = -\dfrac{1}{8}$.

\ans{\text{hole at } \left(-4,\ -\tfrac{1}{8}\right)}

\textit{Listen for:} ``two asymptotes'' --- the standard error. Cancelling
first is what tells the two cases apart.}

\problem{\textbf{Horizontal asymptote of $t(x) = \dfrac{2x+7}{x^2+3x}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The denominator has degree $2$ and the numerator degree $1$,
so the bottom grows faster; a fraction with a fixed-size top and a runaway
bottom is heading for zero.

\textbf{Step 2.} Divide through by $x^2$: $\dfrac{2/x + 7/x^2}{1 + 3/x}$. Every
term with an $x$ underneath tends to $0$, so the whole quotient tends to $0$.

\ans{y = 0}

\textit{In words:} far to the right the graph flattens onto the $x$-axis from
above, getting arbitrarily close without reaching it.}

\problem{\textbf{$u(x) = \dfrac{x^2-x-2}{x^2-4}$: which numerator zero is an
intercept?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factor everything: $\dfrac{(x-2)(x+1)}{(x-2)(x+2)}$. The
numerator is zero at $x = 2$ and $x = -1$.

\ans{x = 2}

\ans{x = -1}

\textbf{Step 2.} $(x-2)$ cancels, so $x = 2$ is not in the domain of $u$ at all
--- a point cannot be an intercept if the function has no value there. Only
$x = -1$ is an $x$-intercept.

\textbf{Step 3.} At the cancelled value, the graph has a hole whose height is
the simplified value there: $\dfrac{2+1}{2+2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

\ans{\text{hole at } \left(2,\ \tfrac{3}{4}\right)}

\textit{Full answer:} $x$-intercept at $(-1, 0)$; hole at
$\left(2, \frac{3}{4}\right)$.}

\problem{\textbf{Jonah's claim about $v(x) = \dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Jonah's rule --- ``denominator zero, therefore vertical
asymptote'' --- skips the factoring step. Factor first:
$\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The $(x-2)$ cancels, leaving $v(x) = x + 2$ for every
$x \neq 2$. Nothing blows up: as $x$ approaches $2$ from either side, $v(x)$
approaches $2 + 2 = 4$.

\ans{\text{hole at } (2,\ 4)}

\textbf{Step 3.} \textit{Why he is wrong (model answer):} a vertical asymptote
needs the function to grow without bound near the value, which happens only
when a factor is left in the denominator after cancelling. Here the factor
cancels, so $v$ agrees with the line $y = x+2$ everywhere except at $x = 2$,
where it simply has no value --- an open circle at $(2, 4)$.

\textit{Manual review:} the explanation is the graded part; the boxed point is
the check.}

\problem{\textbf{$w(x) = \dfrac{2x^2+3x}{x^2-9}$: asymptote, and crossing it.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Equal degrees again, so divide by $x^2$:
$\dfrac{2 + 3/x}{1 - 9/x^2} \to \dfrac{2}{1}$ as $x$ grows.

\ans{y = 2}

\textbf{Step 2.} A crossing means $w(x) = 2$, so solve
$\dfrac{2x^2+3x}{x^2-9} = 2$, i.e. $2x^2 + 3x = 2(x^2 - 9)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The $2x^2$ cancels: $3x = -18$, so $x = -6$. Check it is in
the domain: $(-6)^2 - 9 = 27 \neq 0$, and
$w(-6) = \dfrac{72 - 18}{27} = 2$ $\checkmark$

\ans{x = -6}

\textit{Teaching note:} a horizontal asymptote constrains the far ends of a
graph, not the middle --- crossing it once is perfectly normal.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: full analysis of
$F(x) = \dfrac{2x^2-8}{x^2-x-2}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factor: $\dfrac{2(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+1)}$, so
$F(x) = \dfrac{2(x+2)}{x+1}$ for $x \neq 2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The cancelled factor $(x-2)$ gives a hole at $x = 2$, height
$\dfrac{2(2+2)}{2+1} = \dfrac{8}{3}$.

\ans{\text{hole at } \left(2,\ \tfrac{8}{3}\right)}

\textbf{Step 3.} The surviving factor $(x+1)$ gives the vertical asymptote
$x = -1$; the simplified numerator is zero at $x = -2$, which is the
$x$-intercept.

\ans{x = -1}

\ans{(-2,\ 0)}

\textbf{Step 4.} $y$-intercept: $F(0) = \dfrac{0-8}{0-0-2} = \dfrac{-8}{-2}
= 4$.

\ans{(0,\ 4)}

\textbf{Step 5.} Degrees are equal in the original formula, so the horizontal
asymptote is the ratio of leading coefficients, $\dfrac{2}{1}$.

\ans{y = 2}

\textit{Model sketch (manual review):} two branches of $y = \dfrac{2(x+2)}{x+1}$
separated by the dashed line $x = -1$, both approaching the dashed line
$y = 2$ far left and far right, crossing the axes at $(-2, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$,
with an open circle at $\left(2, \frac{8}{3}\right)$.}
