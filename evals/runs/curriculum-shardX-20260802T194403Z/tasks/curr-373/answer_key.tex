\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x - 3} = 5$.

\textbf{Solution:} the radical is already isolated, so square both sides:
$x - 3 = 25$, hence $x = 28$. \quad Check: $\sqrt{28 - 3} = \sqrt{25} = 5$
$\checkmark$. Both sides were non-negative before squaring, so no extraneous
root could appear. $\;$ $\ans{x = 28}$}

\problem{Rewrite and evaluate $16^{3/4}$.

\textbf{Solution:} a rational exponent is root-then-power:
$16^{3/4} = \left(\sqrt[4]{16}\right)^3 = 2^3 = 8$.
Taking the fourth root first keeps the arithmetic small; the other order needs
$16^3 = 4096$ before rooting. $\;$ $\ans{8}$}

\problem{$\sqrt{x + 6} = x$, squared equation $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$.

\textbf{(a)} $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2) = 0$ gives the candidates
$\ans{x = -2}$ and $\ans{x = 3}$.

\textbf{(b)} Substituting into $\sqrt{x + 6} - x$:
at $x = 3$: $\sqrt{9} - 3 = 0$ $\checkmark$; \quad
at $x = -2$: $\sqrt{4} - (-2) = 2 + 2 = \ans{4} \neq 0$ $\times$.

\textbf{(c)} $x = -2$ is \textbf{extraneous} --- it solves $x + 6 = x^2$ but
makes the original read $2 = -2$, and the principal square root is never
negative. Solution: $\;$ $\ans{x = 3}$}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{3x + 1} = x - 1$.

\textbf{(a)} Squaring: $3x + 1 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, so $x^2 - 5x = 0$ and
$x(x - 5) = 0$, giving candidates $\ans{x = 0}$ and $x = 5$.

\textbf{(b)} At $x = 0$: $\sqrt{3(0) + 1} - (0 - 1) = 1 + 1 = \ans{2} \neq 0$
$\times$ --- the left side is $1$ while the right side is $-1$, so this
candidate is extraneous. At $x = 5$: $\sqrt{16} = 4$ and $5 - 1 = 4$
$\checkmark$.

\textbf{(c)} Solution set: $\;$ $\ans{x = 5}$

\textit{Why it happened:} the right side $x - 1$ is negative at $x = 0$, and a
square root can never equal a negative number.}

\problem{Free fall: $t = \sqrt{h/16}$ with $t = 2.5$ seconds; find $h$ in
feet.

\textbf{Solution:} substitute and solve the radical equation:
\[ 2.5 = \sqrt{\dfrac{h}{16}} \;\Longrightarrow\; 6.25 = \dfrac{h}{16}
   \;\Longrightarrow\; h = 100 \]
Check in the model: $\sqrt{100/16} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.5$ $\checkmark$. Both
sides are non-negative here (a time and a principal root), so the candidate
cannot be extraneous. $\;$ $\ans{h = 100\ \text{ft}}$}

\problem{Skid marks: $s = \sqrt{24x}$ with $s = 60$ miles per hour; find $x$
in feet.

\textbf{Solution:}
\[ 60 = \sqrt{24x} \;\Longrightarrow\; 3600 = 24x \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 150 \]
Check: $\sqrt{24 \cdot 150} = \sqrt{3600} = 60$ $\checkmark$.
$\;$ $\ans{x = 150\ \text{ft}}$

\textit{Domain note:} only $x \geq 0$ makes sense as a skid length, and the
model's own square root already refuses negative inputs.}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x - 2} = -3$, or explain why there is no real solution.

\textbf{Solution:} squaring gives $x - 2 = 9$, so $x = 11$ is the only
candidate. Substituting into $\sqrt{x - 2} + 3$ at $x = 11$:
$\sqrt{9} + 3 = \ans{6} \neq 0$, i.e. $\sqrt{9} = 3 \neq -3$ $\times$.

The principal square root outputs only values $\geq 0$, so
$\sqrt{x - 2} = -3$ is impossible for every real $x$; the candidate is
extraneous and the equation has $\;$ $\ans{\text{no real solution}}$

This is the cleanest illustration of why the check is mandatory: squaring
manufactured a root out of an equation with none.}

\problem{Solve $x^{3/2} = 8$.

\textbf{Solution:} in radical form the equation is
$\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^3 = 8$. Take the cube root of both sides:
$\sqrt{x} = 2$, then square: $x = 4$. \quad Check:
$4^{3/2} = \left(\sqrt{4}\right)^3 = 2^3 = 8$ $\checkmark$.
$\;$ $\ans{x = 4}$

\textbf{No negative solution:} $x^{3/2}$ contains $\sqrt{x}$, which is
undefined for $x < 0$ in the real numbers --- the domain rules negatives out
before any solving happens.}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x + 7} - \sqrt{x} = 1$.

\textbf{Solution:} isolate one radical first, or squaring leaves two of them:
\begin{align*}
 \sqrt{x + 7} &= 1 + \sqrt{x}\\
 x + 7 &= 1 + 2\sqrt{x} + x  &&\text{(square both sides)}\\
 6 &= 2\sqrt{x} \;\Longrightarrow\; \sqrt{x} = 3  &&\text{(isolate the radical)}\\
 x &= 9 &&\text{(square again)}
\end{align*}
Check: $\sqrt{9 + 7} - \sqrt{9} = 4 - 3 = 1$ $\checkmark$.
$\;$ $\ans{x = 9}$}

\problem{$\sqrt{x + 5} = x - 1$ with the table of both sides.

\textbf{(a)} At $x = -1$: $\sqrt{4} = 2$ but $x - 1 = -2$ --- \emph{not
equal}. At $x = 4$: $\sqrt{9} = 3$ and $x - 1 = 3$ --- \emph{equal}. (Squaring
gives $x + 5 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, i.e. $x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$, whose roots are the two
candidates $\ans{x = -1}$ and $x = 4$.)

\textbf{(b)} Solution set: $\;$ $\ans{x = 4}$

\textbf{(c)} The curve $y = \sqrt{x + 5}$ lies on or above the $x$-axis
everywhere, so it can only meet $y = x - 1$ where that line is non-negative,
i.e. for $x \geq 1$ --- one intersection. Squaring replaces the line by the
parabola $y = (x - 1)^2$, which is non-negative on both sides of $x = 1$, so
it also meets the graph of $y = x + 5$ at $x = -1$. The table shows the cost:
at $x = -1$ the two original sides differ by $\ans{4}$, so that intersection
belongs to the squared equation only.}
