\problem{How many beakers hold exactly $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of a litre?}

\textbf{Solution:} Find the tick labelled $\frac{3}{8}$ and count the crosses stacked above it. There are four. \ans{4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How many beakers are recorded on the plot altogether?}

\textbf{Solution:} Add every stack, left to right: $2 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 2$. \ans{16}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Which amount was recorded more often than any other?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare the heights of the stacks, not the amounts underneath them: the tallest stack has four crosses, one more than the next tallest. \ans{\tfrac{3}{8}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{The two beakers at $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of a litre are emptied into one jug. How much water is in the jug?}

\textbf{Solution:} Two equal amounts added is the amount doubled: $2 \times \frac{5}{8} = \frac{10}{8}$, which reduces to $\frac{5}{4}$. \ans{1\tfrac{1}{4} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{What is the difference between the greatest amount and the least amount on the plot?}

\textbf{Solution:} The leftmost cross sits at $\frac{1}{8}$ and the rightmost at $\frac{3}{4}$. Rewrite over eighths and subtract: $\frac{6}{8} - \frac{1}{8}$. \ans{\tfrac{5}{8} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How much water is held altogether by the beakers whose amounts are less than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a litre?}

\textbf{Solution:} Three stacks sit left of $\frac{1}{2}$: two beakers at $\frac{1}{8}$, three at $\frac{1}{4}$, four at $\frac{3}{8}$.

Work in eighths: $2 \times \frac{1}{8} = \frac{2}{8}$, $3 \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{6}{8}$, $4 \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{12}{8}$. Adding gives $\frac{20}{8} = \frac{5}{2}$. \ans{2\tfrac{1}{2} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{The four beakers at $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of a litre are poured together and then shared equally between 3 empty beakers. How much is in each?}

\textbf{Solution:} Pour first: $4 \times \frac{3}{8} = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}$ litres in the jug.

Then share between 3: $\frac{3}{2} \div 3 = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{6}$. \ans{\tfrac{1}{2} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{What is the median of the 16 recorded amounts?}

\textbf{Solution:} The plot is already in order from left to right, so count crosses from the left. With 16 values the median sits between the 8th and the 9th.

Counting: crosses 1--2 are $\frac{1}{8}$, 3--5 are $\frac{1}{4}$, 6--9 are $\frac{3}{8}$. Both the 8th and the 9th cross sit at the same amount, so their midpoint is that amount. \ans{\tfrac{3}{8} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How much water is in the science cupboard altogether?}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply each amount by its stack height and add. In eighths throughout:
\[ 2 \times \tfrac{1}{8} + 3 \times \tfrac{1}{4} + 4 \times \tfrac{3}{8} + 3 \times \tfrac{1}{2} + 2 \times \tfrac{5}{8} + 2 \times \tfrac{3}{4} \]
\[ = \tfrac{2}{8} + \tfrac{6}{8} + \tfrac{12}{8} + \tfrac{12}{8} + \tfrac{10}{8} + \tfrac{12}{8} = \tfrac{54}{8} = \tfrac{27}{4} \]
Written as a mixed number, that is \ans{6\tfrac{3}{4} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How much more water is held by all the beakers at $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a litre together than by all the beakers at $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a litre together?}

\textbf{Solution:} Each group has three beakers. The half-litre group holds $3 \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$; the quarter-litre group holds $3 \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$.

Subtract over quarters: $\frac{6}{4} - \frac{3}{4}$. \ans{\tfrac{3}{4} \text{ L}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How many beakers hold more than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a litre?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``More than'' excludes the stack sitting exactly at $\frac{1}{2}$. Count only the stacks to its right: two crosses at $\frac{5}{8}$ and two at $\frac{3}{4}$, so $2 + 2$. \ans{4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{One more beaker, holding $\dfrac{7}{8}$ of a litre, is added. (a) What is the median of the 17 amounts now? (b) Explain why adding the largest amount did not move the median very far.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With 17 values the median is the 9th from the left. The new cross goes on the far right, so the first nine crosses are unchanged: 1--2 at $\frac{1}{8}$, 3--5 at $\frac{1}{4}$, 6--9 at $\frac{3}{8}$. \ans{\tfrac{3}{8} \text{ L}}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} Look for reasoning about \emph{position}, not size. The median is whatever value sits in the middle place of the ordered list; adding one value at the right-hand end moves that middle place by one step only, and four beakers share the amount sitting there, so the value in the middle place does not change. A response saying only ``because it is just one beaker'' has not explained why the position matters.
