\problem{Maya had $s$ stickers, was given 14 more, and had 32.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Given 14 more'' adds to the start, so
$s + 14 = 32$. Subtract 14 from both sides: $s = 32 - 14 = 18$
stickers. \ans{s = 18}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{A bag of $m$ marbles shared equally by six friends gives 15 each.}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing equally divides, so $\dfrac{m}{6} = 15$. Multiply
both sides by 6: $m = 15 \times 6 = 90$ marbles. \ans{m = 90}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{A tank of $w$ litres lost 26 litres and 48 were left.}

\textbf{Solution:} Draining subtracts, so $w - 26 = 48$. Add 26 to both sides:
$w = 48 + 26 = 74$ litres. \ans{w = 74}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Taxi: 3 dollars to start plus 2 dollars per kilometre; the trip cost 19
dollars. Write and solve for $k$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The distance charge is 2
dollars for each of $k$ kilometres, which is $2k$, and the 3 dollar start charge
is paid once: $2k + 3 = 19$. Accept any equivalent arrangement --- $3 + 2k = 19$,
$19 = 2k + 3$, $19 - 3 = 2k$. An expression with no equals sign, or an equation
that multiplies the start charge by the distance, is not correct.

(b) Subtract the start charge, then divide by the rate:
$2k = 16$, so $k = 8$ kilometres. \ans{k = 8}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{A rope of $r$ cm cut into 8 equal pieces of 2.5 cm each.}

\textbf{Solution:} Cutting into 8 equal pieces divides, so
$\dfrac{r}{8} = 2.5$. Multiply both sides by 8:
$r = 2.5 \times 8 = 20$ centimetres. \ans{r = 20}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Swim club: 12 dollars to join plus 5 dollars a month, 57 dollars paid.}

\textbf{Solution:} The joining fee is paid once and the 5 dollars repeats each
month, so $5n + 12 = 57$. Subtract 12: $5n = 45$. Divide by 5: $n = 9$
months. \ans{n = 9}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Temperature $t$ fell 17 degrees to reach $-4$ degrees.}

\textbf{Solution:} Falling subtracts, so $t - 17 = -4$. Add 17 to both sides:
$t = -4 + 17 = 13$ degrees Celsius. The answer is above zero even though the
finish is below it, because the fall was larger than the finishing
temperature. \ans{t = 13}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Six equal rows of $p$ plants plus 4 extra plants make 58.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} Six rows of $p$ plants is
$6p$, and the 4 potted plants are added once: $6p + 4 = 58$. Accept
$4 + 6p = 58$ or $58 - 4 = 6p$. Writing $6(p + 4) = 58$ is not correct --- that
puts four extra plants in every row.

(b) Subtract 4: $6p = 54$. Divide by 6: $p = 9$ plants in each
row. \ans{p = 9}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Flour is 3 times the sugar; together they weigh 640 grams. Solve for
the sugar $g$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The sugar is $g$ grams and the flour is $3g$ grams, and the
two together are 640 grams: $g + 3g = 640$. Collect like terms:
$4g = 640$. Divide by 4: $g = 160$ grams of sugar (and 480 grams of
flour). \ans{g = 160}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Adult tickets at 8 dollars, 60 child tickets at 5 dollars, 940 dollars
in all. Solve for the number $a$ of adult tickets.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The adult money is $8a$ and
the child money is fixed at $5 \times 60 = 300$ dollars, so
$8a + 300 = 940$. Accept the unsimplified form $8a + 5(60) = 940$. Giving the
child tickets their own letter is not correct here: that number is already
known.

(b) Subtract 300: $8a = 640$. Divide by 8: $a = 80$ adult
tickets. \ans{a = 80}

(c) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} A correct response gives a meaning to each
piece: $a$ is the number of adult tickets, the 8 multiplying it is the price of
one adult ticket in dollars, the 300 (or $5 \times 60$) is the money already
taken from the child tickets, and 940 is the whole amount collected. Full credit
needs a meaning for the letter, for the multiplied term, and for the constant
term.
