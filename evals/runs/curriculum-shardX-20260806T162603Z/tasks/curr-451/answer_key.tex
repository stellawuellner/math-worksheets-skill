\problem{Table of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2-16}{x-4}$ at $x = 3.9,\ 3.99,\ 4.01,\ 4.1$
gives $7.9,\ 7.99,\ 8.01,\ 8.1$. Predict $\lim_{x \to 4} f(x)$, then confirm by
factoring and cancelling.}

\textbf{Solution:} The table values close in on $8$ from both sides, so the
prediction is $8$. Direct substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$, so factor:
\[
  \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4} = \frac{(x-4)(x+4)}{x-4} = x + 4 \qquad (x \neq 4).
\]
The cancelled form $x+4$ agrees with $f$ everywhere except at $x = 4$, where
$f$ has a hole. Limits ignore the single missing point, so substitute into
$x + 4$: $\ \lim_{x \to 4} f(x) = 4 + 4$. \ans{8}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -2} \frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x + 2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substituting $x = -2$ gives $\frac{4 - 10 + 6}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$,
so the numerator must contain the factor $x + 2$:
\[
  \frac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x + 2} = \frac{(x+2)(x+3)}{x+2} = x + 3 \qquad (x \neq -2).
\]
Now substitute: $\ -2 + 3$. \ans{1}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x+9} - 3}{x}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitution gives $\frac{3-3}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$. There is
nothing to factor, so multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate
$\sqrt{x+9} + 3$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\sqrt{x+9} - 3}{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x+9} + 3}{\sqrt{x+9} + 3}
    &= \frac{(x + 9) - 9}{x\left(\sqrt{x+9} + 3\right)} \\[2pt]
    &= \frac{x}{x\left(\sqrt{x+9} + 3\right)}
     = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+9} + 3} \qquad (x \neq 0).
\end{align*}
Substituting $x = 0$ gives $\frac{1}{3 + 3}$. \ans{\tfrac{1}{6}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 9}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$, so factor both parts
completely --- the cancelling factor is $x - 3$ in each:
\[
  \frac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 9} = \frac{(x-3)(x+2)}{(x-3)(x+3)} = \frac{x+2}{x+3}
  \qquad (x \neq 3).
\]
Substituting $x = 3$ gives $\frac{5}{6}$. \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$. Multiply by the conjugate
of the numerator, $\sqrt{x} + 2$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x} + 2}{\sqrt{x} + 2}
    &= \frac{x - 4}{(x-4)\left(\sqrt{x} + 2\right)}
     = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2} \qquad (x \neq 4).
\end{align*}
(The same cancellation follows from factoring $x - 4 = (\sqrt{x}-2)(\sqrt{x}+2)$.)
Substituting $x = 4$ gives $\frac{1}{2 + 2}$. \ans{\tfrac{1}{4}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(2+h)^3 - 8}{h}$, the
instantaneous rate of change of $y = x^3$ at $x = 2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Expand the cube in full --- this is the step the common
error skips:
\[
  (2+h)^3 = 8 + 12h + 6h^2 + h^3 .
\]
So the numerator is $12h + 6h^2 + h^3 = h\left(12 + 6h + h^2\right)$ and
\[
  \frac{(2+h)^3 - 8}{h} = 12 + 6h + h^2 \qquad (h \neq 0).
\]
Letting $h \to 0$ leaves the constant term. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Table of $g(x) = \dfrac{x^3-8}{x^2-4}$ at $x = 1.9,\ 1.99,\ 2.01,\ 2.1$
gives $2.9256,\ 2.9925,\ 3.0075,\ 3.0756$. \textbf{(a)} Give the exact value of
$\lim_{x \to 2} g(x)$. \textbf{(b)} Explain why the table entries alone do not
prove that value.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$. Use the
difference-of-cubes and difference-of-squares factorings:
\[
  \frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4}
  = \frac{(x-2)\left(x^2 + 2x + 4\right)}{(x-2)(x+2)}
  = \frac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2} \qquad (x \neq 2).
\]
Substituting $x = 2$ gives $\frac{4 + 4 + 4}{4} = \frac{12}{4}$. \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} A correct
response must make the sampling point: the table reports four inputs chosen by
the author, and no finite list of nearby inputs can rule out different behaviour
strictly between them or exactly at $x = 2$, so the column is evidence that the
values are heading toward $3$ and not a proof that they do. Full credit also
names what does prove it --- cancelling the common factor $x - 2$ turns $g$ into
a function that is continuous at $2$, so its limit is its value there. Accept a
response that makes the finitely-many-samples point even if the second half is
only implied; do not accept ``because the numbers round to 3.''

\problem{Challenge. Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x-1}{\sqrt{x+3} - 2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$. The radical sits in the
denominator, so multiply above and below by the denominator's conjugate,
$\sqrt{x+3} + 2$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{x-1}{\sqrt{x+3} - 2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x+3} + 2}{\sqrt{x+3} + 2}
    &= \frac{(x-1)\left(\sqrt{x+3} + 2\right)}{(x + 3) - 4} \\[2pt]
    &= \frac{(x-1)\left(\sqrt{x+3} + 2\right)}{x - 1}
     = \sqrt{x+3} + 2 \qquad (x \neq 1).
\end{align*}
Substituting $x = 1$ gives $\sqrt{4} + 2 = 2 + 2$. \ans{4}
