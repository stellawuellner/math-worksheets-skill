\problem{\textbf{SAS: $a = 7$, $b = 9$, $C = 52^\circ$; find $c$.}
Two sides and the angle \emph{between} them is the SAS case, so the law of
cosines applies directly with the unknown side opposite the known angle.
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 7^{2} + 9^{2} - 2(7)(9)\cos 52^\circ = 49 + 81 - 126(0.615661) \\
c^{2} &= 130 - 77.5733 = 52.4267
\end{align*}
Take the positive square root.
$\ans{c \approx 7.24}$
\par\smallskip
The answer is shorter than both given sides, which is right for an angle under
$60^\circ$ squeezed between them.}

\problem{\textbf{SSS: $a = 5$, $b = 7$, $c = 9$; find $C$.}
Three sides is SSS, so rearrange the law of cosines for the angle opposite the
side you name --- here $c$, the longest side.
\begin{align*}
\cos C &= \frac{a^{2} + b^{2} - c^{2}}{2ab} = \frac{25 + 49 - 81}{2(5)(7)}
 = \frac{-7}{70} = -0.1
\end{align*}
A negative cosine means an obtuse angle, and the inverse cosine returns it
directly (no ambiguity, unlike the law of sines).
$\ans{C \approx 95.74^\circ}$}

\problem{\textbf{SAS: $b = 12$, $c = 15$, $A = 38^\circ$; find $a$.}
The angle sits between the two given sides, so the same formula applies with
the letters rotated.
\begin{align*}
a^{2} &= 12^{2} + 15^{2} - 2(12)(15)\cos 38^\circ = 144 + 225 - 360(0.788011) \\
a^{2} &= 369 - 283.684 = 85.316
\end{align*}
$\ans{a \approx 9.24}$}

\problem{\textbf{SSS: $a = 8$, $b = 11$, $c = 6$; find $A$.}
Solve for the angle opposite $a$, so $a^{2}$ is the term that gets subtracted:
\begin{align*}
\cos A &= \frac{b^{2} + c^{2} - a^{2}}{2bc} = \frac{121 + 36 - 64}{2(11)(6)}
 = \frac{93}{132} = 0.704545
\end{align*}
$\ans{A \approx 45.21^\circ}$
\par\smallskip
Check against the figure: $a = 8$ is the middle side, so its angle should be
the middle angle --- and $45.21^\circ$ sits between the angles opposite $6$ and
$11$.}

\problem{\textbf{SAS with area: $a = 9$, $b = 12$, $C = 110^\circ$.}
\textbf{(a)} The included angle is obtuse, so its cosine is negative and the
subtraction becomes an addition --- the third side comes out longer than both
givens, as an obtuse triangle requires.
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 81 + 144 - 2(9)(12)\cos 110^\circ = 225 - 216(-0.342020) \\
c^{2} &= 225 + 73.876 = 298.876
\end{align*}
$\ans{c \approx 17.29}$
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} The area needs no new triangle solving: two sides and the angle
between them give
$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C = \tfrac{1}{2}(9)(12)\sin 110^\circ
= 54(0.939693)$.
$\ans{\text{Area} \approx 50.74}$}

\problem{\textbf{Two ships, $40$ km and $55$ km apart at $70^\circ$.}
The bearings $030^\circ$ and $100^\circ$ differ by $70^\circ$, and that
difference is the angle at the port between the two courses. The two courses and
the line joining the ships form an SAS triangle.
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 40^{2} + 55^{2} - 2(40)(55)\cos 70^\circ = 1600 + 3025 - 4400(0.342020) \\
c^{2} &= 4625 - 1504.89 = 3120.11
\end{align*}
$\ans{c \approx 55.86\ \text{km}}$
\par\smallskip
Note that the ships end up almost exactly as far from each other as the longer
voyage was long --- a useful sense-check that the angle was read as the
\emph{difference} of the bearings.}

\problem{\textbf{SSS: $a = 6$, $b = 10$, $c = 14$.}
\textbf{(a)} Rearranging for the angle opposite $a$ and keeping the fraction
exact:
\begin{align*}
\cos A &= \frac{b^{2} + c^{2} - a^{2}}{2bc} = \frac{100 + 196 - 36}{2(10)(14)}
 = \frac{260}{280}
\end{align*}
Dividing numerator and denominator by $20$ reduces it.
$\ans{\cos A = \frac{13}{14}}$
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Taking the inverse cosine of that exact value:
$\ans{A \approx 21.79^\circ}$
\par\smallskip
A cosine close to $1$ means a small angle, which matches $a = 6$ being much the
shortest side.}

\problem{\textbf{SAS with an obtuse included angle: $b = 23$, $c = 18$,
$A = 124^\circ$.}
\begin{align*}
a^{2} &= 23^{2} + 18^{2} - 2(23)(18)\cos 124^\circ = 529 + 324 - 828(-0.559193) \\
a^{2} &= 853 + 463.01 = 1316.01
\end{align*}
$\ans{a \approx 36.28}$
\par\smallskip
The sign is the whole story here: $\cos 124^\circ$ is negative, so the formula
adds. A student who drops the sign gets about $19.75$ and a triangle that
cannot exist with a $124^\circ$ angle.}

\problem{\textbf{Pilot: $120$ mi, turn, $85$ mi, interior angle $70^\circ$.}
The two legs of the flight and the direct line back to the airfield form a
triangle whose known angle lies between the two known sides.
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 120^{2} + 85^{2} - 2(120)(85)\cos 70^\circ = 14400 + 7225 - 20400(0.342020) \\
c^{2} &= 21625 - 6977.21 = 14647.79
\end{align*}
$\ans{c \approx 121.03\ \text{mi}}$}

\problem{\textbf{SSS: $a = 13$, $b = 14$, $c = 15$; find $B$.}
$B$ is opposite $b$, so $b^{2}$ is the subtracted term:
\begin{align*}
\cos B &= \frac{a^{2} + c^{2} - b^{2}}{2ac} = \frac{169 + 225 - 196}{2(13)(15)}
 = \frac{198}{390} = 0.507692
\end{align*}
$\ans{B \approx 59.49^\circ}$
\par\smallskip
The three sides are nearly equal, so all three angles should land near
$60^\circ$ --- and this one does.}

\problem{\textbf{SAS then an angle: $a = 25$, $b = 32$, $C = 48^\circ$.}
\textbf{(a)} Law of cosines for the side opposite the known angle:
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 625 + 1024 - 2(25)(32)\cos 48^\circ = 1649 - 1600(0.669131) \\
c^{2} &= 1649 - 1070.61 = 578.39
\end{align*}
$\ans{c \approx 24.05}$
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} With all three sides known, use the law of cosines again rather
than the law of sines --- the inverse cosine cannot land on the wrong angle,
while the inverse sine of an obtuse angle returns its acute partner.
\begin{align*}
\cos A &= \frac{b^{2} + c^{2} - a^{2}}{2bc}
 = \frac{1024 + 578.39 - 625}{2(32)(24.05)} = 0.635533
\end{align*}
$\ans{A \approx 50.58^\circ}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Accept the law of sines for (b) as well}, provided the student checks
which of the two candidate angles is correct; here $A$ is acute because it is
not opposite the longest side.}

\problem{\textbf{Survey crew: $18$ km, turn, $26$ km, interior angle
$115^\circ$.}
\textbf{(a)} SAS again, with an obtuse included angle, so the cosine term adds:
\begin{align*}
c^{2} &= 18^{2} + 26^{2} - 2(18)(26)\cos 115^\circ = 324 + 676 - 936(-0.422618) \\
c^{2} &= 1000 + 395.57 = 1395.57
\end{align*}
$\ans{c \approx 37.36\ \text{km}}$
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(b) Reasoning answer --- review by hand.} A model response: the law of
sines pairs each side with the angle opposite it, so using it requires a
side-and-opposite-angle pair to be known. Here the only known angle,
$115^\circ$, sits between the two known sides and its opposite side is exactly
what is being asked for, so every ratio the law of sines could write contains
two unknowns. The law of cosines needs no such pair --- it relates all three
sides to one angle --- so it supplies the missing third side. Once that side is
known there is a complete side-angle pair, and the law of sines becomes the
quicker tool for the two remaining angles.
\par\smallskip
Credit any answer that identifies the missing side-opposite-angle pair as the
obstacle and the third side as what the law of cosines supplies.}
