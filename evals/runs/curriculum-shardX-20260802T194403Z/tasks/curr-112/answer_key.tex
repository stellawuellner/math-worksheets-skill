\problem{$168 \div 4$. \quad Divide from the left. $1$ hundred is smaller than
$4$, so start with $16$ tens: $16 \div 4 = 4$ tens. Multiply and subtract:
$16 - 16 = 0$. Bring down the $8$: $8 \div 4 = 2$ ones. The quotient is
\ans{42}. Check: $42 \times 4 = 168$.}

\problem{$375 \div 5$. \quad $3$ hundreds is smaller than $5$, so start with
$37$ tens: $37 \div 5 = 7$ tens with $2$ left ($5 \times 7 = 35$,
$37 - 35 = 2$). Bring down the $5$ to make $25$: $25 \div 5 = 5$ ones. The
quotient is \ans{75}. Check: $75 \times 5 = 375$.}

\problem{$618 \div 6$. \quad $6 \div 6 = 1$ hundred; $6 - 6 = 0$. Bring down
the $1$: $1$ is smaller than $6$, so write $\mathbf{0}$ in the tens place and
bring down the next digit. That makes $18$: $18 \div 6 = 3$ ones. The quotient
is \ans{103}. The $0$ is not optional --- dropping it gives $13$, which is far
too small, since $13 \times 6 = 78$.}

\problem{Estimating $2{,}835 \div 7$ with the compatible number $2{,}800$:
$28 \div 7 = 4$, so $2{,}800 \div 7 = 400$. Nadia's estimate is \ans{400}.
This also tells her the exact quotient will have $3$ digits.}

\problem{$2{,}835 \div 7$. \quad $28$ hundreds $\div\ 7 = 4$ hundreds;
$28 - 28 = 0$. Bring down the $3$: $3$ is smaller than $7$, so write
$\mathbf{0}$ in the tens place. Bring down the $5$ to make $35$:
$35 \div 7 = 5$ ones. The quotient is \ans{405}, which is very close to the
estimate of $400$ from problem 4 --- a good sign the work is right.}

\problem{$517 \div 4$. \quad $5 \div 4 = 1$ hundred with $1$ left. Bring down
the $1$ to make $11$: $11 \div 4 = 2$ tens with $3$ left ($4 \times 2 = 8$,
$11 - 8 = 3$). Bring down the $7$ to make $37$: $37 \div 4 = 9$ ones with $1$
left ($4 \times 9 = 36$, $37 - 36 = 1$). Since $1$ is smaller than $4$, it stays
as the remainder: \ans{129 \text{ R } 1}. Check:
$4 \times 129 + 1 = 516 + 1 = 517$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Watch for:} an answer written as $129.1$. That is
the remainder pasted on as a decimal digit, which is not what it means --- the
leftover $1$ is $1$ out of $4$, not one tenth.}

\problem{$891 \div 3$. \quad $8 \div 3 = 2$ hundreds with $2$ left. Bring down
the $9$ to make $29$: $29 \div 3 = 9$ tens with $2$ left. Bring down the $1$ to
make $21$: $21 \div 3 = 7$ ones exactly. The quotient is \ans{297}. Check:
$297 \times 3 = 891$.}

\problem{The missing number is the dividend. Division and multiplication undo
each other, so if $\square \div 6 = 143$, then $\square = 143 \times 6$.
Multiply: $143 \times 6 = 858$. The number is \ans{858}. Check by dividing
back: $858 \div 6 = 143$.}

\problem{$4{,}872 \div 8$. \quad $4$ thousands is smaller than $8$, so start
with $48$ hundreds: $48 \div 8 = 6$ hundreds; $48 - 48 = 0$. Bring down the
$7$: $7$ is smaller than $8$, so write $\mathbf{0}$ in the tens place. Bring
down the $2$ to make $72$: $72 \div 8 = 9$ ones. The quotient is \ans{609}.
Check: $609 \times 8 = 4{,}872$.}

\problem{Divide the pencils into groups of $6$: $3{,}275 \div 6$.
$32$ hundreds $\div\ 6 = 5$ hundreds with $2$ left ($6 \times 5 = 30$). Bring
down the $7$ to make $27$: $27 \div 6 = 4$ tens with $3$ left. Bring down the
$5$ to make $35$: $35 \div 6 = 5$ ones with $5$ left. So
$3{,}275 \div 6 = 545$ R $5$. The question asks only for \emph{full} boxes, so
the remainder is ignored here: \ans{545} full boxes.}

\problem{Same division: $3{,}275 \div 6 = 545$ R $5$. This time the question
asks what is left after every full box is packed, and that is exactly the
remainder: \ans{5} pencils. Check with the division equation:
$6 \times 545 + 5 = 3{,}270 + 5 = 3{,}275$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Teaching note:} problems 10 and 11 are the same
division. What changes is what the leftover means --- that decision is the
skill being practised.}

\problem{Two divisions, one after the other.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step 1 --- tickets per class:} $4{,}158 \div 7$.
$41$ hundreds $\div\ 7 = 5$ hundreds with $6$ left ($7 \times 5 = 35$). Bring
down the $5$ to make $65$: $65 \div 7 = 9$ tens with $2$ left. Bring down the
$8$ to make $28$: $28 \div 7 = 4$ ones. Each class gets $594$ tickets.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Step 2 --- tickets per student:} $594 \div 9$.
$5$ is smaller than $9$, so start with $59$: $59 \div 9 = 6$ tens with $5$ left
($9 \times 6 = 54$). Bring down the $4$ to make $54$: $54 \div 9 = 6$ ones.
Each student receives \ans{66} tickets.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Estimate check:} $4{,}158$ is close to $4{,}200$ and
$4{,}200 \div 7 = 600$, so about $600$ per class; $600 \div 9$ is between $60$
and $70$. The answer $66$ fits.}
