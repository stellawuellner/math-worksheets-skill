# AP Calculus — Three Kinds of Evidence About a Limit

Three PDFs are ready: the student worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key, and
a two-page study guide.

**What the worksheet covers.** Ten problems on the AP skill of putting
graphical, numerical and symbolic evidence about a limit together and saying
which one you leaned on. The sheet carries one labelled graph of a function $g$
(shared, and captioned as belonging to problems 3 and 6 only), four
fill-in-the-table problems, four algebraic evaluations, and a synthesis problem
that asks for all three kinds of evidence about the same function.

After a one-problem warm-up the three methods are interleaved — table, graph,
algebra, table, graph, algebra — so your student has to decide which tool the
question calls for rather than repeating one procedure ten times. Difficulty
ramps from a direct-substitution polynomial limit to a compound fraction, a
"find the parameter that makes the limit finite" problem, and finally the
synthesis problem on $p(x)=\frac{x^2+x-6}{x-2}$.

**What the answer key contains.** A worked solution for each problem in numbered
steps that open with *why* that method applies. Every table appears completed,
with each cell boxed, so a parent can grade the numerical work without
recomputing it. Where the graph is involved, the key reads it and then confirms
the same limit algebraically from the branch formula — which is the habit the
topic is really teaching. The key also carries the quick-answer bank and the
curriculum section (LIM-1, LIM-2, with the difficulty range); the course level
prints there and nowhere the student sees it.

**What the study guide contains.** Three teaching sections — symbolic
(factor, cancel, substitute), numerical (close in from both sides), and
graphical (read each side separately) — each with a rule box, a worked example
whose first step states the strategy in words, and a try-it problem with the
answer printed upside down inside the box. A watch-out box closes on the two
errors this unit produces: reading a filled dot as the limit, and treating
$\tfrac00$ as an answer.

**Verification.** 24 machine checks were recomputed independently with SymPy and
all passed — every limit, every table cell, and the parameter value in problem
8. Four items are flagged for manual review, correctly: reading the graph at
$x=1$ and at $x=3$ (problems 3 and 6), the written explanation of why the
one-sided limits in problem 9 do not give a two-sided limit, and the sketch plus
written synthesis in problem 10. Each of those has the expected answer spelled
out in the key with a grading note; they are open-response by nature, not
unverified guesses — the algebraic half of problems 3, 6 and 10 is machine
verified.
