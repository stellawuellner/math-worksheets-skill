\problem{\textbf{One full ten-frame and 4 loose dots. Write the number.}\\
Step 1: the full ten-frame is worth ten, so there is 1 ten.
Step 2: the loose dots are the ones, so there are 4 ones.
Step 3: the tens digit is written first, the ones digit second:
$10 + 4$. The number is $\ans{14}$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} a child who writes 41 has read the loose dots as tens.}

\problem{\textbf{Leo builds 1 ten-stick and 6 ones and writes 61. Write the number
his blocks really show.}\\
Step 1: name the parts --- 1 ten-stick means 1 ten, 6 loose cubes means 6 ones.
Step 2: the tens digit goes in the first (left) place and the ones digit in the
second place, so $10 + 6$ is written with a 1 first and a 6 second.
Step 3: Leo wrote his 6 first, which says 6 tens and 1 one. The blocks really show
$\ans{16}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Nina writes 81 for eighteen. Circle the misplaced digit and write
eighteen correctly.}\\
Step 1: say the number slowly --- eight\emph{teen} means 1 ten and 8 ones.
Step 2: Nina's 8 is sitting in the tens place, so her number says 8 tens and 1 one.
Circle that 8.
Step 3: put the 1 ten first and the 8 ones second: $10 + 8$, so eighteen is
$\ans{18}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Sam builds 1 ten and 3 ones; Ty builds 3 tens and 1 one. Write
$<$ or $>$.}\\
Step 1: build Sam's number --- $10 + 3$ gives thirteen.
Step 2: build Ty's number --- $10 + 10 + 10 + 1$ gives thirty-one.
Step 3: thirteen has only 1 ten and thirty-one has 3 tens, so Sam's number is the
smaller one: $\ans{<}$.\\
\emph{Teaching point:} the same two digits, swapped, are not the same number.}

\problem{\textbf{Ana counts one full ten-frame and 7 more dots and writes 71.
Say why 71 is wrong and write the true number.}\\
Step 1: count the frames --- one frame is full (1 ten) and the second frame has
7 dots (7 ones).
Step 2: 71 would need 7 full ten-frames; there is only one full frame, so 71 is far
too big.
Step 3: 1 ten and 7 ones is $10 + 7$, so the dots show $\ans{17}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Jo swaps the digits of the number made from 1 ten and 9 ones.
Compare Jo's swapped number with the number she meant.}\\
Step 1: the number Jo meant is $10 + 9$, which is nineteen --- 1 ten and 9 ones.
Step 2: swapping the digits puts the 9 in the tens place, giving 9 tens and 1 one,
which is ninety-one.
Step 3: 9 tens is much more than 1 ten, so Jo's swapped number is the bigger one:
$\ans{>}$. The digit in the tens place of Jo's swapped number is the 9.}
