\wstitleblock{Circles, Polygons and Surface --- Diagnosing Radius/Diameter and Area/Perimeter Errors}{Grades 6--7}{}

\noindent\small Two swaps cause most of the wrong answers in this unit. Catch
them before you compute.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Radius or diameter?} $C = 2\pi r = \pi d$ and $A = \pi r^2$.
        The area formula \emph{only} takes a radius, so halve a diameter first.
  \item \textbf{Around or inside?} Circumference and perimeter are lengths (m, cm);
        area is a covering (m$^2$, cm$^2$). The unit is the fastest check.
  \item Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator and round every decimal answer to
        the nearest hundredth. Show the formula you chose before you substitute.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{\textbf{Warm-up.} A circular badge has radius $5$ cm. Find the
circumference of the badge.
\par\vspace*{4.4cm}\answerline{cm}}

\problem{\textbf{Halve it first.} A circular tabletop is $14$ cm across at its
widest point --- that is its diameter. Find the area of the tabletop.
\par\vspace*{4.4cm}\answerline{cm$^2$}}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix.} Rae was asked for the \emph{area} of a circle of
radius $6$ cm. She wrote $A = 2\pi r = 2\pi(6) \approx 37.70$ and gave
$37.70$ cm$^2$ as her answer.
\par\smallskip
Name the formula Rae actually used and say what quantity it measures. Then find
the area she was asked for.
\par\vspace*{4.6cm}\answerline{cm$^2$}}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix.} Marco is buying edging to run all the way around
a rectangular garden bed that is $9$ m long and $4$ m wide. He wrote
$9 \times 4 = 36$ and ordered $36$ m of edging.
\par\smallskip
Name the measurement Marco actually calculated. Then find how many m of edging
the bed really needs.
\par\vspace*{4.6cm}\answerline{m}}

\problem{\textbf{L-shaped plot.} On a coordinate grid, a plot of land has corners
at $(0,0)$, $(10,0)$, $(10,4)$, $(4,4)$, $(4,7)$ and $(0,7)$, joined in that
order. Each grid unit is one metre.
\par\smallskip
A landscaper needs the amount of \emph{ground the plot covers}, not the fence
around it. Find that amount, in square units.
\par\vspace*{5.0cm}\answerline{square units}}

\problem{\textbf{Read the given quantity carefully.} A round rug measures $1.8$ m
from one edge, through the centre, to the opposite edge. Trim is sewn around the
entire outer edge of the rug. How many m of trim are needed?
\par\smallskip
Before substituting, write down whether $1.8$ is a radius or a diameter.
\par\vspace*{4.6cm}\answerline{m}}

\problem{\textbf{Work backwards.} A circular pool is fenced by exactly $44$ m of
railing running around its edge. Find the area of the pool's surface.
\par\smallskip
The number you are given is a length around the circle, so it is not a radius ---
recover the radius from it first, then use the area formula.
\par\vspace*{5.4cm}\answerline{m$^2$}}

\problem{\textbf{Explain.} Circle B has exactly twice the radius of circle A.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why circle B's \emph{circumference} is twice circle A's, but
its \emph{area} is four times circle A's. Use the formulas in your explanation.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Write a two-part habit a classmate could use on every circle problem:
one part about deciding between a length and a covering, one part about checking
the unit of the answer.
\par
\textbf{(c)} Explain how that habit would have caught Rae's mistake in problem 3.
\par\vspace*{6.5cm}
