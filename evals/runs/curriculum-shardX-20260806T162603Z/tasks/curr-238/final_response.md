# Dilations and Similar Figures — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_dilations_curr238.pdf`, 5 pages) — 10 problems, every dilation
  centred at the origin. The sheet moves through four ways of using a dilation:
  dilating coordinates, finding a scale factor from a matching pair of lengths,
  checking that slopes (and therefore parallel sides) survive the dilation, and
  scaling a length in a real situation. Problem 5 gives a coordinate grid to draw the
  image triangle on; problems 3, 6 and 9 are real measurements in centimetres and
  metres, with the unit printed on the answer line.
- **Answer key** (`ak_dilations_curr238.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem showing the
  distance or slope calculation rather than only the result.
- **Study guide** (`ss_dilations_curr238.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections matching the
  four worksheet skills, each with a rule box, a worked example and a try-it.

## What is verified

**6 of the 10 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy — 24 individual checks
across the sheet, because most problems ask for more than one value and each printed
blank has its own check. Four problems are **partly instructor-judged**:

- **Problem 4** — both slopes (2 and 2) are machine-checked; the sentence explaining
  that equal slopes mean parallel segments is judged by you.
- **Problem 5** — the image side length `A'B' = 8` is machine-checked; the drawn
  triangle is judged by you, and the key states the three image vertices
  `A'(-6,-2)`, `B'(2,-2)`, `C'(2,4)` to check the drawing against.
- **Problem 8** — both scale factors (2 along `RS`, 3 along `RT`) are machine-checked;
  the judgement of the student's claim is judged by you.
- **Problem 10** — `KL = 10`, perimeter `24`, image perimeter `36` are all
  machine-checked; the explanation of the perimeter shortcut is judged by you.

That is the same split the key's own "What is verified" note prints, and each
instructor-judged item has a rubric in the key saying what a correct response must
contain.

## Notes for you

- Problem 8 is the deliberate non-example: a figure whose width is doubled and whose
  height is tripled looks plausible but is a stretch, not a dilation, and is not
  similar. If your student answers "yes" there, the two different factors are the
  thing to point at.
- Units: problems 3 and 6 answer in centimetres and problem 9 in metres, and the
  answer lines say so. Coordinate lengths are labelled as grid units.
- Standard covered: 8.G.A across all ten problems.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
