\wstitleblock{Missing Roots and Sign Errors in Quadratic Solutions}{Algebra 1}{}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Two errors account for most lost marks on quadratic equations: reporting an \textbf{incomplete solution set} (one root when there are two), and a \textbf{sign slip} in the quadratic formula.
  \item Unless a problem says otherwise, ``solve'' means \textbf{list every solution}.
  \item Where a worked attempt is shown, the attempt contains exactly one mistake. Find it before you start computing.
  \item Substituting an answer back into the original equation is the check that catches both error types --- use it.
\end{itemize}

\problem[4.5cm]{Solve, and list every solution: \quad $x^2 = 49$}

\problem[7cm]{Dana solves $2x^2 - 32 = 0$ like this: ``$2x^2 = 32$, so $x^2 = 16$, so $x = 4$.''
\par\medskip
(a) Name the mistake in Dana's work.
\par\smallskip
(b) Write the complete solution set. Put it on the answer line.}

\problem[5cm]{Solve, and list every solution: \quad $x^2 - 5x = 0$}

\problem[7cm]{Marco solves $x^2 = 6x$ by dividing both sides by $x$, which gives him $x = 6$.
\par\medskip
(a) Name the error in Marco's method, and say why it costs him a solution.
\par\smallskip
(b) Write the complete solution set. Put it on the answer line.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Solve by factoring, and list every solution: \quad $x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$}

\problem[6cm]{Solve with the quadratic formula, and list every solution: \quad $x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$
\par\smallskip
Write down the values of $a$, $b$ and $-b$ before you substitute.}

\problem[6cm]{Use the quadratic formula on $2x^2 - 7x + 2 = 0$. Give the \emph{larger} of the two solutions, rounded to two decimal places.}

\problem[8cm]{Nadia solves $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$ with the quadratic formula. Her substitution under the square root is correct, but she writes the denominator as $2$ instead of $2a$, and reports $x = 1$ and $x = -6$.
\par\medskip
(a) Name the error in Nadia's work.
\par\smallskip
(b) Write the correct solution set. Put it on the answer line.
\par\smallskip
(c) Explain how substituting one of Nadia's two answers into the original equation would have shown her that something was wrong.}
