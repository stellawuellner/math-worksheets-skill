\problem{\textbf{Expand $3(x+4)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The multiplier outside touches \emph{every} term inside, so
write two products before simplifying: $3 \cdot x$ and $3 \cdot 4$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $3 \cdot x = 3x$ and $3 \cdot 4 = 12$, so the sum is
$3x + 12$.
\ans{3x+12}

\textit{Why:} $a(b+c) = ab + ac$ read left to right.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $5x + 15$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factoring is the same rule read backwards, so first find what
both terms share: $5x = 5 \cdot x$ and $15 = 5 \cdot 3$, so the GCF is 5.

\textbf{Step 2.} Pull the 5 out front and write what is left inside:
$5(x + 3)$.
\ans{5(x+3)}

\textit{Check:} expanding $5(x+3)$ gives $5x + 15$ again.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $-2(x-7)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Treat the subtraction as adding a negative, $x + (-7)$, so the
multiplier $-2$ distributes over two terms and both signs are handled by the
same rule.

\textbf{Step 2.} $-2 \cdot x = -2x$ and $-2 \cdot (-7) = +14$, so the result is
$-2x + 14$.
\ans{-2x+14}

\textit{The common error:} writing $-2x - 14$. A negative times a negative is
positive; the second sign flips.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $4x + 3x + 6$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Like terms must have the identical variable part, so sort the
terms before adding anything: $4x$ and $3x$ are like; 6 has no $x$ at all.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4x + 3x = 7x$, and the 6 is carried along unchanged:
$7x + 6$.
\ans{7x+6}

\textit{Why 6 cannot join:} $7x$ and 6 count different things --- $7x$ depends
on $x$, 6 never does.}

\problem{\textbf{Ana's claim $3(x+4) = 3x+4$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A single substitution can disprove equivalence, so evaluate the
correct expression first and use it as the reference value.

\textbf{Step 2.} At $x = 5$: $3(5+4) = 3 \cdot 9 = 27$.
\ans{27}

\textit{Ana's expression:} $3 \cdot 5 + 4 = 19$. Because $27 \neq 19$, the two
expressions are not equivalent. Her mistake was distributing the 3 to the $x$
only and leaving the 4 untouched; the correct expansion is $3x + 12$.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $12x + 18$ completely.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Completely'' means the terms left inside must share no factor,
so list the factors rather than grabbing the first common one:
$12 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$ and $18 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$, giving a GCF of 6.

\textbf{Step 2.} $12x \div 6 = 2x$ and $18 \div 6 = 3$, so the factored form is
$6(2x+3)$.
\ans{6(2x+3)}

\textit{Why $2(6x+9)$ is not finished:} $6x$ and 9 still share a factor of 3.
Pulling that out as well returns $6(2x+3)$.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $-4(2x - 3y + 1)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Three terms inside means three products, so write the inside as
a sum of signed terms first: $2x + (-3y) + 1$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $-4 \cdot 2x = -8x$, \; $-4 \cdot (-3y) = +12y$, \;
$-4 \cdot 1 = -4$, so the expansion is $-8x + 12y - 4$.
\ans{-8x+12y-4}

\textit{Note:} $-8x$ and $12y$ are not like terms, so nothing collects here.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $2(x+3) + 4x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Nothing can be collected while the parentheses stand, so expand
before combining.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2(x+3) = 2x + 6$, so the expression is $2x + 6 + 4x$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Collect the $x$-terms: $2x + 4x = 6x$, leaving $6x + 6$.
\ans{6x+6}

\textit{Order matters:} adding $2 + 4$ first and writing $6(x+3)$ would be a
different expression --- it equals $6x + 18$.}

\problem{\textbf{Dev's claim $4(2x-5) = 8x-5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Evaluate the correct form first so there is a reference value
to compare against.

\textbf{Step 2.} At $x = 4$: $4(2 \cdot 4 - 5) = 4(8-5) = 4 \cdot 3 = 12$.
\ans{12}

\textit{Dev's expression:} $8 \cdot 4 - 5 = 27$. One disagreement is enough:
$12 \neq 27$, so the expressions are not equivalent. Dev multiplied only the
first term inside; the correct expansion is $8x - 20$.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $8x - 20y$ completely.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The terms carry different letters, so no variable is common ---
only a numeric factor can come out. Compare $8 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2$ with
$20 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$: the GCF is 4.

\textbf{Step 2.} $8x \div 4 = 2x$ and $20y \div 4 = 5y$, and the subtraction sign
stays inside: $4(2x - 5y)$.
\ans{4(2x-5y)}

\textit{Check:} $4 \cdot 2x = 8x$ and $4 \cdot 5y = 20y$, so expanding returns
the original.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand and simplify $-3(4-2x) + 2(x+6)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two sets of parentheses, so distribute each one separately
before any collecting; mixing the steps is where signs get lost.

\textbf{Step 2.} $-3(4-2x) = -12 + 6x$, and $2(x+6) = 2x + 12$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Collect: the $x$-terms give $6x + 2x = 8x$, and the constants
give $-12 + 12 = 0$, so only $8x$ survives.
\ans{8x}

\textit{What is surprising and why:} the constants are exact opposites, so the
expression is a pure multiple of $x$ --- it is 0 when $x$ is 0, no matter what.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: bundling $18x + 24$ stickers.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A bundle size is a common factor, and the largest bundle is the
GCF, so factor rather than guess: $18 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$ and
$24 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$ share $2 \cdot 3 = 6$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $18x \div 6 = 3x$ and $24 \div 6 = 4$, so the factored form is
$6(3x+4)$: six equal bundles of $3x+4$ stickers.
\ans{6(3x+4)}

\textbf{Step 3.} Substitute $x = 2$ into the factored form:
$6(3 \cdot 2 + 4) = 6 \cdot 10 = 60$.
\ans{60}

\textit{Part (c):} the original gives $18 \cdot 2 + 24 = 60$ as well, so the two
agree here. That is strong evidence but not a proof --- two different
expressions can cross at one value. Equivalence means agreeing at \emph{every}
value, which is what the distributive property guarantees.

\textit{The trap:} factoring 6 out of $18x$ only and leaving 24 inside gives
$6(3x+24)$, which is 180 at $x=2$ --- three times too many stickers.}
