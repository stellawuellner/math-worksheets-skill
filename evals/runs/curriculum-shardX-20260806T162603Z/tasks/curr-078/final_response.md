# Finding the Missing Factor — Grade 3 fact fluency (10 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ws_missingfactor_curr078.pdf`** — the student worksheet (4 pages, work space on every problem)
- **`ss_missingfactor_curr078.pdf`** — a 2-page skills summary to keep beside the worksheet
- **`ak_missingfactor_curr078.pdf`** — the full step-by-step answer key (3 pages)

## What the worksheet covers

Every problem asks for the same thing in a different dress: the number that makes a
multiplication equation true, with all totals at 100 or below.

- **Problems 1–3 — arrays and equal groups.** A drawn array of stickers, seedlings and tiles.
  Problem 3 moves the unknown to the *front* of the equation ($\_ \times 3 = 18$), so the
  student has to notice the missing factor is the number of rows, not the row size.
- **Problems 4, 5, 7, 9 — bare equations.** Missing factor in either position, worked from a
  known times-table fact and its related division fact.
- **Problems 6, 8, 10 — story problems** with stated units (boxes, trays, chairs per row).
  The answer line on 6 and 8 prints the unit, because "7" and "7 boxes" are different answers.
- **Problem 10** is the stretch item: solve it, then explain in words how a fact the child
  already knows produces the missing number.

Difficulty ramps 1 → 5 and the three problem types are interleaved after the warm-up, so from
problem 4 on the child has to choose a method rather than repeat one.

## What is verified, and what is not

- **10 of the 11 checks are machine-verified** with SymPy: every numeric answer on the sheet
  was recomputed independently of the answer key, and the build refuses to produce PDFs if any
  disagrees. Nine of the ten problems are therefore fully machine-checked.
- **One response is instructor-judged**: problem 10(b), the written explanation. The Quick
  Answers bank prints `---` for it and the key states exactly what a full-credit answer must
  contain (a named fact or a skip-count, not just the number 8). No machine can grade that, and
  the key does not pretend otherwise.
- Four **common wrong answers** are declared and machine-checked as genuinely wrong, and the key
  prints them: 96 and 448 (multiplying instead of dividing), 15 and 40 (subtracting instead of
  dividing). If your child's answer is one of those numbers, the key tells you which
  misunderstanding produced it.

## The study guide

Three sections — array, known fact, story — each with a rule box, a worked example whose first
step says *why* that method applies, and a try-it problem with the answer printed upside down
inside the box so the child attempts it before checking. A watch-out box flags the most common
error on this topic: multiplying the two visible numbers instead of dividing.

The grade level appears only on the answer key (with the standards codes 3.OA.A.3, 3.OA.A.4 and
3.OA.C.7), never on the pages the child works from.
