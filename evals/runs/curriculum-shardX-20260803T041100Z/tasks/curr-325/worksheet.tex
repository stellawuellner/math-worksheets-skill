\wstitleblock{Choosing a Right-Triangle Method}{High School Geometry}{}

\noindent\textbf{Decide first: two sides means Pythagoras, a 45 or 30 degree
angle means a special triangle, anything else means a trig ratio.}

\smallskip
\noindent\small Every problem below is solved with right-triangle reasoning,
but they do not all want the same tool. Look at \emph{what is given} before you
write anything:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Two sides, find the third} --- Pythagorean theorem
        ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$). No angle needed and none should be used.
  \item \textbf{A $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, or $60^\circ$ acute angle} ---
        special-triangle ratios give an exact answer with no calculator.
  \item \textbf{Any other angle with a side, or two sides and a missing
        angle} --- a trig ratio (SOH-CAH-TOA), or an inverse trig ratio when
        the angle is what is missing.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{On every problem, name the method you chose before you compute}, and
round lengths and angle measures to two decimal places unless the answer is
exact.\normalsize
