Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 7 percent-fluency set.

- **Worksheet** (`ws_pct_part_whole_curr162.pdf`) — 12 problems on the two
  harder percent questions: given a part and a whole, find the **rate**; given a
  part and a rate, find the **whole**. The directions carry two worked patterns
  (one of each) so the student starts from a model rather than a blank page, and
  the sheet then ramps: friendly rates (14 of 40, 18 of 24), missing wholes at
  25% and 70%, the percent equation written out and solved, a rate that lands on
  12.5%, a whole hidden behind 12.5%, a rate needing rounding to the nearest
  tenth, a deposit problem with a follow-on subtraction, and a closing item that
  asks *why* the division shortcut is the same thing as solving the equation. No
  two problems share a skeleton.
- **Answer key** (`ak_pct_part_whole_curr162.pdf`) — Quick Answers bank, a
  generated Curriculum section (6.RP.A.3.c, difficulty 1–5), and full reasoning
  for each item. Problem 10 also carries a "common wrong answer" note: dividing
  60 by 17 instead of 17 by 60 gives about 353%, and a percent above 100 for a
  part of the flock is the tell.
- **Study guide** (`ss_pct_part_whole_curr162.pdf`) — three sections (rate,
  whole, the one equation that covers both), each with a rule, a two-step worked
  example, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box; plus a
  watch-out on dividing the wrong way round.

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 14 responses. **13 are machine-checked**
with SymPy — every rate, every recovered whole, both percent equations solved
from the equation itself rather than from a typed answer, and the rounded rate
checked against the exact value. One misconception trap is machine-checked too:
the gate confirms the wrong method (total ÷ part) produces a number the
problem's own check rejects. **1 response is instructor-judged**: part (b) of
problem 12, the written explanation of why dividing by 0.30 solves the equation.
The key marks it `---` in the bank and states what a correct answer must
contain. All eight study-guide checks (three examples, three try-its) are
machine-verified.
