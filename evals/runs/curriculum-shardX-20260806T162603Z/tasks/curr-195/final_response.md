Here are the three PDFs for the Grades 6–7 interleaved review of multistep
surface-area and scale-design problems.

**Student worksheet (12 problems).** A warm-up on prism and cube surface area
leads into an interleaved mix — blueprint scale conversions, composite areas
from grid coordinates (an L-shaped patio, a T-shaped stage, a house-shaped
sign), a scaled bedroom's real area, wrapping a gift box and rounding up to
whole sheets of paper, an open-top aquarium (no top face!), and an enlarged
logo. The challenge problem designs a 1:20 dollhouse room — model dimensions,
model floor area, and an explanation of why area does not scale by 20. A
value-free reference prism shows how every box is labelled; units print on
every answer line.

**Answer key.** Step-by-step reasoning for every problem, a quick-answers bank
with common-wrong-answer notes under the five trap problems, and a generated
Curriculum section (standards 6.G.A and 7.G.A.1, difficulty range 1–5).

**Study guide (2 pages).** Four sections matching the worksheet's skills —
prism surface area, composite areas, scale-drawing lengths, and the
k-squared rule for scaled areas — each with a rule box, worked example, and
try-it.

**What is verified.** 15 answers are machine-checked with SymPy (every surface
area, composite area via the coordinate shoelace, scale conversion both
directions, and all three numeric parts of the challenge), plus all 8
study-guide examples/try-its. Five planted wrong methods are declared as
misconception traps and machine-proven distinguishable (scaling area only
once, multiplying instead of dividing by a scale, forgetting the k-squared
rule, adding a top to the open tank, using the bounding rectangle). One
response is open — the challenge's part (c) explanation of why area scales by
the square — and is flagged for instructor judgment with guidance in the key.
