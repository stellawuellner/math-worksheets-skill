\problem{Simplify $(6 - 5i)(6 + 5i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The factors are conjugates, so the two middle terms
$+30i$ and $-30i$ cancel and what is left is $36 - 25i^{2}$. Since
$i^{2} = -1$, that last term is $+25$, giving $36 + 25$. \ans{61}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(9 + 4i) - (3 - 7i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The minus sign belongs to both parts of the second number:
$9 + 4i - 3 + 7i$. Collecting, $(9 - 3) + (4 + 7)i$. \ans{6 + 11i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A student wrote $(8 + 2i) - (5 - 6i) = 3 - 4i$. (a) Correct it.
(b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Distributing the minus sign to both parts gives
$8 + 2i - 5 + 6i = (8 - 5) + (2 + 6)i$. \ans{3 + 8i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The student distributed the minus sign
to the real part only. The imaginary term of the second number is $-6i$, and
subtracting it must give $+6i$, not $-6i$; the printed line kept $-6i$. Full
credit requires naming that undistributed minus sign, not just writing the
corrected value.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{10}{1 + 3i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply above and below by the conjugate $1 - 3i$. The
denominator becomes $1 + 9 = 10$, so
$\dfrac{10(1 - 3i)}{10} = 1 - 3i$. \ans{1 - 3i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A student divided $\dfrac{14 + 8i}{3 - 2i}$ and got $\dfrac{26 + 52i}{5}$.
(a) Correct it. (b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The numerator is right:
$(14 + 8i)(3 + 2i) = 42 + 28i + 24i + 16i^{2} = 26 + 52i$. The denominator is
where it goes wrong: $(3 - 2i)(3 + 2i) = 9 - 4i^{2} = 9 + 4 = 13$.
Dividing, $\dfrac{26 + 52i}{13}$. \ans{2 + 4i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The conjugate product in the
denominator is a \emph{sum} of squares, because $-2i \cdot 2i = -4i^{2} = +4$.
The student wrote $9 - 4 = 5$ instead of $9 + 4 = 13$. Full credit names the
denominator as the wrong value and gives $13$.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A student expanded $(5 - 2i)^{2} = 5^{2} + (2i)^{2} = 21$.
(a) Correct it. (b) Name the error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} A square is a product of two binomials, so the middle
term must appear:
\begin{align*}
  (5 - 2i)^{2} &= 25 - 10i - 10i + 4i^{2} \\
               &= 25 - 20i - 4
\end{align*}
\ans{21 - 20i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The student squared each term
separately and dropped the middle term $-20i$. Full credit names the missing
middle term, or writes out all four products. Note that the student's real part
happened to be right, which is exactly why a bare value check would not have
caught this.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(3 - 4i) - (-6 + i) + (2 - 5i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Subtracting $-6 + i$ reverses \emph{both} of its parts, so
the expression becomes $3 - 4i + 6 - i + 2 - 5i$. Collecting,
$(3 + 6 + 2) + (-4 - 1 - 5)i$. \ans{11 - 10i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A student's three-line solution to $\dfrac{20 + 15i}{2 + i}$ ends at
$4 + 3i$. (a) Give the corrected quotient. (b) Which line is first wrong, and
what rule does it break?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Both the numerator and the denominator gain the
conjugate factor. Numerator:
$(20 + 15i)(2 - i) = 40 - 20i + 30i - 15i^{2} = 55 + 10i$. Denominator:
$(2 + i)(2 - i) = 4 + 1 = 5$. Dividing, $\dfrac{55 + 10i}{5}$. \ans{11 + 2i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} Line 2 is the first wrong one. Line 1
is correct: multiplying by $\dfrac{2 - i}{2 - i}$ is multiplying by $1$. But in
line 2 the factor $2 - i$ was applied to the denominator only, and the numerator
was carried down unchanged --- which changes the value of the fraction. Full
credit names line 2 and states that both parts of a fraction must gain the same
factor. A student who checks by multiplying $4 + 3i$ back by $2 + i$ and getting
$5 + 10i$ instead of $20 + 15i$ has produced the evidence and should be credited
for it. Naming line 3 earns nothing: line 3 follows correctly from line 2.
