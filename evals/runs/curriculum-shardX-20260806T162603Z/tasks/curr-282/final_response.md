# Graphing Quadratics from Vertex or Standard Form — three PDFs

- **Study guide (`ss_parabolagraph_curr282.pdf`, 2 pages)** — three sections: reading a graph
  straight off **vertex form** $y=a(x-h)^2+k$; getting the axis of symmetry and vertex out of
  **standard form** $y=ax^2+bx+c$; and seeing every parabola as the parent graph $y=x^2$
  **moved, flipped and stretched**. Each has a rule box, a worked example whose first step
  says why that route is the shortest, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the
  box. A watch-out box covers the single most common slip: reading $(x+2)^2$ as a shift right.
- **Worksheet (`ws_parabolagraph_curr282.pdf`, 6 pages)** — 12 problems on a clean ramp: three
  in vertex form, three in standard form, three transformation problems, then three
  graph-it-yourself problems with printed coordinate grids, ending with a synthesis challenge
  that asks for the axis, the vertex, the intercepts, the sketch and a symmetry check. No two
  problems share a skeleton.
- **Answer key (`ak_parabolagraph_curr282.pdf`, 3 pages)** — Quick Answers bank, a "What is
  verified" note, a generated Curriculum section, and worked reasoning for every part.

## What is verified, honestly

This sheet asks for **27 responses** across 12 problems, and the split is unusually wide
because graphing is partly a drawing task.

- **17 are machine-checked.** Every intercept, every axis of symmetry and every vertex
  $y$-value was recomputed with SymPy, and each printed boxed answer in the key was separately
  confirmed against the verified value. Five problems (1, 3, 4, 5, 6) are fully machine-checked
  end to end.
- **10 are instructor-judged**, and they fall into three honest groups:
  - **sketches** — 10(b), 11(c) and 12(d). A drawn curve cannot be checked by a computer.
  - **written reasoning** — 2(b) ("does it open up or down, and how does the equation tell
    you"), 9(a) (name the three transformations in order) and the symmetry-check half of
    12(d).
  - **equations the student writes** — 7(a) and 8(a), where the answer is a formula rather
    than a number. These have a definite right answer, and the key states it exactly; they are
    flagged because nothing recomputed the student's version.

  Every one of these is marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank, listed by problem number in the
  key's "What is verified" note, and given a specific grading rubric in the worked solution —
  for example, 11(c) requires the peak to be plotted at $(1,4)$, not merely a downward curve
  through the right crossings.

Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`: there is no printed answer without either a machine check or
a written rubric.

## Notes for the adult

- The grade level prints on the answer key only.
- Problems 7 and 8 are where sign errors live. If your child writes $(x-3)^2+2$ for "3 left
  and 2 down", the study guide's watch-out box is the fix.
- On the three grid problems, the useful question is not "is the curve right" but "did you
  plot the vertex before drawing". The key's rubrics are written around that.
