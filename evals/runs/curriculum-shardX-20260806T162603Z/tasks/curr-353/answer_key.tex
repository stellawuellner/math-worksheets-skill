\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{8 + 6i}{2i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The denominator is purely imaginary, so its conjugate is
$-2i$. Multiplying top and bottom by $-2i$ clears the $i$ from the bottom.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{8 + 6i}{2i} \cdot \frac{-2i}{-2i}
    &= \frac{-16i - 12i^2}{-4i^2} = \frac{12 - 16i}{4}
\end{align*}
Dividing both parts by $4$ gives the standard form. \ans{3 - 4i}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{50}{3 + 4i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The conjugate of $3 + 4i$ is $3 - 4i$, and
$(3 + 4i)(3 - 4i) = 9 + 16 = 25$.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{50}{3 + 4i} \cdot \frac{3 - 4i}{3 - 4i}
    &= \frac{150 - 200i}{25} = 6 - 8i
\end{align*}
\ans{6 - 8i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{9 - 12i}{3i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The conjugate of $3i$ is $-3i$; the denominator becomes
$(3i)(-3i) = -9i^2 = 9$.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{9 - 12i}{3i} \cdot \frac{-3i}{-3i}
    &= \frac{-27i + 36i^2}{9} = \frac{-36 - 27i}{9}
\end{align*}
\ans{-4 - 3i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{16 + 11i}{2 + 3i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply by the conjugate $2 - 3i$. Denominator:
$(2 + 3i)(2 - 3i) = 4 + 9 = 13$.
\begin{align*}
  (16 + 11i)(2 - 3i) &= 32 - 48i + 22i - 33i^2 = 65 - 26i \\
  \frac{65 - 26i}{13} &= 5 - 2i
\end{align*}
\ans{5 - 2i}

\problem{An AC source supplies $20$ volts across a component of impedance
$3 + i$ ohms. Current equals voltage divided by impedance. Find the current in
amps.}

\textbf{Solution:} The conjugate of $3 + i$ is $3 - i$, and
$(3 + i)(3 - i) = 9 + 1 = 10$.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{20}{3 + i} \cdot \frac{3 - i}{3 - i}
    &= \frac{60 - 20i}{10} = 6 - 2i
\end{align*}
The real part is the in-phase current and the imaginary part records the phase
shift, which is why the answer is reported as a complex number rather than a
single magnitude. \ans{6 - 2i \text{ amps}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{-8 + 6i}{1 - 2i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply by the conjugate $1 + 2i$; the denominator becomes
$1 + 4 = 5$.
\begin{align*}
  (-8 + 6i)(1 + 2i) &= -8 - 16i + 6i + 12i^2 = -20 - 10i \\
  \frac{-20 - 10i}{5} &= -4 - 2i
\end{align*}
\ans{-4 - 2i}

\problem{A drawing program multiplies each point by $3 + 4i$. One transformed
point landed at $-7 + 24i$. (a) Which point did it start from? (b) Plot that
point and label the axes.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Undo a multiplication by dividing. The conjugate of
$3 + 4i$ is $3 - 4i$, and $(3 + 4i)(3 - 4i) = 9 + 16 = 25$.
\begin{align*}
  (-7 + 24i)(3 - 4i) &= -21 + 28i + 72i - 96i^2 = 75 + 100i \\
  \frac{75 + 100i}{25} &= 3 + 4i
\end{align*}
Check by multiplying back: $(3 + 4i)(3 + 4i) = 9 + 24i - 16 = -7 + 24i$. \ans{3 + 4i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The plotted point should sit $3$ units
along the real (horizontal) axis and $4$ units up the imaginary (vertical) axis,
with both axes labelled. Grade the plot against whatever value the student wrote
in part (a), so a correct plot of a wrong quotient still earns the plotting mark.

\problem{Voltage $26 + 13i$ volts, current $2 + 3i$ amps. Impedance equals
voltage divided by current. Find the impedance in ohms.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply by the conjugate $2 - 3i$; the denominator becomes
$4 + 9 = 13$.
\begin{align*}
  (26 + 13i)(2 - 3i) &= 52 - 78i + 26i - 39i^2 = 91 - 52i \\
  \frac{91 - 52i}{13} &= 7 - 4i
\end{align*}
A negative imaginary part means the reactance is capacitive. \ans{7 - 4i \text{ ohms}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{29i}{5 + 2i}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A purely imaginary numerator changes nothing about the
method: multiply by the conjugate $5 - 2i$, whose product with the denominator
is $25 + 4 = 29$.
\begin{align*}
  29i(5 - 2i) &= 145i - 58i^2 = 58 + 145i \\
  \frac{58 + 145i}{29} &= 2 + 5i
\end{align*}
\ans{2 + 5i}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{3 + i}{1 - i} + \dfrac{3 - i}{1 + i}$, then justify why
the result had to be real.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Handle each quotient with its own conjugate. Both
denominators become $1 + 1 = 2$.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{3 + i}{1 - i} &= \frac{(3 + i)(1 + i)}{2} = \frac{2 + 4i}{2} = 1 + 2i \\
  \frac{3 - i}{1 + i} &= \frac{(3 - i)(1 - i)}{2} = \frac{2 - 4i}{2} = 1 - 2i
\end{align*}
Adding the two results, the imaginary parts cancel. \ans{2}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit justification says the
two quotients are conjugates of one another --- the second fraction is the first
with the sign of every imaginary part reversed --- and that adding a number to
its own conjugate cancels the imaginary parts and leaves twice the real part.
Showing $1 + 2i$ and $1 - 2i$ and stating that their imaginary parts are
opposites earns full credit. Restating the answer $2$ with no reason earns none.
