\problem{6 pencils cost 90 cents. Cents per pencil?}

\textbf{Solution:} Unit rate: $90 \div 6 = 15$ cents per pencil. \ans{15}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Cocoa to milk is 2 to 5. How much cocoa for 20 cups of milk?}

\textbf{Solution:} A ratio table works well: the milk column goes from 5 to 20,
a multiplier of 4. Cocoa scales the same way: $2 \times 4 = 8$ cups. \ans{8}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Split 42 cards in the ratio $3{:}4$; find the larger share.}

\textbf{Solution:} Tape diagram: $3 + 4 = 7$ boxes hold 42 cards, so one box is
$42 \div 7 = 6$ cards. The larger share has 4 boxes: $4 \times 6 = 24$ cards.
\ans{24}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Brand A: 4 lb for 12 dollars. Brand B: 5 lb for 10 dollars. Compare per
pound.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Brand A: $12 \div 4 = 3$ dollars per pound. \ans{3}

(b) Brand B: $10 \div 5 = 2$ dollars per pound. \ans{2}

(c) Comparing unit prices, $3 > 2$, so Brand B is cheaper per
pound. \ans{3 > 2\text{, Brand B}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Mia: 20 pages in 8 minutes. Noah: 27 pages in 12 minutes. Compare over
24 minutes.}

\textbf{Solution:} 24 minutes is a common amount for both readers.

(a) Mia: $24 \div 8 = 3$ blocks of 8 minutes, so $20 \times 3 = 60$ pages.
\ans{60}

(b) Noah: $24 \div 12 = 2$ blocks, so $27 \times 2 = 54$ pages. \ans{54}

(c) Over the same 24 minutes Mia reads more, so Mia is
faster. \ans{60 > 54\text{, Mia}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Team A splits 60 dollars in $2{:}3$; team B splits 63 dollars in
$4{:}5$. Compare the larger shares.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Team A: $2 + 3 = 5$ parts, one part $= 60 \div 5 = 12$,
larger share $= 3 \times 12 = 36$ dollars. \ans{36}

(b) Team B: $4 + 5 = 9$ parts, one part $= 63 \div 9 = 7$, larger share
$= 5 \times 7 = 35$ dollars. \ans{35}

(c) $36 > 35$: team A's larger share wins by a dollar. \ans{36 > 35\text{, team A}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Hiker A: 9 km per 2 h. Hiker B: 12 km per 3 h. Compare after 6 hours.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Hiker A: $6 \div 2 = 3$ blocks, $9 \times 3 = 27$ km.
\ans{27}

(b) Hiker B: $6 \div 3 = 2$ blocks, $12 \times 2 = 24$ km. \ans{24}

(c) $27 > 24$, so hiker A is ahead. \ans{27 > 24\text{, hiker A}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{8 yogurts for 600 cents, or one yogurt for 80 cents. Which is the
better buy?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Pack: $600 \div 8 = 75$ cents per yogurt. \ans{75}

(b) $75 < 80$, so the pack is the better buy. \ans{75 < 80\text{, the pack}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Shelter A: cats to dogs $5{:}3$, 16 animals. Shelter B: 9 cats. Which
has more cats?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Shelter A: $5 + 3 = 8$ parts for 16 animals, one part $=
2$, cats $= 5 \times 2 = 10$. \ans{10}

(b) $10 > 9$, so shelter A has more cats. \ans{10 > 9\text{, shelter A}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Syrup to soda is $3{:}5$; the bowl holds 32 cups. Cups of syrup? Choose
and defend a representation.}

\textbf{Solution:} With a tape diagram: $3 + 5 = 8$ boxes hold 32 cups, one box
$= 32 \div 8 = 4$ cups, syrup $= 3 \times 4 = 12$ cups. \ans{12}

\textbf{Representation choice (instructor judges):} Any of the three tools earns
credit with a matching reason — e.g. a tape diagram because the 32 cups is a
total split into ratio parts; a table or double number line scaling $(3,5)$ up
to a syrup-plus-soda total of 32 also works.

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Express: 210 km in 3 h. Local: 140 km in 2 h. Compare the speeds.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Express: $210 \div 3 = 70$ km per hour. \ans{70}

(b) Local: $140 \div 2 = 70$ km per hour. \ans{70}

(c) The unit rates are equal — the bigger distance does not mean a faster
train, because the times differ too. \ans{70 = 70}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Recipe A: juice to water $2{:}3$. Recipe B: $3{:}5$. Which is stronger,
and how much juice for 40 cups of recipe A?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Compare juice-per-water fractions:
$\frac{2}{3} \approx 0.667$ and $\frac{3}{5} = 0.6$, so recipe A is
stronger. \ans{\tfrac{2}{3} > \tfrac{3}{5}\text{, recipe A}}

(b) Tape diagram for recipe A: $2 + 3 = 5$ boxes hold 40 cups, one box $=
40 \div 5 = 8$ cups, juice $= 2 \times 8 = 16$ cups. \ans{16}

(c) \textbf{Explanation (instructor judges):} Full credit names a comparison
tool for part (a) — unit rates or the fractions — and a total-splitting tool
for part (b) — tape diagram, table, or double number line — with reasons
matching how each was used.
