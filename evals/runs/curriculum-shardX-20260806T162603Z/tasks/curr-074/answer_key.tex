\problem{12 cookies shared equally into 3 gift bags. How many cookies in each bag?}

\textbf{Solution:} The 3 is the number of bags (the group count); the question asks for the group size. $12 \div 3 = 4$, because $3 \times 4 = 12$. Answering 3 would just repeat the number of bags. \ans{4}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{15 tennis balls, 5 balls in each can. How many cans?}

\textbf{Solution:} Here the 5 is the group \emph{size}; the question asks for the group \emph{count}. $15 \div 5 = 3$, because $5 \times 3 = 15$. The coach fills 3 cans --- 5 is how many go \emph{in} each can, not the answer. \ans{3}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{20 grapes equally onto 4 plates. How many grapes on each plate?}

\textbf{Solution:} The 4 counts plates (groups); the answer must count grapes per plate (group size). $20 \div 4 = 5$, because $4 \times 5 = 20$. \ans{5}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Jo says each of the 6 boxes holds 6 crayons (24 crayons in all).}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Jo repeated a number that was already given --- the 6 boxes --- instead of dividing. The problem gives the group count; the question asks for the group size, which must come from $24 \div 6$. A correct answer says Jo never divided, and that 6 counts boxes, not crayons.

\textbf{Solution (b):} $24 \div 6 = 4$, because $6 \times 4 = 24$. Each box holds 4 crayons. \ans{4}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{28 wheels, 4 wheels per wagon. How many wagons?}

\textbf{Solution:} The 4 is the group size (wheels per wagon); the question asks how many groups. $28 \div 4 = 7$, because $4 \times 7 = 28$. The toy maker builds 7 wagons. \ans{7}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{18 stickers shared equally between 2 friends: how many each, and what does the answer count?}

\textbf{Solution:} $18 \div 2 = 9$, because $2 \times 9 = 18$. \ans{9}

The 9 counts stickers \emph{per friend} --- the group size. The group count (2 friends) was given in the story, so a correct second answer names ``the number of stickers each friend gets.''

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ben: ``$30 \div 5 = 6$, so each pouch holds 6 marbles.''}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Ben's arithmetic is right but his \emph{meaning} is wrong. He divided by the group size (5 marbles per pouch), so the quotient 6 counts \emph{pouches}, not marbles per pouch. Each pouch holds the given 5 marbles. A correct answer says the 6 is the group count, not the group size.

\textbf{Solution (b):} Ben fills $30 \div 5 = 6$ pouches. \ans{6}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Two stories for $24 \div 4$, then the quotient.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Sharing story --- the 4 must be the number of groups, e.g. ``24 treats are shared equally among 4 friends. How many treats does each friend get?'' The answer, 6, counts treats \emph{in each group}.

\textbf{Solution (b):} Grouping story --- the 4 must be the size of each group, e.g. ``24 treats are packed 4 to a box. How many boxes are filled?'' The answer, 6, counts \emph{groups}.

\textbf{Solution (c):} $24 \div 4 = 6$, because $4 \times 6 = 24$. \ans{6}
