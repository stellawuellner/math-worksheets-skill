\problem{$f(x)=2x+5$, $g(x)=x^2-3$. (a) $g(4)$. (b) $f(g(4))$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Run the inner machine first: $g(4) = 4^2 - 3 = 16 - 3$.
\ans{g(4) = 13}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Feed that output into $f$: $f(13) = 2(13) + 5 = 26 + 5$.
\ans{f(g(4)) = 31}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=3x-1$, $g(x)=x+4$. Find $(f \circ g)(x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute the whole rule for $g$ into $f$'s formula, keeping
it bracketed:
\[ f(g(x)) = 3\bigl(x+4\bigr) - 1 = 3x + 12 - 1 . \]
\ans{(f \circ g)(x) = 3x + 11}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Same $f$ and $g$. (a) $(g \circ f)(x)$. (b) Do the orders agree?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Now $f$ runs first, and its output goes into $g$:
\[ g(f(x)) = \bigl(3x-1\bigr) + 4 = 3x + 3 . \]
\ans{(g \circ f)(x) = 3x + 3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} No: $3x+11$ and $3x+3$ are
different rules, so $f \circ g$ and $g \circ f$ are different functions. The
cause is which operation happens first. In $f \circ g$ the $+4$ happens before
the multiplication by $3$, so the $4$ gets tripled and contributes $12$; in
$g \circ f$ it is added after the tripling and contributes only $4$. Full credit
needs both the comparison and that reason.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=x^2$, $g(x)=x-6$. (a) $(f \circ g)(2)$. (b) $(g \circ f)(2)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $g$ first: $g(2) = 2 - 6 = -4$; then $f(-4) = (-4)^2$.
\ans{16}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $f$ first: $f(2) = 4$; then $g(4) = 4 - 6$. \ans{-2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=x^2+1$, $g(x)=2x-3$. Find $(f \circ g)(x)$ expanded.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute, then expand the square carefully:
\begin{align*}
  f(g(x)) &= \bigl(2x-3\bigr)^2 + 1 \\
          &= 4x^2 - 12x + 9 + 1 .
\end{align*}
\ans{4x^2 - 12x + 10}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=2x+5$, $g(x)=x-1$. Solve $(f \circ g)(x) = 17$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Build the composed rule first, then solve the ordinary
equation:
\[ f(g(x)) = 2\bigl(x-1\bigr) + 5 = 2x + 3 , \qquad 2x + 3 = 17 . \]
So $2x = 14$. Check: $g(7) = 6$ and $f(6) = 17$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x+1}$, $g(x)=\dfrac{2}{x}$. Find $(f \circ g)(x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute $\dfrac{2}{x}$ for every $x$ in $f$'s rule, then
clear the compound fraction by multiplying top and bottom by $x$:
\[ f(g(x)) = \frac{\dfrac{2}{x}}{\dfrac{2}{x} + 1}
           = \frac{\dfrac{2}{x} \cdot x}{\left(\dfrac{2}{x} + 1\right)x}
           = \frac{2}{2 + x} . \]
\ans{\dfrac{2}{x+2}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=x^3$, $g(x)=x+1$. (a) $(f \circ g)(x)$ expanded.
(b) $(g \circ f)(2)$. (c) Why order changes the function.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $f(g(x)) = \bigl(x+1\bigr)^3$, and expanding gives
\ans{x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $f$ first: $f(2) = 8$; then $g(8) = 8 + 1$. \ans{9}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} At $x = 2$ part (a) gives
$(2+1)^3 = 27$, while part (b) gives $9$. In $f \circ g$ the $1$ is added
\emph{before} cubing, so it is inside the cube; in $g \circ f$ it is added
\emph{after}, so it is merely added on. The rules themselves differ ---
$x^3+3x^2+3x+1$ against $x^3+1$ --- so the disagreement is not a coincidence at
one input: the two orders build different functions. Full credit identifies which
operation runs first in each order.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x)=x^2$, $g(x)=x+3$. Solve $(f \circ g)(x) = 49$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The composed rule is $\bigl(x+3\bigr)^2$, so
$(x+3)^2 = 49$ and $x + 3 = \pm 7$. Both signs give a genuine solution, since
squaring accepts either.
\ans{x = 4 \text{ or } x = -10}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Challenge: $f(x)=\sqrt{x-1}$, $g(x)=x^2+5$. (a) $(f \circ g)(x)$.
(b) Its value at $x=3$. (c) Why every real input works.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Substitute the inner rule under the radical:
\[ f(g(x)) = \sqrt{\bigl(x^2+5\bigr) - 1} = \sqrt{x^2+4} . \]
\ans{\sqrt{x^2+4}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\sqrt{3^2+4} = \sqrt{13} = 3.60555\ldots$ \ans{3.61}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} On its own $f$ needs
$x - 1 \ge 0$, so it fails for every input below $1$. But the composition never
hands $f$ such an input: $g$ outputs $x^2+5$, which is at least $5$ for every
real $x$, comfortably above $1$. After the subtraction the radicand is $x^2+4$,
which is at least $4$ and so never negative. Full credit connects the inner
function's range to the outer function's domain requirement.
