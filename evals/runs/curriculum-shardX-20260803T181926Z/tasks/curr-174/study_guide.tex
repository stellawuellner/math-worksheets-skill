\sstitleblock{Algebraic Expressions --- Distributing and Combining Safely}

\skillheading{Skill 1 --- Distribute to every term, sign included}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Rule:} $k(p+q) = kp + kq$ --- the multiplier outside reaches
\emph{every} term inside, and it keeps its own sign while doing it.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Signs:} negative times negative is positive, so
$-2(x-3) = -2x + 6$. Write the sign as part of the number you are
distributing, and the second term takes care of itself.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{The substitution test:} put any number in both versions. Equivalent
expressions always agree; one disagreement proves the step was illegal.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad Write $6(x-4)$ without parentheses.
\step{Two terms sit inside the parentheses, so distributing must produce two
products --- the subtraction sign belongs to the second one.}
\step{$6 \cdot x = 6x$ and $6 \cdot (-4) = -24$, and at $x = 5$ both versions
give $6$, which is the check.}
$\ans{6x - 24}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad Write $-2(x+7)$ without parentheses.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{-2x - 14}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- Combine only like terms}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Like terms} have exactly the same letter part: $7a$ and $-2a$ are like;
$5x$ and $3y$ are not, and $5a$ and $13$ are not.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Rule:} add or subtract the coefficients and keep the letter part
unchanged --- $7a - 2a = 5a$, never $5a^2$ and never $5$.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Unlike terms simply stay put.} $5x+3y$ is already simplified; an
expression is allowed to have more than one term, and forcing it into one is the
most common error on this page.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad Simplify $4b + 7 - b + 2$.
\step{Sort the terms into groups with the same letter part first, carrying each
sign with its own term, so nothing unlike ever meets.}
\step{The $b$ terms give $4b - b = 3b$; the constants give $7 + 2 = 9$; and
$3b$ and $9$ are unlike, so the work stops there.}
$\ans{3b + 9}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad Simplify $9m - 3 - 4m + 8$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{5m + 5}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Factor out, then distribute back}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Factoring is distributing in reverse:} find the greatest common factor
of the terms, write it outside, and write what is left inside.\par
\smallskip
$kp + kq = k(p+q)$. Divide EVERY term by the common factor --- leaving one term
undivided is the same mistake as distributing to only one term.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Always check:} distribute your answer back. If it does not rebuild the
original expression exactly, the factoring is wrong and you will see it at once.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad Factor $8x + 20$.
\step{Both terms share a numerical factor, so the greatest common factor comes
out front and each term is divided by it.}
\step{The common factor is $4$; dividing gives $8x \div 4 = 2x$ and
$20 \div 4 = 5$, and distributing back returns $8x + 20$ $\checkmark$.}
$\ans{4(2x + 5)}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad Factor $9x + 12$, then distribute back to check.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{3(3x + 4)}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} the two errors are opposites. Distributing to only the
first term does too little; merging unlike terms does too much. One
substitution catches either one in about ten seconds.
\end{watchoutbox}
