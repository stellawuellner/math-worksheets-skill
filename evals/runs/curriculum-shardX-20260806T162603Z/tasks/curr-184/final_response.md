Here are the three PDFs for the Grades 6–7 inequalities practice set, focused on
the one mistake this topic is famous for: forgetting to reverse the inequality
symbol after multiplying or dividing by a negative.

**Student worksheet (8 problems).** A short warm-up (one positive-coefficient
inequality) leads into one-step and two-step inequalities with negative
coefficients, then three error-analysis items: two find-and-fix problems showing
another student's incorrect work (Priya's missing sign flip; Deshaun's
arithmetic slip), and a temperature word problem that ends by asking the student
to explain *why* the symbol reversed. Every problem has marked work space and an
answer blank.

**Answer key.** Full step-by-step reasoning for every problem, a quick-answers
bank for fast grading, a "common wrong answers" note on the trap problems, and a
generated Curriculum section (standard 7.EE.B.4, difficulty range 1–4).

**Study guide (2 pages).** Three sections matching the worksheet's skills —
one-step inequalities, two-step inequalities, and catching a missing sign flip —
each with a rule box, a short worked example, and a try-it with the answer
printed upside-down for self-checking.

**What is verified.** 10 answers are machine-checked with SymPy (every solution
set, the least-integer answer on problem 3, and the boundary value in problem
7), and all 6 study-guide examples/try-its are machine-checked. Two planted
wrong results are declared as misconception traps and machine-proven to be
distinguishably wrong. Three responses are genuinely open — the two "name the
error" explanations (problems 6a, 7a) and the "why did the symbol flip"
explanation (problem 8b) — and are flagged for instructor judgment, with grading
guidance printed in the key. One note: the verifier's trap mechanism does not
support the `inequality` answer type itself, so the direction-flip error in
problem 6 is verified through the full solution-set check rather than a
declared trap; the value-changing errors (problems 3 and 7) carry real traps.
