\problem{\textbf{Tree from a ground distance --- elevation, solve for a side.}

\textbf{Given:} horizontal distance $40$ ft (adjacent to the angle), angle of
elevation $32^\circ$. \textbf{Wanted:} the tree height $h$, which is opposite
that angle.

\textbf{Choose the ratio.} Opposite and adjacent are the two sides in play, so
tangent is the tool: $\tan 32^\circ = \dfrac{h}{40}$.

\textbf{Solve.} $h = 40\tan 32^\circ = 40(0.624869\ldots) = 24.9948\ldots$
\ans{24.99\ \text{ft}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $21.20$ ft --- from $40\sin 32^\circ$, which
treats the $40$ ft ground distance as the hypotenuse. On level ground the
hypotenuse is the slanted line of sight, never the ground.}

\problem{\textbf{Boat from a lighthouse --- depression, solve for a side.}

\textbf{Given:} height $85$ m, angle of depression $12^\circ$.
\textbf{Wanted:} the horizontal distance $d$ from the foot of the lighthouse.

\textbf{Move the angle into the triangle.} The angle of depression is measured
from the horizontal at the top. That horizontal is parallel to the sea, so the
angle of elevation from the boat to the lamp equals it: $12^\circ$ (alternate
interior angles). Inside the right triangle, $85$ m is opposite $12^\circ$ and
$d$ is adjacent.

\textbf{Choose the ratio and solve.}
$\tan 12^\circ = \dfrac{85}{d} \Rightarrow d = \dfrac{85}{\tan 12^\circ}
= \dfrac{85}{0.212556\ldots} = 399.8936\ldots$
\ans{399.9\ \text{m}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $18.07$ m --- multiplying by the tangent instead
of dividing. A sanity check kills it: a $12^\circ$ sight line is very shallow,
so the boat must be far out, not a fifth of the lighthouse's height away.}

\problem{\textbf{Ramp angle --- elevation, solve for the angle.}

\textbf{Given:} rise $3$ ft (opposite), run $20$ ft (adjacent).
\textbf{Wanted:} the angle $\theta$ itself.

\textbf{Choose the ratio.} Two sides known, angle wanted --- so build the ratio,
then undo it with the inverse function:
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{20} = 0.15$.

\textbf{Solve.} $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.15) = 8.5308\ldots^\circ$
\ans{8.5^\circ}

\textit{Note for grading:} $\tan^{-1}$ is the inverse tangent, not
$1/\tan$. A student who computes $1/\tan(0.15)$ gets nothing resembling an
angle. (Real ramps are built at about $4.8^\circ$, so this one is steep.)}

\problem{\textbf{Write the ratio --- no solving.}

\textbf{Given:} the top of the tower is $24$ m above eye level (opposite the
angle of elevation) and $45$ m away horizontally (adjacent).

\textbf{Set up.} By definition, $\tan\theta =
\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{24}{45}$.

\textbf{Reduce.} Both are divisible by $3$:
\ans{\tfrac{8}{15}}

\textit{Why this matters:} the ratio is the exact answer. Turning it into
$\theta \approx 28.07^\circ$ throws away exactness and was not asked for.}

\problem{\textbf{Kite on a taut string --- elevation, hypotenuse given.}

\textbf{Given:} string length $150$ ft --- the \emph{hypotenuse}, because the
string is the line of sight --- and an angle of elevation of $28^\circ$.
\textbf{Wanted:} the height, opposite that angle.

\textbf{Choose the ratio.} Opposite with hypotenuse means sine, not tangent:
$\sin 28^\circ = \dfrac{h}{150}$.

\textbf{Solve.} $h = 150\sin 28^\circ = 150(0.469472\ldots) = 70.4207\ldots$
\ans{70.42\ \text{ft}}

\textit{Teaching note:} problems 1 and 5 look identical in words. The
difference is which side the given length is --- ground distance (adjacent, use
tangent) versus taut string (hypotenuse, use sine).}

\problem{\textbf{Plane and airport --- depression, solve for a side.}

\textbf{Given:} altitude $3200$ ft, angle of depression $15^\circ$.
\textbf{Wanted:} ground distance $d$ from the point directly beneath the plane.

\textbf{Move the angle into the triangle.} As in problem 2, the angle of
elevation from the airport equals the angle of depression from the plane:
$15^\circ$. The altitude is opposite it; $d$ is adjacent.

\textbf{Solve.} $d = \dfrac{3200}{\tan 15^\circ} = \dfrac{3200}{0.267949\ldots}
= 11942.56\ldots$
\ans{11943\ \text{ft}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $857$ ft --- multiplying instead of dividing.
That would put the airport closer than a quarter mile while the plane is still
$3200$ ft up, which no $15^\circ$ glide can do.}

\problem{\textbf{Flagpole measured from eye level --- elevation with an offset.}

\textbf{Given:} eye height $1.6$ m, horizontal distance $12$ m, angle of
elevation $38^\circ$. \textbf{Wanted:} the height of the pole \emph{above the
ground}.

\textbf{Locate the triangle.} The angle is measured at the eye, so the right
triangle sits entirely \emph{above eye level}: its horizontal leg is $12$ m and
its vertical leg is the part of the pole above eye level, call it $y$.

\textbf{Solve the triangle.}
$y = 12\tan 38^\circ = 12(0.781286\ldots) = 9.3754\ldots$ m.

\textbf{Return to the question.} The pole also runs from the ground up to eye
level, so add that back: $1.6 + 9.3754\ldots = 10.9754\ldots$
\ans{10.98\ \text{m}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $9.38$ m --- stopping at the triangle instead of
answering the question asked. The $1.6$ m is the one number on this sheet that
does not belong inside a trig ratio.}

\problem{\textbf{Two boats from one cliff --- two triangles, shared leg.}

\textbf{Given:} cliff height $50$ m; angles of depression $35^\circ$ (nearer
boat) and $20^\circ$ (farther boat). \textbf{Wanted:} the distance between the
boats.

\textbf{Set up two triangles.} Both are right triangles with the same vertical
leg of $50$ m, and both angles transfer to the boats as angles of elevation. Let
$d_{20}$ and $d_{35}$ be the horizontal distances from the foot of the cliff.

\textbf{Solve each.}
\begin{align*}
d_{20} &= \frac{50}{\tan 20^\circ} = \frac{50}{0.363970\ldots} = 137.3739\ldots \text{ m}\\
d_{35} &= \frac{50}{\tan 35^\circ} = \frac{50}{0.700207\ldots} = 71.4074\ldots \text{ m}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Combine.} The boats are on the same side and in line with the cliff, so
the gap is the difference: $137.3739\ldots - 71.4074\ldots = 65.9665\ldots$
\ans{65.97\ \text{m}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $186.60$ m --- subtracting the angles first
($35^\circ - 20^\circ = 15^\circ$) and working a single triangle. Angles cannot
be subtracted that way: the two sight lines start at the same point but land on
different triangles, so the \emph{distances} are what get subtracted.

\textit{Check the smaller angle gives the larger distance:} a shallower look
reaches further out, and $137.37 > 71.41$ confirms it.}
