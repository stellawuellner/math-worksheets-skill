Here are three PDFs on ordering three quantities from least to greatest — an
8-problem worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key, and a two-page study
guide.

**Worksheet** (`ws_orderthree_curr013.pdf`, 3 pages). Every problem gives the
quantities in a real setting with the unit named, and nothing extra to wade
through:

- Problem 1: a small table of sticker counts (5, 8, 3).
- Problem 2: three dot cards — the child counts the pictures first, then
  orders the counts. This is the one problem where the numbers are not printed.
- Problems 3, 5, 6: jars of water in cups, minutes of piano practice, ribbon
  lengths in centimetres. Each says its unit, so the child can see why the
  three numbers are comparable at all.
- Problem 4: a two-quantity comparison (7 books and 10 books) with `<`, `>`
  or `=` — the building block the ordering rests on.
- Problems 7 and 8: order three apple baskets and say how you knew which was
  least; and order three crayon boxes where **two of the counts are the same**
  (6, 8, 6), then explain what you did with the tie. Ruled lines are provided
  for the written part.

Seven of the eight problems put three amounts in order, which is the requested
focus. Each problem has its own answer blanks and real work space.

**Answer key** (`ak_orderthree_curr013.pdf`, 2 pages). Each problem is restated
and worked: the reasoning is always "count up and take each number as you
reach it", plus a note where the unit matters (problem 3) or where the day
labels must not be ordered instead of the minutes (problem 5). A Quick Answers
strip and a Curriculum block (K.CC.C.6, K.CC.C.7) sit at the top.

**Study guide** (`ss_orderthree_curr013.pdf`, 2 pages). Three sections —
ordering three amounts, counting a picture then ordering, and comparing two
amounts — each with a rule box, a worked example, and a try-it whose answer is
printed upside down inside the box.

**How much of this is machine-checked.** 6 of the 8 problems are fully verified
with SymPy: every ordering and the one comparison symbol were recomputed and
matched to the printed key. The other 2 responses are the "explain" halves of
problems 7 and 8. They are genuinely open, so they are flagged rather than
claimed: the Quick Answers strip prints `---` for them, the "What is verified"
note names problems 7 and 8, and the key carries a grading rubric for each,
including what earns half credit. The ordering halves of 7 and 8 (3, 6, 9 and
6, 6, 8) are machine-checked like the rest.
