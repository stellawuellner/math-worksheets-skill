Here are the three PDFs for the limits practice set — study guide first, then the
worksheet, then the answer key.

**Study guide (`ss_limits_curr451.pdf`, 2 pages).** Three sections, each with a
rule box, a worked mini-example, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down
inside the box: factoring and cancelling a common factor, rationalizing with the
conjugate, and reading a table as evidence before doing the algebra. The
watch-out box flags the error that wrecks most conjugate problems —
writing $\sqrt{x+4}$ as $\sqrt{x}+2$.

**Worksheet (`ws_limits_curr451.pdf`, 4 pages).** Eight problems, all on the
requested focus: evaluating algebraic limits that give $0/0$ by factoring or by
rationalizing. Problem 1 opens with a numerical table so the model and the
notation sit side by side; problem 6 is the difference quotient for $y=x^3$ at
$x=2$, which is where this skill is heading; problem 7 pairs a table with a
written justification; problem 8 is the challenge, with the radical in the
denominator instead of the numerator. Difficulty ramps 1 → 4. Every problem has
4.4–6.2 cm of work space, which is why the sheet runs four pages rather than two
— the space is the point.

**Answer key (`ak_limits_curr451.pdf`, 3 pages).** Full worked solutions, not
answers only: each one shows the substitution that produces $0/0$, the rewriting
step, and the cancelled form with its domain restriction. A Quick Answers bank
sits at the top for fast grading, plus a generated Curriculum block (AP Calculus
LIM-1, difficulty 1–4) and a "Common wrong answers" block.

**What is verified.** The sheet carries nine graded responses. **Eight are
machine-checked** — every limit was recomputed independently with SymPy and
compared against the printed key, so a wrong value could not have reached the
PDF. **One is instructor-judged: problem 7(b)**, the written explanation of why
a table of four values is not a proof. That one is marked `---` in the Quick
Answers bank, and the answer key states what a correct response must contain
(the finitely-many-samples point, ideally naming the cancellation as what does
prove it) along with what not to accept.

Two misconception traps are declared and machine-checked as genuinely
distinguishable from the right answer, and they print in the key's "Common wrong
answers" block: on problem 4, cancelling the $x^2$ terms across the subtraction
(gives 1 instead of 5/6); on problem 6, expanding $(2+h)^3$ as $8+h^3$ (gives 0
instead of 12). If you see those numbers on the page you know exactly which step
went wrong.
