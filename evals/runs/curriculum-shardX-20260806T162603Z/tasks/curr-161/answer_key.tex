\problem{Hundredths grid: 30 of 100 parts shaded, garden of 200 plants. How
many plants?}

\textbf{Solution:} Each part of the grid is one hundredth of the garden:
$200 \div 100 = 2$ plants per part. Thirty parts are shaded, so
$30 \times 2 = 60$ plants. In notation, $\dfrac{30}{100} \times 200 = 60$. \ans{60}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Hundredths grid: 45 of 100 parts shaded, shelf of 60 books. How many
books?}

\textbf{Solution:} One part stands for $60 \div 100 = 0.6$ books, so 45 parts
stand for $45 \times 0.6 = 27$ books. In notation,
$\dfrac{45}{100} \times 60 = 27$. \ans{27}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Tape diagram: 350 seats in 10 strips. Find $10\%$ and $30\%$ of the
seats.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The tape is cut into 10 equal strips, so one strip is
$350 \div 10 = 35$ seats. That one strip is $10\%$. \ans{35}

(b) Thirty percent is three of those strips: $3 \times 35 = 105$ seats.
Checking with notation, $\dfrac{30}{100} \times 350 = 105$. \ans{105}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Rack of 84 jackets, $25\%$ blue. Write the percent as a decimal, then
multiply.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) A percent is a number of hundredths, so divide by 100:
$25 \div 100 = 0.25$. \ans{0.25}

(b) Multiply the decimal by the quantity: $0.25 \times 84 = 21$ blue
jackets. \ans{21}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Pizza night cost 60 dollars. Build $15\%$ from ten-percent strips.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) One ten-percent strip is $60 \div 10 = 6$
dollars. \ans{6}

(b) Five percent is half of a ten-percent strip: $6 \div 2 = 3$
dollars. \ans{3}

(c) Fifteen percent is one whole strip plus a half strip:
$6 + 3 = 9$ dollars. Checking, $\dfrac{15}{100} \times 60 = 9$. \ans{9}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{School of 250 students, $8\%$ absent. How many students are absent?}

\textbf{Solution:} Eight percent as a decimal is $8 \div 100 = 0.08$.
Multiplying, $0.08 \times 250 = 20$ students. In fraction form the same
computation reads $\dfrac{8}{100} \times 250 = 20$. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Hundredths grid for a 400 gram bag of trail mix, 35 parts shaded for
raisins.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The 100 parts share the bag evenly, so one part is
$400 \div 100 = 4$ grams. \ans{4}

(b) The raisins are 35 parts: $35 \times 4 = 140$ grams. In notation,
$\dfrac{35}{100} \times 400 = 140$. \ans{140}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Hiking club of 45 members. Find $10\%$ and $60\%$ using ten-percent
strips.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) One strip is $45 \div 10 = 4.5$ members. A half member
is fine here --- the strip is a step on the way, not a head count. \ans{4.5}

(b) Sixty percent is six strips: $6 \times 4.5 = 27$ members. Checking,
$\dfrac{60}{100} \times 45 = 27$. \ans{27}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Repair bill of 320 dollars with a $7.5\%$ service charge.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Divide the percent by 100 and the decimal point moves
two places left: $7.5 \div 100 = 0.075$. \ans{0.075}

(b) Multiply: $0.075 \times 320 = 24$ dollars of service
charge. \ans{24}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Maya claims $20\%$ of 150 dollars beats $25\%$ of 100 dollars because
150 is bigger. Compute both, then judge the reasoning.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $\dfrac{20}{100} \times 150 = 0.2 \times 150 = 30$
dollars. \ans{30}

(b) $\dfrac{25}{100} \times 100 = 0.25 \times 100 = 25$ dollars. \ans{25}

(c) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The conclusion happens to be right --- 30
dollars is more than 25 dollars --- but the stated reason is not. A percent of
a quantity depends on \emph{both} numbers, and a larger starting amount can
easily lose to a larger percent: $20\%$ of 150 dollars is 30 dollars, while
$25\%$ of 200 dollars would be 50 dollars from a start that is larger again.
Full credit needs the comparison of the two computed amounts \emph{and} a
statement that comparing only the two starting amounts is not enough to decide.
