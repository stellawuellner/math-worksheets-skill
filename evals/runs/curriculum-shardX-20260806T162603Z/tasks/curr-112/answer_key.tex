\problem{Divide $84 \div 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $8$ tens $\div\ 4 = 2$ tens; $4$ ones $\div\ 4 = 1$ one. Check:
$21 \times 4 = 84$. \ans{21}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $96 \div 6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $6$ goes into $9$ once with $3$ left, so write $1$ in the tens place and carry the
$3$ tens down: $36 \div 6 = 6$. Check: $16 \times 6 = 96$. \ans{16}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $258 \div 6$ with partial quotients.}

\textbf{Solution:} Take out an easy chunk first:
\[ 6 \times 40 = \boxed{240}, \qquad 258 - 240 = \boxed{18} \]
Now finish the leftover: $18 \div 6 = \boxed{3}$. Add the partial quotients: $40 + 3 = 43$. Check:
$43 \times 6 = 258$. \ans{43}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $475 \div 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $8$ does not go into $4$, so start with $47$: $8 \times 5 = 40$, leaving $7$; bring
down the $5$ to make $75$; $8 \times 9 = 72$, leaving $3$. The remainder $3$ is smaller than $8$, so
the division is finished. Check: $59 \times 8 + 3 = 472 + 3 = 475$. \ans{59} \quad remainder \ans{3}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$3{,}142 \div 6$: (a) round, (b) estimate, (c) exact.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The hundreds digit of $3142$ is $1$, below $5$, so it rounds down. \ans{3000}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $3000 \div 6 = 500$, so expect a quotient a little over five hundred. \ans{500}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $6$ into $31$ goes $5$ times ($30$), leaving $1$; bring down $4$ to make $14$;
$6$ goes twice ($12$), leaving $2$; bring down $2$ to make $22$; $6$ goes $3$ times ($18$), leaving
$4$. The estimate of $500$ sits just below the exact quotient, as expected after rounding down.
\ans{523} \quad remainder \ans{4}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $2{,}568 \div 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $8$ into $25$ goes $3$ times ($24$), leaving $1$; bring down $6$ to make $16$;
$8$ goes twice, leaving $0$; bring down $8$; $8$ goes once. Check:
$321 \times 8 = 2568$. \ans{321}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $618 \div 6$. (a) The quotient. (b) Why must the zero be written?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $6$ into $6$ goes once, leaving $0$; bring down $1$ --- $6$ does not go into
$1$, so the tens digit of the quotient is $0$; bring down $8$ to make $18$, and $6$ goes $3$ times.
Check: $103 \times 6 = 618$. \ans{103}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer explains that there is no whole ten of
groups left after the hundred, so a $0$ has to hold the tens place and keep the $1$ in the hundreds
column and the $3$ in the ones column. Model answer: ``$6$ won't go into $1$, so I write $0$ above
that digit. Without it the answer would read $13$, which is nowhere near $618 \div 6$.'' Giving only
the quotient, or saying the zero appears because a digit was skipped, is not full credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $5{,}207 \div 9$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $9$ into $52$ goes $5$ times ($45$), leaving $7$; bring down $0$ to make $70$;
$9$ goes $7$ times ($63$), leaving $7$; bring down $7$ to make $77$; $9$ goes $8$ times ($72$),
leaving $5$. Check: $578 \times 9 + 5 = 5202 + 5 = 5207$. \ans{578} \quad remainder \ans{5}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$4{,}825 \div 5$: (a) round, (b) estimate, (c) exact.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The hundreds digit of $4825$ is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so it rounds up.
\ans{5000}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $5000 \div 5 = 1000$. \ans{1000}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $5$ into $48$ goes $9$ times ($45$), leaving $3$; bring down $2$ to make $32$;
$5$ goes $6$ times ($30$), leaving $2$; bring down $5$ to make $25$; $5$ goes exactly $5$ times.
No remainder. The exact quotient is just under the estimate of $1000$, which is right because the
dividend was rounded up. \ans{965}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $4{,}872 \div 7$ with partial quotients.}

\textbf{Solution:} Take out a large easy chunk first:
\[ 7 \times 600 = \boxed{4200}, \qquad 4872 - 4200 = \boxed{672} \]
Then finish the leftover: $672 \div 7 = \boxed{96}$ (because $7 \times 90 = 630$ and
$7 \times 6 = 42$). Add the partial quotients: $600 + 96 = 696$. Check:
$696 \times 7 = 4872$. \ans{696}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$1{,}908$ divided by a one-digit number gives exactly $212$. What is the divisor?}

\textbf{Solution:} This is division read backwards. If $1908 \div \square = 212$ then
$212 \times \square = 1908$, so the divisor is $1908 \div 212$. Estimating: $212 \times 9 = 1908$
exactly, while $212 \times 8 = 1696$ is too small. \ans{9}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $7{,}236 \div 4$. (a) The quotient. (b) How could you tell in advance it has four digits?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $4$ into $7$ goes once ($4$), leaving $3$; bring down $2$ to make $32$;
$4$ goes $8$ times, leaving $0$; bring down $3$ --- $4$ does not go into $3$, so write $0$; bring down
$6$ to make $36$, and $4$ goes $9$ times. Check: $1809 \times 4 = 7236$. \ans{1809}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer gives a reason available before
dividing. Model answer 1: ``$4$ goes into the leading $7$ at least once, so the first digit of the
quotient sits above the thousands place, and a quotient that starts in the thousands has four
digits.'' Model answer 2: ``About $7000$ shared four ways is somewhere between $1000$ and $2000$, so
the answer has four digits.'' Simply stating $1809$ is not full credit --- the question asks for the
reasoning that comes first.
