Here are three PDFs for comparing written numerals from 1 through 10 — a
10-problem practice worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key, and a two-page
study guide.

**Worksheet** (`ws_comparenumerals_curr012.pdf`, 3 pages). Ten problems that
ramp from a single comparison to checking someone else's work:

- Problems 1–3, 6: write `<`, `>` or `=` between two numerals (including the
  equal case, 6 and 6, which is the one children most often skip past).
- Problems 4 and 7: count on one / count back one, then compare the number
  with its neighbour — this is where the counting-order idea behind the
  symbol gets used rather than just recited.
- Problems 5 and 8: put three numerals in order from least to greatest.
- Problems 9 and 10: two find-and-fix items. Someone has written `4 > 9`, and
  someone has "ordered" 8, 6, 3 from least to greatest. Each asks for the
  correction *and* for the child to say how they know. There are ruled lines
  for the written answer; a child who is not writing sentences yet can dictate
  it or draw dot groups, which the key accepts.

Every problem has real work space and its own answer blank. No grade level is
printed anywhere a child sees it.

**Answer key** (`ak_comparenumerals_curr012.pdf`, 2 pages). Each problem is
restated with the reasoning written out — the reason is always the same one
("count up; the number you say later is the bigger one"), so a parent can
repeat one explanation rather than eight. A Quick Answers strip at the top is
for fast checking, and a Curriculum block lists the standards (K.CC.C.6,
K.CC.C.7) and the difficulty range.

**Study guide** (`ss_comparenumerals_curr012.pdf`, 2 pages). Four sections —
comparing two numbers, one more and one less, ordering three numbers, and
checking a comparison someone else wrote. Each has a rule box, one worked
example, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the box, so
the child attempts it before checking.

**How much of this is machine-checked.** 8 of the 10 problems are fully
verified with SymPy: every symbol, every ordering and both count-on /
count-back answers were recomputed and matched against the printed key. The
remaining 2 responses are the "explain how you know" halves of problems 9 and
10. Those are genuinely open, so they are flagged for you rather than claimed
as verified: the Quick Answers strip prints `---` for them, the "What is
verified" note names problems 9 and 10 by number, and the key gives a grading
rubric listing which reasons earn full credit, which earn half, and which earn
none. The numeric halves of 9 and 10 (`4 < 9` and the order 3, 6, 8) are
machine-checked like the rest.
