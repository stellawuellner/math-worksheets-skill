\problem{\textbf{Trail mix, 3 cups oats to 2 cups raisins --- complete the
table, then find the raisins for 12 cups of oats.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A table is the right tool because the question asks for
several equivalent ratios at once, not just one. Each new column comes from
multiplying \emph{both} parts by the same number.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6$ cups of oats is $3 \times 2$, so the raisins double:
\ans{4}

\textbf{Step 3.} $9$ cups of oats is $3 \times 3$, so the raisins triple:
\ans{6}

\textbf{Step 4.} $12$ cups of oats is $3 \times 4$, so multiply the raisins by
four: \ans{8}

\textit{What to look for:} the student should name the multiplier ($\times 4$
for the last column), not just fill in the number.}

\problem{\textbf{Five notebooks for 12.50 dollars --- cost of one.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The question asks for the cost of a single item, which is the
definition of a unit rate: divide the total by the number of items.

\textbf{Step 2.} $12.50 \div 5 = 2.50$ dollars each. \ans{2.50}

\textit{Why a unit rate here:} once you know the price of one, any quantity is
a single multiplication --- which is exactly what problem 5 will need.}

\problem{\textbf{Printer: 14 pages every 4 minutes --- complete the table.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Every column is the same ratio, so find the multiplier from
the minutes row and apply it to the pages row.

\textbf{Step 2.} $8$ minutes is $4 \times 2$, so the pages double:
\ans{28}

\textbf{Step 3.} $12$ minutes is $4 \times 3$: \ans{42}

\textbf{Step 4.} $20$ minutes is $4 \times 5$: \ans{70}

\textit{Alternative route:} the unit rate is $14 \div 4 = 3.5$ pages per
minute, and $3.5 \times 20 = 70$. Both representations are correct --- the
table is faster for the easy multiples, the unit rate for the awkward ones.}

\problem{\textbf{Map: 4 cm represents 5 km. How many cm for 15 km?}

\textbf{Step 1.} One unknown value and one known ratio is exactly the shape a
proportion handles, so set the two ratios equal.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{x}{15} = \dfrac{4}{5}$. Multiplying both sides by
$15$ gives $x = 15 \cdot \dfrac{4}{5}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $15 \div 5 = 3$, and $3 \times 4 = 12$. \ans{x = 12}

\textit{Check:} $12$ cm to $15$ km reduces to $4$ cm to $5$ km, the given
ratio.}

\problem{\textbf{Better buy: 5 for 12.50 dollars, or 6 for 17.40 dollars?}

\textbf{Step 1.} The two offers have different pack sizes, so they cannot be
compared as they stand. Put both in the same form --- cost per notebook.

\textbf{Step 2.} Corner store: $12.50 \div 5 = 2.50$ dollars each.
Supermarket: $17.40 \div 6 = 2.90$ dollars each.

\textbf{Step 3.} Written as a comparison of the two rates:
\ans{2.50 < 2.90}

The corner store is the better buy: $2.50$ per notebook is less than $2.90$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} the supermarket, from noticing it sells more
notebooks. More items for more money says nothing until both are per-one.}

\problem{\textbf{174 miles on 6 gallons --- miles per gallon.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Per gallon'' names the unit: divide the miles by the
gallons.

\textbf{Step 2.} $174 \div 6 = 29$. \ans{29}

\textit{Check by scaling back:} $29 \times 6 = 174$, the distance given.}

\problem{\textbf{Sam extended 2 to 5 by writing 4 to 7. Fix it.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Equivalent ratios are made by \emph{multiplying} both
parts by the same number, not by adding. Adding $2$ to both parts changes how
many times bigger one part is than the other: $5$ is $2.5$ times $2$, but $7$
is only $1.75$ times $4$. The mix would come out weaker.

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} From $2$ cups of concentrate to $4$ cups is a multiplier
of $2$, so the water must also double: $5 \times 2$. \ans{10}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $7$ --- the additive mistake this problem is
built to catch. A quick test: does the ratio $4$ to $7$ reduce to $2$ to $5$?
It does not.}

\problem{\textbf{Soup: 8 servings uses 20 oz. How many oz for 14 servings?}

\textbf{Step 1.} $14$ is not a whole-number multiple of $8$, so a table would
take several awkward steps --- a proportion handles it in one.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{x}{14} = \dfrac{20}{8}$, so
$x = 14 \cdot \dfrac{20}{8} = 14 \cdot 2.5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $14 \times 2.5 = 35$ ounces. \ans{x = 35}

\textit{Same answer, other route:} the unit rate is $20 \div 8 = 2.5$ ounces
per serving.}

\problem{\textbf{Runner A: 9 km in 40 min. Runner B: 7 km in 32 min. Who is
faster?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Neither the distances nor the times match, so a direct
comparison is impossible until both are in the same form. Kilometres per
minute works.

\textbf{Step 2.} A: $9 \div 40 = 0.225$ km per minute.
B: $7 \div 32 = 0.21875$ km per minute.

\textbf{Step 3.} Comparing the two rates: \ans{0.225 > 0.21875}

Runner A is faster --- more kilometres in each minute.

\textit{Alternative representation:} minutes per kilometre works too, but then
the \emph{smaller} number wins ($4.44$ against $4.57$). Say which form you
used before deciding which direction is better.}

\problem{\textbf{3 gallons cover 1050 sq ft. How many gallons for 1750 sq ft?}

\textbf{Step 1.} $1750$ is not a neat multiple of $1050$, so go through the
unit rate: coverage per one gallon.

\textbf{Step 2.} $1050 \div 3 = 350$ square feet per gallon.

\textbf{Step 3.} $1750 \div 350 = 5$ gallons. \ans{x = 5}

\textit{Check:} $5 \times 350 = 1750$, the coverage needed.}

\problem{\textbf{Team A won 6 of 9; Team B won 10 of 15. Better ratio?}

\textbf{Step 1.} The two teams have played different numbers of games, so the
win counts cannot be compared directly. Reduce each ratio to lowest terms.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6$ to $9$ divides by $3$, giving $2$ to $3$. $10$ to $15$
divides by $5$, also giving $2$ to $3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The two ratios are the same, so neither team is ahead:
\ans{6/9 = 10/15}

\textit{What makes this hard:} Team B has won more games, so the intuitive
answer is Team B. Reducing both ratios is what settles it --- both teams win
two of every three.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: Recipe R is 2 concentrate to 5 water; Recipe S is 3
concentrate to 8 water.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} ``Stronger'' means more concentrate for the same amount
of water, so compare concentrate per cup of water: $2 \div 5 = 0.4$ and
$3 \div 8 = 0.375$.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Recipe R carries more concentrate in every cup of water,
so it is the stronger mix. Written as a comparison of the two ratios:
\ans{2/5 > 3/8}

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Recipe R: $35$ cups of water is $5 \times 7$, so multiply
the concentrate by seven as well: $2 \times 7$. \ans{14}

\textbf{Step 4 (c).} Recipe S: $24$ cups of water is $8 \times 3$, so
$3 \times 3$. \ans{9}

\textit{Why three different tools appear in one problem:} part (a) needs a
comparison of two rates, parts (b) and (c) need a table-style scale-up, and
each is chosen because of the numbers in front of you --- which is the whole
point of the sheet.}
