# Finding the Sign Errors in Parabola Transformations — three PDFs

- **Study guide (`ss_parabolasigns_curr284.pdf`, 2 pages)** — three sections, each aimed at one
  place a sign goes wrong: which way the bracket moves the graph (and why $(x+3)$ means
  *left*), the minus in front and the number outside (order of operations decides both), and
  checking a finished transformation with a single point or with the symmetry of the two
  intercepts. Rule box, worked example with an explicit strategy step, and a try-it in each;
  a watch-out box names the single commonest wrong answer on the topic.
- **Worksheet (`ws_parabolasigns_curr284.pdf`, 4 pages)** — 8 problems ramped 1 to 5. Three
  are find-and-fix items where a named student's work is shown containing exactly one error
  (Mia drops the minus in front of a square; Owen adds instead of subtracting when undoing
  $x+3$; the synthesis item asks which sign students most often reverse). The rest are direct
  problems whose numbers were chosen so that the canonical sign error lands on a visibly
  different answer.
- **Answer key (`ak_parabolasigns_curr284.pdf`, 3 pages)** — Quick Answers bank, a "What is
  verified" note, a **Common wrong answers** block, a generated Curriculum section, and worked
  solutions that say which step fails, not just what the right number is.

## What is verified

The sheet asks for **13 responses** across 8 problems.

- **8 are machine-checked.** Every intercept set and every substitution was recomputed with
  SymPy, and each boxed answer in the key was separately confirmed against the verified value.
  Four problems (1, 2, 3, 5) are fully machine-checked.
- **5 are instructor-judged: 4(a), 6(a), 7(a), 8(a) and 8(c)** — the "name the error", "write
  the equation" and "explain what each sign does" parts. These are written responses, so they
  are marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank, named in the key's "What is verified" note, and
  each carries a specific rubric. The rubrics are deliberately strict about *where* the error
  is: on 6(a), for instance, Owen's first three lines are correct and a response that condemns
  the whole method has not found the mistake.

Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`.

## The traps are machine-checked

**Eight misconception traps** were declared and every one was proved *distinguishably wrong*
before the PDFs were allowed to compile — that is, the sheet's own check rejects it. That
includes both planted wrong results (Mia's $16$ and Owen's $x = 5, 1$), so the numbers printed
in those stems are derived from the wrong method rather than typed by hand. All eight print in
the answer key under "Common wrong answers", which turns a wrong answer into a diagnosis: if
your child answers $-5$ on problem 1, they read $x-5$ as a shift to the left.

## Notes for the adult

- The grade level prints on the answer key only.
- If only one idea survives this sheet, make it the study guide's question: *what value of $x$
  makes the bracket zero?* That question never gives the wrong direction; reading the sign does.
