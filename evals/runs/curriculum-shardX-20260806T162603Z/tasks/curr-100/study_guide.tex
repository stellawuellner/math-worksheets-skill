\skillheading{Skill 1 --- Reading a Bar Graph or Line Plot}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Match the question to the move:}\par
``How many \emph{are there} of one kind?'' $\Rightarrow$ read one bar or count one column.\par
``How many \emph{altogether}?'' $\Rightarrow$ add every bar or column.\par
``How many \emph{more} than?'' $\Rightarrow$ subtract one from the other.\par
On a line plot the labels are the values and the $\times$ marks are the things counted.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A graph shows red $7$, blue $4$, green $2$. How many more red than blue?
\step{The words how many more compare two groups, so this is a subtraction and I only need two of the three bars.}
\step{Read the two bars and subtract the smaller from the larger: $7 - 4$.}

more red than blue $= \ans{3}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Same graph (red $7$, blue $4$, green $2$). How many are there altogether?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{13}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- Testing a Shape's Attributes}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Test one attribute at a time.}\par
\textbf{Square corners:} the corner of a card fits exactly. A square and a rectangle have four; a
rhombus and a trapezoid have none.\par
\textbf{Parallel sides:} two sides that stay the same distance apart. A square, rhombus and rectangle
each have two pairs; a trapezoid has one.\par
Having four sides is not an attribute that decides anything --- every quadrilateral has four.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad How many square corners does a rhombus have?
\step{The question names one attribute, so I test only the corners and put the side lengths out of my mind.}
\step{A rhombus is a square pushed over: two of its corners are sharp and two are blunt, so a card corner fits none of them.}

square corners on a rhombus $= \ans{0}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad How many pairs of parallel sides does a trapezoid have?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{1}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Using a Shape Fact Inside a Data Question}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Two steps, in this order:}\par
(1) Use the shape rule to decide which groups belong to the question.\par
(2) Only then add or subtract those groups' numbers.\par
Doing step 2 first is the usual mistake: adding every bar answers ``how many altogether,'' which is
almost never the question being asked.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A box holds $5$ squares, $4$ rectangles and $2$ trapezoids. How many
quadrilaterals is that?
\step{Before adding anything I check which groups fit the word in the question, because a quadrilateral is any shape with four straight sides.}
\step{All three kinds have four sides, so all three groups count: $5 + 4 + 2$.}

quadrilaterals $= \ans{11}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad From the same box ($5$ squares, $4$ rectangles, $2$ trapezoids), how many
shapes have four square corners?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{9}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} read the question twice before you add. ``How many altogether,'' ``how many
quadrilaterals'' and ``how many have four square corners'' use the same graph and give three
different answers.
\end{watchoutbox}
