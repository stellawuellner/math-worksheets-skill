# Choosing a Model from the Evidence — Algebra 1 (12 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_models_curr300.pdf`, 6 pages) — 12 problems on deciding whether a
  situation is linear, quadratic, or piecewise *from the evidence*, then using the model.
  The first three problems are blocked one family at a time (table with constant first
  differences, table with constant second differences, a price rule that changes at a
  break point); after that the three families rotate every problem, so your student has
  to choose the method rather than repeat it. The set finishes with a synthesis problem
  where a quadratic revenue rule and a linear cost rule meet at two break-even points.
  Difficulty ramps 1 → 5.
- **Answer key** (`ak_models_curr300.pdf`, 4 pages) — full reasoning for every problem,
  not just answers: the differences are actually computed, the equations are solved line
  by line, and each "which family?" answer states the evidence a correct response needs.
  A Quick Answers bank and a Curriculum section (standards codes and difficulty range)
  sit at the top.
- **Study guide** (`ss_models_curr300.pdf`, 2 pages) — one section per family: what the
  evidence looks like, the general rule, a short worked example, and a try-it with the
  answer printed upside down inside the box. A watch-out box flags the most common error
  on this topic ("the numbers keep going up, so it's linear").

## What is verified, and what is not

**12 of the 18 answers on this sheet are machine-checked with SymPy** — every numeric
answer: the evaluations, the three equations solved for a variable, the two-gym system,
and the break-even quadratic. Nothing numeric on the key was typed by hand without being
recomputed.

**6 responses are flagged for your judgement** — the "(a) which family, and what tells
you?" half of problems 1, 2, 3, 8, 10 and 12. Those are written explanations, and no
computer algebra system can grade prose. The answer key states, for each one, exactly
what a correct response has to contain (the family name *and* the evidence), and the
Quick Answers bank marks them with `---` so you can see at a glance which six need
reading rather than checking. There are no `[unchecked]` marks: every printed answer
blank on the sheet is covered either by a machine check or by one of those six rubrics.

## Notes

Standards tagged: HSA-CED.A.2 (creating equations from context) on problems 3–7, 9, 11,
12; HSF-IF.B (interpreting functions in context) on 1, 2, 8, 10. Problem 10 is a
deliberate error-analysis item — a student's wrong reason is quoted and has to be
diagnosed before the table is used.
