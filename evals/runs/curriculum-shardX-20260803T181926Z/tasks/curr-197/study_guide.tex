\sstitleblock{Measuring the Centre and Spread of a Data Set}

\skillheading{Skill 1 --- Mode and median: the middle of the data}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Order the data first.} Every measure in this section is about position, so nothing
works until the values are written smallest to largest.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Mode} $=$ the value that appears most often (it is a value from the list, not how many
times it appeared). \quad \textbf{Median} $=$ the middle value once ordered. With an odd count
that is the single middle one; with an even count, add the two middle values and halve.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Find the median of $12, 15, 11, 20, 15, 13$.
\step{Six values is an even count, so there is no single middle one --- order them, then
average the two in the middle.}
\step{Ordered: $11, 12, \mathbf{13}, \mathbf{15}, 15, 20$, and halfway between $13$ and $15$ is $14$.}

$\Rightarrow\quad$ median $= \ans{14}$ \quad (the mode here is $15$, the only repeated value)
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Find the median of $8, 5, 9, 6$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: median $= \ans{7}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- The mean: sharing the total out equally}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Mean} $=$ (sum of all the values) $\div$ (how many values there are). It is the size
every value \emph{would} be if the total were shared out equally, so it need not equal any
value in the list.\par
\smallskip
Because every value is added in, the mean feels each one. A single value far from the rest
drags the mean towards it --- the median cannot be moved that way.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Find the mean of $6, 9, 4, 8, 3$.
\step{The mean shares the total out equally, so no ordering is needed --- add everything, then
divide by how many there are.}
\step{$6 + 9 + 4 + 8 + 3$ comes to thirty, and there are five values, so share thirty among five.}

$\Rightarrow\quad$ mean $= \ans{6}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Find the mean of $12, 7, 10, 15$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: mean $= \ans{11}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Range and interquartile range}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Range} $=$ largest $-$ smallest. It uses only two values, so one unusual value decides
it.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Quartiles:} order the data, split it at the median (leave the median itself out of both
halves when the count is odd). $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half, $Q_3$ the median of the
upper half, and the \textbf{interquartile range} is $Q_3 - Q_1$ --- the width of the middle
half. Nothing at the far ends can change it.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Find the range and the IQR of $2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 20$.
\step{Two questions about spread: the range asks how far apart the extremes are, the IQR asks
how wide the middle half is, so one uses the ends and the other uses the quartiles.}
\step{Range: $20 - 2$. Median is $7$; lower half $2, 4, 5$ gives $Q_1 = 4$; upper half
$8, 9, 20$ gives $Q_3 = 9$; so IQR $= 9 - 4$.}

$\Rightarrow\quad$ range $= \ans{18}$, \quad IQR $= \ans{5}$ \quad (the $20$ decides the first
and cannot touch the second)
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Find the IQR of $1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: IQR $= \ans{6}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 4 --- Chance read off a data set}

\begin{formulabox}
If one item is picked from a data set at random and every item is equally likely, then
\textbf{P(event)} $=$ (how many \emph{items} match) $\div$ (how many items there are).\par
\smallskip
Count items, never the different values on the axis: a value that four items share must be
counted four times.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad One value is picked at random from the seven values in the example
above. What is the probability it is greater than $7$?
\step{Every value is equally likely, so this is just counting: how many of the seven values
match, out of seven.}
\step{Three of the seven values sit above that mark.}

$\Rightarrow\quad P = \ans{\tfrac{3}{7}}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad From those same seven values, what is the probability of picking one
less than $5$?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $P = \ans{\tfrac{2}{7}}$}
\end{tryitbox}
