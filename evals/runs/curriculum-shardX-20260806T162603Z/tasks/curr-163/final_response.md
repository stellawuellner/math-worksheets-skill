Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 7 money-percent workshop.

- **Worksheet** (`ws_pct_money_curr163.pdf`) — 10 problems, all in real
  purchase contexts and all stated in dollars with the givens named explicitly:
  sales tax on a book and on a repair bill, an 18% tip, a 15% tip, a 25%-off
  jacket, a game whose sale price hides the discount percent, a chair whose
  price already includes a 20% markup, a coat whose sale price hides the
  original, and a closing tent problem that stacks a 30% discount and 8% tax and
  then asks whether the order matters. No filler detail — every number in a stem
  is a number the problem uses.
- **Answer key** (`ak_pct_money_curr163.pdf`) — Quick Answers bank, generated
  Curriculum section (7.RP.A.3, difficulty 1–5), and worked solutions that show
  both routes where they exist (find the extra and add it, or multiply once by
  1.05 / 0.75 / 1.40). Three problems also print a **common wrong answer**:
  measuring a discount against the sale price instead of the original (33.3%
  instead of 25%), taking 20% off a marked-up price instead of dividing
  ($67.20 instead of $70), and adding 15% back onto a sale price ($78.20 instead
  of $80).
- **Study guide** (`ss_pct_money_curr163.pdf`) — three sections: adding a
  percent on, taking a percent off, and markup plus the working-backwards case
  that trips most students. Each has a rule, a two-step worked example, and a
  try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 17 responses. **16 are machine-checked**
with SymPy — every tax, tip, discount, sale price, markup, recovered cost and
recovered original price, recomputed from the givens. Three declared
misconception traps are machine-checked as well: the gate confirms each wrong
method lands on a number the problem's own check rejects, which is why those
wrong answers can be printed for the grader. **1 response is instructor-judged**:
part (d) of problem 10, the explanation of why discount-then-tax and
tax-then-discount give the same total. The key marks it `---` in the bank and
states what a correct answer must contain (a reason about multiplying by the two
factors, or the second order worked through). All nine study-guide checks are
machine-verified.
