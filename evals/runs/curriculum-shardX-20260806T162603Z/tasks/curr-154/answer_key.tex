\problem{Kim writes the ratio of milk to oats (5 cups milk, 3 cups oats) as
$3{:}5$. Correct it and say what she mixed up.}

\textbf{Solution:} Milk is named first, so the milk count (5) must come first.
The correct ratio of milk to oats is \ans{\tfrac{5}{3}}

\textbf{Diagnosis (instructor judges):} Kim reversed the order — she wrote the
oats-to-milk ratio. Full credit for saying the quantity named first goes first,
so milk to oats is $5$ to $3$, not $3$ to $5$ (which is $0.6$ as a fraction, the
reversed value).

\problem{Jon says $4{:}6$ and $10{:}15$ are equivalent. Is he right?}

\textbf{Solution:} Simplify both:
\[ \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3} \qquad\qquad \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3} \]
Both reduce to the same simplest form, so Jon is right:
\ans{\tfrac{4}{6} = \tfrac{10}{15}}

\textbf{Explanation (instructor judges):} Full credit shows both ratios
simplified to $\frac{2}{3}$ (or one obtained from the other by multiplying both
parts by $2.5$).

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Leo computes the speed of a car that travels 120 miles in 3 hours as
$3 \div 120 = 0.025$ miles per hour. Fix it and name the mistake.}

\textbf{Solution:} Speed in miles per hour is miles divided by hours:
$120 \div 3 = 40$. \ans{40\ \text{mph}}

\textbf{Diagnosis (instructor judges):} Leo divided hours by miles — his 0.025
is hours per mile, the reversed rate. ``Miles per hour'' tells you the order:
the quantity before ``per'' goes on top.

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Rice to milk should be $2{:}3$; the table shows columns (2,3), (4,6),
(6,10), (8,12). Find and fix the bad entry.}

\textbf{Solution:} Test each column: $3 \div 2 = 1.5$, $6 \div 4 = 1.5$,
$10 \div 6 \approx 1.67$, $12 \div 8 = 1.5$. The third column breaks the
pattern.

(a) The wrong entry is the 10 (paired with 6 cups of rice).

(b) For 6 cups of rice the multiplier from the base column is 3, so milk should
be $3 \times 3 = 9$. \ans{9}

The planted 10 comes from adding 4 to the previous milk value instead of
keeping the ratio.

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Mia's proportion for 12 muffins (8 muffins need 2 cups) is
$\frac{2}{8} = \frac{12}{x}$, giving $x = 48$. Explain and fix.}

\textbf{Diagnosis (instructor judges):} The left fraction is cups over muffins,
but the right fraction is muffins over cups — Mia flipped one side. Both sides
must keep the same order.

\textbf{Solution:} With matching orders, $\frac{2}{8} = \frac{x}{12}$, so
$x = \frac{2}{8} \times 12 = 3$ cups of flour. \ans{3}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Which of $6{:}8$, $9{:}10$, $15{:}20$ is not equivalent to $3{:}4$, and
what should the second number be?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $6{:}8$ simplifies to $3{:}4$ and $15{:}20$ simplifies to
$3{:}4$, but $9{:}10$ does not — it comes from \emph{adding} 6 to both parts of
$3{:}4$. The odd one out is $9{:}10$.

(b) Keeping the 9: since $9 = 3 \times 3$, the second number must be
$4 \times 3$. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Raj writes $\frac{7}{3} > \frac{4}{9}$ to compare punch A (3 syrup : 7
water) with punch B (4 syrup : 9 water). What went wrong, and what is the fair
comparison?}

\textbf{Diagnosis (instructor judges):} Raj compared water-per-syrup for A
against syrup-per-water for B — two different kinds of ratios, so the
comparison means nothing. Full credit for naming the mismatched orders.

\textbf{Solution:} Using syrup per water for both: $\frac{3}{7} \approx 0.429$
and $\frac{4}{9} \approx 0.444$, so \ans{\tfrac{3}{7} < \tfrac{4}{9}}

Punch B is actually the stronger one.

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{The $5{:}2$ table shows top 10, 15, 20, 25 over bottom 4, 8, 8, 12.
Fix the two wrong bottom entries and explain the test.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each column must simplify to $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5$. Test:
$10/4 = 2.5$ $\checkmark$, \ $15/8 = 1.875$ ($\times$), \ $20/8 = 2.5$
$\checkmark$, \ $25/12 \approx 2.08$ ($\times$).

(a) Under 15: $15 \div 2.5 = 6$, so the bottom entry is \ans{6}

(b) Under 25: $25 \div 2.5 = 10$, so the bottom entry is \ans{10}

(c) \textbf{Explanation (instructor judges):} Full credit describes a per-column
test — divide top by bottom and check for $\frac{5}{2}$, or simplify each
column ratio. The planted 8 copies the neighbouring column, and the planted 12
adds 4 to the previous entry instead of scaling.
