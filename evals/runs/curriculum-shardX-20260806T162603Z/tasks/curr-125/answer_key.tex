\problem{Write the correct symbol: $\tfrac{5}{8}$ \_\_ $\tfrac{1}{2}$}

\textbf{Solution:} One half in eighths is $\tfrac{4}{8}$, and 5 eighths is
more than 4 eighths, so $\tfrac{5}{8}$ $\ans{>}$ $\tfrac{1}{2}$

\problem{Write $\tfrac{7}{4}$ as a mixed number.}

\textbf{Solution:} $7 \div 4 = 1$ remainder 3, so 7 fourths is 1 whole and 3
fourths: $\ans{1\tfrac{3}{4}}$

\problem{Order least to greatest in sixths: $\tfrac{2}{3}$, $\tfrac{1}{2}$,
$\tfrac{5}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} In sixths: $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{4}{6}$,
$\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{3}{6}$, and $\tfrac{5}{6}$ stays. Comparing 3, 4, and
5 sixths gives
$\ans{\tfrac{1}{2}} < \ans{\tfrac{2}{3}} < \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}}$

\problem{Use the benchmark $\tfrac{1}{2}$ to compare $\tfrac{3}{8}$ and
$\tfrac{5}{9}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Half of 8 is 4, and $3 < 4$, so
$\tfrac{3}{8}$ $\ans{<}$ $\tfrac{1}{2}$
Half of 9 is $4.5$, and $5 > 4.5$, so
$\tfrac{5}{9}$ $\ans{>}$ $\tfrac{1}{2}$
One fraction is below the benchmark and the other above it, so
$\tfrac{3}{8}$ $\ans{<}$ $\tfrac{5}{9}$

\problem{Point $P$ sits at $2\tfrac{1}{3}$. Write $P$ as an improper
fraction in thirds.}

\textbf{Solution:} Two wholes hold $2 \times 3 = 6$ thirds, plus 1 more
third makes 7 thirds: $\ans{\tfrac{7}{3}}$

\problem{Order least to greatest: $\tfrac{7}{8}$, $\tfrac{3}{4}$,
$\tfrac{5}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} In twenty-fourths: $\tfrac{7}{8} = \tfrac{21}{24}$,
$\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{18}{24}$, $\tfrac{5}{6} = \tfrac{20}{24}$. Ordering
18, 20, 21 gives
$\ans{\tfrac{3}{4}} < \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}} < \ans{\tfrac{7}{8}}$

\problem{Order least to greatest as eighths: $1\tfrac{3}{4}$,
$\tfrac{15}{8}$, $1\tfrac{1}{2}$}

\textbf{Solution:} In eighths: $1\tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{14}{8}$,
$\tfrac{15}{8}$ stays, $1\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{12}{8}$. Ordering 12, 14, 15
eighths gives
$\ans{1\tfrac{1}{2}} < \ans{1\tfrac{3}{4}} < \ans{\tfrac{15}{8}}$

\problem{Trails: Pine $\tfrac{5}{12}$ mi, Oak $\tfrac{4}{6}$ mi, Elm
$\tfrac{1}{2}$ mi. Order least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Benchmark check: half of 12 is 6, and $5 < 6$, so Pine is
under $\tfrac{1}{2}$. Half of 6 is 3, and $4 > 3$, so Oak is over
$\tfrac{1}{2}$. Elm is exactly the benchmark. So
$\ans{\tfrac{5}{12}} < \ans{\tfrac{1}{2}} < \ans{\tfrac{4}{6}}$

\problem{Ben says $2\tfrac{1}{4} < \tfrac{9}{4}$ because 2 is less than 9.
(a) Write $2\tfrac{1}{4}$ in fourths. (b) Correct symbol. (c) Explain.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Two wholes are 8 fourths, plus 1 fourth:
$2\tfrac{1}{4} = \ans{\tfrac{9}{4}}$

(b) Both names stand for 9 fourths, so
$2\tfrac{1}{4}$ $\ans{=}$ $\tfrac{9}{4}$

(c) \emph{(instructor judges)} Ben compared the whole number 2 with the
numerator 9 --- two different kinds of numbers. Rewriting $\tfrac{9}{4}$
shows it is 2 wholes and 1 fourth, the very same value, so neither is
smaller.

\problem{Order least to greatest: $\tfrac{5}{6}$, $\tfrac{7}{9}$,
$\tfrac{8}{12}$}

\textbf{Solution:} In thirty-sixths: $\tfrac{5}{6} = \tfrac{30}{36}$,
$\tfrac{7}{9} = \tfrac{28}{36}$, $\tfrac{8}{12} = \tfrac{24}{36}$. Ordering
24, 28, 30 gives
$\ans{\tfrac{8}{12}} < \ans{\tfrac{7}{9}} < \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}}$

\problem{Order least to greatest using benchmarks 1 and $1\tfrac{1}{2}$:
$\tfrac{13}{8}$, $1\tfrac{1}{4}$, $\tfrac{3}{2}$}

\textbf{Solution:} All three are between 1 and 2. $1\tfrac{1}{4}$ is below
$1\tfrac{1}{2}$; $\tfrac{3}{2}$ \emph{is} $1\tfrac{1}{2}$; and
$\tfrac{13}{8} = 1\tfrac{5}{8}$ is above it. So
$\ans{1\tfrac{1}{4}} < \ans{\tfrac{3}{2}} < \ans{\tfrac{13}{8}}$

\problem{Ribbons: A is $1\tfrac{5}{6}$ m, B is $\tfrac{11}{6}$ m, C is
$1\tfrac{2}{3}$ m. (a) Compare A and B. (b) Compare C and B. (c) Shortest?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) In sixths, $1\tfrac{5}{6} = \tfrac{11}{6}$ --- the
same number, so $1\tfrac{5}{6}$ $\ans{=}$ $\tfrac{11}{6}$

(b) $1\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{10}{6}$, and 10 sixths is less than 11 sixths,
so $1\tfrac{2}{3}$ $\ans{<}$ $\tfrac{11}{6}$

(c) \emph{(instructor judges)} Ribbon C is the shortest. Ribbons A and B tie
because 11 sixths regroups as 1 whole and 5 sixths --- two names for one
length.
