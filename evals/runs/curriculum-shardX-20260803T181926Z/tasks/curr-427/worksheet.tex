\wstitleblock{Solving Oblique Triangles with the Law of Cosines}{Precalculus}{}

\noindent\small
Finding the third side of an SAS triangle, finding angles in an SSS triangle,
and solving navigation problems with the law of cosines.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every triangle here is labelled the same way: side $a$ is opposite
        vertex $A$, side $b$ opposite $B$, side $c$ opposite $C$. Each figure
        shows only that problem's own givens, with $?$ on what you are asked
        for.
  \item \textbf{Law of cosines:}
        $c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2} - 2ab\cos C$, and the same pattern for $a$ and
        $b$. Rearranged for an angle:
        $\cos C = \dfrac{a^{2} + b^{2} - c^{2}}{2ab}$.
  \item Use it when you have \textbf{SAS} (two sides and the angle between
        them) or \textbf{SSS} (three sides). The law of sines cannot start
        either case.
  \item Keep full precision until the last line, then round sides to two
        decimal places and angles to two decimal places of a degree.
  \item Sanity-check every answer against the figure: the longest side must sit
        opposite the largest angle.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
