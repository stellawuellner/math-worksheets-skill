\problem{Spherical balloon, $\frac{dr}{dt} = 3$ cm/s. Find $\frac{dV}{dt}$ when
$r = 5$ cm.}

\textbf{Solution.} Differentiate $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ with respect to
\emph{time}, so the chain rule contributes $\frac{dr}{dt}$:
\[ \frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\,\frac{dr}{dt} = 4\pi(5)^2(3) = 300\pi. \]
\[ \boxed{\frac{dV}{dt} \approx 942.48} \]
Units: cubic centimetres per second.

\problem{Cube, $\frac{ds}{dt} = 0.5$ cm/s, $s = 4$ cm. The student wrote
$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2 = 48$. (a) Name the error. (b) Corrected $\frac{dV}{dt}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The student differentiated
$V = s^3$ with respect to $s$, not with respect to $t$. Differentiating with
respect to time attaches the chain-rule factor $\frac{ds}{dt}$:
\[ \frac{dV}{dt} = 3s^2\,\frac{ds}{dt}. \]
A response earns full credit if it identifies the missing $\frac{ds}{dt}$; a
response that only says ``the arithmetic is wrong'' does not.

\textbf{Solution (b).} $\frac{dV}{dt} = 3(4)^2(0.5) = 48(0.5)$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{dV}{dt} = 24} \]

\problem{Pen against a barn wall, $60$ m of fence, $2x + y = 60$,
$A = x(60 - 2x)$. (a) What went wrong in the last step? (b) Corrected $x$.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The student set the \emph{area}
equal to zero instead of its \emph{derivative}. $A = 0$ locates the two ways to
build a pen with no area at all ($x = 0$ and $x = 30$); the largest area sits
where $A'(x) = 0$. Credit any wording that separates $A = 0$ from $A'(x) = 0$.

\textbf{Solution (b).} Expand and differentiate:
\[ A(x) = 60x - 2x^2, \qquad A'(x) = 60 - 4x = 0. \]
\[ \boxed{x = 15} \]
Then $y = 60 - 2(15) = 30$ and the maximum area is $450$ square metres, larger
than the $0$ the student's answer produces --- a useful sanity check.

\problem{Ladder, $x^2 + y^2 = 169$, $\frac{dx}{dt} = 2$ ft/s, $x = 5$, $y = 12$.
The student reported $\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{5}{6}$. (a) Describe the error.
(b) Corrected $\frac{dy}{dt}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Solving
$2x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$ gives
$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y}\cdot\frac{dx}{dt}$, and the student dropped the
minus sign. The top of the ladder is sliding \emph{down}, so $\frac{dy}{dt}$
must be negative. Credit either the algebraic or the physical argument, so long
as the response states that the rate is negative.

\textbf{Solution (b).} $\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{5}{12}(2)$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{5}{6}} \]
That is about $-0.83$ feet per second.

\problem{Open box from a $20$ by $20$ sheet, corner squares of side $x$. The
student wrote $V = x(20 - x)^2$. (a) Which factor is wrong? (b) Critical values.
(c) Which one maximizes $V$?}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Each $20$ cm edge loses a square at
\emph{both} ends, so the base measures $(20 - 2x)$ by $(20 - 2x)$:
\[ V(x) = x(20 - 2x)^2 . \]
Credit any wording that explains why the edge loses $2x$ rather than $x$.

\textbf{Solution (b).} Differentiating gives
$V'(x) = (20-2x)^2 + x\cdot 2(20-2x)(-2) = (20-2x)(20-6x)$. Setting each factor
to zero:
\[ \boxed{x = \frac{10}{3} \text{ or } x = 10} \]

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} At $x = 10$ the base has collapsed
to zero width, so $V = 0$ --- the smallest volume possible, not the largest. At
$x = \frac{10}{3}$ the volume is positive, so that is the maximizer. A
first-derivative sign chart, a second-derivative test, or a direct evaluation at
both values all earn full credit, provided $x = 10$ is rejected for a stated
reason.

\problem{Minimize $f(x) = x + \frac{9}{x}$ on $x > 0$. The student reported the
minimum at $x = -3$. (a) Name the error. (b) Both solutions of $f'(x) = 0$.
(c) The minimum value.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Two things went unchecked: the
stated domain is $x > 0$, which rules $x = -3$ out entirely, and no test was
applied to decide which critical value is a minimum. Since
$f''(x) = \frac{18}{x^3} > 0$ for $x > 0$, the curve is concave up there and the
critical value inside the domain is a minimum. Credit the domain argument, a
second-derivative test, or a sign chart.

\textbf{Solution (b).} $f'(x) = 1 - \frac{9}{x^2} = 0$ gives $x^2 = 9$.
\[ \boxed{x = -3 \text{ or } x = 3} \]

\textbf{Solution (c).} Only $x = 3$ lies in the domain, and
$f(3) = 3 + \frac{9}{3}$.
\[ \boxed{f(3) = 6} \]

\problem{Lamp $15$ ft tall, walker $6$ ft tall moving at $4$ ft/s; $x$ is the
walker's distance from the pole, $s$ the shadow length. The student wrote
$\frac{6}{15} = \frac{s}{x}$ and got $1.6$ ft/s. (a) Describe the error.
(b) Corrected $\frac{ds}{dt}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The two similar triangles share the
shadow \emph{tip}, not the walker's position: the large triangle has base
$s + x$ (pole to tip) and height $15$; the small one has base $s$ and height
$6$. The correct proportion is therefore
\[ \frac{6}{15} = \frac{s}{s + x}, \]
not $\frac{6}{15} = \frac{s}{x}$. Credit any wording that puts $s + x$ in the
denominator.

\textbf{Solution (b).} Cross-multiplying, $6(s + x) = 15s$, so $6x = 9s$ and
$s = \frac{2}{3}x$. Differentiating, $\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 4$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{ds}{dt} = \frac{8}{3}} \]
About $2.67$ feet per second --- faster than the student's figure, as it must
be, since the student's proportion understates the shadow.

\problem{Closed can of volume $1000$ cm$^3$. The student used
$S = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r h$ and got $r \approx 4.30$. (a) Identify the error.
(b) Corrected $S(r)$. (c) Minimizing radius.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} A closed cylinder has two circular
ends \emph{and} a lateral surface that unrolls into a rectangle of width
$2\pi r$, so the side contributes $2\pi r h$, not $\pi r h$. The student's
objective is missing a factor of $2$ on the lateral term. Credit any wording
that names that missing factor.

\textbf{Solution (b).} With $h = \frac{1000}{\pi r^2}$,
\[ S(r) = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r\cdot\frac{1000}{\pi r^2}. \]
\[ \boxed{S(r) = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{2000}{r}} \]

\textbf{Solution (c).} $S'(r) = 4\pi r - \frac{2000}{r^2} = 0$ gives
$4\pi r^3 = 2000$, so $r^3 = \frac{500}{\pi}$.
\[ \boxed{r \approx 5.42} \]
The student's halved objective produces $4.30$ instead, which is exactly the
misconception this problem targets.
