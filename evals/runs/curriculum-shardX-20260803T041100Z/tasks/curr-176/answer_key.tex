\problem{\textbf{Tape diagram: one of three equal parts is 7.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Read the diagram as an equation before computing. One of three
equal parts of $x$ is $\frac{1}{3}x$, and the diagram says that part is 7:
$\dfrac{x}{3} = 7$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The undo of dividing by 3 is multiplying by 3, applied to both
sides: $3 \cdot \dfrac{x}{3} = 3 \cdot 7$.
\ans{x = 21}

\textit{Check:} $21 \div 3 = 7$, and the bar of 21 does split into three parts
of 7.}

\problem{\textbf{$0.4x = 6$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The coefficient is a decimal, so the single operation done to
$x$ was multiplication by $0.4$. Division undoes it.

\textbf{Step 2.} Divide both sides by $0.4$: $x = \dfrac{6}{0.4}$. Multiplying
top and bottom by 10 clears the decimal, giving $\dfrac{60}{4}$.
\ans{x = 15}

\textit{Sense check:} $0.4$ is less than 1, so taking $0.4$ of the jar left
fewer marbles than the jar held --- the solution must be bigger than 6.}

\problem{\textbf{Tape diagram: two of three equal parts are 8, so $\frac{2}{3}x = 8$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): $x = 4$ would make one part $\frac{4}{3}$, and two
parts would be under 3 --- nowhere near 8. The 8 counts \emph{two} parts, not
the whole bar.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply both sides by the reciprocal $\dfrac{3}{2}$:
$\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}x = \dfrac{3}{2}\cdot 8$, so $x = \dfrac{24}{2}$.
\ans{x = 12}

\textit{Check on the diagram:} each part is $12 \div 3 = 4$, and two parts make
8.}

\problem{\textbf{$\dfrac{x}{4} = 9$ ounces.}

\textbf{Step 1.} One quarter of the bag is 9, so the coefficient is the unit
fraction $\frac{1}{4}$ and its reciprocal is simply 4.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply both sides by 4: $x = 4 \cdot 9$.
\ans{x = 36}

\textit{Check:} a bar of 36 cut into 4 equal parts gives parts of 9.}

\problem{\textbf{Testing Mia's claim that $x = 20$ solves $\frac{3}{5}x = 12$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A check is a substitution, not a re-solve: put 20 in place of
$x$ and evaluate the left side only.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{3}{5}\cdot 20 = \dfrac{60}{5}$, which is 12 --- exactly
the right side, so Mia is right.
\ans{12}

\textit{Watch out:} a student who reaches for the reciprocal here computes
$\frac{5}{3}\cdot 20 \approx 33.33$ and wrongly rejects a correct answer. The
reciprocal is for \emph{solving}; a check uses the equation as written.}

\problem{\textbf{$-\dfrac{3}{5}x = 12$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): a negative coefficient times $x$ gives a positive 12,
so $x$ must be negative. Deciding this first turns the sign into a prediction
you can check.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply both sides by the reciprocal, sign included,
$-\dfrac{5}{3}$: \; $x = -\dfrac{5}{3}\cdot 12 = -\dfrac{60}{3}$.
\ans{x = -20}

\textit{Check:} $-\frac{3}{5}\cdot(-20) = 12$, and the sign matches the
prediction.}

\problem{\textbf{$1.25x = 7.5$ dollars.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): the coefficient $1.25$ is greater than 1, so each
gigabyte costs more than a dollar and the number of gigabytes must be smaller
than $7.5$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Divide both sides by $1.25$: $x = \dfrac{7.5}{1.25}$.
Multiplying top and bottom by 100 gives $\dfrac{750}{125}$.
\ans{x = 6}

\textit{Check:} $1.25 \cdot 6 = 7.5$, and 6 is indeed smaller than $7.5$, as
predicted.}

\problem{\textbf{$\dfrac{5}{6}x = \dfrac{2}{3}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Fractions on both sides change nothing about the method: the
coefficient is $\frac{5}{6}$, so its reciprocal $\frac{6}{5}$ is what clears it.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{6}{5}\cdot\dfrac{5}{6}x = \dfrac{6}{5}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}
= \dfrac{12}{15}$, and $\dfrac{12}{15}$ reduces by 3.
\ans{x = \dfrac{4}{5}}

\textit{Check:} $\dfrac{5}{6}\cdot\dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
Note the solution is smaller than the right side, because the coefficient is
less than 1.}

\problem{\textbf{$\dfrac{3}{8}x = 1.5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Mixed forms are easiest in one form: part (a) rewrites $1.5$ as
$\dfrac{3}{2}$, so both sides are now fractions.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply both sides by the reciprocal $\dfrac{8}{3}$:
$x = \dfrac{8}{3}\cdot\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{24}{6}$.
\ans{x = 4}

\textit{Check:} $\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot 4 = \dfrac{12}{8} = 1.5$.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: Devon's error on $\dfrac{2}{3}x = 18$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a) is a substitution, so evaluate the left side at
Devon's value: $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot 12 = \dfrac{24}{3}$.
\ans{8}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): 8 is not 18, so $x = 12$ is not a solution. Devon
multiplied by the coefficient $\frac{2}{3}$ when the undo of multiplying by
$\frac{2}{3}$ is multiplying by its reciprocal $\frac{3}{2}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}x = \dfrac{3}{2}\cdot
18 = \dfrac{54}{2}$.
\ans{x = 27}

\textit{Check:} $\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot 27 = 18$. Note also that $\frac{2}{3}$ is
less than 1, so the solution has to be \emph{larger} than 18 --- Devon's 12 was
on the wrong side of 18 before any arithmetic was checked.}
