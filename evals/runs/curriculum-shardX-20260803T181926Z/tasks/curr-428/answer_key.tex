\problem{\textbf{Case test: $A = 30^\circ$, $b = 10$, $a = 8$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): the height is the perpendicular from $C$ to the base
line, $h = b\sin A = 10\sin 30^\circ$.
\ans{h = 5.00}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): $h = 5 < a = 8 < b = 10$, so the swinging side is
long enough to reach the base line but short enough to reach it twice --- two
triangles.

\textbf{Step 3.} The law of sines gives
$\sin B = \frac{b\sin A}{a} = \frac{5}{8} = 0.625$, so
$B = \arcsin 0.625$.
\ans{B = 38.68^\circ}

\textit{Why $h$ comes first:} it is the only number that decides the case, and
it costs one multiplication. Students who skip it end up discovering the second
triangle by luck.}

\problem{\textbf{Two sightings: $A = 40^\circ$, $b = 12$, first $a = 9$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): $h = 12\sin 40^\circ$.
\ans{h = 7.71}

\textbf{Step 2.} Since $7.71 < 9 < 12$, the measurement is ambiguous: two
triangles. The acute solution comes from
$\sin B = \frac{12\sin 40^\circ}{9} = 0.8571$.
\ans{B = 58.99^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (b): with $a = 6$ the same rule gives
$\sin B = \frac{12\sin 40^\circ}{6}$.
\ans{\sin B = 1.29}

\textbf{Step 4.} No angle has a sine above 1, so no triangle exists --- which
matches the height test, since $a = 6$ is shorter than $h = 7.71$ and the
swinging side cannot reach the base line at all. The second sighting must be a
measurement error.

\textit{Grading note:} accept either justification in part (b) --- the
impossible sine or the comparison with $h$. They are the same fact stated two
ways.}

\problem{\textbf{Both triangles: $A = 35^\circ$, $b = 14$, $a = 9$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $h = 14\sin 35^\circ = 8.03$, and $8.03 < 9 < 14$, so expect
two triangles. From $\sin B = \frac{14\sin 35^\circ}{9} = 0.8922$:
\ans{B_1 = 63.15^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} The supplement has the same sine:
$B_2 = 180^\circ - 63.15^\circ$. Test it:
$A + B_2 = 35^\circ + 116.85^\circ = 151.85^\circ < 180^\circ$, so a third angle
of $28.15^\circ$ remains and the second triangle is real.
\ans{B_2 = 116.85^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} Triangle 1: $C_1 = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 63.15^\circ =
81.85^\circ$, and $c_1 = \frac{a\sin C_1}{\sin A} =
\frac{9\sin 81.85^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ}$.
\ans{c_1 = 15.53}

\textbf{Step 4.} Triangle 2: $C_2 = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 116.85^\circ =
28.15^\circ$, and $c_2 = \frac{9\sin 28.15^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ}$.
\ans{c_2 = 7.40}

\textit{The check that catches everything:} in each triangle the largest side
must face the largest angle. Triangle 1 has $C_1$ largest and $c_1 = 15.53$
largest; Triangle 2 has $B_2$ largest and $b = 14$ largest. Both pass.}

\problem{\textbf{Only one: $A = 48^\circ$, $b = 7$, $a = 10$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): $\sin B = \frac{7\sin 48^\circ}{10} = 0.5202$.
\ans{B = 31.35^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): the obtuse candidate is
$180^\circ - 31.35^\circ = 148.65^\circ$, and adding the given angle gives
$48^\circ + 148.65^\circ$.
\ans{A + B_2 = 196.65^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} That exceeds $180^\circ$, so no triangle can contain both
angles and the obtuse candidate is rejected. This is what $a \ge b$ always
forces: the side opposite the larger angle is longer, so $a > b$ makes
$A > B$ and $B$ cannot be obtuse.

\textbf{Step 4.} Part (c): $C = 180^\circ - 48^\circ - 31.35^\circ = 100.65^\circ$
and $c = \frac{10\sin 100.65^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ}$.
\ans{c = 13.22}

\textit{Worth saying aloud:} ``one triangle'' here is not luck --- it is
guaranteed the moment $a \ge b$, before any arithmetic.}

\problem{\textbf{Search and rescue: $A = 27^\circ$, $b = 40$, $a = 25$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $h = 40\sin 27^\circ = 18.16$, and $18.16 < 25 < 40$: the
bearings allow two positions. From $\sin B = \frac{40\sin 27^\circ}{25} = 0.7264$:
\ans{B_1 = 46.58^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} The supplement passes the test
($27^\circ + 133.42^\circ = 160.42^\circ < 180^\circ$), so it is a genuine second
position.
\ans{B_2 = 133.42^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} Far position: $C_1 = 180^\circ - 27^\circ - 46.58^\circ =
106.42^\circ$, so $c_1 = \frac{25\sin 106.42^\circ}{\sin 27^\circ}$.
\ans{c_1 = 52.82 \ \text{km}}

\textbf{Step 4.} Near position: $C_2 = 180^\circ - 27^\circ - 133.42^\circ =
19.58^\circ$, so $c_2 = \frac{25\sin 19.58^\circ}{\sin 27^\circ}$.
\ans{c_2 = 18.46 \ \text{km}}

\textit{Why this matters operationally:} the two answers are 52.82 km and
18.46 km along the same shoreline. Reporting only the first is not a rounding
error --- it is a search in the wrong place, and it is exactly what the law of
sines will hand you if you never test the supplement.}

\problem{\textbf{The borderline: $A = 30^\circ$, $b = 12$, $a = 6$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): $h = 12\sin 30^\circ$.
\ans{h = 6.00}

\textbf{Step 2.} Here $a = h$ exactly. The swinging side just reaches the base
line, meeting it at the foot of the perpendicular, so the angle at $B$ is a
right angle: $\sin B = \frac{12\sin 30^\circ}{6} = 1$.
\ans{B = 90^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (b): the supplement of $90^\circ$ is $90^\circ$ again ---
there is no second angle to find. The two solutions of the ambiguous case have
merged into one.

\textbf{Step 4.} Part (c): $C = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 90^\circ = 60^\circ$, so
$c = \frac{6\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ}$.
\ans{c = 10.39}

\textit{The boundary case is the useful one:} it is the exact moment the two
triangles of the ambiguous case collapse into each other, which is why $a = h$
separates ``none'' from ``two''.}

\problem{\textbf{Surveying: $A = 62^\circ$, $b = 30$, $a = 45$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): $a = 45$ is longer than $b = 30$, so the angle
opposite $a$ is the larger of the two and $B$ must be acute. No supplement test
is needed --- one triangle, guaranteed. Then
$\sin B = \frac{30\sin 62^\circ}{45} = 0.5886$.
\ans{B = 36.06^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): $C = 180^\circ - 62^\circ - 36.06^\circ = 81.94^\circ$,
so $c = \frac{45\sin 81.94^\circ}{\sin 62^\circ}$.
\ans{c = 50.46 \ \text{m}}

\textit{Check:} the largest angle here is $C = 81.94^\circ$ and the largest side
is $c = 50.46$ m. Consistent.}

\problem{\textbf{Both triangles again: $A = 30^\circ$, $b = 24$, $a = 15$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $h = 24\sin 30^\circ = 12.00$, and $12 < 15 < 24$: two
triangles. From $\sin B = \frac{24\sin 30^\circ}{15} = 0.8000$:
\ans{B_1 = 53.13^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} The supplement passes ($30^\circ + 126.87^\circ = 156.87^\circ$).
\ans{B_2 = 126.87^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} Triangle 1: $C_1 = 96.87^\circ$ and
$c_1 = \frac{15\sin 96.87^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ}$.
\ans{c_1 = 29.78}

\textbf{Step 4.} Triangle 2: $C_2 = 23.13^\circ$ and
$c_2 = \frac{15\sin 23.13^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ}$.
\ans{c_2 = 11.78}

\textbf{Step 5.} Part (c): in the figure the solid apex is the far one, giving
the long base $c_1$; the dashed apex sits nearer $A$, giving the short base
$c_2$. A small given angle $A$ stretches the difference, which is why $29.78$
and $11.78$ are so far apart.

\textit{Fast sanity check:} the two bases differ by $2b\cos B_1$ --- geometry
worth showing students who suspect one of the answers is a slip.}

\problem{\textbf{Two possible positions: $A = 44^\circ$, $b = 26$, $a = 20$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $h = 26\sin 44^\circ = 18.06 < 20 < 26$, so both positions are
geometrically valid. From $\sin B = \frac{26\sin 44^\circ}{20} = 0.9031$:
\ans{B_1 = 64.56^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} The supplement passes ($44^\circ + 115.44^\circ = 159.44^\circ$).
\ans{B_2 = 115.44^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} With $C_1 = 71.44^\circ$:
$c_1 = \frac{20\sin 71.44^\circ}{\sin 44^\circ}$.
\ans{c_1 = 27.29}

\textbf{Step 4.} With $C_2 = 20.56^\circ$:
$c_2 = \frac{20\sin 20.56^\circ}{\sin 44^\circ}$.
\ans{c_2 = 10.11}

\textbf{Step 5.} Part (b) --- open response, read the reasoning. The obtuse
solution $B_2 = 115.44^\circ$ puts the marker between the control point and the
drone (the near apex, $c_2 = 10.11$), so ``the drone is beyond the marker''
rules it out and leaves the acute triangle with $c_1 = 27.29$.

\textit{What a full answer names:} any single extra measurement that separates
the two --- the distance $c$ itself, the angle at $C$, or whether the angle at
$B$ is acute or obtuse. Accept any of these; the point is that ONE more
observation is enough, and that without it both answers must be reported.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge --- the whole case rule: $A = 50^\circ$, $b = 16$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): with $a = 14$,
$\sin B = \frac{16\sin 50^\circ}{14} = 0.8755$.
\ans{B_1 = 61.10^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} The supplement $118.90^\circ$ also passes
($50^\circ + 118.90^\circ = 168.90^\circ < 180^\circ$), so $a = 14$ gives two
triangles.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (b): $h = 16\sin 50^\circ$.
\ans{h = 12.26}

\textbf{Step 4.} Part (c) --- open response, read the reasoning. The complete
rule for $A = 50^\circ$, $b = 16$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=0pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item $a < 12.26$: no triangle. The swinging side is shorter than the
        perpendicular and never reaches the base line.
  \item $a = 12.26$: exactly one, the right triangle where the side meets the
        base line at the foot of the perpendicular.
  \item $12.26 < a < 16$: two triangles. The side is long enough to cross the
        base line on both sides of the perpendicular.
  \item $a \ge 16$: exactly one. The side now reaches past the vertex $A$, so
        only the far intersection lies on the correct side of $A$; equivalently
        $a \ge b$ forces $B$ acute.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Accept} any statement of these four bands with the two boundary values
$12.26$ and $16$ identified, justified either by the swinging side or by the
sine test ($\sin B > 1$, $= 1$, or two admissible angles).}
