\wstitleblock{Samples and Chance --- Wrong Denominators and Loaded Samples}{Grades 6--7}{}

\noindent\small Two questions catch most of the mistakes in this unit. Ask them
every time.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{``Out of what?''} A probability compares the outcomes you want
        with \emph{every} outcome that could happen --- not with the ones you
        do not want, and not with a smaller group inside the whole.
  \item \textbf{``Who got asked?''} A sample only speaks for a group if every
        member of that group had a real chance of being picked. Where a survey
        is taken can decide its answer before anyone replies.
  \item Give probabilities as fractions in lowest terms. Show the denominator
        you chose, and write one line saying what it counts.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\textbf{\small Marble bag} {\small (problems 1 to 3): a bag holds
$5$ red, $3$ blue and $4$ green marbles, and one marble is drawn without
looking.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Warm-up.} Find $P(\text{red})$ for a single draw from the
full bag.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Ines writes
$P(\text{blue}) = \dfrac{3}{9}$, because there are $3$ blue marbles and $9$
marbles that are not blue.
\par\smallskip
Name what Ines put in the denominator, say what belongs there instead, and give
the correct probability in lowest terms.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} One red marble is drawn and \emph{kept out
of the bag}. Marco now says $P(\text{green}) = \dfrac{4}{12}$, ``because there
are still $4$ green marbles.''
\par\smallskip
Marco updated the numerator but not the denominator. What is the total number of
marbles now, and what is the correct probability of drawing green?}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} In a class of $24$ students, $10$ walk to
school and $14$ ride. Of the $10$ walkers, $6$ are in Year $7$. One student is
chosen at random from the class. Max writes
$P(\text{a Year-7 walker}) = \dfrac{6}{10}$.
\par\smallskip
Max used the walkers as the whole group, but the student was chosen from the
class. Give the correct probability in lowest terms.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Nora wants to estimate how many of her
school's $300$ students cycle to school. She asks $20$ students standing at the
bike rack, and $18$ of them say they cycle.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} If Nora's $20$ students really did represent the school, how many
of the $300$ would cycle?
\quad \textbf{(b)} Explain in one or two sentences why that number shows Nora's
sample cannot be trusted, and name the feature of \emph{where she asked} that
caused it.}

\problem[7.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Sam surveys students in the school
library about their favourite after-school club and records this:
\par\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Club} & Chess & Art & Sport & Drama \\
\midrule
\textbf{Students} & $22$ & $12$ & $10$ & $6$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\par\vspace{0.1cm}
Sam concludes: ``chess is the most popular club in the school.''
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} How many students answered the survey altogether?
\quad \textbf{(b)} Explain why the \emph{place} Sam surveyed makes the
conclusion unsafe, and say what he should do instead.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Two samples.} Each list shows the weekly reading hours
reported by $8$ students.
\par\smallskip
Sample A, from the school book club: \ $14,\ 7,\ 6,\ 11,\ 7,\ 9,\ 8,\ 10$.
\par
Sample B, from $8$ students drawn at random from the roll: \
$0,\ 8,\ 3,\ 6,\ 5,\ 1,\ 3,\ 2$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Find the median of Sample A.
\quad \textbf{(b)} Find the median of Sample B.
\quad \textbf{(c)} The school wants one number for a typical student. Which
median should it use, and why is the other one misleading?}

\problem{\textbf{Show what you know.} The student council wants to know what
fraction of the school's $300$ students would use a later bus. Ben plans to ask
the $30$ students already waiting for the current late bus.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why Ben's sample is biased, and say whether his estimate
will come out too high or too low.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Describe a sampling plan that would not be biased, and say what
makes it fair.
\par
\textbf{(c)} The council instead asks $150$ students chosen at random, and $45$
say they would use the later bus. Write that probability as a fraction in lowest
terms, then estimate how many of the $300$ students would use it.
