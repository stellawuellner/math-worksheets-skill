\noindent\textbf{How to use this key.} Each solution shows the model filled in,
then the collected polynomial. Watch for the two errors this sheet is built
around: combining terms that are not like terms, and distributing a minus sign
or a factor to only the first term inside a parenthesis.

\problem{Add $(3x^3 + 2x^2 - 5) + (x^3 - 4x^2 + 7)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Line the terms up by power. The $x^3$ column holds
$3x^3 + x^3 = 4x^3$; the $x^2$ column holds $2x^2 + (-4x^2) = -2x^2$; the $x$
column is empty in both polynomials; the constant column holds $-5 + 7 = 2$.
Only coefficients are added --- the exponents never change, because adding like
terms is the distributive property run backwards:
$3x^3 + x^3 = (3+1)x^3$. Collected in descending order,
$\ans{4x^3-2x^2+2}$.}

\problem{Add $(2x^4 - x^2 + 6x) + (5x^4 + 3x^2 - 6x)$.

\textbf{Solution:} $x^4$: $2x^4 + 5x^4 = 7x^4$. \ $x^2$: $-x^2 + 3x^2 = 2x^2$.
\ $x$: $6x + (-6x) = 0$, so that column vanishes entirely and no $x$ term
appears in the answer. Losing a column lowers no degree here --- the leading
$x^4$ column survived, so the sum still has degree 4. The result is
$\ans{7x^4+2x^2}$.}

\problem{Subtract $(4x^3 - 2x + 9) - (x^3 + 5x - 3)$.

\textbf{Solution:} The subtraction sign belongs to the whole second
polynomial, so distribute it first: $-(x^3 + 5x - 3) = -x^3 - 5x + 3$. Note the
last term became $+3$. Now add columns: $4x^3 - x^3 = 3x^3$;
$-2x - 5x = -7x$; $9 + 3 = 12$. The difference is $\ans{3x^3-7x+12}$.}

\problem{Multiply $(x + 3)(x^2 - 2x + 5)$.

\textbf{Solution:} The area box has two rows and three columns, so six
products.

\vspace{0.1cm}
\noindent
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$ & $x^2$ & $-2x$ & $5$ \\ \hline
$x$ & $x^3$ & $-2x^2$ & $5x$ \\ \hline
$3$ & $3x^2$ & $-6x$ & $15$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.1cm}
Adding the cells and collecting like terms: $x^3$ stands alone;
$-2x^2 + 3x^2 = x^2$; $5x - 6x = -x$; the constant is 15. So the product is
$\ans{x^3+x^2-x+15}$.}

\problem{Subtract $(5x^4 + 3x^3 - x) - (2x^4 - x^3 + 4x - 8)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the minus to all four terms:
$-2x^4 + x^3 - 4x + 8$. The two already-negative terms flip to positive, which
is the step this problem tests. Adding columns: $5x^4 - 2x^4 = 3x^4$;
$3x^3 + x^3 = 4x^3$; $-x - 4x = -5x$; the constant is $0 + 8 = 8$. The
difference is $\ans{3x^4+4x^3-5x+8}$.}

\problem{Check $(x + 3)(x^2 - 2x + 5) = x^3 + x^2 - x + 15$ at $x = 2$.

\textbf{Solution:} Factored form: $x + 3$ is $5$, and $x^2 - 2x + 5$ is
$4 - 4 + 5 = 5$, so the product is $5 \cdot 5 = 25$. Expanded form:
$8 + 4 - 2 + 15 = \ans{25}$. The two agree, which is strong evidence the
expansion is right --- a single value cannot prove it, but any disagreement
would disprove it immediately. Had the 3 never been distributed, the expansion
would have been $x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x$, worth only 10 at $x = 2$, and the check
would have caught it.}

\problem{Multiply $(2x^2 - 3)(x^2 + 4x - 1)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Six cells again.

\vspace{0.1cm}
\noindent
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$ & $x^2$ & $4x$ & $-1$ \\ \hline
$2x^2$ & $2x^4$ & $8x^3$ & $-2x^2$ \\ \hline
$-3$ & $-3x^2$ & $-12x$ & $3$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.1cm}
The only like pair is $-2x^2$ and $-3x^2$, which combine to $-5x^2$; everything
else stands alone. Collected:
\[ \boxed{2x^4+8x^3-5x^2-12x+3} \]}

\problem{Factor $x^3 + x^2 - 4x - 4$ by grouping.

\textbf{Solution:} Group as $(x^3 + x^2) + (-4x - 4)$. The first group gives
$x^2(x + 1)$; the second gives $-4(x + 1)$. The common factor $(x+1)$ comes
out: $(x + 1)(x^2 - 4)$. The second factor is a difference of squares, so it
splits again: $\ans{(x-2)(x+2)(x+1)}$. This matches the claimed factors, so the
expansion was correct.}

\problem{Expand $(x^2 + x - 1)^2$.

\textbf{Solution:} A square is a product of the trinomial with itself, so the
box is three by three --- nine cells, not three.

\vspace{0.1cm}
\noindent
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$ & $x^2$ & $x$ & $-1$ \\ \hline
$x^2$ & $x^4$ & $x^3$ & $-x^2$ \\ \hline
$x$ & $x^3$ & $x^2$ & $-x$ \\ \hline
$-1$ & $-x^2$ & $-x$ & $1$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.1cm}
Collecting: $x^4$; \ $x^3 + x^3 = 2x^3$; \ $-x^2 + x^2 - x^2 = -x^2$; \
$-x - x = -2x$; \ constant $1$. So
\[ \boxed{x^4+2x^3-x^2-2x+1} \]}

\problem{A student writes $(x^3 - 2)^2 = x^6 - 4$.

\textbf{Solution:} The student squared each term separately, treating
``squaring'' as if it distributed over subtraction. It does not: squaring is
multiplication by the whole binomial, so the box has four cells, and the two
cross terms are exactly what was dropped.

\vspace{0.1cm}
\noindent
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$ & $x^3$ & $-2$ \\ \hline
$x^3$ & $x^6$ & $-2x^3$ \\ \hline
$-2$ & $-2x^3$ & $4$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.1cm}
The two cross cells sum to $-4x^3$, and the last cell is $+4$, not $-4$,
because a negative times a negative is positive. The correct expansion is
$\ans{x^6-4x^3+4}$.}
