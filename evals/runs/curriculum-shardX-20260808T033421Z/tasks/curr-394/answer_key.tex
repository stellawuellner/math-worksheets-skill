\problem{$a_n = 4n + 3$ for $n \ge 1$; a student gave $19$ as the fifth term. (a) Correct fifth term. (b) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The rule starts at $n = 1$, so the fifth term is the value at $n = 5$.
\[ a_5 = 4(5) + 3 = 20 + 3 \]
\ans{23}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The student used $n = 4$: $4(4) + 3 = 19$. That
is the \emph{fourth} term. Because the rule is stated for $n \ge 1$, the first term sits at
$n = 1$, so counting from zero shifts every term one place. Full credit names the starting
index and says which value of $n$ the fifth term uses. ``They used the wrong number'' does
not identify the off-by-one.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Evaluate $\sum_{k=1}^{4}(2k+1)$; a student wrote $25$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The notation says $k$ runs $1, 2, 3, 4$ --- four terms, and no $k = 0$
term.
\[ (2 \cdot 1 + 1) + (2 \cdot 2 + 1) + (2 \cdot 3 + 1) + (2 \cdot 4 + 1) = 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 \]
The correct total is \ans{24}
The student's $25$ is this total plus the $k = 0$ term, which is $1$.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$5$, $15$, $45$; a student reported the ratio as $10$. (a) Correct ratio. (b) Sixth term.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} A common ratio is found by \emph{dividing} consecutive terms, not
subtracting them.
\[ \frac{15}{5} = 3, \qquad \frac{45}{15} = 3 \]
Both quotients agree, confirming the sequence is geometric. \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The sixth term is five ratio steps past the first.
\[ a_6 = 5 \cdot 3^5 = 5 \cdot 243 \]
\ans{1215}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$(x+2)^4$. (a) Expand. (b) Correct coefficient of the third term (a student said $32$).}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Row $4$ of Pascal's triangle is $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$, and the powers of $2$
climb as the powers of $x$ fall.
\begin{align*}
  (x + 2)^4 &= x^4 + 4x^3(2) + 6x^2(4) + 4x(8) + 16
\end{align*}
\ans{x^4 + 8x^3 + 24x^2 + 32x + 16}

\textbf{Solution (b):} In descending powers, the third term is the $x^2$ term, which uses
$k = 2$, not $k = 3$:
\[ \binom{4}{2} \cdot 2^2 = 6 \cdot 4 \]
so the coefficient is \ans{24}
Using $k = 3$ gives $\binom{4}{3} \cdot 2^3 = 32$, which is the \emph{fourth} term --- the
same off-by-one as problem $1$, now inside an expansion.

\problem{A student wrote $\frac{3}{1-2} = -3$ for $3 + 6 + 12 + \cdots$. (a) Why it is meaningless. (b) Sum of $3 + \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{4} + \cdots$.}

\vspace{0.05cm}
\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The infinite geometric sum formula is valid
only when the common ratio lies strictly between $-1$ and $1$. Here the ratio is $2$, so the
terms grow without bound and the series has no sum at all; the formula returns a number, but
that number means nothing. A second, simpler check: every term is positive, so no sum of
them could ever be negative. Full credit names the condition on the ratio; the sign
observation is a welcome extra.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b):} Here the ratio is $\tfrac{1}{2}$, which does satisfy the condition.
\[ S = \frac{a}{1 - r} = \frac{3}{1 - \tfrac{1}{2}} = \frac{3}{\tfrac{1}{2}} \]
\ans{6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$a_n = 5n - 2$, $n \ge 1$. Sum of the first $20$ terms; a student summed $21$ terms and got $1008$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The first $20$ terms are $n = 1$ through $n = 20$. Splitting the sum:
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{n=1}^{20} (5n - 2) &= 5\sum_{n=1}^{20} n \; - \; 2(20) \\
                           &= 5 \cdot \frac{20 \cdot 21}{2} - 40 \\
                           &= 1050 - 40
\end{align*}
\ans{1010}
The student's $1008$ is $1010$ plus the $n = 0$ term, which is $-2$: one term too many, taken
from the wrong end.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Coefficient of $x^2$ in $(2x + 3)^5$; a student got $540$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $x^2$ term takes $(2x)$ twice and $3$ three times:
\begin{align*}
  \binom{5}{2}(2x)^2(2)^3 &= 10 \cdot 4x^2 \cdot 27 \\
                          &= 1080x^2
\end{align*}
The whole factor $2x$ is squared, so the $2$ is squared with it. The coefficient is
\ans{1080}
The student's $540$ comes from writing $2$ instead of $2^2 = 4$ --- exactly half.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Positive geometric sequence with third term $20$ and fifth term $80$. (a) Ratio. (b) First term. (c) Sum of six terms.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Two ratio steps separate the third term from the fifth, so
\[ r^2 = \frac{80}{20} = 4 \]
and, since every term is positive, $r$ is the positive root. \ans{2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The third term is two steps past the first, so $a_1 r^2 = 20$:
\[ a_1 = \frac{20}{2^2} = \frac{20}{4} \]
\ans{5}

\textbf{Solution (c):} With $a_1 = 5$, $r = 2$ and six terms,
\[ S_6 = \frac{5(2^6 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 5(64 - 1) = 5(63) \]
\ans{315}
A quick sanity check: $5 + 10 + 20 + 40 + 80 + 160 = 315$.
