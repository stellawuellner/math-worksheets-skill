# Choosing a Right-Triangle Method — High School Geometry

Three PDFs, all gated green:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_rtmethods_curr325.pdf`, 7 pages) — 10 problems. The sheet
  is built so the *method decision* is the work: consecutive problems never call
  for the same tool, and the givens (not the wording) are what distinguish them.
  Every problem carries its own to-scale figure generated by
  `scripts/render_figures.py` from the same verified JSON the answer key uses,
  so no figure can disagree with its answer. A value-free reference triangle
  sits with the directions to fix the $A/B/C$, $a/b/c$ labelling convention.
- **Answer key** (`ak_rtmethods_curr325.pdf`, 3 pages) — every solution opens by
  *naming the method and why the givens select it* ("two sides and no angle
  anywhere — a trig ratio would need an angle this problem never supplies"),
  then computes. Seven declared misconception traps are printed as a "Common
  wrong answers" bank generated from the JSON, and repeated inline under their
  own problems.
- **Study guide** (`ss_rtmethods_curr325.pdf`, 2 pages) — opens with the decision
  rule itself, then one section per method: rule box, worked example, distinct
  try-it with the answer upside down. The trig box covers both directions
  (side missing → ratio; angle missing → inverse ratio) with a worked inverse
  line, because two worksheet problems need it.

## What the sheet actually exercises

| Facet | Problems |
|---|---|
| `pythagorean` | 1, 4, 8 |
| `special-triangle` (45-45-90, 30-60-90) | 2, 6, 9 |
| `trig-method` (ratio and inverse ratio) | 3, 5, 7, 10 |

Max same-facet run = 1 — the student must re-decide on every problem.
Difficulty ramp `1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5`. Problem 4 gives the hypotenuse
as one of the two known sides (so Pythagoras must be rearranged); problem 5
inverts a ratio to get an angle; problem 7 asks only for the ratio, not a
length; problems 8 and 9 are contexts where recognising the triangle is half the
task; problem 10 is the synthesis — a non-right isosceles triangle that must
first be split by an altitude before any right-triangle method applies.
