\problem{Solve $\sqrt{x+5} = 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The radical is already alone, so square both sides:
$x + 5 = 16$, hence $x = 11$. Check: $\sqrt{11+5} = \sqrt{16} = 4$
$\checkmark$ \ans{x = 11}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Fall time $t = 0.45\sqrt{d}$ seconds, $d$ in metres, $t = 2.7$ s. Find
the drop height.}

\textbf{Solution:} Isolate the radical, then square:
\begin{align*}
  0.45\sqrt{d} &= 2.7 \\
  \sqrt{d} &= 6 \\
  d &= 36 .
\end{align*}
Check: $0.45\sqrt{36} = 0.45 \times 6 = 2.7$ $\checkmark$
\ans{d = 36 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\sqrt{2x+3} = x$. (a) Solve. (b) The left side at $x = -1$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Square both sides: $2x + 3 = x^2$, so
$x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$ and $(x-3)(x+1) = 0$. The candidates are $x = 3$ and
$x = -1$. Testing in the \emph{original} equation: $\sqrt{9} = 3$
$\checkmark$, while $\sqrt{1} = 1 \ne -1$ $\times$. Only one survives.
\ans{x = 3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\sqrt{2(-1)+3} = \sqrt{1}$. \ans{1}

The left side is $1$ and the right side is $-1$: a square root cannot be
negative, which is why the squaring step manufactured this candidate.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $2\sqrt{x-1} + 3 = 11$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Isolate the radical before squaring:
$2\sqrt{x-1} = 8$, so $\sqrt{x-1} = 4$, so $x - 1 = 16$.
Check: $2\sqrt{17-1} + 3 = 2(4) + 3 = 11$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 17}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Skid marks: $s = \sqrt{21d}$ mph, $d$ in feet, $s = 42$. Find $d$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Square both sides and divide:
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{21d} &= 42 \\
  21d &= 1764 \\
  d &= 84 .
\end{align*}
Check: $\sqrt{21 \times 84} = \sqrt{1764} = 42$ $\checkmark$
\ans{d = 84 \text{ ft}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\sqrt{x+7} = x-5$. (a) Solve. (b) The left side at $x=2$.
(c) Why $x=2$ is rejected.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Square: $x + 7 = x^2 - 10x + 25$, so
$x^2 - 11x + 18 = 0$ and $(x-9)(x-2) = 0$. Candidates $x = 9$ and $x = 2$.
Test in the original: $\sqrt{16} = 4 = 9 - 5$ $\checkmark$; and
$\sqrt{9} = 3$ while $2 - 5 = -3$ $\times$. \ans{x = 9}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\sqrt{2+7} = \sqrt{9}$. \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} At $x = 2$ the left side is $3$
and the right side is $-3$. Squaring destroys the sign --- $3^2$ and $(-3)^2$ are
the same number --- so the squared equation is satisfied while the original is
not. Since $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ never returns a negative value, no $x$ making the
right side negative can be a solution. A full-credit answer names the sign
mismatch, not just "the numbers did not match".

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $\sqrt{3x+1} = \sqrt{x+9}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Both sides are non-negative, so squaring is safe here:
$3x + 1 = x + 9$, so $2x = 8$. Check: $\sqrt{13} = \sqrt{13}$ $\checkmark$
\ans{x = 4}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Horizon distance $D = 3.57\sqrt{h}$ km, $h$ in metres.
(a) $h$ when $D = 35.7$. (b) $D$ when $h = 250$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $3.57\sqrt{h} = 35.7$ gives $\sqrt{h} = 10$, so
$h = 100$. Check: $3.57\sqrt{100} = 35.7$ $\checkmark$
\ans{h = 100 \text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $D = 3.57\sqrt{250} = 3.57 \times 15.8114\ldots
= 56.4467\ldots$, which rounds to \ans{56.45 \text{ km}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\sqrt{x+4} + 2 = x$. (a) Solve. (b) The left side at $x = 0$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Isolate first: $\sqrt{x+4} = x - 2$. Square:
$x + 4 = x^2 - 4x + 4$, so $x^2 - 5x = 0$ and $x(x-5) = 0$. Candidates $x = 0$
and $x = 5$. Test: $\sqrt{9} + 2 = 5$ $\checkmark$; $\sqrt{4} + 2 = 4 \ne 0$
$\times$. \ans{x = 5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\sqrt{0+4} + 2 = 2 + 2$. \ans{4}

Since $4 \ne 0$, the candidate $x = 0$ is extraneous.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Challenge: $\sqrt{x+5} - \sqrt{x} = 1$. (a) Solve. (b) Check.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Isolate one radical and square:
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{x+7} &= 1 + \sqrt{x} \\
  x + 5 &= 1 + 2\sqrt{x} + x \\
  4 &= 2\sqrt{x} \\
  \sqrt{x} &= 2 \\
  x &= 4 .
\end{align*}
\ans{x = 4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\sqrt{4+5} - \sqrt{4} = 3 - 2$, which equals the right
side of the original equation, so the candidate is genuine.
\ans{1}
