# Drawing and Using Informal Lines of Fit — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_fitline_curr247.pdf`, 6 pages) — 12 problems in two parts. Part A
  (problems 1–7) works on scatter plots: draw a line of fit with a ruler, find the slope
  of a drawn line, use it to predict, write its equation. Part B (problems 8–12) starts
  from the equation and asks what it predicts, what the slope means in context, and when
  a prediction stops being trustworthy. No two problems share the same skeleton, and the
  ramp runs from reading one value off a plot to a pulse-and-exercise problem that ends
  with the correlation-is-not-causation question.
- **Answer key** (`ak_fitline_curr247.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem showing which two
  points were used for the slope and the substitution behind each prediction.
- **Study guide** (`ss_fitline_curr247.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections: drawing the line
  (with the mean-of-the-outputs anchor tip), finding its slope, predicting with it, and
  writing its equation from two points. Each has a rule box, a worked example and a
  try-it.

## What is verified

**5 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy (problems 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8) —
21 individual checks in all. The other seven are **partly instructor-judged**:

| Problem | Machine-checked | Instructor-judged |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the greatest plotted value, 28 | the drawn line of fit |
| 4 | the mean, 14 | the drawn line of fit |
| 7 | slope 3, and 31 at x = 8 | the written equation y = 3x + 7 |
| 9 | the prediction, 72 | what the slope 6 means in context |
| 10 | slope −3, and 5 at x = 9 | the written equation y = −3x + 32 |
| 11 | the prediction, 27 | why a day-40 prediction is not trustworthy |
| 12 | pulse 68, change −4 per 10 minutes | whether the line shows causation |

That is the same split the key's own "What is verified" note prints. Note that a
student's *drawn* line and *written equation* are things only you can grade — a line of
fit is not unique, so the key gives criteria (through the middle of the cloud, roughly
equal numbers of points either side, not just first-to-last) rather than a single right
answer.

## Notes for you

- Problem 12 is the one to talk about. A student who says "yes, exercise causes a lower
  pulse" has done the arithmetic right and the statistics wrong.
- Problem 11 is the extrapolation trap: the same equation that predicts day 6 perfectly
  well says nothing reliable about day 40.
- Standard covered: 8.SP.A.1 across all twelve problems.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
