\problem{\textbf{Method: Pythagorean theorem.}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} Two legs are given and the hypotenuse is missing, with no
angle anywhere in the problem --- that is the Pythagorean signature. A trig
ratio here would need an angle the problem does not supply.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $c = \sqrt{100} = \ans{10}$ \quad (the $3$--$4$--$5$ triple
scaled by two, so the whole-number answer is a good sign).}

\problem{\textbf{Method: special triangle ($45$--$45$--$90$).}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} The right angle sits at $C$ and one acute angle is $45^\circ$,
so the third angle is also $45^\circ$: this is the isosceles right triangle,
whose sides are in the ratio $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} In that ratio the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}$ times a leg, and
the given leg is $b = 7$, so $c = 7\sqrt{2}$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $c = 7\sqrt{2} \approx \ans{9.90}$}

\problem{\textbf{Method: trig ratio (tangent).}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} An angle of $35^\circ$ is given --- not a special angle --- with
the side \emph{adjacent} to it, and the side \emph{opposite} it is wanted. The
ratio linking opposite to adjacent is the tangent, so no hypotenuse is needed.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} $\tan 35^\circ = \dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{a}{9}$, so
$a = 9\tan 35^\circ$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $a \approx \ans{6.30}$\\
\textit{If they got $5.16$: used sine instead of tangent --- sine would need
the hypotenuse, which this problem never gives.}}

\problem{\textbf{Method: Pythagorean theorem (missing leg).}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} Again two sides and no angle, so Pythagoras --- but here one
of the given sides is the \emph{hypotenuse} $c$, so the theorem is rearranged
rather than applied straight.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} $b^2 = c^2 - a^2 = 15^2 - 9^2 = 225 - 81 = 144$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $b = \sqrt{144} = \ans{12}$\\
\textit{If they got $17.49$: added the squares instead of subtracting, which
treats the hypotenuse as a leg.}}

\problem{\textbf{Method: inverse trig ratio.}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} Here the missing quantity is an \emph{angle} and two sides are
known, so the tool is an inverse ratio. Relative to angle $A$, the side
$a = 5$ is opposite and $c = 13$ is the hypotenuse --- opposite over hypotenuse
is sine, so use $\sin^{-1}$.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} $\sin A = \dfrac{5}{13}$, so
$A = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{5}{13}\right)$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $A \approx \ans{22.62^\circ}$\\
\textit{If they got $67.38^\circ$: applied $\cos^{-1}$ to the same ratio, which
returns the other acute angle $B$.}}

\problem{\textbf{Method: special triangle ($30$--$60$--$90$).}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} A $30^\circ$ acute angle with the right angle at $C$ makes
this the half-equilateral triangle, sides in the ratio $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$ ---
exact, so no calculator is needed.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} The side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle is the short leg, and
it is half the hypotenuse. Here $a$ is opposite $A = 30^\circ$ and $c = 12$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $a = \tfrac{1}{2}(12) = \ans{6}$\\
\textit{If they got $10.39$: used the ratio the wrong way round and returned
the long leg $b = 6\sqrt{3}$.}}

\problem{\textbf{Method: definition of the ratio (no computation).}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} The question asks for a ratio, not a length, so nothing needs
solving --- read the two sides off the figure relative to angle $A$.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} Opposite $A$ is $a = 8$; adjacent to $A$ (and not the
hypotenuse) is $b = 15$. Tangent is opposite over adjacent.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $\tan A = \ans{\,\dfrac{8}{15}}$ \quad (already in lowest
terms, since $8$ and $15$ share no factor).}

\problem{\textbf{Method: Pythagorean theorem in context.}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} Wall, ground and ladder form a right triangle; the wall is
vertical so the right angle is between wall and ground. Two legs are known and
the ladder is the hypotenuse, and no angle is given --- Pythagoras.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} $\text{ladder}^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $\text{ladder} = \sqrt{169} = \ans{13 \text{ ft}}$\\
\textit{If they got $10.91$: subtracted the squares, which treats the
$12$ ft wall height as the hypotenuse --- but the ladder is the longest side.}}

\problem{\textbf{Method: special triangle ($45$--$45$--$90$) in context.}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} A square's diagonal makes a $45^\circ$ angle with each side,
so half the square is the isosceles right triangle: the diagonal is the
hypotenuse and the two sides are the equal legs.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} With hypotenuse $10$, a leg is
$10\sin 45^\circ = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{2}} = 5\sqrt{2}$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} side $= 5\sqrt{2} \approx \ans{7.07 \text{ cm}}$\\
\textit{If they got $5$: halved the diagonal, which is what a rectangle's
\emph{other} diagonal would do, not the $45$--$45$--$90$ ratio.}}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge --- method: split the isosceles triangle, then cosine.}\\
\textbf{Step 1.} The triangle is not right angled, so no single ratio applies
to it as drawn. The altitude from $C$ to the base $c$ bisects the base
(because the two slanted sides are equal), producing two congruent right
triangles --- and \emph{that} is where a ratio can be used.\\
\textbf{Step 2.} In one of those right triangles, the base angle $A$ sits with
an adjacent side of $\tfrac{1}{2}(12) = 6$ and hypotenuse $10$. Adjacent over
hypotenuse is cosine, so
$\cos A = \dfrac{6}{10}$.\\
\textbf{Step 3.} $A = \cos^{-1}(0.6) \approx \ans{53.13^\circ}$, and by
symmetry the other base angle is the same.\\
\textit{If they got $36.87^\circ$: used $\sin^{-1}$ on the same ratio, which
gives the angle at the apex half, not the base angle.}}
