\problem{Break-even prices for $P(x) = (x-2)(x-8)$, profit in dollars, $x$ the
price per cup in dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} A product is zero exactly when one of its factors is zero.
$x - 2 = 0$ gives $x = 2$; $x - 8 = 0$ gives $x = 8$. Both are sensible prices,
so the stand breaks even at \ans{x = 2 \text{ or } x = 8 \text{ dollars}}

\problem{$g(x) = (x+3)^2(x-1)$. (a) All real zeros. (b) The zero where the curve
turns back, with a reason.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Set each factor to zero: $(x+3)^2 = 0$ gives $x = -3$, and
$x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = 1$. \ans{x = -3 \text{ or } x = 1}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The zero is $x = -3$. The factor
$(x+3)$ appears to the second power, so that zero has multiplicity $2$ --- an even
multiplicity. Near an even-multiplicity zero the factor $(x+3)^2$ never changes
sign, so $g$ keeps the same sign on both sides and the curve meets the axis and
turns back instead of passing through. A full-credit answer names $x = -3$
\emph{and} gives the even-multiplicity reason.

\problem{Tray volume $V(x) = x^3 - 9x^2 + 20x$ cubic centimetres. (a) Factor
completely. (b) Values of $x$ with zero volume.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Every term carries an $x$, so take the greatest common
factor first:
\[ V(x) = x\left(x^2 - 9x + 20\right). \]
Then $x^2 - 9x + 20$ needs two numbers with product $20$ and sum $-9$: those are
$-4$ and $-5$. \ans{V(x) = x(x-4)(x-5)}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Each factor gives one zero: $x = 0$, $x = 4$, $x = 5$.
\ans{x = 0,\ 4,\ 5}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{$f(x) = (x-1)(x+4)^2$. (a) All real zeros. (b) The $y$-intercept $f(0)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = 1$; $(x+4)^2 = 0$ gives $x = -4$
(multiplicity $2$). \ans{x = 1 \text{ or } x = -4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Substitute $x = 0$: $f(0) = (0-1)(0+4)^2 = (-1)(16)$.
\ans{f(0) = -16}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{$p(x) = -(x+2)(x-3)^2$. (a) $p(1)$. (b) End behavior, and behavior at
$x = 3$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $p(1) = -(1+2)(1-3)^2 = -(3)(4)$. \ans{p(1) = -12}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} Multiplying out, the leading term
is $-x^3$: odd degree with a negative leading coefficient. So the curve rises
without bound far to the left and falls without bound far to the right. At
$x = 3$ the factor $(x-3)$ is squared, an even multiplicity, so the curve meets
the axis there and turns back rather than crossing. A full-credit answer states
both ends correctly and names even multiplicity as the reason for the turn.

\problem{$q(x) = x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$. (a) Factor completely. (b) All real zeros.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} This is a quadratic in $x^2$. Two numbers with product $36$
and sum $-13$ are $-4$ and $-9$:
\[ q(x) = \left(x^2 - 4\right)\left(x^2 - 9\right). \]
Each bracket is a difference of two squares.
\ans{q(x) = (x-2)(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)}

\textbf{Solution (b):} One zero per factor.
\ans{x = -3,\ -2,\ 2,\ 3}

\problem{Build a degree-3 model that turns back at $x = -1$, crosses at $x = 4$,
leading coefficient $1$. (a) Factored form and zeros. (b) Standard form.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Turning back at $x = -1$ needs even multiplicity, and
degree $3$ with a single crossing at $x = 4$ leaves multiplicity $2$ for $x = -1$:
\[ M(x) = (x+1)^2(x-4). \]
Its zeros are \ans{x = -1 \text{ or } x = 4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Expand $(x+1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$, then multiply by $x - 4$:
\begin{align*}
  (x^2 + 2x + 1)(x - 4) &= x^3 + 2x^2 + x - 4x^2 - 8x - 4 \\
                        &= x^3 - 2x^2 - 7x - 4 .
\end{align*}
\ans{M(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 - 7x - 4}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{$C(x) = a(x-1)(x-4)(x-6)$ dollars, with $C(0) = 48$ dollars. Find $a$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute $x = 0$ and set the result equal to $48$:
\begin{align*}
  a(0-1)(0-4)(0-6) &= 48 \\
  a(-1)(-4)(-6) &= 48 \\
  -24a &= 48 .
\end{align*}
Dividing by $-24$ gives \ans{a = -2}

\problem{$f(x) = (x+1)(x-2)^2$. (a) Zeros. (b) $f(1)$. (c) Sketch showing the
behavior at each zero.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $x + 1 = 0$ gives $x = -1$ (multiplicity $1$); $(x-2)^2 = 0$
gives $x = 2$ (multiplicity $2$). \ans{x = -1 \text{ or } x = 2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $f(1) = (1+1)(1-2)^2 = (2)(1)$. \ans{f(1) = 2}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} A correct sketch crosses the
$x$-axis at $x = -1$, meets the axis at $x = 2$ and turns back without crossing,
passes through $(1, 2)$, and --- since the leading term is $x^3$ --- falls far to
the left and rises far to the right. The model curve is shown below.

\problem{Berm height $R(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + 4x$ metres. (a) Factor completely.
(b) Zeros. (c) Meaning of the repeated zero.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Take out the common $x$, then recognise a perfect square:
\[ R(x) = x\left(x^2 - 4x + 4\right) = x(x-2)^2 . \]
\ans{R(x) = x(x-2)^2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $x = 0$ from the first factor and $x = 2$ from the squared
factor. \ans{x = 0 \text{ or } x = 2}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The zero $x = 2$ has multiplicity
$2$. Because that factor is squared it never changes sign, so the berm height
comes down to the path at $2$ metres along, touches it, and rises again --- it
does not pass below the path. At $x = 0$ the single factor means the height
does cross the path level. A full-credit answer reads the even exponent as the
reason for the turn.
