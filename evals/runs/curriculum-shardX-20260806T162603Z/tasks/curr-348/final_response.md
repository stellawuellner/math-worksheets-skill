Three PDFs are ready:

## What the worksheet covers

Every problem is a real use of a dilation, with the units stated in the stem and
no story detail that does not do work:

1. A design grid point shrunk by a factor of 0.5.
2. A photograph enlarged from 12 cm to 30 cm — the scale factor as a ratio.
3. A blueprint at 1 cm : 40 cm.
4. A plan dilated by 3, with the segment measured before and after so the student
   sees a length multiplied by exactly 3.
5. A model car against the real car, with the unit conversion built in.
6. The classic shadow problem — similar right triangles, flagpole height.
7. A logo dilated about a centre that is *not* the origin.
8. The challenge: a courtyard scaled by 2.5, where the perimeter grows by 2.5 and
   the area by 6.25, and the student has to say why.

## What is verified, and what is not

The sheet asks for **15 separate responses** across the 8 problems.

- **14 are machine-checked** with SymPy — every image coordinate (an $x$ and a $y$
  are two answers, not one), every scale factor, both segment lengths in problem 4,
  and the perimeter and area in problem 8. Declared units (cm, m, m²) are bound in
  both directions between the sheet's answer lines and the key's boxes.
- **1 is instructor-judged: problem 8(c)**, the written explanation of why one
  scale factor produces two different multipliers. It is marked `---` in the Quick
  Answers bank, and the key gives a model response with a full / half / no-credit
  rubric. The key's own "What is verified" note says 14 of 15.

There are no `[unchecked]` marks.

## Grading help

Three problems carry declared misconception traps, each confirmed distinguishable
by the verifier and printed in the key under **Common wrong answers**:

- 18 — the two widths subtracted instead of divided (problem 2)
- 0.25 — metres divided into centimetres with no conversion (problem 5)
- 20.25 — the shadow proportion set up upside down (problem 6)

Problem 7's key also names the near-miss that is not a trap: dilating about the
origin instead of the given centre gives (20, 4), the right size in the wrong
place.

## The study guide

Opens by stating what a dilation does (multiplies lengths, preserves angles, and
so produces a similar figure). Three sections follow — image coordinates including
the non-origin centre, finding a scale factor from two lengths with a warning
about units, and setting up a proportion for shadows and scale drawings. Each has
a worked example whose first step is the reasoning move and a try-it with the
answer upside down inside the box. The closing watch-out box flags the $k$ versus
$k^2$ trap that problem 8 is built on.
