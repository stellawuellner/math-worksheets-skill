\begin{center}\small\textit{Choosing and computing the right summary of center and spread
once the shape of the distribution is known}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{Directions:}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Three displays are printed below: two dot plots of quiz scores and one histogram of
        commute times. Every problem names the display it uses.
  \item Use the school method for quartiles: $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half of the
        ordered data and $Q_3$ is the median of the upper half, with the overall median
        excluded when the count is odd.
  \item Round decimal answers to the nearest hundredth unless a problem says otherwise.
  \item Where a problem asks for a description of \textbf{shape}, the words are
        \emph{symmetric}, \emph{skewed right}, \emph{skewed left}, and \emph{outlier}.
\end{itemize}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Class A --- what the two centres tell you about the shape.} You now
have the mean ($66.67$) and the median ($65$) for Class A.

\smallskip
(a) Write $>$, $<$, or $=$ between them: \quad mean \underline{\hspace{1.2cm}} median.

\smallskip
(b) A long tail pulls the mean toward it and leaves the median where it is. Use your
comparison and the dot plot to name the shape of the Class A distribution.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Class B --- a symmetric distribution.}

\smallskip
(a) Find the mean of the Class B scores. \quad (b) Find the median.

\smallskip
(c) Compare the two answers, then say what that agreement tells you about the shape of

\problem[8cm]{\textbf{Two measures of spread, disagreeing.}

\smallskip
(a) Find the range of the Class B scores. \quad (b) Find the IQR of the Class B scores.

\par\smallskip
Class B range $= $~\ansblank \qquad Class B IQR $= $~\ansblank

\smallskip
(c) Class A's range was $44$ and its IQR was $12.5$; Class B's answers are above. Put the
two ranges side by side, then the two IQRs. One pair says the classes are very different and
the other says they are nearly the same. Explain which measure is telling the truth about
the bulk of the data, and why the other one disagrees.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{The commute histogram --- reading the shape.}

\smallskip
(a) Which interval holds the most workers? \quad (b) How many workers are in the sample
altogether?

\par\smallskip
modal interval~\ansblank \qquad total workers~\ansblank

\smallskip
(c) Describe the shape of this distribution in one sentence, naming which side the tail is
on and what that predicts about the mean compared with the median.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{The commute histogram --- a tail probability.} One worker is chosen
at random from the sample.

\smallskip
Find the probability that the worker's commute is at least $40$ minutes. Give the answer as
an exact fraction in lowest terms.

\smallskip
Show which bars you added.}

\problem[9cm]{\textbf{Challenge --- reporting the right summary.} A school newsletter has
room for exactly one measure of centre and one measure of spread for each of the three
distributions on this sheet.

\smallskip
For \textbf{Class A}, \textbf{Class B}, and the \textbf{commute times}, choose either
``mean and standard deviation'' or ``median and IQR'', and justify each choice from the
shape of that distribution. Your justification must say what the shape is and what would go
wrong with the other choice --- not simply which one you picked.}
