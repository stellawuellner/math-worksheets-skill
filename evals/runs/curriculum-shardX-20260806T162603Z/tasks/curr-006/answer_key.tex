\problem{Diagonal pattern of dots --- how many?}

\textbf{Solution:} The diagonal line of dots is a pattern to know on sight:
one dot in each corner-to-corner step. It shows \ans{3}

\problem{Game-die pattern --- how many?}

\textbf{Solution:} Four corners and one in the middle --- the X pattern from a
die. Children who know the pattern name it without counting: \ans{5}

\problem{Five-frame with one empty box: how many dots, how many empty boxes?}

\textbf{Solution:} The frame holds five boxes. It is almost full --- just one
box has no dot, so there is \ans{1} empty box.

Almost-full means one less than five: $5 - 1 = 4$, so the dots are \ans{4}

\problem{Square pattern: write the parts and the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} The square splits into a top pair and a bottom pair. The
first part is \ans{2}

The second part is \ans{2}

Two and two make $2 + 2 = 4$, so the total is \ans{4}

\problem{Full five-frame: how many dots, how many empty boxes?}

\textbf{Solution:} A full frame is the picture of five --- no counting needed:
\ans{5}

Every box has a dot, so the empty boxes number \ans{0}

\problem{Domino with two halves: write the parts and the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} Left half shows the triangle pattern: \ans{3}

Right half shows the pair: \ans{2}

Three and two make $3 + 2 = 5$, so the total is \ans{5}

\problem{Card A (square pattern) and card B (die pattern): numbers, then
compare.}

\textbf{Solution:} Card A is the square of four: \ans{4}

Card B is the X of five: \ans{5}

Four comes before five when counting, so \ans{4 < 5}

\problem{Challenge: write the two parts you see and the total, then tell how
you saw it.}

\textbf{Solution:} The square of four sits on the left: \ans{4}

One extra dot sits beside it: \ans{1}

Four and one more make $4 + 1 = 5$, so the total is \ans{5}

\textbf{Telling how (instructor-judged):} accept any parts-based seeing ---
``I saw the square of 4 and 1 more,'' or ``4 and 1 make 5.'' Counting all
five dots one by one gets the number but does not show the see-it-fast skill;
invite the child to look for the square inside the pattern.
