\problem{Order the four statements about $\angle FGH$ and $\angle HGK$ and give the reasons.}

\textbf{Order and reasons:}

1. $\overrightarrow{GF}$ and $\overrightarrow{GK}$ are opposite rays, and $\overrightarrow{GH}$ is a third ray from $G$ --- \emph{Given.}

2. $\angle FGH$ and $\angle HGK$ form a linear pair --- \emph{Definition of a linear pair} (adjacent angles whose non-common sides are opposite rays).

3. $m\angle FGH + m\angle HGK = 180$ --- \emph{Linear Pair Postulate.}

4. $m\angle FGH = 180 - m\angle HGK$ --- \emph{Subtraction property of equality.}

\textbf{Grading:} the order is forced by the reasons: nothing before line 1 is available, and line 4 can only come from line 3. Full credit needs both the order and the four reasons.

\problem{Order the five statements proving a midpoint halves a segment.}

\textbf{Order and reasons:}

1. $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$ --- \emph{Given.}

2. $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{MB}$ --- \emph{Definition of midpoint.}

3. $AM = MB$ --- \emph{Definition of congruent segments.}

4. $AM + MB = AB$ --- \emph{Segment Addition Postulate.}

5. $2\,AM = AB$, so $AM = \frac{1}{2}AB$ --- \emph{Substitution, then the division property of equality.}

\textbf{Grading:} lines 2 and 3 are two different reasons and must be kept apart --- the first gives congruence, the second turns congruence into an equation of lengths.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$M$ midpoint of $\overline{AB}$, $AM = 4x - 1$, $MB = 2x + 7$ cm. (a) $x$. (b) $AB$.}

\textbf{(a)} Midpoint gives $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{MB}$, so the lengths are equal:
\begin{align*}
  4x - 1 &= 2x + 7 \\
  2x &= 8
\end{align*}
So $x = 4$. \ans{x = 4}

\textbf{(b)} $AM = 4(4) - 1 = 15$ and $MB = 2(4) + 7 = 15$, so by the Segment Addition Postulate $AB = 2\,AM = 2(15)$. \ans{AB = 30 \, \text{cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Intersecting lines at $V$: $m\angle AVB = 3x + 15$, $m\angle BVC = 5x + 5$, linear pair. (a) $x$. (b) $m\angle CVD$.}

\textbf{(a)} The Linear Pair Postulate gives a sum of $180$:
\begin{align*}
  (3x + 15) + (5x + 5) &= 180 \\
  8x + 20 &= 180 \\
  8x &= 160
\end{align*}
So $x = 20$. Check: $75 + 105 = 180$. \ans{x = 20}

\textbf{(b)} $\angle CVD$ is vertical to $\angle AVB$, so the two are congruent and have equal measures: $m\angle CVD = m\angle AVB = 3(20) + 15$. \ans{m\angle CVD = 75^\circ}

\problem{Order the statements proving complements of the same angle are congruent.}

\textbf{Order and reasons:}

1. $\angle P$ and $\angle Q$ are complementary; $\angle R$ and $\angle Q$ are complementary --- \emph{Given.}

2. $m\angle P + m\angle Q = 90$ and $m\angle R + m\angle Q = 90$ --- \emph{Definition of complementary angles.}

3. $m\angle P + m\angle Q = m\angle R + m\angle Q$ --- \emph{Substitution} (both sums equal $90$).

4. $m\angle P = m\angle R$, so $\angle P \cong \angle R$ --- \emph{Subtraction property of equality}, then the \emph{definition of congruent angles}.

\textbf{Grading:} the substitution step is the one students drop; without it there is no link between the two separate sums.

\problem{$A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ in order with $AB = CD$. (a) Argue $AC = BD$. (b) With $AB = CD = 4.2$ cm and $BC = 11$ cm, find $AC$.}

\textbf{(a) Model argument:}

1. $AB = CD$ --- \emph{Given.}

2. $AB + BC = BC + CD$ --- \emph{Addition property of equality} (the shared stretch $BC$ added to both sides).

3. $AB + BC = AC$ and $BC + CD = BD$ --- \emph{Segment Addition Postulate}; therefore $AC = BD$ by \emph{substitution}.

\textbf{Grading:} the argument must add the shared $BC$ with a named property and rename both sums with the postulate. ``The pieces are equal so the wholes are equal'' with no reasons is partial credit.

\textbf{(b)} $AC = AB + BC = 4.2 + 11$. \ans{AC = 15.2 \, \text{cm}}

\problem{$\overrightarrow{QS}$ bisects $\angle PQR$; $\angle PQR$, $\angle RQT$ a linear pair with $m\angle RQT = 110$; $m\angle PQS = 2x + 5$. (a) The chain. (b) $x$.}

\textbf{(a) The chain:}

1. $\angle PQR$ and $\angle RQT$ form a linear pair --- \emph{Given}; so $m\angle PQR + 110 = 180$ --- \emph{Linear Pair Postulate}.

2. $\overrightarrow{QS}$ bisects $\angle PQR$ --- \emph{Given}; so $m\angle PQR = 2\,m\angle PQS$ --- \emph{Definition of an angle bisector} (with the Angle Addition Postulate).

3. Substituting $m\angle PQS = 2x + 5$ into step 2 and that result into step 1 gives $2(2x + 5) + 110 = 180$ --- \emph{Substitution}.

\textbf{(b)}
\begin{align*}
  2(2x + 5) + 110 &= 180 \\
  4x + 10 + 110 &= 180 \\
  4x &= 60
\end{align*}
So $x = 15$. Check: $m\angle PQS = 35$, $m\angle PQR = 70$, and $70 + 110 = 180$. \ans{x = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$N$ midpoint of $\overline{RT}$, $RN = 3x + 2$, $NT = x + 10$ cm. (a) $x$. (b) $RT$.}

\textbf{(a)}
\begin{align*}
  3x + 2 &= x + 10 \\
  2x &= 8
\end{align*}
So $x = 4$. \ans{x = 4}

\textbf{(b)} $RN = 3(4) + 2 = 14$ and $NT = 4 + 10 = 14$, so $RT = 2\,RN = 2(14)$. \ans{RT = 28 \, \text{cm}}

\problem{$\angle J$, $\angle K$ complementary; $\angle L$, $\angle K$ complementary; $m\angle J = 4x - 6$, $m\angle L = 2x + 18$. (a) Argue $\angle J \cong \angle L$. (b) Find $x$.}

\textbf{(a) Model argument:}

1. $\angle J$ and $\angle K$ are complementary; $\angle L$ and $\angle K$ are complementary --- \emph{Given.}

2. $m\angle J + m\angle K = 90$ and $m\angle L + m\angle K = 90$ --- \emph{Definition of complementary angles.}

3. $m\angle J + m\angle K = m\angle L + m\angle K$ --- \emph{Substitution.}

4. $m\angle J = m\angle L$, so $\angle J \cong \angle L$ --- \emph{Subtraction property of equality} and the \emph{definition of congruent angles}.

\textbf{(b)} The result of (a) lets you set the two expressions equal:
\begin{align*}
  4x - 6 &= 2x + 18 \\
  2x &= 24
\end{align*}
So $x = 12$. Check: $m\angle J = 42$ and $m\angle L = 42$. \ans{x = 12}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\overrightarrow{QS}$ bisects $\angle PQR$; $\angle PQR$, $\angle RQT$ a linear pair; $m\angle PQS = 2x + 15$, $m\angle RQT = 6x$. (a) The whole argument. (b) $x$.}

\textbf{(a) Model argument:}

1. $\overrightarrow{QS}$ bisects $\angle PQR$ --- \emph{Given.}

2. $m\angle PQR = 2\,m\angle PQS = 2(2x + 15)$ --- \emph{Definition of an angle bisector} with the Angle Addition Postulate, then \emph{substitution}.

3. $\angle PQR$ and $\angle RQT$ form a linear pair, so $m\angle PQR + m\angle RQT = 180$ --- \emph{Given} and the \emph{Linear Pair Postulate}.

4. $2(2x + 15) + 6x = 180$ --- \emph{Substitution} of steps 2 and the given $m\angle RQT = 6x$ into step 3.

\textbf{(b)}
\begin{align*}
  2(2x + 15) + 6x &= 180 \\
  4x + 30 + 6x &= 180 \\
  10x &= 150
\end{align*}
So $x = 15$. Check: $m\angle PQS = 45$, $m\angle PQR = 90$, $m\angle RQT = 90$, and $90 + 90 = 180$. \ans{x = 15}

\textbf{Grading (a):} both givens used, the bisector definition doubling the marked half, the linear pair postulate supplying the sum, and a substitution that lands on one equation in $x$.
