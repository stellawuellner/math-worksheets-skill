\problem{Hypotenuse $20$ cm, $m\angle A = 34^\circ$; the student computed
$20\cos 34^\circ = 16.58$ cm for the side opposite $\angle A$. (a) Correct
length. (b) Name the error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The wanted side is \emph{opposite} $\angle A$
and the known side is the hypotenuse, so the pairing is sine:
\[ \sin 34^\circ = \frac{\text{opp}}{20} \qquad \text{opp} = 20\sin 34^\circ = 11.1838\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{11.18 \text{ cm}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
opposite-with-hypotenuse is the sine ratio, and that cosine returns the
\emph{adjacent} side instead --- so $16.58$ cm is the length of the other leg,
not the one asked for. Award full credit only when the response names which side
each ratio actually returns.

\problem{Hypotenuse $15$ in, $m\angle A = 52^\circ$; the student entered
$15\cos 52$ and got $-2.44$ in for the side adjacent to $\angle A$. (a) Correct
length. (b) Name the error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} Adjacent-with-hypotenuse is cosine, and the ratio
is correct --- only the angle mode was wrong:
\[ \text{adj} = 15\cos 52^\circ = 9.2349\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{9.23 \text{ in}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
the calculator was in radian mode, so $52$ was read as $52$ radians rather than
$52$ degrees. Award full credit only when the response also names the
impossibility that exposes it: a side length cannot be negative, and no leg can
exceed the hypotenuse.

\problem{Opposite side $7$ cm, hypotenuse $13$ cm; the student wrote
$m\angle A = \tan^{-1}(7/13) = 28.30^\circ$. (a) Correct angle. (b) Name the
error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The two known sides are the opposite side and
the hypotenuse, so their ratio is $\sin A$ and the angle is the inverse sine:
\[ \sin A = \frac{7}{13} \qquad A = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{7}{13}\right) = 32.5789\ldots^\circ \]
Therefore \ans{32.58^\circ}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
the inverse tangent belongs to the opposite-with-adjacent pairing, and the second
known side here is the hypotenuse, not the adjacent side. Award full credit only
when the pairing is named, not just the corrected number.

\problem{Adjacent side $11$ m, hypotenuse $19$ m; the student divided and
reported $m\angle A = 0.58^\circ$. (a) Correct angle. (b) Name the error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The division gives the cosine of the angle, so
the inverse cosine must still be applied:
\[ \cos A = \frac{11}{19} = 0.5789\ldots \qquad
   A = \cos^{-1}(0.5789\ldots) = 54.6234\ldots^\circ \]
Therefore \ans{54.62^\circ}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
the quotient is $\cos A$, not $A$, and the inverse cosine is what undoes it.
Award full credit only when the response also gives a sanity check: two sides
this close in length cannot subtend an angle of a fraction of one degree.

\problem{A $24$ m cable at $38^\circ$ to the ground; the student entered
$24\sin 38$ and got $7.11$ m. (a) Correct height. (b) Name the error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The height is opposite the $38^\circ$ angle and
the cable is the hypotenuse, so sine is the right ratio in degree mode:
\[ \text{height} = 24\sin 38^\circ = 14.7758\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{14.78 \text{ m}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
the calculator was in radian mode, so $38$ was treated as $38$ radians. Award
full credit only when a rough estimate is supplied: $38^\circ$ is a little over a
third of a right angle, so the rise should be a little under two thirds of the
$24$ m cable --- around $15$ m, not half that.

\problem{$m\angle A = 29^\circ$ with adjacent side $26$ ft; the student computed
$26\tan 29^\circ = 14.41$ ft for the hypotenuse. (a) Correct hypotenuse.
(b) Name the error.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} Tangent relates the two legs, so it cannot
produce the hypotenuse. With the adjacent side known, cosine is the ratio, and
the unknown sits underneath:
\[ \cos 29^\circ = \frac{26}{c} \qquad c = \frac{26}{\cos 29^\circ} = 29.7272\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{29.73 \text{ ft}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
tangent is the ratio of the two legs and can never return the hypotenuse, and
that with the adjacent side and the angle known the hypotenuse comes from cosine
with the unknown in the denominator. Award full credit only when the response
also names the size check: the hypotenuse is the longest side of a right
triangle, so an answer shorter than the $26$ ft leg is impossible.
