\problem{$3$ adult and $2$ child tickets cost $61$ dollars; $1$ adult and $4$ child cost $47$ dollars. Find the two prices.}

\textbf{Solution:} $3x + 2y = 61$ and $x + 4y = 47$. The second equation is already solved
for $x$ in one step, so substitute.
\begin{align*}
  x &= 47 - 4y \\
  3(47 - 4y) + 2y &= 61 \\
  141 - 12y + 2y &= 61 \\
  -10y &= -80
\end{align*}
So $y = 8$ and $x = 47 - 32$. Adult \ans{x = 15} dollars, child \ans{y = 8} dollars.

\problem{Four crates and three boxes weigh $47$ kg; two crates weigh $9$ kg less than five boxes. Find each mass.}

\textbf{Solution:} $4x + 3y = 47$ and $2x - 5y = -9$. Neither variable is isolated, so
eliminate: double the second equation.
\begin{align*}
  4x - 10y &= -18 \\
  (4x + 3y) - (4x - 10y) &= 47 - (-18) \\
  13y &= 65
\end{align*}
So $y = 5$, and $4x + 15 = 47$ gives $x = 8$. Crate \ans{x = 8} kg, box \ans{y = 5} kg.

\problem{$20$ kg of blend from coffees at $12$ and $18$ dollars per kg, costing $288$ dollars. Matrix equation and solution.}

\textbf{Solution:} The two conditions are $x + y = 20$ (mass) and $12x + 18y = 288$ (cost):
\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 12 & 18 \end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 20 \\ 288 \end{bmatrix} \]
Substituting $y = 20 - x$ into the cost row:
\begin{align*}
  12x + 18(20 - x) &= 288 \\
  12x + 360 - 18x &= 288 \\
  -6x &= -72
\end{align*}
So \ans{x = 12} kg of the cheaper coffee and \ans{y = 8} kg of the dearer one.

\problem{Where does $y = -x^2 + 8x + 9$ meet $y = 3x + 15$?}

\textbf{Solution:} Both expressions equal $y$, so set them equal --- one linear and one
quadratic equation always reduce to a single quadratic.
\begin{align*}
  -x^2 + 8x + 9 &= 3x + 15 \\
  0 &= x^2 - 5x + 6 \\
  0 &= (x - 2)(x - 3)
\end{align*}
At $x = 2$: $y = 3(2) + 15 = 21$. At $x = 3$: $y = 3(3) + 15 = 24$.
The jet meets the wire at \ans{x = 2,\ y = 21} and \ans{x = 3,\ y = 24} (metres).

\problem{$16$ items; cutting $2/3/1$ hours totalling $34$; finishing $3/1/2$ hours totalling $29$. Find the counts.}

\textbf{Solution:} The three conditions are
\[ x + y + z = 16, \qquad 2x + 3y + z = 34, \qquad 3x + y + 2z = 29 \]
Subtracting the first from the second removes $z$ at once:
\begin{align*}
  (2x + 3y + z) - (x + y + z) &= 34 - 16 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x + 2y = 18 \\
  (3x + y + 2z) - 2(x + y + z) &= 29 - 32 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x - y = -3
\end{align*}
From the second, $x = y - 3$; substituting, $3y = 21$, so $y = 7$, $x = 4$ and
$z = 16 - 11$. Chairs \ans{x = 4}, tables \ans{y = 7}, stools \ans{z = 5}.

\problem{$24$ litres of juice at $3$ and $5$ dollars per litre costing $88$ dollars. (a) Matrix equation and solution. (b) What each row records.}

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 24 \\ 88 \end{bmatrix} \]
Substituting $y = 24 - x$ into the second row:
\begin{align*}
  3x + 5(24 - x) &= 88 \\
  -2x &= -32
\end{align*}
So \ans{x = 16} litres of orange and \ans{y = 8} litres of mango.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Row $1$ records the volume condition: each
litre of either juice counts once toward the $24$ litres, which is why both entries are $1$.
Row $2$ records the cost condition, and its entries are the prices per litre. Full credit
ties each row to one of the two stated conditions and says what the entries inside a row
measure. Describing the columns instead, or restating the two equations, does not answer it.

\problem{Where does $y = x + 1$ cross $x^2 + y^2 = 25$?}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute the line into the circle --- the line is already solved for
$y$, so substitution beats elimination here.
\begin{align*}
  x^2 + (x + 1)^2 &= 25 \\
  2x^2 + 2x + 1 &= 25 \\
  x^2 + x - 12 &= 0 \\
  (x - 3)(x + 4) &= 0
\end{align*}
At $x = 3$, $y = 4$; at $x = -4$, $y = -3$. Both points are $5$ metres from the centre, as
the circle demands. The crossings are \ans{x = 3,\ y = 4} and \ans{x = -4,\ y = -3}.

\problem{Plan A: $40$ dollars $+\ 6$ per hour. Plan B: $25$ dollars $+\ 9$ per hour. (a) Break-even. (b) Which is cheaper at $10$ hours.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $y = 6x + 40$ and $y = 9x + 25$. Both are solved for $y$, so set the
right-hand sides equal.
\begin{align*}
  6x + 40 &= 9x + 25 \\
  15 &= 3x
\end{align*}
So $x = 5$ hours, and $y = 6(5) + 40$. The plans agree at \ans{x = 5} hours and
\ans{y = 70} dollars.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} At $10$ hours Plan A costs
$6(10) + 40 = 100$ dollars and Plan B costs $9(10) + 25 = 115$ dollars, so Plan A is cheaper
by $15$ dollars. The reason: past the break-even point at $5$ hours, the plan with the
smaller hourly rate wins, because the extra $15$ dollars of fixed charge has by then been
repaid by the $3$ dollars per hour saved. Full credit needs both costs (or their difference)
and the rate-versus-fixed-charge reasoning.

\problem{$20$ drinks at $3$, $4$, $5$ dollars raising $79$ dollars, with $3$ more mediums than smalls. Find the counts.}

\textbf{Solution:} The conditions are $x + y + z = 20$, $3x + 4y + 5z = 79$ and $y = x + 3$.
Substituting the third into the other two:
\begin{align*}
  x + (x + 3) + z &= 20 &&\Longrightarrow\quad 2x + z = 17 \\
  3x + 4(x + 3) + 5z &= 79 &&\Longrightarrow\quad 7x + 5z = 67
\end{align*}
From the first, $z = 17 - 2x$; substituting, $7x + 85 - 10x = 67$, so $-3x = -18$.
Smalls \ans{x = 6}, mediums \ans{y = 9}, larges \ans{z = 5}.

\problem{A rectangle of perimeter $34$ m and area $60$ square metres. (a) All pairs. (b) Why they describe one garden.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The conditions are $x + y = 17$ and $xy = 60$. Substituting
$y = 17 - x$:
\begin{align*}
  x(17 - x) &= 60 \\
  x^2 - 17x + 60 &= 0 \\
  (x - 12)(x - 5) &= 0
\end{align*}
The pairs are \ans{x = 12,\ y = 5} and \ans{x = 5,\ y = 12} (metres).

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Swapping $x$ and $y$ changes neither the sum
nor the product, so the two conditions cannot tell the two side lengths apart: a $12$ by $5$
rectangle and a $5$ by $12$ rectangle are the same garden seen from a different side. Full
credit names that symmetry. ``They are the same numbers'' without saying why the equations
cannot distinguish them is only half the answer.

\problem{$100$ kg of three grains; protein $2/1/3$ per kg totalling $220$; fibre $3/2/1$ per kg totalling $170$. Solve.}

\textbf{Solution:} The blend is
\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}
   = \begin{bmatrix} 100 \\ 220 \\ 170 \end{bmatrix} \]
Eliminate with the mass row:
\begin{align*}
  (2x + y + 3z) - (x + y + z) &= 220 - 100 &&\Longrightarrow\quad x + 2z = 120 \\
  (3x + 2y + z) - 2(x + y + z) &= 170 - 200 &&\Longrightarrow\quad x - z = -30
\end{align*}
Subtracting gives $3z = 150$, so $z = 50$, $x = 20$, and $y = 100 - 70$.
\ans{x = 20} kg, \ans{y = 30} kg, \ans{z = 50} kg.

\problem{Three-sided fence of $120$ m enclosing $1600$ square metres. (a) All pairs. (b) Wider frontage, and are both usable?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The conditions are $x + 2y = 120$ and $xy = 1600$. Substituting
$x = 120 - 2y$:
\begin{align*}
  (120 - 2y)y &= 1600 \\
  2y^2 - 120y + 1600 &= 0 \\
  y^2 - 60y + 800 &= 0 \\
  (y - 40)(y - 20) &= 0
\end{align*}
At $y = 40$, $x = 40$; at $y = 20$, $x = 80$. The designs are \ans{x = 40,\ y = 40} and
\ans{x = 80,\ y = 20} (metres).

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The second design has the wider river
frontage, $80$ metres against $40$ metres. Both are genuinely usable: $80 + 2(20) = 120$
metres of fence and $80 \times 20 = 1600$ square metres, and likewise for the square design,
so neither root is extraneous. Full credit compares the two frontages \emph{and} checks both
pairs against both conditions --- this is the problem where a student is most tempted to
discard a perfectly good second solution.
