\problem{\textbf{Fifths bar:} $\tfrac{1}{5} + \tfrac{3}{5}$.

\textbf{Solution:} One fifth is 1 shaded part; three fifths is 3 more parts of the
same size. On the bar that is $1 + 3 = 4$ shaded parts out of 5, so only the
count of parts changes --- the size of a part is still one fifth.
\[ \frac{1}{5} + \frac{3}{5} = \frac{1+3}{5} = \ans{\frac{4}{5}} \]
Already in simplest form (4 and 5 share no factor but 1).}

\problem{\textbf{Eighths bar:} $\tfrac{2}{8} + \tfrac{5}{8}$.

\textbf{Solution:} Count eighths: 2 shaded, then 5 more shaded, giving $2+5 = 7$
eighths. The denominator stays 8 because the bar is still cut into 8 equal parts.
\[ \frac{2}{8} + \frac{5}{8} = \frac{2+5}{8} = \ans{\frac{7}{8}} \]
The bar is not quite full: 7 of the 8 parts are shaded.}

\problem{\textbf{Ninths bar:} $\tfrac{7}{9} - \tfrac{4}{9}$.

\textbf{Solution:} Start with 7 shaded ninths and cross out 4 of them.
$7 - 4 = 3$ ninths are still shaded.
\[ \frac{7}{9} - \frac{4}{9} = \frac{7-4}{9} = \frac{3}{9} \]
Now simplify: 3 and 9 are both divisible by 3, and $3 \div 3 = 1$, $9 \div 3 = 3$.
\[ \ans{\frac{1}{3}} \]
On the bar, the 3 shaded ninths line up exactly with one third of the bar, which
is why the two names describe the same amount.}

\problem{\textbf{Sixths bar:} $\tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{1}{6}$.

\textbf{Solution:} $5 - 1 = 4$ sixths remain shaded.
\[ \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} \]
Both 4 and 6 divide by 2, so $\tfrac{4}{6} = \ans{\frac{2}{3}}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Two eighths bars:} $\tfrac{5}{8} + \tfrac{4}{8}$.

\textbf{Solution:} Counting eighths across both bars: $5 + 4 = 9$ eighths.
\[ \frac{5}{8} + \frac{4}{8} = \ans{\frac{9}{8}} \]
Nine eighths is more than one whole. Eight of the nine eighths fill one whole
bar and one eighth is left over, so the same amount is 1 whole and 1 eighth.

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} an answer of $\tfrac{9}{16}$ --- that comes from adding
the denominators, which would mean the pieces got smaller when nothing was cut.}

\problem{\textbf{Missing numerator:} $\tfrac{2}{9} + \underline{\ \ } = \tfrac{7}{9}$.

\textbf{Solution:} The pieces are all ninths, so the question is really ``2 ninths
plus how many ninths makes 7 ninths?'' Count up on the bar from 2 to 7: that is
$7 - 2 = 5$ more ninths.
\[ \frac{2}{9} + \frac{5}{9} = \frac{7}{9} \quad \checkmark \qquad \ans{\frac{5}{9}} \]}

\problem{\textbf{Chocolate bar story:} $\tfrac{11}{12} - \tfrac{5}{12}$.

\textbf{Solution:} ``Eats'' means take away, so subtract. The bar is cut into 12
equal pieces, so both fractions count twelfths and the denominator does not change.
\[ \frac{11}{12} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{11-5}{12} = \frac{6}{12} \]
Simplify by dividing both parts by 6: $\ans{\frac{1}{2}}$ of the bar is left.

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} $\tfrac{16}{12}$ --- that is what adding gives, and an
answer larger than what Lena started with cannot be what is left.}

\problem{\textbf{Three shaders on one sixths bar:} $\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6}$.

\textbf{Solution:} All three friends shade sixths of the same bar, so add the
numerators only: $1 + 2 + 2 = 5$.
\[ \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \ans{\frac{5}{6}} \]
The bar is \emph{not} full: 5 of its 6 parts are shaded, so one sixth is still blank.}

\problem{\textbf{Work backwards:} $\underline{\ \ } - \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{4}{8}$.

\textbf{Solution:} The amount before pouring is the amount poured out plus the
amount still there --- undo the subtraction by adding.
\[ \frac{3}{8} + \frac{4}{8} = \frac{7}{8} \]
Check by subtracting: $\tfrac{7}{8} - \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{4}{8}$ $\checkmark$.
The jug held $\ans{\frac{7}{8}}$ of a jug before the pouring.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} Jae wrote $\tfrac{3}{7} + \tfrac{2}{7} = \tfrac{5}{14}$.

\textbf{Solution:} Jae added the denominators as well as the numerators. That
cannot be right: $\tfrac{5}{14}$ is smaller than $\tfrac{3}{7}$, and adding a
positive amount can never make the total smaller. On a sevenths bar, shading 3
parts and then 2 more leaves 5 shaded parts, and the parts are still sevenths ---
nothing re-cut the bar into fourteenths.
\[ \frac{3}{7} + \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3+2}{7} = \ans{\frac{5}{7}} \]}
