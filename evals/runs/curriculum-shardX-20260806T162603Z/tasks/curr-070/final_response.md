# Multiplication Three Ways (Grades 2–3)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_multmoves_curr070.pdf`) — 12 problems that move among repeated addition, arrays, and multiplication facts (the subtitle on the sheet says exactly that). A short warm-up drills rewriting sums like 4 + 4 + 4 as a product; after that the three forms are interleaved so your child must decide each time which translation is being asked for. Problem 11 is a missing-factor twist (8 × n = 40) and problem 12 is the synthesis challenge: one chair-array story answered all three ways.
- **Answer key** (`ak_multmoves_curr070.pdf`) — full reasoning for all 12 problems (including the acceptable alternate forms, e.g. 7+7+7+7 or 4 added seven times), a quick-answers bank with a per-slot label for every blank, a Curriculum section (3.OA.A.1/3/4), and common-wrong-answer notes for the two declared traps (adding the two numbers instead of multiplying).
- **Study guide** (`ss_multmoves_curr070.pdf`) — 2 pages, one section per direction of translation (addition to product, array to product, product back to addition), each with a rule box, worked example, and try-it.

**What is verified:** 17 of the 24 graded responses are machine-checked with SymPy — every numeric blank (group counts, totals, the missing factor) on all 12 problems. The 7 responses where your child writes a *form* (a multiplication fact or a repeated-addition sentence) are flagged for instructor judgment, because more than one correct form exists; the key lists the acceptable forms for each and the quick-answers bank marks these slots with `---`. Both declared misconception traps were machine-proven distinguishable from the correct answers.
