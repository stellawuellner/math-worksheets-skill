\problem{Exactly one pair of parallel sides; angles $(3x)^\circ$, $(3x)^\circ$,
$(2x)^\circ$, $(2x)^\circ$. (a) Find $x$. (b) Most specific name, justified.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The four interior angles of a quadrilateral add
to $360^\circ$:
\[ 3x + 3x + 2x + 2x = 360 \qquad 10x = 360 \]
so \ans{x = 36}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The angles are $108^\circ$,
$108^\circ$, $72^\circ$ and $72^\circ$. A complete response names the figure an
\emph{isosceles trapezoid}: exactly one pair of parallel sides makes it a
trapezoid, and the two pairs of equal base angles make that trapezoid isosceles.
Naming trapezoid alone, or naming isosceles trapezoid without citing the angle
pairs, earns partial credit.

\problem{Diagonals of $JKLM$ cut each other into equal parts, one diagonal split
into $(2x+1)$ cm and $13$ cm. (a) Find $x$. (b) Most specific name, justified.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The two parts of that diagonal are equal:
$2x + 1 = 13$, so $2x = 12$ and \ans{x = 6}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response names
the figure a \emph{parallelogram}: a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each
other is a parallelogram, which is exactly what equal parts on either side of the
crossing point mean. It must also note that nothing given decides whether the
figure is a rectangle, rhombus or square, so parallelogram is the most specific
name available.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Four congruent sides, one written $(3x-4)$ cm and one $20$ cm;
diagonals congruent. (a) Find $x$. (b) Most specific name, justified.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} All four sides are congruent, so
$3x - 4 = 20$, giving $3x = 24$ and \ans{x = 8}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response names
the figure a \emph{square}, built from both properties: four congruent sides make
it a rhombus, and congruent diagonals make a parallelogram a rectangle --- a
figure that is both a rhombus and a rectangle is a square. Naming square without
connecting each property to the step it supplies earns partial credit.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$ABCD$ with both pairs of opposite angles congruent, measuring
$(2x+10)^\circ$, $(3x-20)^\circ$, $(2x+10)^\circ$, $(3x-20)^\circ$. (a) Find $x$.
(b) The larger pair of angles. (c) Classify, and give an extra condition making
it a rectangle.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The four angles add to $360^\circ$:
\[ 2(2x+10) + 2(3x-20) = 360 \qquad 10x - 20 = 360 \]
so $10x = 380$ and \ans{x = 38}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b).} $2x + 10 = 86$ and $3x - 20 = 94$, so the larger
pair measures \ans{94^\circ}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response names
$ABCD$ a \emph{parallelogram}, since both pairs of opposite angles being
congruent is one of the standard parallelogram tests, and then supplies a
workable extra condition for a rectangle: one angle is a right angle, or all four
angles are equal, or the diagonals are congruent. Both parts are needed for full
credit.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Always, sometimes or never: (a) a rhombus is a square; (b) a square is
a rhombus; (c) a trapezoid is a parallelogram.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} \emph{Sometimes.} A
complete response says a rhombus has four congruent sides but its angles need not
be right angles; a slanted rhombus is not a square, while a rhombus with right
angles is one, so both outcomes occur.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \emph{Always.} A complete
response says a square has four congruent sides, which is the defining property
of a rhombus, so every square qualifies; the extra right angles do not disqualify
it.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} \emph{Never}, under the
definition stated in the directions. A complete response says a parallelogram
needs both pairs of opposite sides parallel while a trapezoid is defined here as
having exactly one, so the two conditions cannot hold at once. A response that
argues correctly from the inclusive definition of trapezoid may be accepted
provided it states which definition it is using.

\problem{$EFGH$: diagonals congruent and bisecting each other, but not
perpendicular; one diagonal $(4x+5)$ cm and the other $33$ cm. (a) Most specific
name. (b) Find $x$. (c) Why can it not be a square?}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response names
$EFGH$ a \emph{rectangle}: diagonals that bisect each other make it a
parallelogram, congruent diagonals make that parallelogram a rectangle, and since
the diagonals are stated not to be perpendicular it is not a square --- so
rectangle is the most specific name.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b).} The two diagonals are congruent, so
$4x + 5 = 33$, giving $4x = 28$ and \ans{x = 7}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says a
square's diagonals are perpendicular, and these are stated not to be, so the
figure fails a property every square has. Citing instead that a square's
diagonals bisect its angles, or that all four sides would have to be congruent,
is acceptable provided the response ties the failure to a property squares must
have.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$ABCD$ with $AB = AD = (3x-1)$ cm, $CB = CD = 14$ cm and $AB = 17$ cm.
(a) Find $x$. (b) Most specific name, and why it is not a rhombus.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} $3x - 1 = 17$, so $3x = 18$ and \ans{x = 6}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response names
$ABCD$ a \emph{kite}: its congruent sides are adjacent in two pairs (the pair at
$A$ and the pair at $C$) rather than opposite, and the two pairs have different
lengths, $17$ cm and $14$ cm. A rhombus would require all four sides congruent,
which fails here; that contrast must appear for full credit.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A student claims two pairs of congruent sides force a parallelogram.
(a) Counterexample. (b) $(5x-8)$ cm congruent to $27$ cm: find $x$. (c) $19$ cm
congruent to $(2y+3)$ cm: find $y$.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response gives a
\emph{kite} and describes the arrangement: its two pairs of congruent sides are
adjacent, sharing a vertex, rather than opposite each other, so no pair of
opposite sides need be parallel or congruent. Any correct counterexample with an
accurate description of how its congruent sides sit earns full credit; naming a
shape alone does not.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b).} $5x - 8 = 27$, so $5x = 35$ and \ans{x = 7}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c).} $2y + 3 = 19$, so $2y = 16$ and \ans{y = 8}
