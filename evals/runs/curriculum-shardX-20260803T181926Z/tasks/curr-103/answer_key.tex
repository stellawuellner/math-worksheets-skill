\problem{\textbf{Round $3847$ to the nearest thousand.}
\par
The thousands below and above are $\ans{3000}$ and $4000$, and halfway between
them is $\ans{3500}$.
\par
$3847$ is past $3500$, so it is nearer the upper benchmark.
\par
$\ans{4000}$}

\problem{\textbf{Round $2614$ to the nearest hundred.}
\par
The hundreds around $2614$ are $2600$ and $\ans{2700}$, and halfway is
$\ans{2650}$.
\par
$2614$ has not reached $2650$, so it stays with the lower hundred.
\par
$\ans{2600}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} the tens digit $1$ is what decides it. Looking at the ones
digit $4$, or at the whole ``$14$'', is the usual slip.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $48362$ to the nearest thousand.}
\par
The thousands around it are $48000$ and $\ans{49000}$; halfway is
$\ans{48500}$.
\par
$48362$ comes before $48500$, so it rounds down. The digits to the right of the
thousands place all become zeros, and the $48$ thousands are untouched.
\par
$\ans{48000}$}

\problem{\textbf{Round $7952$ to the nearest hundred.}
\par
The hundreds around it are $\ans{7900}$ and $8000$; halfway is $\ans{7950}$.
\par
$7952$ is $2$ past $7950$, so it rounds up --- and rounding up here carries
into the thousands place, turning $79$ hundreds into $80$ hundreds.
\par
$\ans{8000}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Answer to ``how do you know'':} the tens digit is $5$ and the ones digit
is $2$, so the number sits just after the halfway mark.}

\problem{\textbf{Stadium crowd of $26481$, to the nearest thousand.}
\par
The thousands around it are $26000$ and $\ans{27000}$; halfway is
$\ans{26500}$.
\par
$26481$ is $19$ short of $26500$, so it has not reached halfway and rounds
down.
\par
$\ans{26000}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $30000$ means the crowd was rounded to the nearest ten
thousand instead. Read which place the question names before rounding.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $4182 + 2759$ to the nearest hundred.}
\par
For $4182$: the hundreds are $\ans{4100}$ and $4200$, halfway is $\ans{4150}$,
and $4182$ is past it, so
$\ans{4200}$.
\par
For $2759$: halfway between $2700$ and $2800$ is $2750$, and $2759$ is past it,
so $\ans{2800}$.
\par
Now add the rounded numbers: $4200 + 2800$.
\par
$\ans{7000}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} the exact sum is $6941$, so the estimate is close and easy to get
mentally --- which is the point of estimating.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $5499$ to the nearest hundred, then to the nearest
thousand.}
\par
\textbf{Nearest hundred:} the hundreds are $5400$ and $5500$, halfway is
$5450$, and $5499$ is past it.
\par
$\ans{5500}$
\par
\textbf{Nearest thousand:} the thousands are $5000$ and $\ans{6000}$, halfway
is $5500$, and $5499$ is just \emph{before} it.
\par
$\ans{5000}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Answer to ``why opposite directions'':} the two questions use different
halfway marks. Against $5450$ the number is above; against $5500$ it is below,
by one. Rounding a number that has already been rounded ($5500 \to 6000$) is
exactly the mistake this problem is built to catch.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $8206 - 3891$ to the nearest thousand.}
\par
For $8206$: the thousands are $\ans{8000}$ and $\ans{9000}$, halfway is
$\ans{8500}$, and $8206$ is before it, so it rounds to $8000$.
\par
For $3891$: halfway between $3000$ and $4000$ is $3500$, and $3891$ is past it,
so it rounds to $4000$.
\par
Estimate: $8000 - 4000$.
\par
$\ans{4000}$
\par
Exact: $8206 - 3891$.
\par
$\ans{4315}$
\par
$\ans{4000 < 4315}$, so the estimate is smaller than the exact difference.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Why:} the first number was rounded down and the second was rounded up, and
both of those changes make a difference smaller.}

\problem{\textbf{River trail $12438$ m, ridge trail $12509$ m.}
\par
Halfway between $12000$ and $13000$ is $\ans{12500}$.
\par
River: $12438$ is before halfway, so $\ans{12000}$.
\par
Ridge: $12509$ is past halfway, so $\ans{13000}$.
\par
The exact lengths compare as
$\ans{12438 < 12509}$, so the ridge trail is longer --- and here the rounded
numbers agree with that.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Worth saying to the student:} they agree this time, but two numbers only
$71$ apart landed on rounded values $1000$ apart. Rounded numbers are for
reporting, not for deciding which of two close measurements is larger.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: which whole numbers round to $4600$?}
\par
Rounding to the nearest hundred means choosing between $4500$ and $4600$ below,
and between $4600$ and $4700$ above.
\par
\textbf{Least:} halfway between $4500$ and $4600$ is $4550$, and a number
exactly on halfway rounds up, so $4550$ is the smallest that reaches $4600$:
$\ans{4600}$ is what it rounds to.
\par
\textbf{Greatest:} halfway between $4600$ and $4700$ is $4650$, which rounds up
to $4700$. So the largest whole number that still rounds down to $4600$ is one
less, $4649$, and it rounds to $\ans{4600}$.
\par
In order: $\ans{4550 < 4649}$
\par\smallskip
So every whole number from $4550$ to $4649$ rounds to $4600$ --- one hundred
numbers in all.}
