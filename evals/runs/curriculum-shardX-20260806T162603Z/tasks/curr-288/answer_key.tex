\problem{A square sheet of copper has area $x^2$ square centimetres. A square hole of area $36$ square centimetres is punched out of its middle, leaving $x^2 - 36$ square centimetres of metal. Write the remaining area as a product of two binomials.}

\textbf{Solution:} Two terms, both perfect squares, separated by a minus sign --- this is $a^2 - b^2$ with $a = x$ and $b = 6$, because $6^2 = 36$.
\[ x^2 - 36 = x^2 - 6^2 = (x-6)(x+6) \]
The two factors are the side lengths, in centimetres, of the rectangle you get by rearranging the leftover metal. \ans{(x-6)(x+6)}

\problem{A square patio is tiled so that its area is $x^2 + 10x + 25$ square metres. Factor the area completely so that the side length of the patio, in metres, can be read from the answer.}

\textbf{Solution:} Three terms. The first and last are perfect squares: $x^2 = (x)^2$ and $25 = 5^2$. Check the middle term against $2ab$: $2 \cdot x \cdot 5 = 10x$, which matches, so the pattern is $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a+b)^2$.
\[ x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x+5)^2 \]
The patio is $(x+5)$ metres on a side. \ans{(x+5)^2}

\problem{A square banner measures $3x$ centimetres on each side. A square logo measuring $7$ centimetres on each side is cut out of it, leaving $9x^2 - 49$ square centimetres of fabric. Write the remaining area as a product of two binomials.}

\textbf{Solution:} Write each term as a square first: $9x^2 = (3x)^2$ and $49 = 7^2$. Now the difference-of-squares pattern applies with $a = 3x$, $b = 7$.
\[ 9x^2 - 49 = (3x)^2 - 7^2 = (3x-7)(3x+7) \]
\ans{(3x-7)(3x+7)}

\problem{The floor of a square storage shed has area $4x^2 + 12x + 9$ square feet. Factor the area completely so that the side length of the shed, in feet, can be read from the answer.}

\textbf{Solution:} $4x^2 = (2x)^2$ and $9 = 3^2$, so try $a = 2x$, $b = 3$. The middle-term test decides it: $2ab = 2(2x)(3) = 12x$, which is exactly the middle term.
\[ 4x^2 + 12x + 9 = (2x+3)^2 \]
The shed floor is $(2x+3)$ feet on a side. \ans{(2x+3)^2}

\problem{A rectangular deck has area $2x^2 - 50$ square feet. Factor the expression completely. Take out the common factor first.}

\textbf{Solution:} Both terms share a factor of $2$. Pull it out before looking for a pattern --- otherwise neither term is a perfect square and the difference-of-squares pattern is hidden.
\begin{align*}
  2x^2 - 50 &= 2(x^2 - 25) \\
            &= 2(x-5)(x+5)
\end{align*}
\ans{2(x-5)(x+5)}

\problem{A rectangular window pane measures $(x+7)$ inches along one edge and $(x-7)$ inches along the other. Write its area, in square inches, in standard form with no parentheses.}

\textbf{Solution:} Area is length times width. Multiply the binomials; the two middle products cancel, which is exactly why the answer is a difference of squares.
\begin{align*}
  (x+7)(x-7) &= x^2 - 7x + 7x - 49 \\
             &= x^2 - 49
\end{align*}
\ans{x^2 - 49}

\problem{Two square vegetable plots lie side by side. The larger plot has side $5x$ metres and the smaller plot has side $4y$ metres, so the difference of their areas is $25x^2 - 16y^2$ square metres. Write that difference as a product of two binomials.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each term is a perfect square in its own variable: $25x^2 = (5x)^2$ and $16y^2 = (4y)^2$. The pattern does not care that two different letters appear.
\[ 25x^2 - 16y^2 = (5x)^2 - (4y)^2 = (5x-4y)(5x+4y) \]
\ans{(5x-4y)(5x+4y)}

\problem{A square rug and its carpet border together cover $3x^2 - 30x + 75$ square feet. Factor the expression completely: take out the greatest common factor first, then look at what is left.}

\textbf{Solution:} The greatest common factor of $3x^2$, $30x$ and $75$ is $3$. After it comes out, the leftover trinomial is a perfect square: $x^2 = (x)^2$, $25 = 5^2$, and $2 \cdot x \cdot 5 = 10x$ matches the middle term.
\begin{align*}
  3x^2 - 30x + 75 &= 3(x^2 - 10x + 25) \\
                  &= 3(x-5)^2
\end{align*}
\ans{3(x-5)^2}

\problem{The volume of a shipping crate, in cubic inches, is modelled by $x^3 - 16x$, where $x$ is a length in inches. Factor the expression completely so that the three dimensions of the crate can be read from the answer.}

\textbf{Solution:} The common factor is $x$. Take it out first; what remains is a difference of squares, so the factoring is not finished until that is broken up too.
\begin{align*}
  x^3 - 16x &= x(x^2 - 16) \\
            &= x(x-4)(x+4)
\end{align*}
The three dimensions, in inches, are $x$, $(x-4)$ and $(x+4)$. \ans{x(x-4)(x+4)}

\problem{A square window pane has area $16x^2 - 24x + 9$ square inches. (a) Factor the area completely. (b) Explain how the numbers $16$, $24$ and $9$ tell you this expression is a perfect-square trinomial rather than a difference of squares.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $16x^2 = (4x)^2$ and $9 = 3^2$, so the candidate pattern is $a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ with $a = 4x$, $b = 3$. Test the middle term: $2ab = 2(4x)(3) = 24x$, and the sign is negative, so the factored form uses a minus. \ans{(4x-3)^2}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} A full answer must make three points: the expression has \emph{three} terms, while a difference of squares has only two; the first and last terms are the perfect squares of $4x$ and $3$; and the middle term $-24x$ is twice the product of $4x$ and $3$, which is the signature of $a^2 - 2ab + b^2$. An answer that stops at ``both ends are squares'' has not checked the middle term and should not get full credit --- $16x^2 - 20x + 9$ has square ends and does not factor this way.
