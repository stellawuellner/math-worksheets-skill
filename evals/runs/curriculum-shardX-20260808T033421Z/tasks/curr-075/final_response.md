# Division Three Ways (Grades 2–3)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_divmatch_curr075.pdf`) — 12 problems that move among division stories, drawings of equal groups, and equations (the subtitle on the sheet says exactly that). After a short story warm-up, the three representations are interleaved so your child must decide each time what they are translating from: stories to equations, dot drawings to equations, and equations back to invented stories. Problem 12 is the synthesis challenge — a berry story and a drawing shown together, with three candidate equations to choose among (including the backwards 4 ÷ 24 and the subtraction 24 − 4).
- **Answer key** (`ak_divmatch_curr075.pdf`) — full reasoning for every problem, including why the wrong challenge equations fail, example stories for the equation-to-story items, a labeled quick-answers bank, a Curriculum section (3.OA.A.2/3), and a common-wrong-answer note for the declared subtraction trap.
- **Study guide** (`ss_divmatch_curr075.pdf`) — 2 pages: story-to-equation, drawing-to-equation, and equation-to-story sections, each with rule box, worked example, and try-it, plus a watch-out on writing the total first.

**What is verified:** 12 of the 24 graded responses are machine-checked with SymPy — every quotient on all 12 problems. The other 12 responses are the written forms (division equations your child writes, and the invented stories), which admit multiple correct phrasings, so each is flagged for instructor judgment with a rubric stating what to accept; the quick-answers bank marks these slots `---`. The one declared trap (24 − 4 = 20 in the challenge) was machine-proven distinguishable from the correct 6.
