# Diagnosing sign and like-term errors — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_signerrors_curr254.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: distributing a minus
  sign across a whole bracket, combining only genuinely like terms, and the test-value check
  (substitute an easy number into both the original and your answer; equivalent expressions agree
  at every value, so one disagreement proves a mistake). Each has a rule box, a worked example
  that opens by naming the strategy, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_signerrors_curr254.pdf`, 4 pages) — 8 problems, difficulty 1 → 5. Four of them
  (3, 5, 6, 8) are find-and-fix items showing another student's work: name the error, check the
  original with a test value, then write the corrected expression. Problem 8 carries two separate
  errors in a single line. The other four are clean practice on the same two rules so the student
  has the correct pattern in hand before diagnosing someone else's.
- **Answer key** (`ak_signerrors_curr254.pdf`, 4 pages) — full reasoning, a Quick Answers bank, a
  Curriculum block (HSA-APR.A.1, difficulty 1–5), and a **Common wrong answers** table generated
  from the declared misconception traps: for each planted error it prints the number a student
  following that error would get (14, 72, −29, 12, 36) beside the misconception it diagnoses. If
  a student hands in one of those numbers, you know exactly which rule to reteach.

## What is verified, honestly

- **All five planted wrong answers are machine-checked traps.** SymPy recomputed each wrong
  method's value and confirmed the problem's own check rejects it — so every planted error is
  provably distinguishable from the correct answer, not just plausibly so.
- **4 of the 8 problems are fully machine-checked** (1, 2, 4, 7) — every simplification recomputed
  independently of the key.
- **The other 4 (3, 5, 6, 8) are part machine-checked, part instructor-judged.** In each, the
  test-value computation and the corrected expression are verified; the "name the error" part is
  prose, and no program can grade prose. Those are flagged for you and the key prints the rubric —
  what a full-credit diagnosis must say, and what earns half credit. The Quick Answers bank marks
  them `—` rather than pretending they were checked.
- No blanks are marked as working space; every printed blank is an answer.

The grade level prints on the answer key only.
