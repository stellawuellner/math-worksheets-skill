\problem{\textbf{Add $(3x^2 + 5x - 2) + (x^2 - 8x + 7)$.}
\par
Addition needs no sign change --- drop the parentheses and collect like terms
by degree.
\par
$x^2$ terms: $3x^2 + x^2 = 4x^2$. \quad
$x$ terms: $5x - 8x = -3x$. \quad
constants: $-2 + 7 = 5$.
\par
$\ans{4x^2-3x+5}$}

\problem{\textbf{Subtract $(7x^2 - 4x + 1) - (2x^2 + 6x - 9)$.}
\par
The minus sign applies to \emph{every} term of the second polynomial, so
rewrite it as an addition first:
\[ (7x^2 - 4x + 1) + (-2x^2 - 6x + 9) \]
Now collect: $7x^2 - 2x^2 = 5x^2$, \ $-4x - 6x = -10x$, \ $1 + 9 = 10$.
\par
$\ans{5x^2-10x+10}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} an answer of $5x^2 + 2x - 8$ means only the first term of
the second polynomial was negated. Insist on writing the rewritten sum line.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $-3x^2(4x^3 - 2x + 5)$.}
\par
Distribute the monomial across all three terms. Multiply coefficients, add
exponents on $x$, and carry the negative sign through every product.
\par
$-3x^2 \cdot 4x^3 = -12x^5$, \quad
$-3x^2 \cdot (-2x) = 6x^3$, \quad
$-3x^2 \cdot 5 = -15x^2$.
\par
$\ans{-12x^5+6x^3-15x^2}$}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(x + 6)(x - 4)$.}
\par
Every term of the first factor times every term of the second (FOIL):
\[ x \cdot x + x \cdot (-4) + 6 \cdot x + 6 \cdot (-4)
   = x^2 - 4x + 6x - 24 \]
The two middle terms are like terms: $-4x + 6x = 2x$.
\par
$\ans{x^2+2x-24}$}

\problem{\textbf{Add $(2x^3 - x + 4) + (5x^2 + x - 9)$.}
\par
Neither polynomial has all four degrees, so line the terms up by degree and
leave a gap where a degree is missing.
\par
$x^3$: only $2x^3$. \quad $x^2$: only $5x^2$. \quad
$x$: $-x + x = 0$, so the $x$ term vanishes. \quad
constants: $4 - 9 = -5$.
\par
$\ans{2x^3+5x^2-5}$}

\problem{\textbf{Subtract $(4x^3 + x^2 - 6) - (4x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x - 6)$.}
\par
Distribute the minus sign:
\[ 4x^3 + x^2 - 6 - 4x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x + 6 \]
The $x^3$ terms cancel because they are identical and one is being subtracted
from the other; the constants cancel for the same reason, since
$-6 - (-6) = 0$.
\par
What is left: $x^2 + 3x^2 = 4x^2$ and the lone $-2x$.
\par
$\ans{4x^2-2x}$}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(2x - 5)(3x + 4)$.}
\par
\[ 2x \cdot 3x + 2x \cdot 4 + (-5) \cdot 3x + (-5) \cdot 4
   = 6x^2 + 8x - 15x - 20 \]
Combine the middle terms: $8x - 15x = -7x$.
\par
$\ans{6x^2-7x-20}$}

\problem{\textbf{Add $(3x^2y - 5xy + 2y^2) + (x^2y + 5xy - 9y^2)$.}
\par
With two variables, like terms must match on \emph{both} letters and both
exponents. $x^2y$ and $xy$ are not like terms.
\par
$3x^2y + x^2y = 4x^2y$, \quad $-5xy + 5xy = 0$, \quad
$2y^2 - 9y^2 = -7y^2$.
\par
$\ans{4x^2y-7y^2}$}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $4xy^2(2x - 3y)$.}
\par
Distribute, multiplying coefficients and adding exponents on each variable
separately.
\par
$4xy^2 \cdot 2x = 8x^2y^2$ \quad (the $x$ exponents add: $1 + 1 = 2$).
\par
$4xy^2 \cdot (-3y) = -12xy^3$ \quad (the $y$ exponents add: $2 + 1 = 3$).
\par
$\ans{8x^2y^2-12xy^3}$}

\problem{\textbf{Why is $(x - 3)^2 \neq x^2 - 9$?}
\par
Squaring a binomial means multiplying it by itself, and the distributive
property produces \emph{four} products, not two. Squaring each term separately
throws away the two middle products.
\[ (x-3)^2 = (x-3)(x-3) = x^2 - 3x - 3x + 9 \]
\par
$\ans{x^2-6x+9}$
\par\smallskip
A one-line numeric disproof is worth showing: at $x = 5$ the correct expression
gives $(5-3)^2 = 4$, while $x^2 - 9$ gives $16$.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $x^2 - 9$ is the expansion of $(x-3)(x+3)$, a different
product. Students who confuse the two are pattern-matching on the numbers
instead of distributing.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(3x - 2)(x + 7)$ and evaluate at $x = 2$.}
\par
\[ 3x \cdot x + 3x \cdot 7 + (-2) \cdot x + (-2) \cdot 7
   = 3x^2 + 21x - 2x - 14 = 3x^2 + 19x - 14 \]
Substitute $x = 2$: $3(4) + 19(2) - 14 = 12 + 38 - 14$.
\par
$\ans{36}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} the classmate's $-2$ comes from multiplying only the first
terms and only the last terms --- $3x \cdot x - 2 \cdot 7$, which is
$3x^2 - 14$, and at $x = 2$ that is $12 - 14 = -2$. The outer and inner
products were skipped, which is exactly the $19x$ that went missing.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: simplify
$(x + 2)(x^2 - 3x + 5) - (x - 1)(x + 4)$.}
\par
First product --- distribute $x$ and then $2$ across the trinomial:
\[ (x+2)(x^2-3x+5) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 5x + 2x^2 - 6x + 10 = x^3 - x^2 - x + 10 \]
Second product:
\[ (x-1)(x+4) = x^2 + 4x - x - 4 = x^2 + 3x - 4 \]
Now subtract the second from the first, distributing the minus sign across all
three of its terms:
\[ x^3 - x^2 - x + 10 - x^2 - 3x + 4 \]
Collect by degree: $x^3$ stays; $-x^2 - x^2 = -2x^2$; $-x - 3x = -4x$;
$10 + 4 = 14$.
\par
$\ans{x^3-2x^2-4x+14}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} the most common slip here is subtracting only the $x^2$
term of the second product. Check the constant: $+14$, not $+6$.}
