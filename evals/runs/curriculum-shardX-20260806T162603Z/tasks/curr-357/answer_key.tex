\problem{Divide $\dfrac{x^{2} + 7x + 12}{x + 3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $x^{2} \div x = x$; $x(x + 3) = x^{2} + 3x$; subtracting
leaves $4x + 12$. Then $4x \div x = 4$; $4(x + 3) = 4x + 12$; subtracting leaves
$0$. \ans{x + 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{x^{2} + 5x + 9}{x + 2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} First step gives $x$ and leaves $3x + 9$; second step gives
$3$ and leaves $9 - 6 = 3$. Degree $0$ is below the divisor's degree $1$, so the
division stops there and $3$ is the remainder.
\ans{x + 3 + \dfrac{3}{x + 2}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{2x^{3} - 3x^{2} - 11x + 6}{x - 3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The three steps give $2x^{2}$, then $3x$, then $-2$:
\begin{align*}
  2x^{2}(x - 3) &= 2x^{3} - 6x^{2}, &&\text{leaving } 3x^{2} - 11x + 6 \\
  3x(x - 3)     &= 3x^{2} - 9x,     &&\text{leaving } -2x + 6 \\
  -2(x - 3)     &= -2x + 6,         &&\text{leaving } 0
\end{align*}
\ans{2x^2 + 3x - 2}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{x^{3} - 8}{x - 2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Write the dividend as $x^{3} + 0x^{2} + 0x - 8$ first --- the
placeholders are what keep the columns aligned. The steps give $x^{2}$, then
$2x$, then $4$, with nothing left over. This is the difference-of-cubes
factorisation seen from the other side. \ans{x^2 + 2x + 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{3x^{2} - 5x + 4}{x - 1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $3x^{2} \div x = 3x$; $3x(x - 1) = 3x^{2} - 3x$, leaving
$-2x + 4$. Then $-2(x - 1) = -2x + 2$, leaving $2$.
\ans{3x - 2 + \dfrac{2}{x - 1}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{x^{4} - 5x^{2} + 4}{x^{2} - 1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Write the dividend as $x^{4} + 0x^{3} - 5x^{2} + 0x + 4$.
Dividing leading terms, $x^{4} \div x^{2} = x^{2}$, and
$x^{2}(x^{2} - 1) = x^{4} - x^{2}$, leaving $-4x^{2} + 4$. Then
$-4(x^{2} - 1) = -4x^{2} + 4$, leaving $0$. \ans{x^2 - 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\dfrac{x^{3} + 2x^{2} - 5x + 1}{x - 2}$. (a) Quotient and remainder.
(b) Value of the dividend at $x = 2$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The steps give $x^{2}$, then $4x$, then $3$, and the last
subtraction leaves $7$.
\ans{x^2 + 4x + 3 + \dfrac{7}{x - 2}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $8 + 8 - 10 + 1$. \ans{7}

\noindent The two answers agree, and they always will: that is the remainder
theorem. Dividing by $x - a$ leaves the remainder $P(a)$.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{2x^{3} + x^{2} - 8x + 5}{x + 3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Dividing by $x + 3$ means $a = -3$. The steps give $2x^{2}$
(leaving $-5x^{2} - 8x + 5$), then $-5x$ (leaving $7x + 5$), then $7$, and the
last subtraction leaves $5 - 21 = -16$.
\ans{2x^2 - 5x + 7 - \dfrac{16}{x + 3}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{x^{3} + 4x^{2} - 3x + 2}{x^{2} + 1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The divisor has degree $2$, so the division stops as soon as
what remains has degree $1$ or less.
\begin{align*}
  x(x^{2} + 1) &= x^{3} + x,  &&\text{leaving } 4x^{2} - 4x + 2 \\
  4(x^{2} + 1) &= 4x^{2} + 4, &&\text{leaving } -4x - 2
\end{align*}
Degree $1$ is below degree $2$, so stop.
\ans{x + 4 - \dfrac{4x + 2}{x^2 + 1}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Divide $\dfrac{2x^{4} - 3x^{3} + x - 6}{x - 2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $x^{2}$ term is missing, so write
$2x^{4} - 3x^{3} + 0x^{2} + x - 6$. The four steps give $2x^{3}$, $x^{2}$,
$2x$, $5$, and the last subtraction leaves $-6 + 10 = 4$.
\ans{2x^3 + x^2 + 2x + 5 + \dfrac{4}{x - 2}}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\dfrac{x^{3} - 7x + 6}{x - 1}$. (a) Quotient. (b) Factor
$x^{3} - 7x + 6$ completely.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With the placeholder, the dividend is
$x^{3} + 0x^{2} - 7x + 6$. The steps give $x^{2}$, then $x$, then $-6$, and
nothing is left. \ans{x^2 + x - 6}

\textbf{Solution (b):} A zero remainder means $x - 1$ is a factor, so
$x^{3} - 7x + 6 = (x - 1)(x^{2} + x - 6)$, and the quadratic factors further
into $(x + 3)(x - 2)$. \ans{(x-1)(x+3)(x-2)}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\dfrac{x^{3} + kx^{2} - 4x + 12}{x - 2}$ leaves no remainder.
(a) Find $k$. (b) Explain the shortcut.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} By the remainder theorem the remainder is the dividend
evaluated at $x = 2$:
\[
  8 + 4k - 8 + 12 = 4k + 12.
\]
Setting that to $0$ gives $4k = -12$. \ans{k = -3}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit explanation states the
remainder theorem --- dividing by $x - 2$ leaves a remainder equal to the value
of the dividend at $2$ --- and then says that "no remainder" therefore means
"the value at $2$ is zero", which is a single linear equation in $k$ instead of
a full division. A student who writes the substitution and the resulting
equation has shown it. Describing the long division the theorem was meant to
replace earns nothing.
