\problem{Complete the pattern: $7 \times 10 = 70$; \ $7 \times 100$; \ $7 \times 1{,}000$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiplying by 10 shifts every digit one place left, so each
line gains one more zero than the line before. $7 \times 100 = 700$ \ans{700}

$7 \times 1{,}000 = 7{,}000$ \ans{7000}

\problem{$26 \times 10$}

\textbf{Solution:} Shift each digit one place left: 2 tens become 2 hundreds and
6 ones become 6 tens. $26 \times 10 = 260$. \ans{260}

\problem{$40 \times 1{,}000$}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply $4 \times 1{,}000 = 4{,}000$, then keep the factor
of ten from 40: shift once more. $40 \times 1{,}000 = 40{,}000$. \ans{40000}

\problem{$10^3 = 10 \times 10 \times 10$}

\textbf{Solution:} The exponent 3 counts the factors of ten: three shifts of the
digit 1, which writes a 1 followed by three zeros. \ans{1000}

\problem{$3{,}500 \div 10$}

\textbf{Solution:} Dividing by 10 shifts every digit one place right: 3
thousands become 3 hundreds, 5 hundreds become 5 tens. \ans{350}

\problem{$62{,}000 \div 100$}

\textbf{Solution:} Dividing by 100 shifts two places right — remove two zeros:
$62{,}000 \div 100 = 620$. \ans{620}

\problem{$45 \times 10^2$}

\textbf{Solution:} $10^2 = 100$, so shift the digits of 45 two places left:
$45 \times 100 = 4{,}500$. \ans{4500}

\problem{$8{,}000 \div 10^3$}

\textbf{Solution:} $10^3 = 1{,}000$, so shift three places right:
$8{,}000 \div 1{,}000 = 8$. \ans{8}

\problem{In $66{,}000$, the ten-thousands 6 is how many times the thousands 6?
Explain.}

\textbf{Solution:} The first 6 is worth $60{,}000$; the second is worth
$6{,}000$. $60{,}000 \div 6{,}000 = 10$. \ans{10}

Full credit for the explanation: each place is worth \emph{10 times} the place
to its right, and the ten-thousands place sits one place to the left of the
thousands place — so the same digit is worth 10 times as much there.

\problem{The two 5s in $52{,}600$ and $5{,}260$.}

\textbf{Solution:} In $52{,}600$ the 5 is in the ten-thousands place:
$5 \times 10{,}000 = 50{,}000$. \ans{50000}

In $5{,}260$ the 5 is in the thousands place: $5 \times 1{,}000 = 5{,}000$.
\ans{5000}

The digit moved one place, so the first value is $50{,}000 \div 5{,}000 = 10$
times the second. \ans{10}

\problem{How many times as large as $10^2$ is $10^5$?}

\textbf{Solution:} $10^5 = 100{,}000$ and $10^2 = 100$. Dividing,
$10^5 \div 10^2 = 100{,}000 \div 100 = 1{,}000$ — three factors of ten remain,
which matches subtracting the exponents: $5 - 2 = 3$. \ans{1000}

\problem{One box holds 10 markers; one case holds 100 boxes. Markers in 6 cases?}

\textbf{Solution:} One case holds $100 \times 10 = 1{,}000$ markers (a box of
boxes multiplies the powers of ten). Six cases hold
$6 \times 1{,}000 = 6{,}000$ markers. \ans{6000}
