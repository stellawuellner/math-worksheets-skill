\problem{The ten frame shows 8 counters. How many more fill the frame? $8 + \_\_ = 10$}

\textbf{Solution:} The frame has 10 spaces and 8 are filled, so count on from
8: 9, 10 --- that is 2 more counters. \ans{2}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Part--whole box: whole 12, one part 7. $7 + \_\_ = 12$}

\textbf{Solution:} The whole is 12 and one part is 7. Count on from 7 up to
12: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 --- that is 5 jumps, so the missing part is 5. \ans{5}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Jo has 9 grapes and wants 15. How many more grapes? $9 + \_\_ = 15$}

\textbf{Solution:} Count on from 9 to 15: 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 --- six
jumps. Jo needs 6 more grapes. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{6 kids on the bus, more get on, now 11. How many got on? $6 + \_\_ = 11$}

\textbf{Solution:} Count on from 6 to 11: 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 --- five jumps, so
5 kids got on at the stop. \ans{5}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Part--whole box: whole 16, one part 9. $9 + \_\_ = 16$}

\textbf{Solution:} Whole 16, part 9. Make ten first: 9 and 1 is 10, then 6
more reaches 16. That is $1 + 6 = 7$, so the missing part is 7. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{The first part is missing: $\_\_ + 8 = 14$}

\textbf{Solution:} The missing number and 8 make 14 together, so subtract:
$14 - 8 = 6$. Check: $6 + 8 = 14$. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{(a) $7 + \_\_ = 13$ \quad (b) the subtraction that checks it: $13 - 7 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count on from 7 to 13: six jumps, so the missing
number is \ans{6}

(b) The same fact family, written as subtraction: $13 - 7$ takes the known
part away from the whole and leaves \ans{6}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Lia had some shells, found 6 more, now has 14. How many did she start
with? $\_\_ + 6 = 14$}

\textbf{Solution:} Work backwards from the whole: take away the 6 she found,
$14 - 6 = 8$. Check by counting on: $8 + 6 = 14$. She started with 8 shells.
\ans{8}
