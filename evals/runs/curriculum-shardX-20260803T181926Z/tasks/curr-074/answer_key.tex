\problem{\textbf{$18$ stickers into $3$ bags. How many in each bag?}

Step 1. Sort the numbers by their job. The total is $18$. The story gives the
number of \emph{groups}: $3$ bags. The question asks for the size of each group.

Step 2. Share the total into the groups: $18 \div 3$. Skip-count by $3$ to
check: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18$ --- six threes.

Step 3. Each bag gets $\ans{6}$ stickers.

Step 4. Multiply back: $3$ bags $\times\ 6$ in each bag $= 18$ $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} an answer of $3$. That is the number of bags, which the story
already told us --- it cannot also be the answer.}

\problem{\textbf{$20$ stickers, $5$ in each bag. How many bags?}

Step 1. Sort the numbers. Total $= 20$. This time the story gives the size of
each group ($5$ in a bag), and the question asks how many groups.

Step 2. Keep taking $5$ away, or skip-count by $5$: $5, 10, 15, 20$. That is
four fives.

Step 3. Mia fills $\ans{4}$ bags.

Step 4. Multiply back: $4$ bags $\times\ 5$ in each bag $= 20$ $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} an answer of $5$. That is how many go in one bag, not how many
bags there are.}

\problem{\textbf{Ravi: $24$ crackers, $4$ on each plate --- he says $4$ plates.}

Step 1. Find the number Ravi wrote back. The story said ``$4$ crackers on a
plate,'' so $4$ is the \emph{size of each group}. Ravi used it again as the
number of plates.

Step 2. The question asks how many plates, so share the total into groups of
four: $24 \div 4$. Skip-count by $4$: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24$ --- six fours.

Step 3. There are really $\ans{6}$ plates.

Step 4. Multiply back: $6$ plates $\times\ 4$ crackers $= 24$ $\checkmark$.
Ravi's answer would need $4 \times 4 = 16$ crackers, which is not the total the
story gave.}

\problem{\textbf{Leo: $35$ seeds in $5$ pots --- he says $5$ seeds in each pot.}

Step 1. Find the repeat. The story said $5$ \emph{pots}, so $5$ is the number of
groups. Leo used that same number for the seeds in each pot.

Step 2. The question asks for the size of one group: $35 \div 5$. Skip-count by
$5$ to $35$: $5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35$ --- seven fives.

Step 3. Each pot gets $\ans{7}$ seeds.

Step 4. Multiply back: $5$ pots $\times\ 7$ seeds $= 35$ $\checkmark$. Leo's
answer would only use $5 \times 5 = 25$ seeds, leaving ten in his hand --- a
good way for a child to see the error without being told.}

\problem{\textbf{Ana: $48$ apples, $8$ in each basket. Fill in
$\underline{\hspace{1.2cm}} \times 8 = 48$.}

Step 1. Read the equation in story words: (number of baskets) $\times$ ($8$
apples in each basket) $=$ $48$ apples in all. The blank is the number of
groups.

Step 2. Count up by eights until you reach the total: $8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48$.
That is six eights.

Step 3. The missing number is $\ans{6}$, so Ana fills six baskets.

Step 4. Check: $6 \times 8 = 48$ $\checkmark$. Writing the multiplication first
is the surest cure for this mix-up --- the blank sits in exactly one of the two
jobs, so it cannot be confused with the other.}

\problem{\textbf{Rio: $40$ markers, $8$ to each group --- he says $8$ groups.}

Step 1. Check Rio's answer the way the problem asks. If there were $8$ groups
with $8$ markers each, the teacher would need $8 \times 8 = 64$ markers. That is
bigger than $40$, so Rio's answer cannot be right.

Step 2. Rio repeated the group size ($8$ markers to each group) as the number of
groups.

Step 3. Share the total into groups of eight: $40 \div 8$. Skip-count:
$8, 16, 24, 32, 40$ --- five eights.

Step 4. Five groups get markers: $\ans{5}$, and the check is
$5 \times 8 = 40$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Jen has $36$ shells with $4$ in each row; Ben has $36$ shells
in $4$ rows. How many shells in one of Ben's rows?}

Step 1. The two stories use the same two numbers, but $4$ has a different job in
each. For Jen, $4$ is the size of each row. For Ben, $4$ is the number of rows.

Step 2. The question is about Ben, so $4$ is a group count and the missing
number is the group size: $36 \div 4$.

Step 3. Skip-count by $4$ to $36$: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36$ --- nine
fours. Ben has $\ans{9}$ shells in each row.

Step 4. Multiply back: $4$ rows $\times\ 9$ shells $= 36$ $\checkmark$. Jen's
rows hold only $4$ shells each, so answering $4$ here means answering Jen's
story instead of Ben's.}

\problem{\textbf{(a) How do you tell the two jobs apart? (b) $30$ pencils, $6$ in
each cup --- how many cups?}

\textbf{(a)} \emph{Open response --- flagged for manual review. Model answer
below; accept any equivalent wording.}\\
Look at the words right next to the number. ``$6$ cups'' counts the groups.
``$6$ in each cup'' tells the size of one group --- the words ``in each'' are the
giveaway. The biggest number is usually the total, the amount being shared out.
Then ask what the question wants, and remember that the answer is never a number
the story already gave you.\\
\emph{What a full-credit answer must contain:} the ``in each'' test (or an
equivalent description of it), and the idea that the number the story gives
cannot also be the answer.

\textbf{(b)} Here $6$ comes with the words ``in each cup,'' so it is the group
size, and the question asks for the number of groups.

Step 1. Share the total into sixes: $30 \div 6$.

Step 2. Skip-count by $6$: $6, 12, 18, 24, 30$ --- five sixes.

Step 3. Five cups are filled: $\ans{5}$, checked by $5 \times 6 = 30$
$\checkmark$.}
