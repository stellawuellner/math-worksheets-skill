Here are the three PDFs for a Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra guided-concept set on
**systems of two linear equations, focused on understanding the intersection
point as the solution of the system**.

**Skills Summary (study guide)** — `ss_intersections_curr226.pdf`, 2 pages.
Four sections, each tying a model to the notation: testing a pair in both
equations, reading the crossing point off a graph (with a small worked grid),
finding the shared row in two tables, and reading what the intersection means
in a story. Each has a rule box, a two-step worked example whose first step
says why that method applies, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down
inside the box.

**Worksheet** — `ws_intersections_curr226.pdf`, 6 pages, 10 problems.
A shared **Graph Bank** at the top draws four labelled lines, with the equation
of each printed in the caption; the caption also says that only the problems
naming a line by letter use it. The models rotate rather than being blocked:
algebraic pairs, the graph, a pair of tables, a gym-cost story, back to the
graph, and so on. Two problems are deliberately parallel systems — one from the
algebra, one from the graph — so "no solution" is met as a real case rather
than an exception. Problem 7 prints a claimed solution that satisfies one
equation and fails the other, which is the specific misconception this focus
exists to break. Every part has its own answer blank.

**Answer key** — `ak_intersections_curr226.pdf`, 3 pages.
Quick Answers bank labelled by response, then a full worked solution for each
problem that shows both the graphical reading and the algebraic confirmation.
Curriculum block: 8.EE.C.8a, 8.EE.C.8b and 8.EE.C.8c, difficulty 1–4.

**What is verified, honestly.** Eighteen checks were declared across the ten
problems. **Thirteen are machine-checked with SymPy**: every system is solved
independently and each stated solution is confirmed to satisfy *every*
equation, with the count matching SymPy's full solution set — and the two
parallel systems are machine-confirmed to be inconsistent, so "no solution" is
a verified result rather than an assertion. The table readings are checked
against the same data the printed tables come from. Worksheet prose numbers
match the verification data 42 out of 42.

**Five responses are instructor-judged, not machine-checked**: the "explain
what this means" part of problems 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10. Those are the conceptual
heart of the sheet and no computer algebra system can grade them. The Quick
Answers bank prints `---` for each, the key's generated note names those five
problems, and every worked solution states what a correct explanation must
contain and what should not earn credit.
