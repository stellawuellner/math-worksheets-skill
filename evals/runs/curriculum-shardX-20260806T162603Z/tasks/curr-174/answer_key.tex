\problem{Combine like terms: \quad $5x + 3x$}

\textbf{Solution:} Both terms are $x$-terms, so add the coefficients and keep the
variable: $5 + 3 = 8$ copies of $x$. \ans{8x}

\problem{Expand: \quad $6(x + 4)$}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the 6 to \emph{both} terms: $6 \cdot x + 6 \cdot 4$. \ans{6x + 24}

\problem{Combine like terms: \quad $4x + 7 + 3x - 2$}

\textbf{Solution:} $x$-terms: $4x + 3x = 7x$. Constants: $7 - 2 = 5$. \ans{7x + 5}

\problem{Consider $3(x + 2)$ and $3x + 2$. (a) evaluate the first at $x = 5$;
(b) evaluate the second at $x = 5$; (c) are they equivalent?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $3(5 + 2) = 3 \cdot 7 = 21$. \ans{21}

(b) $3 \cdot 5 + 2 = 15 + 2 = 17$. \ans{17}

(c) Model answer: they are \emph{not} equivalent --- substituting $x = 5$ gives $21$
for one and $17$ for the other, and equivalent expressions must give the same value
for every $x$. (Instructor judges the sentence; it must point to the two different
values, or to the missing $3 \cdot 2$.)

\problem{Deshawn evaluates $4(x + 3)$ when $x = 2$ and writes $4 \cdot 2 + 3 = 11$.
(a) name the error; (b) find the correct value.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Deshawn distributed the 4 to only the first term: he
multiplied the $x$ by 4 but left the $+3$ alone. The 4 multiplies the \emph{whole}
sum $(x + 3)$.

(b) $4(2 + 3) = 4 \cdot 5 = 20$. \ans{20}

\problem{Ava simplifies $6x - 4x$ and writes $6x - 4x = 2$ ``because the $x$'s
cancel.'' (a) name the error; (b) find the value of $6x - 4x$ when $x = 7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Ava dropped the variable: $6x - 4x$ means 6 copies of $x$
minus 4 copies of $x$, which leaves $2x$ --- two copies of $x$, not the number 2.
The $x$'s do not cancel; they are what is being counted.

(b) $6x - 4x = 2x$, and at $x = 7$ that is $2 \cdot 7 = 14$. \ans{14}

\problem{Expand: \quad $-3(2x - 5)$}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute $-3$ to both terms, watching signs:
$-3 \cdot 2x = -6x$ and $-3 \cdot (-5) = +15$. \ans{-6x + 15}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge.} Jaylen claims $2(3x + 4) = 6x + 4$. (a) evaluate
$2(3x + 4)$ at $x = 5$; (b) evaluate $6x + 4$ at $x = 5$; (c) what did he forget?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $2(3 \cdot 5 + 4) = 2(15 + 4) = 2 \cdot 19 = 38$. \ans{38}

(b) $6 \cdot 5 + 4 = 30 + 4 = 34$. \ans{34}

(c) Model answer: Jaylen multiplied only the $3x$ by 2 and left the $+4$ alone; the
4 must be doubled too, so the correct expansion is $6x + 8$. The two different
values in (a) and (b) prove his claim wrong. (Instructor judges the sentence.)
