# Zero and Negative Exponents — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready: the student worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key, and a
two-page study guide.

**Worksheet (5 pages, 12 problems).** Every problem uses a zero or a negative
exponent. The ramp is:

- **Evaluate** (1, 2, 3): $7^0$, then $5^{-2}$, then a negative power of a fraction,
  where the reciprocal idea becomes visible as a flip.
- **Rewrite with positive exponents** (4, 9): including the classic $3x^{-2}$, where
  only the $x$ moves and the coefficient stays put.
- **Apply the laws** (5, 8, 12): product, power-of-a-power, and a full quotient with
  coefficients and two letters as the closing challenge.
- **Compare** (6, 10): $2^{-3}$ against $3^{-2}$ — where comparing exponents instead
  of values gives the wrong answer — and ordering $5^{-1}, 5^{0}, 5^{1}$.
- **Error analysis** (11): Dana wrote $4^{-2} = -16$; the student names the mistake
  and gives the correct value.
- **Scientific notation** (7): a mass of $4.5 \times 10^{-3}$ g written as a decimal,
  which is the negative exponent doing real work.

Work space runs 4–8 cm per problem, with an answer blank on each.

**What was verified.** All 12 answers were recomputed with SymPy before typesetting:
5 numeric evaluations, 5 symbolic equivalence checks (each simplified answer proved
equal to the original expression as a function, not just similar-looking), and 2
comparison checks. The answer key's boxed answers were then bound to those verified
values problem by problem. **Nothing is flagged for manual review** — even problem 11
is machine-checked, because the correction is an ordinary evaluation and the planted
wrong answer is declared as a trap.

Three misconception traps are declared and confirmed distinguishable, and they print
in the answer key as "common wrong answers": negating instead of reciprocating
($5^{-2} \to -25$ and $4^{-2} \to -16$), and moving the decimal point the wrong way
in scientific notation ($4.5 \times 10^{-3} \to 4500$).

**Answer key (3 pages).** Each solution names the definition or law being used first,
then works the arithmetic, and several add a teaching note — including why $a^0 = 1$
follows from the quotient law, and why comparing the exponents of $2^{-3}$ and
$3^{-2}$ reverses the answer. The quick-answer bank sits at the top for fast grading;
the generated Curriculum section carries the level and the standard.

**Study guide (2 pages).** Four sections — what the definitions mean (with the
divide-a-power-by-itself argument), rewriting with positive exponents, the laws with
negative results, and comparing powers — each with a rule box, a worked example, and
a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the box. All eight study-guide
answers were verified by the same gate as the worksheet.
