\problem{Legs $8$ cm and $15$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Both given sides are legs, so add the squares:
$c = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = \sqrt{64 + 225} = \sqrt{289}$. \ans{17 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(7, 10)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The gaps are $7 - 1 = 6$ across and $10 - 2 = 8$ up. Those are the legs
of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the segment itself:
$d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100}$. \ans{d = 10}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Hypotenuse $20$ cm, one leg $12$ cm. Find the other leg.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The longest side is given, so the missing side is a leg and the squares
are subtracted: $\sqrt{20^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{400 - 144} = \sqrt{256}$. \ans{16 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Field $30$ m by $40$ m. Find the diagonal, and the saving over walking two sides.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The two sides meet at a right angle, so they are the legs and the
diagonal is the hypotenuse: $\sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{900 + 1600} = \sqrt{2500} = 50$. Rosa
walks $30 + 40 = 70$ m, so the saving is $70 - 50$.
\[ \boxed{\text{diagonal} = 50 \text{ m} \qquad \text{saving} = 20 \text{ m}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Triangle $P(0,0)$, $Q(6,0)$, $R(6,8)$: find all three sides and the perimeter.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $PQ$ is horizontal, so $PQ = 6$. $QR$ is vertical, so $QR = 8$. For the
slanted side, $PR = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$. Adding: $6 + 8 + 10$.
\[ \boxed{PQ = 6 \qquad QR = 8 \qquad PR = 10 \qquad \text{perimeter} = 24} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Box: base $12$ cm by $9$ cm, height $8$ cm. Find $d$ and $D$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Stage one is the base rectangle, whose sides are the legs:
$d = \sqrt{12^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{144 + 81} = \sqrt{225} = 15$. Stage two uses that answer as a leg
alongside the height: $D = \sqrt{15^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{225 + 64} = \sqrt{289}$.
\[ \boxed{d = 15 \text{ cm} \qquad D = 17 \text{ cm}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $x^2 + 576 = 625$ and say which root is the leg.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Isolate the square, then take both roots:
\[ x^2 = 625 - 576 = 49, \qquad x = \pm 7 \]
\[ \boxed{x = -7 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 7} \]

\textbf{Reason (instructor-judged).} A correct response chooses $7$ and explains that squaring
hides the sign, so the equation has two solutions, but the side of a triangle is a length and
cannot be negative. A chosen number with no reason earns no credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Pencil box $20$ cm by $15$ cm by $9$ cm. Longest line inside, and will a $26$ cm pencil
fit?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The longest straight line inside a box is its space diagonal, which
takes all three measurements at once:
\[ D = \sqrt{20^2 + 15^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{400 + 225 + 81} = \sqrt{706} = 26.5707\ldots \]
\ans{26.57 \text{ cm}}

\textbf{Decision (instructor-judged).} A correct response says the pencil fits: the longest line
available inside the box is about $26.57$ cm and the pencil is $26$ cm, so it goes in corner to
corner even though it is longer than the $20$ cm edge. Accept any wording that compares the
pencil with the space diagonal rather than with one edge.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ramp rising $1.5$ m over a run of $3.6$ m. Find the ramp and the total edging.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The rise and the run are the legs, so the sloping ramp is the
hypotenuse: $\sqrt{1.5^2 + 3.6^2} = \sqrt{2.25 + 12.96} = \sqrt{15.21} = 3.9$. The edging runs
along all three sides: $1.5 + 3.6 + 3.9$.
\[ \boxed{\text{ramp} = 3.9 \text{ m} \qquad \text{edging} = 9 \text{ m}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A(-1,2)$, $B(3,5)$, $C(6,1)$: find the three sides and show the triangle is isosceles.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad
\[ AB = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25}, \qquad BC = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25}, \qquad
   AC = \sqrt{7^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{50} \]
The first two are exactly equal; the third is $\sqrt{50} = 7.0710\ldots$, which rounds to $7.07$.
\[ \boxed{AB = 5 \qquad BC = 5 \qquad AC = 7.07} \]

\textbf{Conclusion (instructor-judged).} A correct response says the triangle is isosceles
because two sides are equal and the third is not: $AB$ and $BC$ are both $5$, while $AC$ is about
$7.07$. Three lengths with no conclusion is not an argument.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Pool $25$ m by $10$ m by $2$ m deep. Find the surface diagonal and the rope.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Stage one is the rectangle at the surface:
$\sqrt{25^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{625 + 100} = \sqrt{725} = 26.9258\ldots$, which rounds to $26.93$.
Stage two adds the depth as a third leg:
\[ \sqrt{25^2 + 10^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{625 + 100 + 4} = \sqrt{729} = 27 \]
\[ \boxed{\text{surface diagonal} = 26.93 \text{ m} \qquad \text{rope} = 27 \text{ m}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Garden $(0,0)$, $(12,0)$, $(12,5)$, $(0,5)$ in metres. Find the support, the perimeter,
and the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The support is the diagonal of the rectangle:
$\sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$. The perimeter is
$2(12 + 5) = 34$, and the two together come to $34 + 13$.
\[ \boxed{\text{support} = 13 \text{ m} \qquad \text{perimeter} = 34 \text{ m} \qquad
   \text{total} = 47 \text{ m}} \]

\textbf{Reason (instructor-judged).} A correct response says half the perimeter is exactly
$12 + 5 = 17$, the two legs added, while the support is the hypotenuse of that same right
triangle --- and a hypotenuse is always shorter than the two legs added together, so $13 < 17$.
Accept any wording that says the straight route beats going along two sides.
