\problem{\textbf{Table 5, 8, 11, 14 --- constant difference.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Test both patterns before naming one. Differences: $8-5=3$,
$11-8=3$, $14-11=3$ --- constant. Ratios: $8/5 = 1.6$ but $11/8 = 1.375$ --- not
constant.

\textbf{Step 2.} A constant difference is the signature of linear change, so
$f(n) = 5 + 3n$ with $a = 5$ and $d = 3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $f(6) = 5 + 3 \cdot 6 = 5 + 18$.
\ans{23}

\textit{Read the table, not the shape:} four increasing values could be either
model. Only the difference row settles it.}

\problem{\textbf{Table 4, 12, 36, 108 --- constant ratio.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The differences 8, 24, 72 are growing, which rules out linear
change, so test the ratios: $12/4 = 3$, $36/12 = 3$, $108/36 = 3$ --- constant.

\textbf{Step 2.} A constant ratio is the signature of exponential change:
$g(n) = 4 \cdot 3^{\,n}$ with $a = 4$ and $r = 3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $g(5) = 4 \cdot 3^5 = 4 \cdot 243$.
\ans{972}

\textit{Note:} the differences of an exponential pattern grow in the same ratio
--- 8, 24, 72 also triples. Growing differences are the fingerprint of a
constant ratio.}

\problem{\textbf{Rosa's savings: 200 dollars plus 25 dollars a month.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Classify from the verb before computing. ``Adds 25'' is
repeated addition of a fixed amount, so the balance changes by a constant
difference --- linear, not exponential. No interest means nothing is multiplied.

\textbf{Step 2.} The rule is $B(n) = 200 + 25n$, so
$B(8) = 200 + 25 \cdot 8 = 200 + 200$.
\ans{400}

\textit{Contrast:} an account paying 25 percent interest would multiply by 1.25
each month and reach a far larger balance --- same-looking numbers, different
operation.}

\problem{\textbf{6 cells doubling every hour.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Doubles'' means multiply by 2, and a repeated multiplication
by a fixed factor is exponential change with $r = 2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Starting at $a = 6$, the rule is $C(n) = 6 \cdot 2^{\,n}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $C(7) = 6 \cdot 2^7 = 6 \cdot 128$.
\ans{768}

\textit{Why not linear:} the colony adds 6 cells in the first hour but 384 in
the seventh. The amount added is not fixed --- the multiplier is.}

\problem{\textbf{Plan L versus Plan E, both starting at 100 dollars.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Plan L adds, so $L(n) = 100 + 50n$; Plan E multiplies, so
$E(n) = 100 \cdot 1.5^{\,n}$. Write both rules first --- the comparison is
between rules, not between two numbers.

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): Plan E is behind early because 50 percent of a small
balance is small, but the amount E adds grows every step while L's stays at 50.
Once E's step passes 50 dollars it gains ground and never gives it back.

\textbf{Step 3.} At step 5: $E(5) = 100 \cdot 1.5^5 = 100 \cdot 7.59375 =
759.375$ dollars, and $L(5) = 100 + 250 = 350$ dollars. The gap is
$759.375 - 350$.
\ans{409.38}

\textit{Watch out:} computing $100 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 5 = 750$ instead of
$100 \cdot 1.5^5$ gives a gap of 400 dollars. Multiplying by the number of steps
is the linear move; exponential change raises the factor to that power.}

\problem{\textbf{Table 2, 8, 18, 32 --- neither model.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): the first ratio is $h(2) \div h(1) = 8 \div 2$.
\ans{4}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): the next ratio is $h(3) \div h(2) = 18 \div 8$, which
is $\dfrac{9}{4}$.
\ans{2.25}

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): 4 and 2.25 are different, so there is no constant
ratio and $h$ is not exponential. The differences 6, 10, 14 are not constant
either, so $h$ is not linear. It is neither --- in fact $h(n) = 2n^2$.

\textit{Why this problem matters:} ``not linear'' does not mean ``exponential.''
Both rows have to be tested, and a pattern is allowed to fail both.}

\problem{\textbf{Linear pattern through $(2, 17)$ and $(5, 32)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} For a linear pattern the rate of change is the same between any
two points, so compute it from the pair given rather than assuming consecutive
inputs.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{32 - 17}{5 - 2} = \dfrac{15}{3}$.
\ans{5}

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (b): back up from $(2, 17)$ by two steps of 5 to get
$f(0) = 7$, so $f(n) = 7 + 5n$. Check: $f(5) = 7 + 25 = 32$.

\textit{Part (c):} no. An exponential function through those points has a
constant \emph{ratio}, and its rate of change grows from one interval to the
next --- which is exactly what distinguishes it from this line.}

\problem{\textbf{A 24000-dollar car: 12 percent a year versus 2400 dollars a year.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Dealer A takes a percent of the \emph{current} value, so each
year multiplies by $1 - 0.12 = 0.88$ --- exponential decay. Dealer B subtracts a
fixed amount, so it is linear. Classifying first prevents the usual error.

\textbf{Step 2.} $A(n) = 24000 \cdot 0.88^{\,n}$ and $B(n) = 24000 - 2400n$.

\textbf{Step 3.} At $n = 5$: $A(5) = 24000 \cdot 0.88^5 = 24000 \cdot
0.5277319 = 12665.57$ dollars, and $B(5) = 24000 - 12000 = 12000$ dollars. The
difference is $12665.566 - 12000$.
\ans{665.57}

\textit{Watch out:} reading ``12 percent a year'' as 12 percent of the original
price every year gives $24000(1 - 0.60) = 9600$ dollars and makes Dealer A look
2400 dollars \emph{worse}. That single misreading turns an exponential model
into a linear one and flips the answer's sign.}

\problem{\textbf{Exponential population: 500 at time 0, 4000 at time 3.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): exponential means $P(n) = 500 \cdot r^{\,n}$, so the
given pair becomes one equation in $r$: $500 r^3 = 4000$, hence $r^3 = 8$.
\ans{r = 2}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): the rule is $P(n) = 500 \cdot 2^{\,n}$, so
$P(5) = 500 \cdot 32$.
\ans{16000}

\textit{Compare with linear:} a linear model through the same two points would
add $\frac{4000-500}{3} \approx 1166.7$ per step and reach about 6333 at time 5
--- far less than 16000. Same two points, wildly different futures.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: flat 1000 dollars a day versus a doubling cent.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Part (a): day 1 pays 0.01 dollars and has been doubled zero
times, so the exponent counts the days \emph{after} day 1:
$D(n) = 0.01 \cdot 2^{\,n-1}$. The flat plan is $F(n) = 1000$, a constant ---
the extreme case of linear, with $d = 0$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): on day 10, $D(10) = 0.01 \cdot 2^9 = 5.12$ dollars,
so the flat plan pays far more that day. The doubling plan is still tiny.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): on day 20, $D(20) = 0.01 \cdot 2^{19} = 0.01 \cdot
524288 = 5242.88$ dollars, and the flat plan pays 1000 dollars, so the gap is
$5242.88 - 1000$.
\ans{4242.88}

\textit{The whole point:} between day 10 and day 20 the doubling plan went from
1000 times behind to 4 times ahead, without changing its rule. A constant ratio
always overtakes a constant difference eventually --- the only question is when.

\textit{Watch out:} $0.01 \cdot 2 \cdot 19 = 0.38$ dollars is the linear
misreading, and it leaves the doubling plan 999.62 dollars behind.}
