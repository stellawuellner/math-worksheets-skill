\problem{$S(t) = 320 \cdot (1.25)^{\,t}$ thousand subscribers.
At launch $t = 0$, and $b^{\,0} = 1$, so $S(0) = 320 \cdot 1$.
\ans{S(0) = 320 \text{ thousand}}

The base is the yearly multiplier. Writing $b = 1 + r$ gives $1.25 = 1 + r$, so the
growth rate is $r = 1.25 - 1$, i.e. a $25$ percent increase per year.
\ans{r = 0.25}}

\problem{$N(t) = a \cdot b^{\,t}$ with $N(0) = 240$ and $N(3) = 810$.
From $t = 0$: $a \cdot b^{\,0} = a = 240$. Substituting into the second point gives
$240 \cdot b^{\,3} = 810$, so $b^{\,3} = 810/240 = 3.375$.
Taking the cube root, $b = \sqrt[3]{3.375}$.
\ans{b = 1.5}
Check: $240 \cdot (1.5)^3 = 240 \cdot 3.375 = 810$ cells, as recorded.}

\problem{Solve $240 \cdot (1.5)^{\,t} = 1215$.
Divide by 240: $(1.5)^{\,t} = 5.0625$. Take natural logs of both sides:
$t \ln 1.5 = \ln 5.0625$, so $t = \dfrac{\ln 5.0625}{\ln 1.5} = \dfrac{1.62186}{0.405465} = 4$.
\ans{t = 4}
The culture reaches 1215 cells after exactly 4 hours; check $240 \cdot (1.5)^4 = 1215$.}

\problem{Rewrite $240 \cdot (1.5)^{\,t}$ as $240 \cdot e^{k t}$.
The two forms agree when $e^{k} = 1.5$, i.e. $(1.5)^{\,t} = e^{(\ln 1.5)t}$ for every $t$,
so $k = \ln 1.5$ exactly. Numerically $\ln 1.5 = 0.405465\ldots$, which rounds to
$0.405$.
\ans{k = \ln 1.5 \approx 0.405}
The continuous form is $N(t) = 240e^{0.405t}$, and $k > 0$ confirms growth.}

\problem{$A(t) = 80 \cdot (0.85)^{\,t}$ milligrams.

(a) The base is the hourly multiplier. Writing $b = 1 - r$ gives $0.85 = 1 - r$, so
$r = 1 - 0.85$: the patient loses 15 percent of the current amount each hour.
\ans{0.15}

(b) Substitute $t = 4$: $A(4) = 80 \cdot (0.85)^4 = 80 \cdot 0.52200625 = 41.7605$,
which rounds to $41.76$.
\ans{A(4) \approx 41.76 \text{ mg}}}

\problem{$C(t) = a \cdot b^{\,t}$ with $C(1) = 36$ and $C(4) = 121.5$ square metres.

(a) Divide the second equation by the first so that $a$ cancels:
$\dfrac{a b^{\,4}}{a b^{\,1}} = b^{\,3} = \dfrac{121.5}{36} = 3.375$, so $b = \sqrt[3]{3.375}$.
\ans{b = 1.5}

(b) Now use $C(1) = a \cdot b = 36$, so $a = 36 / 1.5$.
\ans{a = 24}
Check: $24 \cdot (1.5)^4 = 121.5$ square metres, matching the second measurement.}

\problem{Doubling means $320 \cdot (1.25)^{\,t} = 2 \cdot 320$. Dividing by 320 removes it
entirely: $(1.25)^{\,t} = 2$. That is why the doubling time does not depend on the
initial value. Taking logs, $t = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.25} = \dfrac{0.693147}{0.223144}
= 3.1063\ldots$
\ans{t \approx 3.11}}

\problem{The monthly factor satisfies $b^{12} = 1.25$, so $b = (1.25)^{1/12}$.

(a) $(1.25)^{1/12} = 1.018769\ldots$, which rounds to $1.0188$.
\ans{b \approx 1.0188}

(b) The rate is the factor minus 1: $1.018769\ldots - 1 = 0.018769\ldots$, about
1.88 percent per month.
\ans{r \approx 0.0188}
Note that twelve months of this factor multiply back to 1.25, while twelve times the
monthly rate would overstate the yearly growth --- percentages compound, they do not add.}

\problem{$P(t) = 500 \cdot (1.08)^{\,t}$ people.

(a) $P(1) - P(0) = 500 \cdot 1.08 - 500 = 540 - 500$.
\ans{40 \text{ people}}

(b) $P(6) - P(5) = 500 \cdot (1.08)^6 - 500 \cdot (1.08)^5 = 793.437\ldots - 734.664\ldots
= 58.7731\ldots$, which rounds to $58.77$.
\ans{58.77 \text{ people}}

The increases are not equal, so the growth is not a constant number of people per year.
The base $1.08$ multiplies the \emph{current} population by 1.08 --- an 8 percent
increase --- so the yearly gain itself grows. A student answering ``8'' has read a
growth factor as though it were a constant difference.}

\problem{$M(t) = a \cdot b^{\,t}$ milligrams with $M(0) = 12$ and $M(6) = 3$.

(a) From $t = 0$, $a = 12$. Then $12 \cdot b^{\,6} = 3$, so $b^{\,6} = 3/12 = 0.25$ and
$b = (0.25)^{1/6} = 0.793700\ldots$, which rounds to $0.7937$.
\ans{b \approx 0.7937}

(b) Solve $12 \cdot b^{\,t} = 1$, i.e. $b^{\,t} = 1/12$. Taking logs,
$t = \dfrac{\ln (1/12)}{\ln b} = \dfrac{-\ln 12}{-\tfrac{1}{6}\ln 4}
= \dfrac{6 \ln 12}{\ln 4} = \dfrac{6 \cdot 2.484907}{1.386294} = 10.7548\ldots$
\ans{t \approx 10.75 \text{ hours}}}
