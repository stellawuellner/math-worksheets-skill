\noindent\small Every answer below is written the way a third grader should say it out loud:
\emph{what the shape was cut into}, then \emph{the numbers}. Accept any correct decomposition ---
a shape can be cut into rectangles in more than one way, and the total must come out the same.
\normalsize

\problem{Sticker, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} Cut across at the height of $3$: a bottom rectangle $8$ by $3$ and a top
rectangle $4$ by $3$.
\begin{align*}
\text{bottom} &= 8 \times 3 = 24 \\
\text{top} &= 4 \times 3 = 12 \\
\text{total} &= 24 + 12 = 36
\end{align*}
\ans{36\text{ cm}^2}
\emph{Check the other way:} the whole $8 \times 6$ rectangle is $48$, minus the missing
$4 \times 3 = 12$ corner, which is also $36$.

\problem{Ribbon, perimeter.}
\textbf{Solution:} Walk all the way around and add every side, without skipping the two short
sides inside the notch:
\[ 10 + 4 + 5 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 34 \]
\ans{34\text{ cm}}
\emph{Watch for:} students who add only the four ``outside'' sides ($10+4+5+7$) get $26$ and have
missed two sides.

\problem{Two rectangles, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} Cut across at the height of $3$: a bottom rectangle $9$ by $3$, and an upper
right rectangle $5$ wide by $4$ tall.
\begin{align*}
\text{bottom} &= 9 \times 3 = 27 \\
\text{upper right} &= 5 \times 4 = 20 \\
\text{total} &= 27 + 20 = 47
\end{align*}
\ans{47\text{ cm}^2}

\problem{Name tag, perimeter.}
\textbf{Solution:} There are eight sides. Add them in order around the shape:
\[ 4 + 4 + 2 + 3 + 8 + 3 + 2 + 4 = 30 \]
\ans{30\text{ cm}}
\emph{Note:} area is not asked for here. A student who multiplies has answered a different question.

\problem{Maya's way (subtracting), area.}
\textbf{Solution:} The whole rectangle would be $10$ across and $6$ tall. The bite taken out of the
corner is $3$ across and $2$ tall.
\begin{align*}
\text{whole rectangle} &= 10 \times 6 = 60 \\
\text{missing corner} &= 3 \times 2 = 6 \\
\text{shape} &= 60 - 6 = 54
\end{align*}
\ans{54\text{ cm}^2}
\emph{Check by adding instead:} $10 \times 4 = 40$ and $7 \times 2 = 14$, and $40 + 14$ is the same
total.

\problem{Missing side.}
\textbf{Solution:} The two top pieces lie along the same straight line as the bottom, so together
they must be as long as the bottom.
\[ 5 + ? = 12 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad ? = 12 - 5 = 7 \]
\ans{7\text{ cm}}

\problem{Rug, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} Cut across at the height of $5$: a bottom rectangle $12$ by $5$ and a top
rectangle $5$ by $3$.
\begin{align*}
\text{bottom} &= 12 \times 5 = 60 \\
\text{top} &= 5 \times 3 = 15 \\
\text{total} &= 60 + 15 = 75
\end{align*}
\ans{75\text{ ft}^2}

\problem{Patio with a flower bed, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} Start with the whole rectangle and take away the flower bed.
\begin{align*}
\text{whole patio} &= 15 \times 10 = 150 \\
\text{flower bed} &= 4 \times 6 = 24 \\
\text{paved part} &= 150 - 24 = 126
\end{align*}
\ans{126\text{ ft}^2}

\problem{Garden fence, perimeter.}
\textbf{Solution:} Add the six sides all the way around:
\[ 16 + 5 + 9 + 7 + 7 + 12 = 56 \]
\ans{56\text{ ft}}
\emph{Nice pattern to point out:} the two horizontal pieces ($9$ and $7$) add to the bottom ($16$),
and the two vertical pieces ($5$ and $7$) add to the left side ($12$) --- so this perimeter is the
same as a $16$ by $12$ rectangle's.

\problem{Missing side on the deck.}
\textbf{Solution:} The right-hand side and the short piece above it stack up to make the whole left
side, because both go from the bottom edge to the top edge.
\[ ? + 5 = 13 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad ? = 13 - 5 = 8 \]
\ans{8\text{ ft}}

\problem{Challenge --- porch floor, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} First the unlabelled side. The left side is $9$ and the right side is $6$, so the
short vertical piece is $9 - 6 = 3$. Now take the whole $12$ by $9$ rectangle and subtract the
missing corner, which is $4$ across by $3$ tall.
\begin{align*}
\text{whole rectangle} &= 12 \times 9 = 108 \\
\text{missing corner} &= 4 \times 3 = 12 \\
\text{porch} &= 108 - 12 = 96
\end{align*}
\ans{96\text{ ft}^2}
\emph{Adding instead:} $12 \times 6 = 72$ plus $8 \times 3 = 24$ gives the same total.

\problem{Challenge --- three steps, area.}
\textbf{Solution:} Cut the shape into three flat steps, each $3$ tall:
\begin{align*}
\text{bottom step} &= 12 \times 3 = 36 \\
\text{middle step} &= 8 \times 3 = 24 \\
\text{top step} &= 4 \times 3 = 12 \\
\text{total} &= 36 + 24 + 12 = 72
\end{align*}
\ans{72\text{ ft}^2}
\emph{Watch for:} a student who uses $12$, $8$ and $4$ as the \emph{heights} as well as the widths
has cut the shape into overlapping pieces --- have them shade each step before multiplying.
