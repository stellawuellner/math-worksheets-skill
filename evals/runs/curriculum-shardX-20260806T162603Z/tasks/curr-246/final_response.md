# Direction, Form, and Strength in Scatter Plots — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_scatter_curr246.pdf`, 6 pages) — 10 problems, each built on its own
  scatter plot drawn from real-looking data: revision hours against test score,
  television hours against score, temperature through a day, hours worked against pay,
  shoe size against books read, practice against goals, adverts against orders, days
  against bacteria counts, car age against price, and a reading challenge with one odd
  week. Every problem has the same two-part shape: read one number off the plot, then
  put the pattern into words.
- **Answer key** (`ak_scatter_curr246.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem that shows the
  reading or the calculation and then models the description a full-credit answer would
  give.
- **Study guide** (`ss_scatter_curr246.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections, one per feature:
  direction (which way does the cloud go), form (straight line or curve), strength (how
  tightly do the points fit). Each has a rule box, a worked mini example with its own
  small plot, and a try-it.

## What is verified — please read this part

**No problem on this sheet is fully machine-checked, and that is by design.** Every one
of the ten problems has two responses:

- **the number** — 10 checks, all machine-verified by SymPy: the value read at a given
  input, a maximum, a maximum's input, a mean, a median, a range, a difference. The
  chart on the page is drawn from exactly the same data the check uses, so the plot and
  the answer cannot disagree.
- **the description** — 10 responses, all **instructor-judged**. Whether a student has
  correctly called an association "positive", "non-linear" or "moderately strong" is a
  judgement about words, and no computer algebra system can grade it. Each one has a
  written rubric in the key stating what a correct response must contain.

So the key's Quick Answers column shows a value and a `---` for every problem, and its
"What is verified" note says 0 of 10 fully machine-checked. That is the honest reading:
half of every problem is checked and half needs your eye.

## Notes for you

- Problem 6 asks the student to circle the outlier, and problem 10 asks what an outlier
  does to the impression a plot gives. Those two are the most valuable to discuss.
- Problem 5 (shoe size against books read) has no association at all. Students who have
  been trained to find a trend will invent one; the rubric expects "no association".
- Problem 9 is the synthesis: one sentence naming all three features at once.
- Standard covered: 8.SP.A.1 across all ten problems.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
