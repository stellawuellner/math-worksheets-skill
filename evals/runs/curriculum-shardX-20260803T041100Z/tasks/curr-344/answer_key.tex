\noindent\small\textbf{The single idea being tested:} the exponent on the scale factor
counts \emph{dimensions}. Lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$; running
the rule backwards takes the matching root. Every wrong answer on this sheet is the right
factor raised to the wrong power.\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{Area 12 after a dilation with $k = 3$.}\par
A dilation multiplies every length by $k$, and an area is built from two perpendicular
lengths, so both of them stretch: the area is multiplied by $k \cdot k = k^2$.
\[ A' = 12 \cdot 3^2 = 12 \cdot 9 \]
So the image has area $\ans{108 \text{ cm}^2}$.\par
\textit{Watch for:} $12 \cdot 3 = 36$, which scales the area as if it were a length.}

\problem{\textbf{Similar cones, linear factor 2, smaller volume 54.}\par
A volume is built from three perpendicular lengths, and a dilation stretches all three,
so the volume is multiplied by $k \cdot k \cdot k = k^3$. With $k = 2$, that factor is 8.
\[ V' = 54 \cdot 2^3 = 54 \cdot 8 \]
The larger cone holds $\ans{432 \text{ cm}^3}$.\par
\textit{Watch for:} 108 (the factor used once, as if volume were a length) and 216 (the
factor squared, which is the rule for areas).}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake: similar triangles, $5 : 2$, smaller area 18.}\par
(a) Rey used the \emph{linear} ratio on an \emph{area}. The ratio $5 : 2$ compares
corresponding sides, so it scales lengths. Area needs the square of that ratio, because
both dimensions of the triangle grow.\par
(b) Correct work: $k = \dfrac{5}{2}$, so the area ratio is
$k^2 = \dfrac{25}{4}$, and
\[ A_{\text{larger}} = 18 \cdot \left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2
   = 18 \cdot \frac{25}{4} = \frac{450}{4} \]
which gives $\ans{112.5 \text{ cm}^2}$.\par
\textit{Sanity check:} the larger triangle is more than twice as long in each direction,
so more than a doubling of area is expected --- and $112.5$ is more than six times 18,
while Rey's 45 was only two and a half times.}

\problem{\textbf{Areas in the ratio $25 : 9$: solve $9k^2 = 25$.}\par
(a) The equation says the area ratio is the \emph{square} of the linear factor, so
isolate $k^2$ and take roots. Squaring destroys sign information, so the equation has two
solutions:
\[ k^2 = \frac{25}{9} \qquad k = \pm\sqrt{\frac{25}{9}} \]
which are $\ans{k = \tfrac{5}{3}}$ and $\ans{k = -\tfrac{5}{3}}$.\par
(b) A scale factor multiplies lengths, and lengths are positive, so only
$k = \dfrac{5}{3}$ describes a pair of similar figures; the negative root is an artifact
of squaring and is rejected. As a check, $\left(\tfrac{5}{3}\right)^2 = \tfrac{25}{9}$,
the given area ratio.}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake: similar cylinders of volume 250 and 2000.}\par
(a) Nia reported the \emph{volume} ratio as if it were the linear factor: $2000 / 250 = 8$
is $k^3$, not $k$. Kim took a square root, which is the rule for going back from an
\emph{area} ratio; on a volume ratio it gives about 2.83, a number that is neither the
linear factor nor anything else in the problem.\par
(b) Volume ratio is $k^3$, so the linear factor is the \emph{cube} root:
\[ k^3 = \frac{2000}{250} = 8 \qquad k = \sqrt[3]{8} \]
The correct linear scale factor is $\ans{k = 2}$.\par
\textit{Check it forwards:} $250 \cdot 2^3 = 2000$, which is exactly the larger volume,
so the factor is confirmed --- a check neither student ran.}

\problem{\textbf{``Double every edge: twice the paint, twice the sand.''}
\quad (open response --- read the sketch and the reasoning)\par
The claim is wrong in both halves, and by different amounts. With every edge doubled the
linear scale factor is $k = 2$. Paint covers the outside, which is surface area, so it
scales by $k^2 = 4$: the box needs four times as much paint. Sand fills the inside, which
is volume, so it scales by $k^3 = 8$: it holds eight times as much sand. Only a
\emph{length} --- an edge, a diagonal, the perimeter of a face --- doubles.\par
$\ans{\text{paint} \times 4, \ \text{sand} \times 8}$\par
\textit{Full credit} shows a sketch of the original box and the doubled box with edges
labelled, states $k^2$ for surface area and $k^3$ for volume, and explains that the
exponent counts how many dimensions are being stretched at once (a face is stretched in
two directions, the interior in three). \textit{Not full credit:} the numbers 4 and 8
with no link to dimensions, or a claim that only the volume changes.}
