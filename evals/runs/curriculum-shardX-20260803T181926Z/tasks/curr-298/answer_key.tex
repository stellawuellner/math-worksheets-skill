\problem{\textbf{City garage: \$4.00 up to 2 hours, \$3.00 per additional hour,
up to 8 hours. Find $C(5)$.}
\par\smallskip
The flat fee covers everything up to the breakpoint at $t = 2$; past it, only
the hours \emph{beyond} the first 2 are charged, which is $t - 2$ of them.
\[
C(t) = \begin{cases} 4.00, & 0 < t \le 2 \\[2pt] 4.00 + 3.00(t - 2), & 2 < t \le 8 \end{cases}
\]
A 5-hour stay satisfies $2 < 5 \le 8$, so use the second branch:
$C(5) = 4.00 + 3.00(5 - 2) = 4.00 + 3.00 \cdot 3 = 4.00 + 9.00$ dollars.
\ans{C(5) = 13.00}}

\problem{\textbf{Shipper: \$6.00 up to 3 pounds, then \$1.25 per pound over 3.
Find $C(7)$.}
\par\smallskip
Same shape, different units. The charged quantity past the breakpoint is
$w - 3$ pounds.
\[
C(w) = \begin{cases} 6.00, & 0 < w \le 3 \\[2pt] 6.00 + 1.25(w - 3), & w > 3 \end{cases}
\]
A 7-pound package uses the second branch: $7 - 3 = 4$ pounds are charged at
\$1.25 each, so $C(7) = 6.00 + 1.25 \cdot 4 = 6.00 + 5.00$ dollars.
\ans{C(7) = 11.00}}

\problem{\textbf{Utility: 500 kilowatt-hours at \$0.11, everything above 500 at
\$0.16. Priya used 800 and claims \$128.00.}
\par\smallskip
\[
C(n) = \begin{cases} 0.11n, & 0 \le n \le 500 \\[2pt] 0.11(500) + 0.16(n - 500), & n > 500 \end{cases}
\]
\textbf{Priya's mistake:} she applied the second-tier rate to the whole month,
computing $0.16 \times 800 = 128.00$. The higher rate applies only to the
kilowatt-hours \emph{above} the 500 allowance; the first 500 always bill at
\$0.11.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Correct bill:} the first 500 kilowatt-hours cost
$0.11 \times 500 = 55.00$ dollars. The remaining $800 - 500 = 300$
kilowatt-hours cost $0.16 \times 300 = 48.00$ dollars.
Total: $55.00 + 48.00$ dollars.
\ans{C(800) = 103.00}}

\problem{\textbf{Taxi: \$3.50 for the first mile, \$2.25 per additional mile.
Find the trip length whose fare is \$17.00.}
\par\smallskip
\[
C(m) = \begin{cases} 3.50, & 0 < m \le 1 \\[2pt] 3.50 + 2.25(m - 1), & m > 1 \end{cases}
\]
A \$17.00 fare is well past the \$3.50 minimum, so it lives on the second
branch. Set that branch equal to the fare and solve:
\begin{align*}
3.50 + 2.25(m - 1) &= 17.00 \\
2.25(m - 1) &= 13.50 \\
m - 1 &= 6 \\
m &= 7
\end{align*}
Check: $3.50 + 2.25 \cdot 6 = 3.50 + 13.50 = 17.00$ dollars. The trip was
7 miles. \ans{m = 7 \text{ mi}}}

\problem{\textbf{Pool: \$8.00 for the first 3 hours, $k$ dollars per additional
hour; a 7-hour visit costs \$22.00. Find $k$.}
\par\smallskip
\[
C(t) = \begin{cases} 8.00, & 0 < t \le 3 \\[2pt] 8.00 + k(t - 3), & t > 3 \end{cases}
\]
A 7-hour visit is charged for $7 - 3 = 4$ extra hours:
\begin{align*}
8.00 + k(7 - 3) &= 22.00 \\
4k &= 14.00 \\
k &= 3.50
\end{align*}
Each hour past the first 3 costs \$3.50, so the rule is
$C(t) = 8.00 + 3.50(t - 3)$ for $t > 3$. \ans{k = 3.50}}

\problem{\textbf{Phone plan: \$30.00 base with 5 gigabytes included, \$12.00 per
gigabyte above 5, bill capped at \$90.00. Find $C(9)$.}
\par\smallskip
Three regions, so three branches --- the cap is a branch of its own:
\[
C(g) = \begin{cases}
30.00, & 0 \le g \le 5 \\[2pt]
30.00 + 12.00(g - 5), & 5 < g \le 10 \\[2pt]
90.00, & g > 10
\end{cases}
\]
At $g = 9$ the middle branch applies, and $9 - 5 = 4$ gigabytes are charged:
$C(9) = 30.00 + 12.00 \cdot 4 = 30.00 + 48.00$ dollars, which is under the
\$90.00 cap, so the cap does not bind. \ans{C(9) = 78.00}}

\problem{\textbf{Same plan: find the usage at which the middle branch first
reaches the \$90.00 cap.}
\par\smallskip
The cap takes over exactly where the middle branch's value hits \$90.00:
\begin{align*}
30.00 + 12.00(g - 5) &= 90.00 \\
12.00(g - 5) &= 60.00 \\
g - 5 &= 5 \\
g &= 10
\end{align*}
So the middle branch runs on $5 < g \le 10$ and the flat \$90.00 branch takes
over for $g > 10$. \ans{g = 10 \text{ GB}}}

\problem{\textbf{Explain why the middle branch and the capped branch must agree
where they meet.} \emph{(Open response --- flagged for manual review.)}
\par\smallskip
\textbf{Model answer:} At $g = 10$ the middle branch gives
$30.00 + 12.00(10 - 5) = 90.00$ dollars and the capped branch gives \$90.00.
They agree, so the graph of $C$ is one connected path with no vertical break.
If they disagreed --- say the cap were \$85.00 instead --- then a customer using
exactly 10 gigabytes would pay \$90.00 while a customer using 10.1 gigabytes
would pay \$85.00. Using \emph{more} data would lower the bill, so a customer
could save money by streaming a video they did not want, and two customers whose
usage differs by a rounding error would see bills \$5.00 apart. A fee schedule
is only fair if cost never jumps down as usage goes up, which is exactly the
requirement that the branches match at the breakpoint.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} full credit for any answer that (a) evaluates both branches
at the breakpoint and observes they agree, and (b) describes the jump a customer
would experience otherwise. Naming it ``continuity'' is welcome but not
required at this level.}

\problem{\textbf{Garage A (\$4.00 up to 2 hours, then \$3.00 per hour) versus
Garage B (\$2.50 per hour). Find the stay longer than 2 hours on which they
cost the same.}
\par\smallskip
For $t > 2$, Garage A costs $4.00 + 3.00(t - 2)$ and Garage B costs $2.50t$.
Let $y$ be the shared cost in dollars:
\[
\begin{cases} y = 4.00 + 3.00(t - 2) \\[2pt] y = 2.50t \end{cases}
\]
Substituting the second equation into the first:
\begin{align*}
2.50t &= 4.00 + 3.00(t - 2) \\
2.50t &= 3.00t - 2.00 \\
2.00 &= 0.50t \\
t &= 4
\end{align*}
Then $y = 2.50 \cdot 4 = 10.00$ dollars, and the check on Garage A gives
$4.00 + 3.00(4 - 2) = 10.00$ dollars as well. The answer $t = 4$ lies in the
$t > 2$ region the system was written for, so it is a legitimate solution.
\ans{t = 4, \; y = 10.00}}

\problem{\textbf{Print shop: \$25.00 setup plus \$0.40 per page for the first
200 pages, then $r$ dollars per page beyond 200; a 500-page job costs \$153.00.
Find $r$.}
\par\smallskip
\[
C(p) = \begin{cases}
25.00 + 0.40p, & 0 \le p \le 200 \\[2pt]
25.00 + 0.40(200) + r(p - 200), & p > 200
\end{cases}
\]
The first 200 pages always cost $25.00 + 0.40 \times 200 = 105.00$ dollars, and
a 500-page job has $500 - 200 = 300$ pages at the reduced rate:
\begin{align*}
105.00 + r(500 - 200) &= 153.00 \\
300r &= 48.00 \\
r &= 0.16
\end{align*}
So the reduced rate is \$0.16 per page and the rule is
$C(p) = 105.00 + 0.16(p - 200)$ for $p > 200$. \ans{r = 0.16}}
