\wstitleblock{Choosing Linear, Quadratic, or Piecewise Models}{Algebra 1 --- Modeling and Functions}{}

\noindent\small
Evidence that a situation is linear, evidence that it is quadratic, evidence
that it is piecewise, and comparing two models against each other. Before you
compute, decide which kind of model the evidence supports:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Linear} --- equal steps in the input produce \emph{equal} steps
        in the output (constant first differences), or the story names one
        fixed rate that never changes.
  \item \textbf{Quadratic} --- the first differences change but change by the
        same amount each time (constant second differences), or the story
        involves a product of two quantities that both depend on the input
        (area, revenue, projectile height).
  \item \textbf{Piecewise} --- the rule itself changes at a stated boundary:
        ``first 2 hours\ldots{} after that\ldots''. One formula cannot cover
        both sides.
  \item Say what your evidence is, then build the model, then answer.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\problem[7cm]{A tutoring service posts this price table.
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Hours } $h$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\ \hline
\textbf{Total cost (dollars)} & $25$ & $40$ & $55$ & $70$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\par
The first differences are equal, so a linear model fits. Write the model, then
use it to find the total cost of $6$ hours of tutoring, in dollars.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Using the same tutoring model from problem 1, how many hours
were bought if the total cost was $175$ dollars?}

\problem{A ball is launched straight up. Its height above the ground is
recorded each second.
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Time } $t$ \textbf{ (s)} & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\ \hline
\textbf{Height (ft)} & $0$ & $48$ & $64$ & $48$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\par
The second differences are constant, and the model that fits is
$h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t$. Find the height of the ball at $t = 3.5$ seconds.
\par\vspace*{7.5cm}\answerline{ft}}

\problem[6cm]{Using the same height model $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t$ from problem 3,
find every time at which the ball is at ground level. Give both values of $t$,
in seconds.}

\problem[5.5cm]{A parking garage charges $4$ dollars for the first $2$ hours
and $3$ dollars for each additional hour after that.
\par\smallskip
Find the cost, in dollars, of parking for $5$ hours.}

\problem{A pool is being filled. The depth is measured each minute.
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Minutes } $x$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ \\ \hline
\textbf{Depth (cm)} & $7$ & $12$ & $17$ & $22$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\par
(a) Decide which kind of model fits, write it, and predict the depth at
$x = 10$ minutes.
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}
\hfill (a) \underline{\hspace{2.6cm}}
\par\smallskip
(b) Explain what feature of the table shows the relationship is linear rather
than quadratic.
\par\vspace*{3.5cm}\noansline}

\problem[6cm]{Return to the parking garage from problem 5: $4$ dollars for the
first $2$ hours, then $3$ dollars per additional hour.
\par\smallskip
A driver paid $22$ dollars. How many hours did the car stay in the garage?}

\problem[7cm]{A shop finds that when it sets the price of a hoodie at $p$
dollars, it sells $60 - 2p$ hoodies. Its revenue is therefore
$R(p) = p(60 - 2p)$.
\par\smallskip
Find both prices at which the revenue is $400$ dollars.}

\problem[7cm]{Two gyms both charge a fixed joining fee plus a fixed monthly
rate --- so each is a linear model.
\par\smallskip
Gym A: $20$ dollars to join, then $8$ dollars a month. \quad
Gym B: $44$ dollars to join, then $5$ dollars a month.
\par\smallskip
After how many months $x$ do the two plans cost the same, and what is that cost
$y$?}

\problem[9cm]{A quadratic model $y = x^2 - 4x + 7$ and a linear model
$y = 2x + 2$ describe the same quantity in two different studies.
\par\smallskip
Find every point $(x, y)$ where the two models agree.}

\problem[9cm]{A population is counted each year.
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Year } $t$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\ \hline
\textbf{Population } $P$ & $5$ & $9$ & $17$ & $29$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\par
Rae looks at the first step, sees an increase of $4$, treats the growth as a
constant rate and predicts $P = 29$ at year $6$.
\par\smallskip
Explain what Rae's evidence actually shows about the model, then use the
correct model $P(t) = 2t^2 + 2t + 5$ to find the population at year $6$.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis challenge.} A rideshare charges a $3$ dollar

$1.25$ dollars per mile for every mile after that.
\par\smallskip
(a) Find the cost of a $10$ mile ride, in dollars.
\par\vspace*{3.5cm}
\hfill (a) \underline{\hspace{2.6cm}}
\par\smallskip
(b) A ride costs $27.50$ dollars. How many miles was it?
\par\vspace*{3.5cm}
\hfill (b) \underline{\hspace{2.6cm}}
\par\smallskip
(c) Explain why no single linear function can describe this fare for every
distance, and describe what the graph of the fare looks like.
