\problem{Solve, and list every solution: $x^2 = 49$}

\textbf{Solution:} Taking the square root of both sides produces two values, because $(-7)^2$ and $7^2$ both equal $49$.
\[ x^2 = 49 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = \pm\sqrt{49} \]
\[ \boxed{x = -7 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 7} \]

\problem{Dana solves $2x^2 - 32 = 0$ like this: ``$2x^2 = 32$, so $x^2 = 16$, so $x = 4$.'' (a) Name the mistake. (b) Write the complete solution set.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} Every line of Dana's arithmetic is right; the error is in the \emph{last step}. Taking a square root of both sides yields two values, $+4$ and $-4$, and Dana kept only the positive one, so the solution set is incomplete. A response that says ``the arithmetic is wrong'' has not identified the error --- $4$ genuinely is a solution.

\textbf{Solution (b):}
\begin{align*}
  2x^2 - 32 &= 0 \\
  x^2 &= 16 \\
  x &= \pm 4
\end{align*}
Check: $2(-4)^2 - 32 = 32 - 32 = 0$, so $-4$ really is a solution.
\[ \boxed{x = -4 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4} \]

\problem{Solve, and list every solution: $x^2 - 5x = 0$}

\textbf{Solution:} There is a common factor of $x$. Factor it out and use the zero-product property --- do \emph{not} divide by $x$, because $x$ may be zero.
\begin{align*}
  x^2 - 5x &= 0 \\
  x(x-5) &= 0 \\
  x = 0 \quad &\text{or} \quad x - 5 = 0
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 5} \]

\problem{Marco solves $x^2 = 6x$ by dividing both sides by $x$, giving $x = 6$. (a) Name the error and say why it costs him a solution. (b) Write the complete solution set.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} Dividing both sides by $x$ is only legal when $x \neq 0$. Here $x = 0$ satisfies the original equation ($0^2 = 6 \cdot 0$), so Marco's division deleted a genuine solution. Full credit requires naming the lost root $x = 0$, not just saying ``you cannot divide by a variable''.

\textbf{Solution (b):} Move everything to one side and factor instead of dividing.
\begin{align*}
  x^2 &= 6x \\
  x^2 - 6x &= 0 \\
  x(x-6) &= 0
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 6} \]

\problem{Solve by factoring, and list every solution: $x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$}

\textbf{Solution:} Look for two numbers with product $-15$ and sum $+2$. Because the product is negative the numbers have opposite signs, and the larger of the two must carry the sign of the sum: $+5$ and $-3$.
\begin{align*}
  x^2 + 2x - 15 &= 0 \\
  (x+5)(x-3) &= 0
\end{align*}
Getting $(x+3)(x-5)$ instead reverses both signs and gives $x = -3$ or $x = 5$, which fails the check: $(-3)^2 + 2(-3) - 15 = -12 \neq 0$.
\[ \boxed{x = -5 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3} \]

\problem{Solve with the quadratic formula, and list every solution: $x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$}

\textbf{Solution:} Here $a = 1$, $b = -6$, $c = 5$. The formula opens with $-b$, and $-b = -(-6) = +6$ --- writing $-6$ there is the sign error this problem is built around.
\[ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36-20}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm 4}{2} \]
\[ \boxed{x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 5} \]

\problem{Use the quadratic formula on $2x^2 - 7x + 2 = 0$. Give the larger solution, rounded to two decimal places.}

\textbf{Solution:} $a = 2$, $b = -7$, $c = 2$, so $-b = +7$ and the denominator is $2a = 4$.
\[ x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{(-7)^2 - 4(2)(2)}}{2(2)} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{33}}{4} \]
The larger root takes the plus sign: $(7 + 5.7446)/4 = 3.1861\ldots$, which rounds to \ans{3.19}

\problem{Nadia solves $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$ with the quadratic formula, writes the denominator as $2$ instead of $2a$, and reports $x = 1$ and $x = -6$. (a) Name the error. (b) Write the correct solution set. (c) Explain how substituting one of her answers would have shown her that something was wrong.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The denominator of the quadratic formula is $2a$, not $2$. With $a = 2$ it should be $4$, so every value Nadia produced is twice as far from zero as it should be. Full credit requires naming the denominator specifically.

\textbf{Solution (b):} $a = 2$, $b = 5$, $c = -3$.
\[ x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 + 24}}{4} = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{4} \]
\[ \boxed{x = -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = \tfrac{1}{2}} \]

\textbf{(c) --- instructor-judged.} A full answer describes substitution and carries it out on at least one of Nadia's values. Model response: put $x = 1$ into the original equation --- $2(1)^2 + 5(1) - 3 = 4$, not $0$, so $x = 1$ cannot be a solution and the work must be re-checked. A general remark about ``checking your answers'', with no substitution shown, is not full credit.
