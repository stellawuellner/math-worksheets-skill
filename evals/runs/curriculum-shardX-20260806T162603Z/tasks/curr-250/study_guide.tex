\sstitleblock{Making and Qualifying Predictions from Scatter Plots}

% =================== SKILL 1 ===================
\skillheading{Skill 1 --- Reading a scatter plot}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Each dot is one individual}, placed by its two measurements: across for the $x$ variable,
up for the $y$ variable. To read a dot, drop straight down to the $x$-axis and straight across to
the $y$-axis. The \textbf{direction} of the cloud is what a prediction will follow: rising to the
right is a positive association, falling is negative, no tilt means no association.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A plot shows practice hours against free throws made for six players, with
points at $(1, 6)$, $(2, 8)$, $(3, 9)$, $(4, 13)$, $(5, 14)$ and $(6, 18)$. How many free throws did
the player who practised $4$ hours make?
\step{The question asks for one player's actual result, not a prediction, so I read the dot itself rather than any line drawn through the cloud.}
\step{Go across to $4$ on the practice axis, up to the dot, then across to the free-throw axis.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{13}$ free throws. The cloud rises to the right, so the association is positive.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad From the same plot, how many free throws did the player who practised $2$
hours make?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{8}$}
\end{tryitbox}

% =================== SKILL 2 ===================
\skillheading{Skill 2 --- Predicting with the trend line}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{The trend line summarises the cloud} with one straight line, written $y = mx + b$. To
predict, substitute the $x$ you were given and work out $y$. The answer is a \emph{typical} value
for that $x$, not a guarantee: real points sit above and below the line, and how far they sit from
it is how far your prediction may be out.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad The trend line for that plot is $y = 2.5x + 3.5$. Predict the free throws
made after $5$ hours of practice.
\step{A typical value is wanted rather than one player's result, so this is a job for the line and not for the dots.}
\step{Substitute $x = 5$: \ $2.5 \times 5 = 12.5$, and $12.5 + 3.5 = 16$.}
\step{The player who did practise $5$ hours actually made $14$, so a prediction here is good to about two throws.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{16}$ free throws
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Use $y = 2.5x + 3.5$ to predict the free throws made after $2$ hours of
practice.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{8.5}$}
\end{tryitbox}

% =================== SKILL 3 ===================
\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Deciding whether to trust a prediction}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Three questions to ask of every number the line produces:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.75cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Is the $x$ \emph{inside} the range that was measured? Outside it you are extrapolating, and
        nothing in the data says the pattern continues.
  \item Is the answer \emph{possible}? A score above the maximum mark, or a negative time, means the
        model has been pushed past where it applies.
  \item Is there really a pattern? A cloud with no tilt supports no prediction at all --- and even a
        strong pattern shows association, never cause.
\end{itemize}
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Use $y = 2.5x + 3.5$ to predict the free throws made after $40$ hours of
practice, then judge the answer.
\step{The line will happily return a number for any input, so before trusting it I check the input against the range of practice hours that were actually measured, namely 1 to 6.}
\step{Substitute $x = 40$: \ $2.5 \times 40 = 100$, and $100 + 3.5 = 103.5$.}
\step{That $x$ is far outside the measured range, and no player attempts more than a few dozen throws, so the number is arithmetic, not evidence.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{103.5}$ --- calculated correctly, and not a prediction anyone should use.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad What does $y = 2.5x + 3.5$ give for $30$ hours of practice, and would you
trust it?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{78.5}$ --- far outside the measured range}
\end{tryitbox}
