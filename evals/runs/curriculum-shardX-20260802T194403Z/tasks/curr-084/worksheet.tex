\wstitleblock{Fraction Models: Are the Parts Equal?}{Grade 2--3 Mathematics}{}

\noindent\textbf{Remember:} a fraction only names the parts of a whole when
\emph{every part is the same size}. Cutting a shape into four pieces does not
make one piece a fourth --- the four pieces must all be the same size first.

\smallskip
\noindent Each picture below is somebody's model. Some of them are wrong.
Show your thinking in the work space, then write your final answer on the
answer line.

\problem[4.5cm]{Look at this model. All of its parts are the same size.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}
  \fill[gray!35] (0,0) rectangle (2,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (0,0) rectangle (8,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (2,0) -- (2,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (4,0) -- (4,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (6,0) -- (6,1.2);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\noindent Are all of the parts the same size? Write the fraction of the whole
rectangle that is shaded.}

\noindent Ben says the shaded piece is $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of the strip, because it

\problem[4.5cm]{These two paper strips are exactly the same length. Strip A is
folded into equal parts, and so is Strip B. One part of each strip is shaded.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}
  \node[left] at (-0.15,2.2) {Strip A};
  \fill[gray!35] (0,1.6) rectangle (2,2.8);
  \draw[thick] (0,1.6) rectangle (8,2.8);
  \draw[thick] (2,1.6) -- (2,2.8);
  \draw[thick] (4,1.6) -- (4,2.8);
  \draw[thick] (6,1.6) -- (6,2.8);
  \node[left] at (-0.15,0.6) {Strip B};
  \fill[gray!35] (0,0) rectangle (1.3333,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (0,0) rectangle (8,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (1.3333,0) -- (1.3333,1.2);
  \draw[thick] (2.6667,0) -- (2.6667,1.2);

\noindent The shaded part of Strip A is $\tfrac{1}{4}$ and the shaded part of
