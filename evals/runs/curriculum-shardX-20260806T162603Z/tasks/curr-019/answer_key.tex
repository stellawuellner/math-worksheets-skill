\problem{Look at the number bond. The whole is 8. One part is 3. Write the
missing part. \ $3\;+\;\underline{\hspace{1cm}}\;=\;8$}

\textbf{Solution:} The two parts must add up to the whole, 8. One part is 3,
so we count on from 3 up to 8: \ 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 --- that is 5 counts.
Check: $3 + 5 = 8$. The missing part is \ans{5}

\problem{This bond is not finished. The whole is 10. One part is 7. Write the
missing part. \ $7\;+\;\underline{\hspace{1cm}}\;=\;10$}

\textbf{Solution:} The parts must make the whole, 10. Count on from 7 up to
10: \ 8, 9, 10 --- that is 3 counts. Check: $7 + 3 = 10$. The missing part
is \ans{3}

\problem{The bond shows the whole 9 with parts 4 and 5. Two facts of its
family are done: $4+5=9$ and $9-4=5$. Write the two missing facts.}

\textbf{Solution:} A bond family uses the same three numbers in every fact.
The two addition facts swap the parts, and the two subtraction facts take
each part away from the whole.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4cm, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Addition with the parts swapped: $5 + 4$ is the same total as
        $4 + 5$, so $5 + 4 = \ans{9}$
  \item Taking the part 5 from the whole: $9 - 5$ leaves the other part,
        so $9 - 5 = \ans{4}$
\end{itemize}

\problem{Ana made this bond: the whole is 10, and she wrote the parts 6 and 5.
Check her bond! Cross out the part that is wrong, and write the part that
really goes with 6 to make 10. Then tell how you know.}

\textbf{Solution:} Check Ana's parts: $6 + 5 = 11$, and 11 is too big --- the
whole is 10. So the part 5 is wrong and gets crossed out. Count on from 6 to
10: \ 7, 8, 9, 10 --- that is 4 counts, and $6 + 4 = 10$. The correct part
is \ans{4}

\emph{Tell how you know (instructor judges):} a good answer says that
$6+5$ makes 11, not 10, so 5 cannot be a part; the part that goes with 6 must
be 4 because $6+4=10$.

\problem{Sam wrote the fact family for the bond 10 with parts 3 and 7:
$3+7=10$, $7+3=10$, $10-3=7$, $10-3=7$. One fact is written two times, so one
fact is missing! Write the missing fact, and tell why Sam's list is not a
full family.}

\textbf{Solution:} Sam copied $10-3=7$ twice. The family still needs the fact
that takes the \emph{other} part, 7, away from the whole:
$10 - 7$ leaves the part 3, so $10 - 7 = \ans{3}$

\emph{Tell why (instructor judges):} a good answer says a full family for one
bond has four \emph{different} facts --- two additions and two subtractions ---
and Sam's list shows the same subtraction two times, so the family is missing
$10-7=3$.

\problem{Challenge! Ben's bond shows the whole 10 with parts 8 and 2. He wrote
only two facts: $8+2=10$ and $10-8=2$. Finish his family, and then tell why a
bond with two different parts always has four facts.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ben has one addition and one subtraction. The family still
needs the addition with the parts swapped and the subtraction of the other
part.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4cm, itemsep=2pt]
  \item Parts swapped: $2 + 8$ makes the same whole, so $2 + 8 = \ans{10}$
  \item Take away the part 2: $10 - 2$ leaves the other part, so
        $10 - 2 = \ans{8}$
\end{itemize}

\emph{Tell why (instructor judges):} a good answer says the two parts can be
added in either order (two addition facts), and each of the two parts can be
taken from the whole (two subtraction facts) --- with two different parts
that makes four different facts.
