\wstitleblock{Comparing Distributions and Compound Events}{Grade 6--7 Statistics and Probability}{}

\noindent\small
Comparing the centers of two distributions, comparing their spreads, finding
the probability of a single event, and finding the probability of a compound
event. Read the question before you reach for a formula:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Center or spread?} Mean and median describe where a
        distribution sits. Range and IQR describe how spread out it is. Two
        sets can share a center and still be very different.
  \item \textbf{One event or two?} A single draw is a simple probability. Two
        things happening together is a compound event --- multiply the stage
        probabilities, and check whether the first stage changes the second.
  \item Leave every probability as a fraction in lowest terms.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\problem[4.5cm]{One marble is drawn at random from the bag. Find the

\problem[7cm]{Find the interquartile range (IQR) of Team Bravo's scores.
\par\smallskip
Write $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ before you subtract --- with 8 values, each half of the
data has 4 values in it.}

\problem[6cm]{The spinner is spun once and the number cube is rolled once.
Find the probability that the spinner lands on red \emph{and} the cube shows a
$5$.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Find the mean of Team Bravo's scores. Show the total you
divided.}

\problem[7cm]{Two marbles are drawn from the bag, one after the other, and the
first marble is \emph{not} put back. Find the probability that both marbles are
red.
\par\smallskip
The second draw comes from a smaller bag than the first --- say what is left
before you multiply.}

\problem[7cm]{Use the club sign-up graph.
\par\smallskip
(a) How many students signed up for a club altogether?
\par\smallskip
(a) \underline{\hspace{2.4cm}}
\par\smallskip
(b) One sign-up slip is chosen at random from all of them. Find the probability

\problem[8cm]{The spinner is spun \emph{twice}. Find the probability that at
least one of the two spins lands on red.
\par\smallskip
``At least one'' is easier through its opposite: first find the probability
that \emph{neither} spin is red.}
