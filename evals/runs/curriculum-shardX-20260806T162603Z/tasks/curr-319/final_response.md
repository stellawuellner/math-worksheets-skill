Here are the three PDFs for the similarity error-analysis set:

- **Worksheet** — `ws_similarity_curr319.pdf` (4 pages)
- **Answer key** — `ak_similarity_curr319.pdf` (3 pages)
- **Study guide** — `ss_similarity_curr319.pdf` (2 pages)

**What the worksheet covers.** Six "find the mistake" problems, all on the two
errors that account for most lost marks in similarity work: matching the wrong
vertices (reversed correspondence) and applying the scale factor the wrong way
round. Each problem prints a piece of work whose arithmetic is correct but whose
setup is not, and asks the student to (a) redo it correctly and (b) name the
error. Contexts are concrete — a poster-shop reduction, a scaled triangle pair,
an angle chased through a similarity statement.

The set ramps from a single mispaired ratio (problems 1–2) through a reversed
scale factor and a mismatched third angle (3–4) to a reduction where the
direction of the factor is the whole point (5) and an algebraic proportion where
the inversion inflates the unknown (6).

**What is verified, honestly.** The verification file holds 13 entries — one per
response, because a "find and fix" item is two answers, not one.

- **7 entries are machine-checked** with SymPy: every corrected value (12 cm,
  6 in, the scale factor 1.5 and 30 m, 65°, 10.5 cm, x = 4).
- **6 entries are instructor-judged**: the "name the error" half of every
  problem. Written reasoning cannot be checked by computer algebra, so each
  carries a grading rubric printed in the answer key beside the worked solution.

Because every problem pairs a computed answer with a written diagnosis, **no
problem is fully machine-checked end to end** — the key's "What is verified"
note says exactly that, and the Quick Answers bank shows `---` in the (b) slot of
all six. Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`: every printed answer blank is either
verified or explicitly flagged for you to read.

Each problem also has its wrong answer declared as a misconception trap, so the
key's "Common wrong answers" block tells you which specific error produced
13.5 cm, 37.5 in, 13.33 m, 75°, 168 cm or x = 14 if a student reproduces it.

**Study guide.** Three sections: reading the correspondence, using the scale
factor in the right direction, and a diagnostic section with the two checks that
catch almost every setup mistake (same order twice; does the answer move the way
the scaling should move it). Each has a rule box, a worked example that opens
with the reasoning in words, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down.
