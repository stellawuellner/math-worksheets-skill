\wstitleblock{Identifying Conics from Their Equations}{Algebra 2}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Naming a conic from its equation, then reading off a circle's
center and radius, a parabola's vertex and axis, and an ellipse's vertices}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{How to use this sheet.}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every conic can be written in the \emph{general form}
        $Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$. On this sheet $B = 0$ every time, so the
        curve's axes line up with the grid.
  \item The \emph{indicator} $B^2 - 4AC$ decides the family before you do any other work.
        Read the model below, then use it on every naming problem.
  \item Once you know the family, the standard form gives you the key features. Show the
        completing-the-square work --- the answer blank is for the finished feature.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{Circle: find the center and the radius.} \quad
$x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0$

\par\smallskip
This is problem 1's equation again, now that you know it is a circle. Group the $x$ terms
and the $y$ terms, complete both squares, and move the constant across:
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}

$h = -\dfrac{D}{2}$, \quad $k = -\dfrac{E}{2}$, \quad $r = \sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + 12}$
\end{center}

\noindent $h =$~\ansblank \qquad $k =$~\ansblank \qquad $r =$~\ansblank

\noindent Standard form: \underline{\hspace{6cm}}

\problem{\textbf{A nonlinear model.} A ball is thrown from the ground and its path over
level ground follows $y = -x^2 + 6x$, where $x$ is the horizontal distance in metres and
$y$ is the height in metres.

\par\smallskip
The highest point of any parabola sits on its axis of symmetry, and here that axis is the
line $x = 3$. So the highest point is the single solution of the system
\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
$y = -x^2 + 6x$ \qquad and \qquad $x = 3$.
\end{center}
\par\smallskip
Substitute and solve. Then say in one sentence what each coordinate of the answer means
for the ball.
\par\smallskip
\noindent Highest point $=($~\ansblank$,$~\ansblank$)$
\par\vspace*{4cm}}

\problem{\textbf{Explain the difference.} Mia looks at
$4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36$ and $4x^2 - 9y^2 = 36$ and says: ``Both have an $x$ square and a $y$
square, so both are ellipses.''

\par\smallskip
She is right about one of them. Compute the indicator for each equation, then write two or
three sentences explaining what in the equations makes them different curves --- and what
Mia was looking at instead.
