\problem{Area model for width $x$, length $x + 5$.

\textbf{Solution:} The two cells are $x \cdot x = x^2$ and $x \cdot 5 = 5x$,
so the area is the sum of those two cells:
$\;$ $\ans{x^2 + 5x}$

\textbf{Reading the answer:} two terms. The coefficient of $x$ is $5$; the
coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$ (an invisible $1$ is still a coefficient). Degree
$2$, because the largest exponent among the terms is $2$.}

\problem{Model for $(x + 3)(x + 5)$.

\textbf{Solution:} cells $x^2$, $5x$, $3x$, $15$. The two middle cells are
like terms ($5x + 3x = 8x$), so four cells collapse to three terms:
$\;$ $\ans{x^2 + 8x + 15}$

\textbf{Why fewer terms:} $5x$ and $3x$ have the same variable to the same
power, so they combine. Cells count multiplications; terms count what is left
after combining like terms.}

\problem{Terms out of order: $-9$, $6x$, $4x^3$, $-x^2$.

\textbf{Solution:} coefficient and degree of each term ---
$-9$: coefficient $-9$, degree $0$ (a constant is $-9x^0$);
$6x$: coefficient $6$, degree $1$;
$4x^3$: coefficient $4$, degree $3$;
$-x^2$: coefficient $-1$, degree $2$.
Standard form orders the terms by decreasing degree:
$\;$ $\ans{4x^3 - x^2 + 6x - 9}$

The polynomial has degree $3$ (the largest term degree) and leading
coefficient $4$ (the coefficient of the highest-degree term, not the number
written first in the original list).}

\problem{Factor $x^2 + 7x + 12$.

\textbf{Solution:} find two numbers with product $12$ and sum $7$: $3$ and
$4$.
$\;$ $\ans{(x + 3)(x + 4)}$

\textbf{Degrees:} each factor has degree $1$, and the product has degree
$1 + 1 = 2$ --- multiplying polynomials adds their degrees.}

\problem{$3(2x + 5)$ --- sum of terms, or product of factors?

\textbf{Solution:} \textbf{Ann} is describing it as written: $3(2x + 5)$ is a
single term built as a product of the factors $3$ and $(2x + 5)$. Distributing
rewrites it as a sum:
\[ 3(2x + 5) = 3 \cdot 2x + 3 \cdot 5 = 6x + 15 \]
$\;$ $\ans{6x + 15}$

Factored form: factors $3$ and $(2x + 5)$; one term. Expanded form: terms $6x$
and $15$; no common factor written out. Jo is right about the expanded form
only.}

\problem{Add $(3x^2 + 5x - 1) + (-3x^2 + 2x + 4)$.

\textbf{Solution:} combine like terms by degree:
$3x^2 + (-3x^2) = 0$, \; $5x + 2x = 7x$, \; $-1 + 4 = 3$.
$\;$ $\ans{7x + 3}$

\textbf{What happened to the degree:} the two degree-$2$ terms are opposites,
so the $x^2$ term has coefficient $0$ and disappears. The sum has degree $1$.
Adding polynomials can lower the degree; it can never raise it.}

\problem{Model for $(2x - 3)(x + 4)$.

\textbf{Solution:} cells $2x \cdot x = 2x^2$, \; $2x \cdot 4 = 8x$, \;
$-3 \cdot x = -3x$, \; $-3 \cdot 4 = -12$. Combine $8x + (-3x) = 5x$:
$\;$ $\ans{2x^2 + 5x - 12}$

The circled cell is the bottom-right one, $-3 \cdot 4 = -12$: the constant
term always comes from multiplying the two constant parts. The coefficient of
$x$ is $5$.}

\problem{Factor $6x^3 - 15x^2$ completely.

\textbf{Solution:} the greatest common factor of $6x^3$ and $15x^2$ is
$3x^2$ ($3$ divides $6$ and $15$; $x^2$ is the highest power in both):
\[ 6x^3 - 15x^2 = 3x^2(2x - 5) \]
$\;$ $\ans{3x^2(2x - 5)}$

\textbf{Factors:} $3$ (degree $0$), $x^2$ (degree $2$), and $(2x - 5)$
(degree $1$). Degrees add: $0 + 2 + 1 = 3$, matching the degree of the
original polynomial.}

\problem{Multiply $(2x^3 - x)(x^2 + 4)$.

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
 (2x^3 - x)(x^2 + 4) &= 2x^3 \cdot x^2 + 2x^3 \cdot 4 - x \cdot x^2 - x \cdot 4\\
 &= 2x^5 + 8x^3 - x^3 - 4x
\end{align*}
Combine the like $x^3$ terms: $8x^3 - x^3 = 7x^3$.
$\;$ $\ans{2x^5 + 7x^3 - 4x}$

\textbf{Degree and leading coefficient:} degree $5$, leading coefficient $2$.
Predictable without multiplying everything: the highest-degree term of the
product is the product of the two highest-degree terms,
$2x^3 \cdot x^2 = 2x^5$, so the degree is $3 + 2 = 5$ and the leading
coefficient is $2 \cdot 1 = 2$.}

\problem{Is $x^2 + 8x + 12$ equivalent to $(x + 2)(x + 6)$?

\textbf{Solution:} expand the product --- two numbers with product $12$ and
sum $8$ are $2$ and $6$:
\[ (x + 2)(x + 6) = x^2 + 6x + 2x + 12 = x^2 + 8x + 12 \]
$\;$ $\ans{x^2 + 8x + 12}$ \quad so the two expressions ARE equivalent.

\textbf{Explanation (accept any equivalent reasoning; read the student's
words).} ``Sum of three terms'' and ``product of two factors'' describe two
\emph{forms} of writing, not two different expressions. The same number comes
out for every value of $x$, so it is one expression with two useful forms:
the factored form shows the zeros ($x = -2$ and $x = -6$), the expanded form
shows the degree, the leading coefficient, and the constant term. Maya is
confusing how an expression is written with what it is.}
