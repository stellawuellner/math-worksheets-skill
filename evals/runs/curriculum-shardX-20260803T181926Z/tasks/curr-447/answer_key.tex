\problem{National test, $\mu = 500$, $\sigma = 100$. Find the $z$-score of $640$ and interpret it.}

\textbf{Solution.} Standardising, $z = \dfrac{640 - 500}{100} = \dfrac{140}{100}$. The score sits $1.4$ standard deviations ABOVE the mean. That single number is what makes scores from different scales comparable --- it says nothing about the raw units.

\ans{z = 1.4}

\problem{Same test. Find the $z$-score of $430$.}

\textbf{Solution.} $z = \dfrac{430 - 500}{100} = \dfrac{-70}{100}$, so the score is $0.7$ standard deviations BELOW the mean. The sign carries the direction; a student who computes $\dfrac{\mu - x}{\sigma}$ gets $+0.7$ and reports the score as above average, which is the misconception this problem is built to catch.

\ans{z = -0.7}

\problem{What proportion of a normal distribution lies below $z = 1.00$, and what percentile is that?}

\textbf{Solution.} Read the column headed $1.00$: the area to the left is $0.8413$. Since $84.13\%$ of the distribution lies below that point, the value sits at roughly the $84$th percentile. ``Percentile'' is the same information as ``area to the left'', expressed as a percentage.

\ans{0.8413}

\problem{What proportion lies below $z = 1.50$, and what proportion lies above it?}

\textbf{Solution.} The table gives the area to the LEFT of $1.50$ as $0.9332$. The whole distribution has area $1$, so the area to the right is $1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668$. Reporting $0.9332$ as the answer to the second half is the standard error: the table is always a left-tail area, never a right-tail one.

\ans{0.9332 \text{ below},\ 0.0668 \text{ above}}

\problem{Heights $\mu = 170$ cm, $\sigma = 8$ cm. Find $z$ for $182$ cm and the proportion shorter than that.}

\textbf{Solution.} $z = \dfrac{182 - 170}{8} = \dfrac{12}{8} = 1.5$. Because the table gives left-tail areas and ``shorter than'' is a left-tail question, read the entry directly: the proportion of adults shorter than $182$ cm is $0.9332$, about $93\%$.

\ans{z = 1.5,\ 0.9332}

\problem{Proportion of heights between $162$ cm and $182$ cm.}

\textbf{Solution.} Standardise both endpoints: $z_{1} = \dfrac{162 - 170}{8} = -1.00$ and $z_{2} = \dfrac{182 - 170}{8} = 1.50$. The area BETWEEN two values is the difference of their left-tail areas, larger minus smaller: $0.9332 - 0.1587$. So about $77.45\%$ of adults fall in that interval. Adding the two entries instead of subtracting is the usual slip --- a sum bigger than $1$ is the tell.

\ans{0.9332 - 0.1587 = 0.7745}

\problem{Which height is at the $90$th percentile? ($\mu = 170$ cm, $\sigma = 8$ cm.)}

\textbf{Solution.} The $90$th percentile is the value with area $0.90$ to its left. The closest table entry is $0.8997$, at $z = 1.28$. Now reverse the standardising with $x = \mu + z\sigma$:
\[
x = 170 + 1.28(8) = 170 + 10.24 .
\]
The $90$th-percentile height is about $180.24$ cm. Adding $1.28$ straight onto the mean --- forgetting to multiply by $\sigma$ --- gives $171.28$ cm and is the trap built into this problem.

\ans{0.8997 \to z = 1.28,\ x = 180.24}

\problem{Scholarship for the top $2.28\%$ on the national test. Find the cut-off score.}

\textbf{Solution.} Top $2.28\%$ means area $0.0228$ to the RIGHT, so area $1 - 0.0228 = 0.9772$ to the left. The table gives that area at $z = 2.00$. Then
\[
x = 500 + 2(100) = 700 .
\]
A candidate needs at least $700$. Note the first move: convert the right-tail statement into a left-tail area, because that is the only thing the table reports.

\ans{0.9772 \to z = 2,\ x = 700}

\problem{Ana: $640$ on the test ($\mu=500$, $\sigma=100$) and $28$ on an exam with $\mu=22$, $\sigma=4$. Which is relatively stronger?}

\textbf{Solution.} First result: $z = \dfrac{640 - 500}{100} = 1.4$. Second result: $z = \dfrac{28 - 22}{4} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$. Comparing, $1.4 < 1.5$, so the SECOND result is the relatively stronger one: it sits further above its own mean measured in that distribution's own standard deviations. Comparing the raw scores $640$ and $28$ is meaningless --- they are on different scales, which is exactly why standardising exists.

\ans{1.4 < 1.5}

\problem{About $228$ of every $10{,}000$ candidates score above $700$. Probability a random candidate did?}

\textbf{Solution.} The probability is the relative frequency $\dfrac{228}{10000}$. Dividing numerator and denominator by $4$ gives $\dfrac{57}{2500}$, which is $0.0228$ --- the same right-tail area found in problem 8, as it must be, since $700$ is the $z = 2.00$ cut-off.

\ans{\tfrac{57}{2500} = 0.0228}

\problem{Squad scores $12$, $14$, $16$, $18$, $20$, $22$, $24$ (whole population). Find $\mu$, $\sigma$, and the $z$-score of $24$.}

\textbf{Solution.} (a) The seven values are evenly spaced, so the mean is the middle one: $\dfrac{12+14+16+18+20+22+24}{7} = \dfrac{126}{7} = 18$.
\par
(b) Deviations from $18$ are $-6, -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, 6$; their squares sum to $36+16+4+0+4+16+36 = 112$. Dividing by $n = 7$ (population, because the squad IS the population) gives a variance of $16$, so $\sigma = 4$.
\par
(c) $z = \dfrac{24 - 18}{4} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$. Dividing by $n-1$ instead would give $\sigma \approx 4.32$ and a different $z$; the problem says to treat the squad as the whole population, so $n$ is correct here.

\ans{\mu = 18,\ \sigma = 4,\ z = 1.5}

\problem{Bottles: $\mu = 500$, $\sigma = 2.5$, label $495$. (a) $z$; (b) proportion underfilled; (c) new mean; (d) the assumption.}

\textbf{Solution.} (a) $z = \dfrac{495 - 500}{2.5} = \dfrac{-5}{2.5} = -2$.
\par
(b) ``Underfilled'' is a left-tail question, so read the table at $z = -2.00$: about $0.0228$ of bottles, a little over two in a hundred.
\par
(c) An accepted proportion of $0.1003$ to the left corresponds to $z = -1.28$. We need $495$ to sit at that $z$ under the new mean, so from $z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$ we get $\mu = x - z\sigma = 495 - (-1.28)(2.5) = 495 + 3.2 = 498.2$. Note this LOWERS the mean, allowing more underfilled bottles, not fewer --- $0.1003$ is a looser standard than the $0.0228$ found in (b), which is worth pointing out to a student who expects every adjustment to tighten quality.
\par
(d) Everything above assumes the fill volumes really follow a normal model. If the distribution were skewed --- say a jammed valve produced a cluster of very low fills --- the table areas would not describe it, and every proportion quoted here would be wrong even though the arithmetic was right.

\ans{z = -2,\ 0.0228;\ 0.1003 \to \mu = 498.2}
