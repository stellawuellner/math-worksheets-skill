\problem{Taxi: $\$3$ pick-up plus $\$2$ per mile up to $5$ miles; beyond that, $\$13$ plus $\$1.50$ per extra mile. (a) Write $C(m)$. (b) The fare for $4$ miles.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ C(m) = \begin{cases} 3 + 2m, & 0 \le m \le 5 \\ 13 + 1.5(m-5), & m > 5 \end{cases} \]
Full credit needs the conditions written down and, in the second branch, both the $13$ already charged at $5$ miles and the $(m-5)$ so the cheaper rate applies only to the miles past the fifth. A second branch written $1.5m$ is the standard error: it forgets everything already paid.

\textbf{(b)} $4$ miles satisfies $0 \le m \le 5$, so use the first branch: $3 + 2(4) = 3 + 8$. \ans{11}

\problem{Using the taxi rule, find $C(2)$ and $C(9)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Check the condition before computing. $2 \le 5$, so the first branch; $9 > 5$, so the second.
\begin{align*}
  C(2) &= 3 + 2(2) = 3 + 4 \\
  C(9) &= 13 + 1.5(9-5) = 13 + 6
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{C(2) = 7, \quad C(9) = 19} \]

\problem{Pool: $\$5$ covers up to $2$ hours, then $\$3$ per extra hour. (a) Write $C(t)$. (b) A $5$-hour visit.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ C(t) = \begin{cases} 5, & 0 \le t \le 2 \\ 5 + 3(t-2), & t > 2 \end{cases} \]
Full credit needs a first branch with no $t$ in it at all --- the price does not depend on the length of a short visit --- and the $(t-2)$ in the second, so the extra charge begins with the third hour.

\textbf{(b)} $5 > 2$, so the second branch: $5 + 3(5-2) = 5 + 9$. \ans{14}

\problem{Phone plan: $\$25$ up to $5$ GB, then $\$10$ per extra GB. (a) Write $F(g)$. (b) The charge for $8$ GB.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ F(g) = \begin{cases} 25, & 0 \le g \le 5 \\ 25 + 10(g-5), & g > 5 \end{cases} \]
Both conditions must be stated, and the extra $\$10$ must apply only beyond the fifth gigabyte.

\textbf{(b)} $8 > 5$, so $25 + 10(8-5) = 25 + 30$. \ans{55}

\problem{A taxi ride cost $\$25$. (a) How far was it? (b) How did you know which branch to use?}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} At exactly $5$ miles the fare is $3 + 2(5) = \$13$, the largest fare the first branch can ever produce. Since $\$25$ is more than that, the ride must be longer than $5$ miles, so the second branch applies. Full credit requires that comparison with the boundary value \emph{and} a check at the end that the answer really is more than $5$. Trying a branch at random is not the method.

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\begin{align*}
  13 + 1.5(m-5) &= 25 \\
  1.5(m-5) &= 12 \\
  m - 5 &= 8
\end{align*}
The ride was $13$ miles, and $13 > 5$, which confirms the branch was right. \ans{13}

\problem{Library: no fine for $3$ days, then $\$0.25$ per day beyond the third. (a) Write $F(d)$. (b) The fine at $10$ days.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ F(d) = \begin{cases} 0, & 0 \le d \le 3 \\ 0.25(d-3), & d > 3 \end{cases} \]
Full credit needs the exact zero on the first branch and the $(d-3)$ on the second, so that a book four days late costs $25$ cents rather than a dollar.

\textbf{(b)} $10 > 3$, so $0.25(10-3) = 0.25 \times 7$. \ans{1.75}

\problem{Electricity: $\$0.12$ per kWh up to $300$ kWh, then $\$36$ plus $\$0.18$ per extra kWh. Find $E(250)$ and $E(500)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $250 \le 300$ uses the first branch; $500 > 300$ uses the second.
\begin{align*}
  E(250) &= 0.12(250) \\
  E(500) &= 36 + 0.18(500-300) = 36 + 36
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{E(250) = 30, \quad E(500) = 72} \]
Note that the two branches agree at $k = 300$: $0.12(300) = 36$. A price list that jumped at the boundary would charge less for more electricity.

\problem{Pay: $\$18$/hour up to $40$ hours, $\$27$/hour beyond. (a) Write $P(h)$. (b) Pay for $46$ hours.}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ P(h) = \begin{cases} 18h, & 0 \le h \le 40 \\ 720 + 27(h-40), & h > 40 \end{cases} \]
The $720$ is $18 \times 40$, the pay already earned at the boundary; the overtime rate applies only to $(h-40)$. Writing the second branch as $27h$ pays overtime on the whole week and is the classic error.

\textbf{(b)} $46 > 40$, so $720 + 27(46-40) = 720 + 162$. \ans{882}

\problem{A worker earned $\$963$ in a week. (a) How many hours? (b) How did the amount tell you the branch?}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} A full $40$-hour week pays $18 \times 40 = \$720$, which is the most the first branch can produce. $\$963$ exceeds it, so the week ran into overtime. Full credit requires that comparison and a final check that the answer really exceeds $40$.

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\begin{align*}
  720 + 27(h-40) &= 963 \\
  27(h-40) &= 243 \\
  h - 40 &= 9
\end{align*}
The worker put in $49$ hours, and $49 > 40$, confirming the branch. \ans{49}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis challenge.} Plan A: $A(n) = 30 + 0.1n$. Plan B: $\$20$ for the first $200$ minutes, then $\$0.15$ per extra minute. (a) Write $B(n)$. (b) Where do they cost the same? (c) Which is cheaper below that?}

\textbf{(a) --- instructor-judged.} The rule is
\[ B(n) = \begin{cases} 20, & 0 \le n \le 200 \\ 20 + 0.15(n-200), & n > 200 \end{cases} \]
Both conditions and the $(n-200)$ are required.

\textbf{Solution (b):} Set the two charges equal on the region where $n > 200$.
\begin{align*}
  30 + 0.1n &= 20 + 0.15(n-200) \\
  30 + 0.1n &= 0.15n - 10 \\
  40 &= 0.05n
\end{align*}
So the plans cost the same at $n = 800$ minutes, and $800 > 200$, so the branch used was the right one. \ans{n = 800}

\textbf{(c) --- instructor-judged.} Plan B is cheaper for every number of minutes below $800$ and dearer above it. Full credit requires evidence, not an assertion: either a test value on each side --- at $n = 400$, $A = \$70$ and $B = \$50$; at $n = 1000$, $A = \$130$ and $B = \$140$ --- or the rate argument, that past $200$ minutes B climbs $\$0.15$ a minute while A climbs only $\$0.10$, so B must eventually overtake A, and $800$ is where it does.
