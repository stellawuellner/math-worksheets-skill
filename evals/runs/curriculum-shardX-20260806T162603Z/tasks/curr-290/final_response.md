# Choosing and Combining Factoring Techniques — three PDFs

- **Study guide (`ss_mixedfactoring_curr290.pdf`, 2 pages)** — four short sections, one per
  method: take the common factor out first, trinomials that start with a plain $x^2$,
  trinomials with a number in front (grouping), and the difference of two squares. Each
  section has a rule box, a worked example whose first step says *why that method was chosen*,
  and a "try it" with the answer printed upside down inside the box. The first section also
  carries the three-question routine the worksheet asks the student to run on every problem.
- **Worksheet (`ws_mixedfactoring_curr290.pdf`, 5 pages)** — 12 problems. The first four are a
  short warm-up; after that the four methods are deliberately interleaved so no two
  consecutive problems use the same one and the student has to *decide* rather than repeat.
  It ends with a synthesis challenge, $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$, which needs two techniques in
  sequence. Work space is 4.5–7.5 cm per problem with its own answer line.
- **Answer key (`ak_mixedfactoring_curr290.pdf`, 3 pages)** — Quick Answers bank, a
  "What is verified" note, a generated Curriculum section, and a full worked solution for
  every problem that begins by naming the decision ("no common factor, three terms, leading
  coefficient not 1, so grouping") rather than jumping to the answer.

## What is verified

The sheet asks for **13 responses** across 12 problems.

- **12 are machine-checked.** Every factoring was recomputed with SymPy and confirmed
  equivalent to the original expression, and problem 10's expansion was checked as an
  identity. A separate gate confirms each boxed answer in the key is the value that was
  verified, so the key cannot drift from the check. Eleven of the twelve problems are
  therefore fully machine-checked.
- **1 is instructor-judged: problem 12(b)** — "explain which technique you used first and
  why, then name the technique that finished the job." That is written reasoning, so it is
  marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank, named in the key's "What is verified" note, and the
  key spells out what full credit requires: the trinomial-in-$x^2$ move named first *with its
  justification*, and the difference of squares named as the finisher.

No answer on this sheet is marked `[unchecked]`.

## Notes for the adult

- Problem 6 ($4x^2-36$) and problem 11 ($2x^3-8x^2-24x$) are the two that separate students
  who factor completely from students who stop one step early. If your child stops at
  $4(x^2-9)$, that is the conversation to have.
- Problem 10 runs the process in reverse (multiply out to test a claim). Students who can only
  factor forwards cannot check their own work, which is why it is on an interleaved sheet.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only, never on the worksheet or study guide.
