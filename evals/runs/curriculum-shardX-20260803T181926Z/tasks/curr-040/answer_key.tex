\problem{\textbf{$8 + 5 = \ $?}

\textbf{Step 1.} A plus sign, and the blank is at the end --- this one is a
straight addition.

\textbf{Step 2.} Make a ten: $8$ needs $2$ more to reach $10$, and $5$ splits
into $2 + 3$. So $8 + 2 = 10$, then $10 + 3 = 13$.

\textbf{Step 3.} On the number line, start at $8$ and hop forward $5$.
\ans{13}}

\problem{\textbf{$12 - 5 = \ $?}

\textbf{Step 1.} A minus sign with the blank at the end --- take away.

\textbf{Step 2.} Get down to ten first: $12 - 2 = 10$, and there are $3$ left to
take away, so $10 - 3 = 7$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check by adding back: $7 + 5 = 12$ $\checkmark$ \ans{7}}

\problem{\textbf{$7 + \ $\underline{\hspace{0.8cm}}$ \ = 15$}

\textbf{Step 1.} The blank is in the middle, not at the end. The question is
not ``what is $7$ plus something'' --- it is ``how far is it from $7$ up to
$15$?''

\textbf{Step 2.} Count on from $7$: to $10$ is $3$ hops, and from $10$ to $15$
is $5$ more, so $3 + 5 = 8$ hops altogether.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check: $7 + 8 = 15$ $\checkmark$ \ans{8}}

\problem{\textbf{$9 + 7 = \ $?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Plus sign, blank at the end --- addition again.

\textbf{Step 2.} Make a ten: $9$ needs $1$, and $7$ splits into $1 + 6$. So
$9 + 1 = 10$, then $10 + 6 = 16$.

\textbf{Step 3.} A common near-miss here is $15$ (counting one of the numbers
twice). Counting on from the larger number, $9$, is the reliable way.
\ans{16}}

\problem{\textbf{$16 - 7 = \ $?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Minus sign, blank at the end --- take away. Notice this is the
undoing of problem $4$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Think addition: $7 + \ $? $ = 16$. From $7$ to $10$ is $3$, and
from $10$ to $16$ is $6$, so the answer is $9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check: $9 + 7 = 16$ $\checkmark$ \ans{9}}

\problem{\textbf{\underline{\hspace{0.8cm}}$ \ - 4 = 9$}

\textbf{Step 1.} This time the blank is the number we \emph{started} with, so
subtracting the two printed numbers would be wrong. We know what is left after
taking $4$ away.

\textbf{Step 2.} Put the $4$ back: $9 + 4 = 13$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check by doing the subtraction the problem shows:
$13 - 4 = 9$ $\checkmark$

\textit{Watch for:} a child who writes $5$ here has computed $9 - 4$. Ask them
to read the sentence aloud --- ``something take away $4$ leaves $9$'' --- and the
error usually corrects itself. \ans{13}}

\problem{\textbf{Maya's stickers.}

\textbf{Step 1.} She starts with some and \emph{gets more}, so the two groups
join: this is addition.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6 + 8$. Make a ten: $8$ needs $2$, and $6$ splits into
$2 + 4$, so $8 + 2 = 10$ and $10 + 4 = 14$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Answer the question in words: Maya has $14$ stickers.
\ans{14}}

\problem{\textbf{Apples in the bowl.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The family \emph{eats} some, so apples leave the bowl: this is
subtraction.

\textbf{Step 2.} $15 - 7$. Down to ten first: $15 - 5 = 10$, then $10 - 2 = 8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check by adding back: $8 + 7 = 15$ $\checkmark$ There are $8$
apples left. \ans{8}}

\problem{\textbf{Sam's marbles.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Sam is not putting groups together and he is not taking any
away --- he wants to know how many are \emph{missing}. The sentence is
$5 + \ $? $ = 12$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Count on from $5$: to $10$ is $5$ hops, then $10$ to $12$ is
$2$ more, so $5 + 2 = 7$ hops.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check: $5 + 7 = 12$ $\checkmark$ Sam needs $7$ more marbles.

\textit{Watch for:} $17$ is the tell-tale wrong answer --- it means the child
added the two numbers instead of finding the difference. \ans{7}}

\problem{\textbf{Put it all together: the family of $9$, $4$ and $13$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} These three numbers make one family: the two parts are $9$ and
$4$, and the whole is $13$. Every fact in the family uses all three.

\textbf{Step 2.} Turn the addition around --- adding in either order gives the
same total, so $4 + 9 = 13$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Each subtraction fact takes one part away from the whole:
$13 - 9 = 4$ and $13 - 4 = 9$.

\textit{Manual note:} accept the two additions in either order. If a child
writes $13 - 9 = 5$, have them check with the number line: hopping back $9$ from
$13$ lands on $4$. \ans{13 \quad 4 \quad 9}}
