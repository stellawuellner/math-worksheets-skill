# Where Is the Shape? Position Words on a Shelf — Kindergarten / Grade 1

Three PDFs are ready: the student worksheet, a full answer key written for the
adult sitting beside the child, and a two-page study guide.

**Worksheet (4 pages, 8 problems).** The whole sheet works from one picture: a
three-shelf unit holding seven shapes — circle, triangle, square, hexagon,
rectangle, oval, diamond — with the shelves numbered 1 to 3 from the bottom and
the spots numbered 1 to 4 from the left. Every question asks the child to
locate shapes in that scene with position words: above, below, left of, right
of, between, and ordering from bottom to top or right to left.

The design choice worth naming: each position word is tied to a number the
child can point at. "Above" means a bigger shelf number, "to the right of"
means a bigger spot number. That turns a vocabulary question into something the
child can check for themselves, and it is why the answers can be verified at
all. The directions block states both rules in one line each so a grown-up can
read them aloud before starting.

Difficulty ramps from 1 to 4. Problems 1–3 compare two shapes on one dimension.
Problem 4 orders three shelf numbers bottom to top; problem 7 orders four spot
numbers right to left. Problem 8 is the hardest: the triangle and the oval share
neither a shelf nor a spot, so it takes two position words together to say where
the triangle is.

**What was machine-verified.** Eight of the nine checks were recomputed
independently and the build was blocked until each agreed with the answer key:
every $<$ / $>$ comparison (3 > 2, 1 < 4, 1 < 2, 4 > 2, 3 > 1, 3 > 2) and both
orderings (1, 2, 3 bottom to top; 4, 3, 2, 1 right to left). The answer key's
boxed answers were bound problem by problem to those results.

**Flagged for manual review.** Problem 6 asks the child to draw a triangle on
the middle shelf between the square and the hexagon and then finish a sentence
with a position word. That is an open response and cannot be machine-checked, so
it is labelled manual. The answer key lists the position words that are all
equally correct there — *to the right of*, *beside*, *next to* — and notes that
no vertical word applies, since all three shapes share a shelf. It also says
what to do if the triangle was drawn on the wrong shelf: grade the sentence
against where it really is, then re-read the question together.

**Study guide (2 pages).** Four short sections — above and below, left and
right, putting shapes in order, and telling what is between — each with a rule
in plain words, a worked example, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside
down inside the box. The watch-out box flags the mistake this topic produces
most: answering in the wrong order, which flips $<$ and $>$ and turns *below*
into *above*.

**One note on the standards tag.** Every problem is tagged `K.G / 1.G` from the
skill's standards map. The comparison work also touches early number comparison,
but the sheet's purpose is geometric position, so it is tagged as geometry only.
