\problem{\textbf{Tile strip: ratio of red tiles to blue tiles, all three forms
and simplest form.}\\
Step 1 (read the model): count the letters --- 4 R tiles and 6 B tiles.
Step 2 (write the three forms): 4 to 6, \ 4:6, \ $\frac{4}{6}$. All three name the
same comparison; only the notation changes.
Step 3 (simplify): divide both parts by the common factor 2, giving 2 to 3, or
2:3, or $\ans{\frac{2}{3}}$.\\
\emph{Note:} simplifying a ratio does not change the strip --- it says
``for every 2 red there are 3 blue.''}

\problem{\textbf{Same strip: red tiles to all the tiles.}\\
Step 1 (find the whole): the whole handful is $4 + 6 = 10$ tiles, so this is a
part-to-whole comparison, not part-to-part.
Step 2 (write it): red to all is $\frac{4}{10}$.
Step 3 (simplify and interpret): $\ans{\frac{2}{5}}$ --- 2 out of every 5 tiles in
the handful are red. Part-to-whole ratios are the ones that can also be read as a
fraction of the group.}

\problem{\textbf{Ratio table: flour and water in the ratio 3 to 2; flour for
8 cups of water.}\\
Step 1 (find the multiplier): the water row goes 2, 4, 6, 8 --- each column is the
first column times 1, 2, 3, 4.
Step 2 (scale both rows the same way): flour goes 3, 6, 9, 12. Equivalent ratios
come from multiplying \emph{both} quantities by the same number.
Step 3 (read the asked column): $\frac{x}{8} = \frac{3}{2}$ gives $2x = 24$, so
$x = \ans{12}$ cups of flour.}

\problem{\textbf{Value of the ratio 12 strawberries to 3 smoothies.}\\
Step 1 (write the ratio in the asked order): strawberries to smoothies is
12 to 3, or $\frac{12}{3}$.
Step 2 (find the value): the value of a ratio is the quotient of its two numbers,
$12 \div 3$.
Step 3: $\ans{4}$ strawberries per smoothie. Writing the ratio the other way,
$\frac{3}{12}$, answers a different question (smoothies per strawberry).}

\problem{\textbf{Tape diagram: cats to dogs is 3 to 5, each box is 4 animals;
total animals.}\\
Step 1 (count boxes): 3 boxes of cats and 5 boxes of dogs, so the diagram has
$3 + 5 = 8$ equal boxes in all.
Step 2 (use the box value): every box is worth the same 4 animals, so the total is
$8 \times 4$.
Step 3: $\ans{32}$ animals (12 cats and 20 dogs, which checks: $12:20$ simplifies
back to $3:5$).}

\problem{\textbf{Ratio table: 5 pencils cost 2 dollars; cost of 20 pencils.}\\
Step 1 (find the multiplier): from 5 pencils to 20 pencils is a factor of 4.
Step 2 (scale both rows): cost goes $2 \times 4$; the middle column (10 pencils)
is $2 \times 2 = 4$ dollars, which is a useful check.
Step 3: $\frac{x}{20} = \frac{2}{5}$ gives $5x = 40$, so the bundle costs
$\ans{8}$ dollars.}

\problem{\textbf{Part to whole from a $2:7$ part-to-part ratio.}\\
Step 1 (name the parts): the ratio $2:7$ compares walkers with riders --- both are
parts, so 7 is not the whole.
Step 2 (build the whole): the class is $2 + 7 = 9$ equal shares.
Step 3 (write the fraction): walkers are 2 of those 9 shares, so the fraction of
the class that walks is $\ans{\frac{2}{9}}$. That is where the 9 comes from: it is
$2 + 7$, the total number of shares.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare Mix A ($4$ scoops in $10$ cups) with Mix B ($6$ scoops in
$14$ cups).}\\
Step 1 (write both as fractions): Mix A is $\frac{4}{10}$ and Mix B is
$\frac{6}{14}$.
Step 2 (find each value): $4 \div 10 = 0.4$ scoops per cup;
$6 \div 14 = 0.428\ldots$ scoops per cup. (Common denominators work too:
$\frac{4}{10} = \frac{28}{70}$ and $\frac{6}{14} = \frac{30}{70}$.)
Step 3 (compare in the asked order): $0.4$ is less than $0.428\ldots$, so
value of Mix A $\ans{<}$ value of Mix B --- Mix B is the stronger mix.}

\problem{\textbf{Ratio table: 9 lemons per 6 cups; lemons for 14 cups.}\\
Step 1 (take the easy step first): divide both quantities by 3 to reach the
2-cup column --- 6 cups $\div\, 3 = 2$ cups and 9 lemons $\div\, 3 = 3$ lemons.
Step 2 (scale up from there): 14 cups is 7 times 2 cups, so multiply both by 7:
$3 \times 7 = 21$ lemons.
Step 3 (check with the proportion): $\frac{x}{14} = \frac{9}{6}$ gives
$6x = 126$, so $x = \ans{21}$ lemons.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: blue to yellow is 3 to 2, and 45 liters of mixed
paint are needed; liters of blue.}\\
Step 1 (model the whole): the mix is $3 + 2 = 5$ equal boxes of paint, so the
part-to-whole ratio for blue is $\frac{3}{5}$.
Step 2 (find one box): the 5 boxes together are 45 liters, so one box is
$45 \div 5 = 9$ liters.
Step 3 (take the blue boxes): blue is 3 boxes, $3 \times 9 = 27$, so Nia needs
$\ans{27 \text{ L}}$ of blue (and 18 L of yellow, which totals 45 L).}
