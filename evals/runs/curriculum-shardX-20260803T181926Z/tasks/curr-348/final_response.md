Here are the three documents for the geometry dilation workshop — eight
problems on using dilations to build and measure similar figures.

**Worksheet (`ws_dilations_curr348.pdf`, 4 pages).** Eight application problems
with units stated in every one: a slide projector, a sail cut from a pattern, a
blueprint enlargement, a map zoom, a photo enlargement, a coordinate triangle, a
scale model car, and a plot-and-justify problem. Three skills rotate so the
student has to choose the method:

- dilating points on the coordinate plane, with the centre at the origin
  (problems 1, 8) and away from it (problem 4)
- finding a scale factor from two corresponding lengths (problems 3, 6)
- carrying a scale factor onto lengths, perimeter and area of the similar
  figure (problems 2, 5, 7)

Difficulty ramps 1 to 5. Problem 4 moves the centre off the origin, problem 7 is
an error-analysis task (a claimed model windshield area of 0.06 m² that scaled
an area by k instead of k²), and problem 8 asks the student to plot the image,
draw the three lines joining corresponding vertices, notice that they meet at
the centre, and justify the similarity. Problems 6 and 8 have full coordinate
grids to plot on; every problem has 5–6 cm of work space.

**Answer key (`ak_dilations_curr348.pdf`, 3 pages).** Quick-answer bank at the
top, then a full solution for each problem that names the rule before using it
and finishes with an independent check — the ray/distance check on problem 1,
the actual areas 150 and 864 on problem 5, and the "0.06 m² cannot fit on a car
0.4 m long" sanity check on problem 7. Problem 8's open part carries a model
answer and a "look for" line: concurrency at the centre, and similarity argued
from a constant side ratio rather than from appearance. The grade level and the
curriculum summary print here only.

**Study guide (`ss_dilations_curr348.pdf`, 2 pages).** Three sections — dilating
a point, finding k from two lengths, and using k on lengths, perimeter and area
— each with a rule box, a worked example, and a try-it with the answer upside
down inside the box. It closes with the two errors that cost the most marks:
scaling an area by k, and forgetting to subtract the centre when it is not the
origin.

**Verification.** All 17 machine checks pass: coordinates of every image point,
both coordinate distances (computed by the checker from the raw points, not
from my arithmetic), each scale factor, and the area factor k². Problem 7's
trap was machine-checked as distinguishably wrong (0.06 against the correct
0.005). One item is flagged for manual review, correctly — problem 8's
plot-and-explain part, which the key answers with a model response.

**One note on the standard.** The task lists HSG-CO.A / HSG-CO.D, the congruence
cluster. Dilations and similarity live in the similarity cluster, so the
problems are tagged HSG-SRT.A.1 (dilation properties) and HSG-SRT.A.2
(similarity as a dilation image), both from the skill's standards map.
