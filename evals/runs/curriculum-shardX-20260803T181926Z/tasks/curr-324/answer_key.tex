\problem{\textbf{Hypotenuse $12$, $\angle A = 35^\circ$; find $x$ opposite
$\angle A$.}

Step 1. Name the two sides in play. The unknown is OPPOSITE $\angle A$; the
given side is the HYPOTENUSE. Opposite with hypotenuse is sine --- the S of
SOH-CAH-TOA.

Step 2. Set up and solve: $\sin 35^\circ = \dfrac{x}{12}$, so
$x = 12 \sin 35^\circ$.

Step 3. In degree mode, $12 \sin 35^\circ = 6.8829\ldots$, which rounds to
$\ans{6.88}$

Step 4. Sanity check: $6.88$ is shorter than the hypotenuse $12$, as every leg
must be. Cosine would have given $9.83$ --- also shorter than $12$, which is
exactly why the size check alone does not catch a wrong ratio. Naming the two
sides first does.}

\problem{\textbf{Dana: $x = 14\tan 52^\circ$, calculator shows $-84.75$.}

Step 1. Confirm the setup. The unknown is opposite $\angle A$, the given leg
$14$ is adjacent to it, and opposite over adjacent is tangent: $x = 14\tan
52^\circ$ is correct.

Step 2. Diagnose the number. A length can never be negative, so the fault is in
how the calculator read $52$. In radian mode it evaluates the tangent of $52$
radians, which is a completely different angle and can land anywhere ---
here at $-84.75$.

Step 3. Switch the calculator to DEG (look for the DEG/RAD indicator) and
recompute: $14\tan 52^\circ = 14(1.2799\ldots) = 17.9192\ldots$, so
$x = \ans{17.92}$

Step 4. Sanity check: $\angle A = 52^\circ$ is bigger than $45^\circ$, so the
opposite leg should be LONGER than the adjacent leg $14$ --- and $17.92$ is.}

\problem{\textbf{Eli: $\angle A = \tan^{-1}(24/7) = 73.74^\circ$, from legs $7$
opposite and $24$ adjacent.}

Step 1. Write the ratio from the definition before touching the inverse:
$\tan A = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{7}{24}$. Eli
inverted the fraction, which is the most common inverse-trig error --- the
calculator cannot see that the sides went in upside down.

Step 2. Apply the inverse to the correct ratio:
$\angle A = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{7}{24}\right) = 16.2602\ldots$

Step 3. Rounded: $\ans{A \approx 16.26^\circ}$

Step 4. Sanity check: the side opposite $\angle A$ is much shorter than the
adjacent side, so $\angle A$ must be the SMALL acute angle --- well under
$45^\circ$. Eli's $73.74^\circ$ is the other acute angle of this same triangle,
and the two sum to $90^\circ$, which is a quick way to spot the swap.}

\problem{\textbf{Hypotenuse $20$, $\angle A = 41^\circ$; find the adjacent leg
$b$.}

Step 1. Name the sides. The unknown is ADJACENT to $\angle A$ and the given side
is the HYPOTENUSE, so the ratio is cosine, not sine.

Step 2. $\cos 41^\circ = \dfrac{b}{20}$, so $b = 20 \cos 41^\circ$.

Step 3. In degree mode: $20 \cos 41^\circ = 15.0942\ldots$, so
$b = \ans{15.09}$

Step 4. The classmate's sine gives $20 \sin 41^\circ = 13.12$, which is the
OTHER leg of this triangle. Both numbers are plausible lengths; only the
opposite/adjacent naming separates them.}

\problem{\textbf{Flagpole: $40$ ft away, angle of elevation $38^\circ$, screen
shows $12.41$.}

Step 1. Draw the right triangle: the horizontal distance $40$ is adjacent to the
$38^\circ$ angle, and the height is opposite it. Opposite over adjacent is
tangent, so height $= 40 \tan 38^\circ$. Ravi's setup is correct.

Step 2. Test the displayed number. At $38^\circ$ --- close to $45^\circ$ --- the
height should be nearly as large as the $40$-foot distance. A height of $12.41$
would need an angle of about $17^\circ$, so the number is wrong even though the
formula is right.

Step 3. The calculator is in radian mode: it returned $40\tan(38 \text{ rad})$.
Switch to degrees and recompute: $40\tan 38^\circ = 40(0.78128\ldots) =
31.2514\ldots$

Step 4. The pole rises $\ans{31.25\text{ ft}}$ above Ravi's eye level.

Step 5. Sanity check: $31.25 < 40$, which is right for an angle under
$45^\circ$, and the ratio $31.25/40 = 0.78$ is indeed $\tan 38^\circ$.}

\problem{\textbf{$30$-$60$-$90$ triangle, hypotenuse $10$: find $a$ opposite
$30^\circ$, and identify Sasha's $8.66$.}

\textbf{(a)} Step 1. In a $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle the sides are in the ratio
$1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$, with the shortest side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle and the
hypotenuse opposite the right angle. So the side opposite $30^\circ$ is exactly
half the hypotenuse.

Step 2. $a = \dfrac{10}{2} = 5$, and the trigonometric route agrees:
$a = 10 \sin 30^\circ = 10(0.5) = 5$, because $\sin 30^\circ$ is exactly
$\dfrac{1}{2}$. $\ans{a = 5}$

\textbf{(b)} Sasha computed $10 \sin 60^\circ = 8.66$. That is the side opposite
the $60^\circ$ angle --- the LONGER leg, $5\sqrt{3} = 8.6602\ldots$ --- so the
number itself is a real length in this triangle, just not the one asked for. The
error is the classic one: the ratio was taken from the other acute angle. Naming
the angle you are working from, before choosing sin or cos, is what prevents it.

\emph{Grading note:} accept "the longer leg, opposite the $60^\circ$ angle,
found with $\sin 60^\circ$ (or $\cos 30^\circ$)" as a full-credit answer to
part (b).}
