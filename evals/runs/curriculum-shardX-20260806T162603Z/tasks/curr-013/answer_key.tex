\problem{Ana has $5$ stickers, Ben has $8$, Cara has $3$. Write the three sticker counts in order from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Count up and take each number as you reach it: $3$ arrives first, then $5$, then $8$. So Cara's count is least and Ben's is greatest. \ans{3,\ 5,\ 8}

\problem{Count the dots on each card (Card A, Card B, Card C), then write the three dot counts in order from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Touch and count each row: Card A has $7$ dots, Card B has $4$, Card C has $9$. Counting up, $4$ arrives first, then $7$, then $9$. \ans{4,\ 7,\ 9}

\problem{The red jar holds $6$ cups, the blue jar $2$ cups, the green jar $9$ cups. Write the three amounts in cups, least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} All three amounts are measured in the same unit, cups, so the counts can be ordered directly. Counting up: $2$, then $6$, then $9$. \ans{2,\ 6,\ 9}

\problem{The top shelf holds $7$ books, the bottom shelf $10$ books. Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between the two counts.}

\textbf{Solution:} Counting up, $7$ is said before $10$, so $7$ is the smaller count. The wide open end of the symbol faces the bigger number, $10$. \ans{7 < 10}

\problem{Leo practised $10$ minutes on Monday, $4$ minutes on Tuesday, $7$ minutes on Wednesday. Write the three practice times in minutes, least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Order the minutes, not the days. Counting up: $4$ comes first (Tuesday), then $7$ (Wednesday), then $10$ (Monday). \ans{4,\ 7,\ 10}

\problem{The gold ribbon is $8$ centimetres, the silver $10$ centimetres, the white $6$ centimetres. Write the three lengths in centimetres, least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same unit on every ribbon, so compare the numbers. Counting up: $6$, then $8$, then $10$. The white ribbon is shortest and the silver is longest. \ans{6,\ 8,\ 10}

\problem{Baskets hold $9$, $3$ and $6$ apples. (a) Write the counts least to greatest. (b) Explain how you knew which basket held the least.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Counting up, $3$ arrives first, then $6$, then $9$. \ans{3,\ 6,\ 9}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response gives a reason for the smallest, not just the number. Full credit for any of these in the child's own words: $3$ is the first of the three you say when counting up from $1$; a row of $3$ apples runs out before a row of $6$ or a row of $9$ when matched one to one; $3$ apples make the shortest line. Naming $3$ as the least with no reason at all earns half credit. A ``reason'' that only repeats the ordered list earns no credit.

\problem{Crayon boxes hold $6$, $8$ and $6$ crayons. (a) Write the counts least to greatest. (b) Two counts are the same --- explain what you did with them.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Counting up, the two boxes of $6$ arrive together, then $8$. Equal amounts sit side by side in the order. \ans{6,\ 6,\ 8}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response says the two equal counts neither comes before the other, so both are written first, before the $8$. Any wording earns full credit: ``they tie'', ``they are the same so I put them next to each other'', ``it does not matter which $6$ I write first''. An answer saying the two equal amounts could be written either way round is also fully correct. Changing one of the amounts so that they differ earns no credit.
