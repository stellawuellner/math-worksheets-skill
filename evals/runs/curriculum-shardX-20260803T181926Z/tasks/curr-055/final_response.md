Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 2–3 place-value review.

**Worksheet — `ws_roundorder_curr055.pdf` (5 pages, 12 problems).**
Every problem is built around the number-line picture rather than a digit rule.
Nine of the twelve come with a blank number line: the child labels the two
benchmarks and the halfway mark themselves, plots the number, and circles the
end it is nearer. That is the whole idea of rounding, and it is why the sheet
does not lead with "look at the ones digit" — the digit rule is a shortcut for
the picture, and problem 10 makes the child count the hops both ways to see it.

After a short warm-up on rounding to the nearest ten, the tasks are deliberately
mixed — round to a ten, round to a hundred, compare two numbers, put a list in
order — so the child has to decide what the question is asking instead of
repeating one move twelve times. Two problems land exactly on the halfway mark
(365 and 650), which is where the "exactly halfway goes up" convention has to be
said out loud. Problem 9 shows a classmate's wrong comparison (409 > 490) and
asks what she should have compared first. Problem 12 is the challenge: three
can-drive counts to order, round, and then explain why the rounded numbers
cannot settle who collected the most.

**Answer key — `ak_roundorder_curr055.pdf` (3 pages).**
Each problem is worked through in the same language the child used on the sheet
— name the two ends, find the middle, decide which end is nearer — so you can
see where an answer went wrong, not just that it did. A quick-answer strip at
the top makes grading fast, and a Curriculum section lists the standard covered
and the difficulty range. Several problems carry a note about the common wrong
answer (rounding "up" by habit; comparing the last digit first).

**Study guide — `ss_roundorder_curr055.pdf` (2 pages).**
Four short sections: rounding to the nearest ten, rounding to the nearest
hundred, comparing two numbers, and putting numbers in order. The first two
carry a drawn number line with the number plotted on it, so the child can match
the picture to the one on their worksheet. Each section ends with a try-it
problem whose answer is printed upside down inside the box.

**What was verified.** 17 of the 18 checks behind the 12 problems were
recomputed independently — every rounding result, every comparison symbol, and
every ordering was checked by machine against the printed key, so a transposed
answer could not reach the page.

**One item is flagged for manual review, on purpose:** problem 12(c) asks the
child to *explain* why three counts that all round to 300 cannot say who
collected the most. That is a written sentence, not a computation, so it is
marked for you to read rather than machine-scored. The answer key gives a model
sentence and says what to accept.

**One note on standards tagging.** The problems are tagged to the place-value
row (1.NBT / 2.NBT.A), which is what the task asked for and covers comparing and
ordering three-digit numbers. The skill's standards map has no row for the
grade-3 rounding code (3.NBT.A.1), so rounding is tagged to the place-value
understanding it rests on rather than to an invented code.
