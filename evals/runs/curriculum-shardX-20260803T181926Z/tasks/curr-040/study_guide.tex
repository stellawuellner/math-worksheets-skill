\sstitleblock{Adding, Subtracting, and Missing Numbers to 20}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{First ask: what is this one asking?}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Plus sign, blank at the end} $\rightarrow$ put the groups
together. Words that go with it: \emph{and, more, altogether, in all}.
\item \textbf{Minus sign, blank at the end} $\rightarrow$ take some away. Words
that go with it: \emph{eats, gives away, left, how many are gone}.
\item \textbf{Blank at the start or in the middle} $\rightarrow$ a number is
missing. Words that go with it: \emph{how many more, how many to get to}.
\end{itemize}
The $=$ sign means \emph{the same as}. Whatever you write in the blank, both
sides have to match.
\end{formulabox}

\skillheading{1. Adding to 20: make a ten}

\begin{formulabox}
Ten is the easiest number to add to, so build one first. Take just enough from
the second number to fill the first one up to $10$, then add what is left over.
Adding in either order gives the same total, so start from the bigger number to
save hops.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} $8 + 6$
\step{The blank is at the end and the sign is plus, so I am putting two groups
together --- and I can make a ten to do it quickly.}
\step{$8$ needs $2$ more to reach ten, and $6$ is $2$ and $4$. So
$8 + 2 = 10$, and $10 + 4$ is next.}
$8 + 6 = \ans{14}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} $9 + 4 = \ $?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{13}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Subtracting to 20: get down to ten, or think addition}

\begin{formulabox}
Two safe ways. \textbf{Down to ten:} take away just enough to land on $10$,
then take away the rest. \textbf{Think addition:} ask what you would add to the
smaller number to get back to the bigger one --- subtraction and addition undo
each other, so the answer is the same.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} $15 - 8$
\step{Minus sign with the blank at the end, so something is being taken away
--- and $15$ is close to ten, so I will step down to ten first.}
\step{$15 - 5 = 10$, and there are $3$ still to take away, so $10 - 3$ comes
next. Check it by adding back.}
$15 - 8 = \ans{7}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} $14 - 6 = \ $?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{8}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Finding the missing number}

\begin{formulabox}
When the blank is not at the end, do not just add or subtract the two numbers
you can see. Read the sentence out loud instead, then \textbf{count on} from the
smaller number to the bigger one and count your hops. Always check by putting
your answer into the original sentence.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} $6 + \ $\underline{\hspace{0.7cm}}$ \ = 13$
\step{The blank sits in the middle, so this is asking how far it is from $6$ up
to $13$ --- not what $6$ and $13$ make together.}
\step{Count on from $6$: four hops reach ten, then three more hops reach
thirteen, so that is seven hops in all.}
$6 + \ans{7} = 13$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} $5 + \ $\underline{\hspace{0.7cm}}$ \ = 14$\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{9}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} The $=$ sign does not mean ``the answer comes next''.
In $7 + \ $\underline{\hspace{0.7cm}}$ \ = 15$ the answer goes in the middle, and
adding $7$ and $15$ would be wrong. Cover the blank with your finger and say the
sentence aloud before you write anything.
\end{watchoutbox}
