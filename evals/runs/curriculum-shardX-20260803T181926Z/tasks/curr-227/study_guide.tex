\sstitleblock{Solving Systems of Two Linear Equations by Graphing}

\noindent\small A system asks for the pair $(x, y)$ that makes \emph{both}
equations true at once. Graphed, each equation is a line and each point on it is
one pair that works, so the pair that works for both is exactly where the two
lines cross.\normalsize

\skillheading{1. Graphing from slope-intercept form}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Rule:} in $y = mx + b$, plot the intercept $(0, b)$ first, then use
$m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$ to step to a second point and draw the
line. Do this for both equations; the crossing point is the solution.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Always check} by substituting the point into both equations --- a
graph read to the nearest square can be a square off.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad $y = x - 1$ \ and \ $y = -2x + 5$
\step{Both are already solved for $y$, so plot each intercept and step off each
slope; no rearranging is needed.}
\step{From $(0, -1)$ step up $1$, right $1$. From $(0, 5)$ step down $2$, right
$1$. The lines cross once, and substituting confirms it: $1 = 2 - 1$ and
$1 = -4 + 5$.}

$\ans{(2,\, 1)}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad $y = 2x - 4$ \ and \ $y = -x + 5$\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{(3,\, 2)}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Graphing from standard form}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Rule:} for $Ax + By = C$, the fastest two points are the intercepts.
Set $x = 0$ and solve for $y$; set $y = 0$ and solve for $x$. Plot both and
join them.\\[3pt]
If the intercepts land off the grid, rearrange into $y = mx + b$ instead ---
the two methods give the same line.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad $x + 2y = 6$ \ and \ $x - y = 3$
\step{Neither equation is solved for $y$, and both have small whole-number
intercepts, so the intercept method is quicker than rearranging.}
\step{$x + 2y = 6$ passes through $(0, 3)$ and $(6, 0)$; $x - y = 3$ passes
through $(0, -3)$ and $(3, 0)$. Substituting the crossing point gives
$4 + 2 = 6$ and $4 - 1 = 3$.}

$\ans{(4,\, 1)}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad $2x + y = 6$ \ and \ $x - y = 3$\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{(3,\, 0)}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. When the lines do not cross once}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Same slope, different intercept:} the lines are parallel, never meet,
and the system has \textbf{no solution}.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Same slope and same intercept:} the two equations describe one line
drawn twice, so every point on it works --- \textbf{infinitely many solutions}.
Check for this by simplifying one equation; if it turns into the other, they
are the same line.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad $y = 2x + 3$ \ and \ $y = 2x - 1$
\step{Compare the slopes before graphing: both are $2$, so the lines rise
identically and can only be parallel or identical.}
\step{The intercepts differ ($3$ and $-1$), so the lines stay a fixed distance
apart. Setting the right sides equal gives $3 = -1$, which is impossible.}

$\ans{\text{no solution}}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad $y = -x + 5$ \ and \ $2x + 2y = 10$. How many solutions?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: one line, $\ans{y = -x + 5}$, so infinitely many}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{4. Reading the crossing point in a story}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Rule:} write each situation as $y = (\text{rate})x + (\text{start})$,
graph both, and read the crossing point. The $x$-coordinate says \emph{when}
the two are equal and the $y$-coordinate says \emph{what} they are both equal
to.\\[3pt]
Before the crossing point the line that started higher is on top; after it, the
steeper line is.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad Gym A charges $12$ dollars to join plus $3$ dollars a
month; Gym B charges $6$ to join plus $5$ a month. When do they cost the same?
\step{Each cost is a start value plus a repeated rate, so both are lines:
$y = 3x + 12$ and $y = 5x + 6$.}
\step{Gym A starts higher but climbs more slowly, so B catches up. At the
crossing point $3(3) + 12 = 21$ and $5(3) + 6 = 21$.}

$\ans{(3,\, 21)}$ --- equal at month $3$, at $21$ dollars
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad $y = 2x + 10$ and $y = 4x + 4$. When are they equal, and
at what value?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{(3,\, 16)}$}
\end{tryitbox}
