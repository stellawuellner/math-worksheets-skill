\wstitleblock{Classifying Quadrilaterals from Defining Properties}{High School Geometry \quad Polygons}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Naming the most specific quadrilateral from the marks on a figure,
classifying from what the diagonals do, and classifying from vertex
coordinates}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Every problem asks for the \emph{most specific} name the given properties
force --- ``parallelogram'' is wrong when the marks already force ``rhombus''.
\item Classify from the marks and the stated facts, never from how the drawing
looks; some figures are labelled \emph{not to scale} for that reason.
\item Then use \emph{that} family's defining property to set up the equation,
and write the property you used beside your work.
\item Matching ticks mean congruent, matching chevrons mean parallel, and a
small square means a right angle.
\end{itemize}
