Here are the three PDFs for the elevation-and-depression set:

- **Worksheet** — `ws_elevation_curr323.pdf` (5 pages)
- **Answer key** — `ak_elevation_curr323.pdf` (3 pages)
- **Study guide** — `ss_elevation_curr323.pdf` (2 pages)

**What the worksheet covers.** Eight applied right-triangle problems, all set in
elevation or depression contexts: a ladder against a wall, a flagpole sighting, a
lighthouse-to-boat depression, a guy wire, a drone's line to its landing pad, a
hiker's line of sight to a cliff top, a tower measured from a raised window, and
a zip line. Every problem states its givens and its units, and every one carries
its own diagram **generated from the same data the answer is checked against**,
drawn to scale, so a figure cannot disagree with the key. A value-free reference
triangle above problem 1 fixes the labelling convention (right angle at C,
hypotenuse c).

The ramp runs from single-ratio problems (1–2), through depression setups and an
inverse-trig angle (3–4), to two-step work: the drone and cliff hypotenuses
(5–6), a tower height that must have the window height added back (7), and a
three-part zip-line problem (8).

**What is verified, honestly.** The verification file holds 10 entries, one per
response.

- **9 entries are machine-checked** with SymPy: 5.30 m, 28.01 ft, 76.94 m,
  53.1°, 182.91 m, 160.40 ft, 44.99 m, 48.46 m and 43.18 m. **7 of the 8
  problems are fully machine-checked end to end.**
- **1 entry is instructor-judged**: problem 8(c), "explain why the angle of
  depression at the platform equals the angle of elevation at the landing
  point". That is a written justification, so the key prints the grading rubric
  (parallel lines, transversal, alternate interior angles) instead of an answer.
  The key's "What is verified" note names problem 8 as the only one with an
  instructor-judged part, and the Quick Answers bank shows `---` there.

Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`. Five problems (2, 3, 5, 6, 7) carry declared
misconception traps, so the "Common wrong answers" block in the key identifies a
student who used sine where tangent belonged (22.94 ft), multiplied by a ratio
instead of dividing (8.12 m, 78.73 m, 45.04 ft), or forgot to add the window
height back (29.99 m).

**Study guide.** Three sections: choosing the ratio and knowing whether to
multiply or divide; recovering an angle with inverse trig; and the setup move
that makes every depression problem ordinary — slide the depression angle down
to the bottom vertex, where it is the equal angle of elevation. Each has a rule
box, a worked example that opens with the reasoning, and a try-it with its answer
printed upside down. The watch-out box flags the most common setup error:
measuring the depression angle from the vertical instead of the horizontal.
