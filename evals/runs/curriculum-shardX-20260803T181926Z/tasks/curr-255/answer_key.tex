\problem{\textbf{Expand $(x+5)(x+3)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The question asks for a sum of terms, so the product has to be
distributed --- every term of the first factor times every term of the second.

\textbf{Step 2.} $x\cdot x=x^2$, then $x\cdot 3+5\cdot x=8x$, then
$5\cdot 3=15$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Collecting the terms gives $\ans{x^2+8x+15}$.

\textit{What the form shows:} the constant $15$ is the value of the expression
at $x=0$, which the factored form only hides.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $2x(3x-7)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A monomial multiplying a binomial needs the distributive
property once; there is no FOIL here.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2x\cdot 3x=6x^2$ and $2x\cdot(-7)=-14x$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The expanded form is $\ans{6x^2-14x}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $6x^2-7$ --- the $2x$ was distributed only to the
first term.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $x^2-9x+20$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The question asks what makes the expression zero, so the goal
is a product: find two numbers whose product is $20$ and whose sum is $-9$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Both numbers must be negative because the product is positive
and the sum is negative. The pair is $-4$ and $-5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} So $x^2-9x+20=\ans{(x-4)(x-5)}$.

\textit{What the form shows:} the expression is zero exactly at $x=4$ and
$x=5$, which is unreadable in expanded form.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $x^2-49$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} There is no middle term and both pieces are perfect squares,
which is the signature of the difference-of-squares pattern
$A^2-B^2=(A-B)(A+B)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Here $A=x$ and $B=7$, since $49=7^2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Therefore $x^2-49=\ans{(x-7)(x+7)}$.

\textit{Listen for:} ``it does not factor because there is no middle term.'' The
missing middle term is the reason it \emph{does}.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $(x-6)(x+2)$ at $x=6$ without expanding.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The expression is already a product, and a product is zero as
soon as one factor is zero --- so check the factors before doing any arithmetic.

\textbf{Step 2.} At $x=6$ the first factor is $6-6=0$. The second factor is
$6+2=8$, but it no longer matters.

\textbf{Step 3.} The value is $0\cdot 8$, so the answer is $\ans{0}$.

\textit{Which form told you:} the factored one. Expanded, this is
$x^2-4x-12=36-24-12$, which is the same answer after three more steps.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $8$ --- the two factor values were added instead
of multiplied.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $(2x-3)^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A square of a binomial is that binomial times itself, so
distribute rather than squaring the two pieces separately.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(2x)^2=4x^2$; the two cross terms are each $2x\cdot(-3)=-6x$,
giving $-12x$; and $(-3)^2=9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The expanded form is $\ans{4x^2-12x+9}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $4x^2+9$ --- the two cross terms were dropped.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $3x^2+11x-4$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The leading coefficient is not $1$, so the pair of numbers has
to multiply to $3\cdot(-4)=-12$ and add to $11$.

\textbf{Step 2.} That pair is $12$ and $-1$. Split the middle term:
$3x^2+12x-x-4$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Group: $3x(x+4)-1(x+4)$, so the common factor is $x+4$ and
$3x^2+11x-4=\ans{(3x-1)(x+4)}$.

\textit{Check:} $(3x-1)(x+4)=3x^2+12x-x-4=3x^2+11x-4$.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $(x+3)^2-(x-3)^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Nothing cancels while the squares are unexpanded, so this is a
case where the expanded form is the useful one.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(x+3)^2=x^2+6x+9$ and $(x-3)^2=x^2-6x+9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtracting, the $x^2$ terms and the $9$s cancel and the
middle terms add: $6x-(-6x)$, so the result is $\ans{12x}$.

\textit{What the form shows:} the difference of these two squares is a multiple
of $x$ --- it is $0$ at $x=0$ and grows in a straight line, neither of which is
visible before expanding.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $(x+2)(x^2-2x+4)$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Each of the two terms in the first factor multiplies all three
terms in the second, so expect six products before collecting.

\textbf{Step 2.} $x(x^2-2x+4)=x^3-2x^2+4x$ and $2(x^2-2x+4)=2x^2-4x+8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Adding, $-2x^2+2x^2=0$ and $4x-4x=0$, leaving
$\ans{x^3+8}$.

\textit{What the form shows:} the product collapses to a sum of cubes,
$x^3+2^3$. Recognising that pattern backwards is how the factored form is found
in the first place.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor $2x^3-18x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Completely'' means the greatest common factor comes out
first; only then is the pattern in what is left visible.

\textbf{Step 2.} The GCF of $2x^3$ and $18x$ is $2x$, so
$2x^3-18x=2x(x^2-9)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Now $x^2-9$ is a difference of squares, giving
$\ans{2x(x-3)(x+3)}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $2x(x-3)^2$ --- $x^2-9$ was read as a perfect
square. The zeros are $x=0$, $x=3$ and $x=-3$.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $(m-3)(m+3)$ at $m=100$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Multiplying $97$ by $103$ by hand is slow, so look at the
structure first: this is a difference of squares waiting to be expanded.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(m-3)(m+3)=m^2-9$, and at $m=100$ that is $10000-9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The value is $\ans{9991}$.

\textit{Which form was chosen:} the expanded one --- here expanding replaces a
two-digit multiplication with a subtraction.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $9409$ --- the square-of-a-binomial pattern was
used instead of the difference of squares.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: $x^4-13x^2+36$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Only even powers appear, so treat $u=x^2$: the expression
is $u^2-13u+36$, an ordinary quadratic.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Two numbers multiplying to $36$ and adding to $-13$ are
$-4$ and $-9$, so $u^2-13u+36=(u-4)(u-9)=(x^2-4)(x^2-9)$.

\textbf{Step 3 (a).} Each bracket is a difference of squares, so the complete
factorisation is $\ans{(x-2)(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)}$.

\textbf{Step 4 (b).} Substituting $x=5$ into the factored form gives
$(3)(7)(2)(8)$, four small products instead of a fourth power.

\textbf{Step 5 (b).} $3\cdot 7=21$, $2\cdot 8=16$, and $21\cdot 16$ is
$\ans{336}$.

\textit{Check:} the original form gives $625-325+36=336$ as well --- the same
number, with a fourth power to compute.}
