Here are the three PDFs for a Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra workshop set on **solving
systems of two linear equations algebraically, by substitution or elimination**.

**Skills Summary (study guide)** — `ss_algsys_curr228.pdf`, 2 pages.
An opening box states what the two methods have in common (both turn two
equations in two unknowns into one equation in one unknown) and how they
differ. Three sections follow — substitution, elimination, and choosing between
them — each with a rule box saying *when* to reach for that method, a two-step
worked example, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down inside the
box. A watch-out box flags the commonest elimination slip: subtracting only the
variable terms and leaving the constant alone.

**Worksheet** — `ws_algsys_curr228.pdf`, 5 pages, 10 problems.
Every problem is a real situation with its quantities and units stated —
adults and children, hot dogs and drinks, pens and notebooks, box masses in
kilograms, ticket prices in dollars, a board cut in metres, bakery prices, gym
memberships, catering quotes, and a boat against a current. The system is
printed alongside the story so the algebra is the work, not the translation.
Substitution and elimination alternate rather than being blocked, and three
problems ask the student to name the method they chose and say why. Problem 9
is a pair of catering quotes that contradict each other, so "no solution"
arrives as a real business fact rather than an abstraction. Work space runs
6–8.5 cm per problem, with a separate blank for each part.

**Answer key** — `ak_algsys_curr228.pdf`, 3 pages.
Quick Answers bank labelled by response, then a worked solution per problem
that shows which term was eliminated or substituted and finishes with a check
in the second equation. Curriculum block: 8.EE.C.8b and 8.EE.C.8c,
difficulty 1–4.

**What is verified, honestly.** Fourteen checks were declared across the ten
problems, and **ten are machine-checked with SymPy**: every system is solved
independently and each stated pair is confirmed to satisfy *every* equation,
with the count matching SymPy's full solution set, and the contradictory
catering system is machine-confirmed inconsistent so its "no solution" is a
verified result. That matches the key's generated note: 10 of 14 answers
machine-checked across 10 problems. Worksheet prose numbers match the
verification data 38 out of 39.

**Four responses are instructor-judged, not machine-checked**: part (b) of
problems 5, 8, 9 and 10, where the student explains the method choice or what
the contradictory quotes mean. Method justification is prose and no computer
algebra system can grade it. The Quick Answers bank prints `---` for each, and
every worked solution states what a correct justification must contain and
which shortcuts do not earn credit.
