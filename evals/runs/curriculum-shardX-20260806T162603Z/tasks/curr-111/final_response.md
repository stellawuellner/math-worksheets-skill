# Quotients and Remainders with Models — Grade 4 (10 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ws_division_curr111.pdf`** — the student worksheet (4 pages)
- **`ss_division_curr111.pdf`** — a 2-page skills summary
- **`ak_division_curr111.pdf`** — the full step-by-step answer key (3 pages)

## What the worksheet covers

The whole sheet is built on the sentence *total = groups × group size + remainder*, and on the idea
that the arithmetic never changes but the meaning of the remainder does.

- **1–2** are model-first: 23 counters to circle in fives, and a tile pattern in rows of six. Each
  asks for the quotient and the remainder separately, so the child names both rather than writing
  "4 r 3" without thinking about which is which.
- **3–4** are the pair that makes the point: 38 students into 8-seat vans (round *up* — the leftover
  six still travel) and 45 cookies into boxes of 6 (drop it — three cookies do not make a box). Same
  division shape, opposite decision, and the key says so explicitly.
- **5, 9** estimate first: round the total to a friendly number, divide to get a ballpark, then divide
  exactly and see whether the answer lands where the rounding predicted.
- **6–7** read a quotient off a bar model and off an equal-groups model with leftovers, the second one
  working backwards from 7 groups plus 4 left over to the size of one group.
- **8** asks only for the remainder, which is the answer children most often replace with the quotient.
- **10** closes with all three readings of one division: 83 pencils among 6 friends gives 13 each with
  5 over, but packing the same 83 into boxes of 6 needs 14 boxes — and the child explains why.

Difficulty ramps 1 → 5.

## What is verified, and what is not

- **20 of the 22 checks are machine-verified** with SymPy, covering **7 of the 10 problems fully**.
  Every quotient, every remainder, every rounded number and every estimate was recomputed
  independently of the answer key.
- **Three responses are instructor-judged** and are marked `---` in the key's Quick Answers list:
  the circled model in problem 1 (a machine cannot see where the rings went — the key says to look
  for four rings of five with three counters outside, and flags a fifth ring round the leftovers as
  the error to catch), the explanation in 3(b), and the explanation in 10(d).
- Three **common wrong answers** are declared and machine-checked as genuinely wrong, and printed in
  the key: 4 on problem 3 (reported full vans and left six students behind), 8 on problem 4 (rounded
  the leftover cookies up into a box that is not full), and 5 on problem 8 (gave the number of bags
  instead of the marbles left over). Those three are the whole remainder-interpretation story.

## The study guide

Three sections: reading a quotient off a model, deciding what the remainder means (with the three
cases named — drop it, add one more group, or the remainder *is* the answer), and estimating before
dividing. Each has a rule box, a worked example that opens with the strategy, and a try-it with the
answer upside down inside the box. The try-it in section 2 deliberately reuses the example's numbers
with a different question, so the child sees the same division give a different answer.

The grade level and standards code (4.NBT.B.6) appear on the answer key only.
