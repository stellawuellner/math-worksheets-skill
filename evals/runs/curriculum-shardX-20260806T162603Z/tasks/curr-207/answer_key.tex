\problem{Evaluate $7^0$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Any non-zero base to the power zero is one. Dividing that base's
first power by itself is the proof: the quotient is one, and the quotient law writes the
same division with exponent zero. \ans{1}

\problem{Evaluate $4^0 + 9^0$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Each term is a non-zero base to the power zero, so each is one,
and one plus one is the sum. The bases play no part. \ans{2}

\problem{Evaluate as an exact fraction: $2^{-3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad A negative exponent means the reciprocal, so this is one over the
third power of the base, and that power is eight. \ans{\tfrac{1}{8}}

\problem{Evaluate as an exact fraction: $5^{-2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Flip first, then evaluate: one over the second power of the base,
and that power is twenty-five. \ans{\tfrac{1}{25}}

\problem{Simplify $x^3 \cdot x^{-3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Same base, so the product law adds the exponents: $3 + (-3) = 0$,
giving the base to the power zero. \ans{1}

\problem{Simplify $(2n)^0$, for $n$ not zero.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The zero exponent sits on the whole bracket, so the entire
quantity inside --- the number factor and the variable together --- is raised to the power
zero. \ans{1}

\problem{Rewrite with a positive exponent: $y^{-6}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The negative exponent moves the base across the fraction bar and
its sign flips. \ans{\dfrac{1}{y^6}}

\problem{Simplify with a positive exponent: $\dfrac{x^2}{x^7}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The quotient law subtracts: $2 - 7 = -5$, giving $x^{-5}$. There
are more factors on the bottom than the top, so the leftovers stay in the denominator.
\ans{\dfrac{1}{x^5}}

\problem{Evaluate as an exact fraction: $10^{-4}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad One over the fourth power of ten. Multiplying ten by itself four
times gives ten thousand, so this is one ten-thousandth --- the same factor that a negative
power of ten contributes in scientific notation. \ans{\tfrac{1}{10000}}

\problem{Simplify with a positive exponent: $(3m^{-2})(4m^5)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Group the number factors and the powers: $3 \cdot 4$ for the
numbers, and the product law for the powers, adding $-2 + 5$. The exponent comes out
positive, so nothing needs moving across the bar. \ans{12m^3}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between $6^0$ and $6^{-1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The zero power is one; the exponent $-1$ gives the reciprocal of
the base, which is a proper fraction. One is larger than a proper fraction, so the zero
power is the bigger quantity --- and notice both are positive, even though one exponent is
negative. \ans{>}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge.} Dara writes $3^{-2} = -9$. (a) Correct value? (b) What does
the negative exponent do?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Reciprocal first: one over the second power of the base, and
that power is nine. \ans{\tfrac{1}{9}}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Dara read the exponent's minus sign as a
minus sign on the answer. A negative exponent means reciprocal: the base moves to the
denominator, and the value stays positive --- it simply becomes small. The pattern makes it
plain: each time the exponent drops by one the value is divided by the base, so a positive
base can never cross into negative values. Full credit requires that reciprocal statement,
not just the right fraction.
