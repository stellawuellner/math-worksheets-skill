\begin{center}\small\textit{Evaluating absolute-value functions, choosing the correct
branch of a piecewise rule, and reading a piecewise model in context}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{Directions:}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every problem gives you a rule and an input. Your job is to produce the output.
  \item With absolute value, finish everything \emph{inside} the bars first, then take the
        absolute value, then do what is left outside.
  \item With a piecewise rule, \textbf{read the condition before you touch the formula}.
        Ask which interval the input falls in; only then substitute.
  \item Show the substitution, not just the answer --- the substitution is what a grader
        can follow.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\problem[8cm]{Look at $p(x)$ once more. Substituting $x = 2$ into the \emph{top} formula
gives $3(2) + 1 = 7$, and substituting it into the \emph{bottom} formula gives
$2^2 - 3 = 1$.

\smallskip
Explain in your own words: why do the two formulas disagree at $x = 2$, which value does
the definition of $p$ actually assign to $p(2)$, and what does the graph of $p$ do at that
input? Use the words \emph{branch} and \emph{condition} in your answer.}

\problem[9cm]{\textbf{Challenge.} The two branch formulas of $p$ are $y = 3x + 1$ and
$y = x^2 - 3$. Ignore the conditions for a moment and treat them as two ordinary
equations.

\smallskip
Find every point $(x, y)$ where the two formulas produce the same output. Then say
whether either of those inputs is a place where $p$ itself would be continuous, and why.}
