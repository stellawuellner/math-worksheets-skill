\sstitleblock{Adding Three-Digit Numbers with Regrouping}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{What regrouping means.} Ten ones make one ten, and ten tens make one
hundred. So a column can never hold ten or more --- the extra moves one place
to the left.\\[3pt]
\textbf{The order of work:} ones first, then tens, then hundreds. Always right
to left, because a carry only ever travels leftwards.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Where the carry goes:} write it as a small digit above the next
column, and add it in with that column.
\end{formulabox}

\skillheading{1. Add three-digit numbers with regrouping}

\begin{formulabox}
Line the numbers up by place value. Add one column at a time. If a column
total is ten or more, write its last digit under the column and carry the ten
to the next column on the left.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Add $367 + 285$.
\step{Both numbers have hundreds, tens and ones, so stack them by place and
add column by column, starting at the ones.}
\step{Ones: $7 + 5 = 12$, write $2$ and carry one ten. Tens: $6 + 8 = 14$ plus
the carried ten is $15$ tens --- write $5$, carry one hundred. Hundreds:
$3 + 2 = 5$ plus the carried hundred is $6$.}

The sum is $\ans{652}$.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Add $254 + 178$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{432}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Estimate a sum by rounding to hundreds}

\begin{formulabox}
Round each number to the nearest hundred, then add the rounded numbers. Fifty
or more rounds up, less than fifty rounds down. An estimate tells you the size
of the answer, so a sum far from it is a sign to check your carries.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Estimate $384 + 429$, then add exactly.
\step{The question asks roughly how much, so round each addend to the nearest
hundred first and add the easy numbers.}
\step{$384$ rounds up to $400$ and $429$ rounds down to $400$, and four
hundreds plus four hundreds is eight hundreds.}

Estimate: $\ans{800}$. \quad Exact sum: $\ans{813}$, close to it.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Estimate $275 + 518$ by rounding to the nearest hundred, then
add exactly.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: estimate $\ans{800}$, exact $\ans{793}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Add to answer a story question}

\begin{formulabox}
Words like \emph{altogether}, \emph{in all}, \emph{both} and \emph{total} mean
the two amounts are being put together, so add them. Read the last sentence
again before you write your answer, and label what your number counts.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} The library has $236$ picture books and $185$ chapter books.
How many books in all?
\step{The question asks for both groups together, and putting two amounts
together is addition.}
\step{Add $236 + 185$: ones $6 + 5 = 11$, write $1$ carry one ten; tens
$3 + 8 = 11$ plus the carry is $12$ tens --- write $2$, carry one hundred;
hundreds $2 + 1 = 3$ plus the carry is $4$.}

There are $\ans{421}$ books.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A shop sold $348$ apples on Saturday and $276$ apples on
Sunday. How many apples is that altogether?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{624}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} the two mistakes that cost the most marks are writing
a two-digit total under one column, and carrying a ten but then forgetting to
add it in. Both leave a sum that is far from your estimate --- which is
exactly why the estimate is worth doing first.
\end{watchoutbox}
