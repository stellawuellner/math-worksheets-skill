\wstitleblock{Solving Systems Algebraically by Substitution or Elimination}{Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra \quad Expressions and Equations}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Solving by substitution, eliminating by adding or
subtracting, and eliminating after scaling one or both equations}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Before you solve, decide how.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Substitution} is fastest when one equation already gives a
        variable by itself, or gives it after one easy step.
  \item \textbf{Elimination by adding or subtracting} works when a variable has
        the same coefficient, or opposite coefficients, in both equations.
  \item \textbf{Elimination after scaling} is the general method: multiply one
        or both equations so a variable's coefficients match, then add or
        subtract.
  \item Every problem here names two real quantities. Write what $x$ and $y$
        stand for, \emph{with their units}, before you write either equation,
        and check your pair in \textbf{both} original equations at the end.
\end{itemize}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{School play.} A school play sold 40 tickets in all. The
number of child tickets sold was 3 times the number of adult tickets sold.

\noindent Let $x$ be the number of adult tickets and $y$ the number of child
tickets, so
\[ x + y = 40, \qquad y = 3x. \]
Solve by substitution. How many of each kind of ticket were sold?}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Garden.} A rectangular garden has a perimeter of 36
metres. Its length is 4 metres more than its width.

\noindent Let $x$ be the length in metres and $y$ the width in metres, so
\[ 2x + 2y = 36, \qquad x - y = 4. \]
Rearrange the second equation and solve by substitution. Give the length and the
width in metres.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Boat and current.} A boat travels downstream at 18 km/h
and upstream at 12 km/h. Downstream the current helps the boat; upstream it
holds the boat back.

\noindent Let $x$ be the boat's speed in still water and $y$ the current's
speed, both in km/h, so
\[ x + y = 18, \qquad x - y = 12. \]
The $y$ terms are already opposites. Add the equations to eliminate $y$, then
find both speeds.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Cafe order.} Four muffins and three juices cost 17
dollars. Four muffins and five juices cost 23 dollars.

\noindent Let $x$ be the price of one muffin and $y$ the price of one juice, in
dollars, so
\[ 4x + 3y = 17, \qquad 4x + 5y = 23. \]
Nothing needs multiplying here. Explain in one line which operation --- adding
or subtracting --- removes $x$, then carry it out and find both prices.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Stationery.} Three pens and two notebooks cost 16 dollars.
Five pens and four notebooks cost 30 dollars.

\noindent Let $x$ be the price of a pen and $y$ the price of a notebook, in
dollars, so
\[ 3x + 2y = 16, \qquad 5x + 4y = 30. \]
Multiplying \emph{one} equation is enough to line up a variable. Say which
equation you scale and by what, then solve.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Ticket sales.} A concert sold 200 tickets in total. Adult
tickets cost 5 dollars each and student tickets cost 3 dollars each, and the
sale brought in 780 dollars.

\noindent Let $x$ be the number of adult tickets and $y$ the number of student
tickets, so
\[ x + y = 200, \qquad 5x + 3y = 780. \]
Scale the first equation so that one variable cancels, then solve. How many
tickets of each kind were sold?}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Two gyms.} Gym A charges a 20 dollar joining fee plus 5
dollars a month. Gym B charges a 50 dollar joining fee plus 2 dollars a month.

\noindent Let $x$ be the number of months and $y$ the total cost in dollars, so
\[ y = 20 + 5x, \qquad y = 50 + 2x. \]
Both equations already give $y$, so substitution is one step: set the two
expressions for $y$ equal. After how many months is the total cost the same, and
what is that cost?}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Crates.} Four crates of apples and three crates of pears
weigh 30 kilograms. Three crates of apples and five crates of pears weigh 28
kilograms.

\noindent Let $x$ be the weight of an apple crate and $y$ the weight of a pear
crate, in kilograms, so
\[ 4x + 3y = 30, \qquad 3x + 5y = 28. \]
Here no coefficient matches, so \emph{both} equations must be scaled. Choose a
variable, write the two multipliers you will use, and solve.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Two receipts.} One receipt says two sandwiches and three
drinks cost 19 dollars. A second receipt from the same shop, on the same day,
says four sandwiches and six drinks cost 34 dollars.

\noindent Let $x$ be the price of a sandwich and $y$ the price of a drink, in
dollars, so
\[ 2x + 3y = 19, \qquad 4x + 6y = 34. \]
Try to eliminate a variable. Write down exactly what happens, say what the
result tells you about the two receipts, and explain what it means for these two
lines that they never meet.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Challenge --- sports equipment.} A school buys 3 footballs
and 2 basketballs for 31 dollars, and 5 footballs and 4 basketballs for 55
dollars.

\noindent Let $x$ be the price of a football and $y$ the price of a basketball,
in dollars.

\textbf{(a)} Write the system and solve it by elimination. Give both prices, and
check them in both original purchases.

\textbf{(b)} A supplier's catalogue claims that 6 footballs and 4 basketballs
cost 60 dollars. Show that this claim cannot be true alongside the two purchases
above, and explain what it means for three equations in two unknowns to have no
solution that fits all three.}
