\noindent\small Each solution names the unknown-factor equation first, then the multiplication
fact that unlocks it. Accept a matching division sentence as equivalent reasoning.\normalsize

\problem{Maya's stickers: 4 equal rows, 24 stickers in all. How many in each row?

\textbf{Solution:} The rows are the known factor, so the equation is $4 \times ? = 24$.
Count up by fours: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24$ --- that is six fours. The related division fact
$24 \div 4$ says the same thing. Each row has $\ans{6}$ stickers.}

\problem{28 seeds planted in equal rows of 4. How many rows?

\textbf{Solution:} Here the row size is the known factor, so the unknown factor counts the rows:
$? \times 4 = 28$. From the four-times table, $7 \times 4 = 28$, so the missing factor is 7.
The picture confirms it: seven rows of four dots. Number of rows: $\ans{7}$.}

\problem{Find the unknown factor: $7 \times ? = 56$.

\textbf{Solution:} Ask ``seven times what makes 56?'' Skip-count by sevens:
$7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56$ --- the eighth number. Check with the related division fact:
$56 \div 7 = 8$. The unknown factor is $\ans{8}$.}

\problem{Nina shares 45 stickers equally among 5 friends.

\textbf{Solution:} ``5 equal groups, 45 in all'' is $5 \times ? = 45$. The five-times table gives
$5 \times 9 = 45$, and the related division fact $45 \div 5 = 9$ agrees. Each friend gets
$\ans{9}$ stickers.}

\problem{Sam knows $6 \times 8 = 48$. Use it to find $48 \div 8$.

\textbf{Solution:} A division question asks for a missing factor: $48 \div 8$ means
$8 \times ? = 48$. Sam's fact $6 \times 8 = 48$ already supplies that missing factor, because
multiplication can be done in either order. So no new work is needed:
$48 \div 8 = \ans{6}$.}

\problem{Find the unknown factor: $? \times 9 = 72$.

\textbf{Solution:} The unknown factor is in front, but the question is the same: ``what times 9
makes 72?'' From the nine-times table, $8 \times 9 = 72$. Check by dividing:
$72 \div 9 = 8$. The unknown factor is $\ans{8}$.}

\problem{A tray holds 42 muffin cups in equal rows of 6. How many rows?

\textbf{Solution:} Each row is a group of 6, and the total is 42, so $? \times 6 = 42$.
Count by sixes: $6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42$ --- seven sixes. Related division fact:
$42 \div 6 = 7$. The tray has $\ans{7}$ rows.}

\problem{6 tables, the same number of chairs at each, 54 chairs in all.

\textbf{Solution:} Six equal groups with a total of 54 gives $6 \times ? = 54$. Nine sixes make
54, since $6 \times 9 = 54$; the related division fact is $54 \div 6 = 9$. Each table has
$\ans{9}$ chairs.}

\problem{A mystery number times 7 equals 42. What is that mystery number times 8?

\textbf{Solution:} Step 1 --- find the mystery number with an unknown-factor equation:
$7 \times ? = 42$, and since $7 \times 6 = 42$, the mystery number is 6.
Step 2 --- multiply that number by 8: $6 \times 8 = 48$.
The answer is $\ans{48}$. (Watch the trap: the question does not ask for the mystery number
itself, which is 6.)}

\problem{48 rolls, 6 rolls per bag, 2 bags per box. How many boxes?

\textbf{Solution:} Step 1 --- bags. Each bag is a group of 6 rolls out of 48, so
$6 \times ? = 48$, and $6 \times 8 = 48$ gives 8 bags.
Step 2 --- boxes. Each box holds 2 bags out of those 8 bags, so $2 \times ? = 8$, and
$2 \times 4 = 8$ gives 4 boxes.
The baker fills $\ans{4}$ boxes.}
