\problem{Expand $(x + 1)^3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Row $3$ of Pascal's triangle is $1, 3, 3, 1$, and every power of $1$ is
$1$, so the coefficients are the row itself. \ans{x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Expand $(x - 4)^3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same row $1, 3, 3, 1$, now with $B = -4$, so the powers $(-4)^1$,
$(-4)^2$, $(-4)^3$ alternate in sign.
\begin{align*}
  (x - 4)^3 &= x^3 + 3x^2(-4) + 3x(16) + (-64)
\end{align*}
\ans{x^3 - 12x^2 + 48x - 64}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Coefficient of $x^3$ in $(x + 2)^5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} With $n = 5$, the $x^3$ term needs $x$ three times and $2$ twice, so
$k = 2$.
\[ \binom{5}{2} x^3 2^2 = 10 \cdot 4 \cdot x^3 \]
The coefficient is \ans{40}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Sum of all coefficients of $(2x + 3)^4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substituting $x = 1$ turns every power of $x$ into $1$, so each term
collapses to its own coefficient and the whole expression becomes the coefficient total.
\[ (2 \cdot 1 + 3)^4 = 5^4 \]
\ans{625}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Evaluate $\sum_{k=0}^{5}\binom{5}{k}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The sum runs over the whole of row $5$: $1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1$.
\[ 1 + 5 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 1 = 32 \]
\ans{32}
This is $2^5$, and that is not a coincidence: it is problem $4$'s substitution with
$A = B = 1$.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Expand $(2x - 1)^4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Row $4$ is $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$, with $A = 2x$ (so each power of $A$ squares,
cubes, or fourth-powers the $2$ as well) and $B = -1$.
\begin{align*}
  (2x - 1)^4 &= 16x^4 + 4(8x^3)(-1) + 6(4x^2)(1) + 4(2x)(-1) + 1
\end{align*}
\ans{16x^4 - 32x^3 + 24x^2 - 8x + 1}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Coefficient of $x^2$ in $(3x - 2)^5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $x^2$ term takes $3x$ twice and $-2$ three times, so $k = 3$.
\begin{align*}
  \binom{5}{3}(3x)^2(-2)^3 &= 10 \cdot 9x^2 \cdot (-8)
\end{align*}
The whole factor $3x$ is squared, and $(-2)^3$ keeps its sign. The coefficient is
\ans{-720}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{(a) Compute $\binom{9}{3}$. (b) Why does it equal $\binom{9}{6}$?}

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\[ \binom{9}{3} = \frac{9!}{3!\,6!} = \frac{9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{6} = \frac{504}{6} \]
\ans{84}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Choosing which $3$ of the $9$ factors contribute
their second term is the same act as choosing which $6$ do not, so the two counts must be
equal. Written out, both are $9!$ over $3!\,6!$ --- the same denominator with its two factors
in the other order. In the expansion this is the left-right mirror symmetry of a row of
Pascal's triangle. Full credit gives a reason of that kind. Computing $\binom{9}{6} = 84$ and
noting the match is a check, not an explanation --- partial credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Evaluate $\sum_{n=0}^{5} 2^n$.}

\textbf{Solution:} This is a geometric series with first term $1$ and ratio $2$, six terms
long.
\[ 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 = \frac{2^6 - 1}{2 - 1} \]
\ans{63}
Each term is one row's coefficient total, so this is the number of entries' worth of weight
in the first six rows of Pascal's triangle taken together.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Coefficient of $x^4y^2$ in $(x + y)^6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Two letters, no numerical factors, so the coefficient is the binomial
coefficient alone. The $y$ appears twice, so $k = 2$:
\[ \binom{6}{2} = \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2} = \frac{30}{2} \]
\ans{15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Expand $(x^2 + 3)^3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Treat $A = x^2$, so each power of $A$ doubles the exponent: $A^3 = x^6$,
$A^2 = x^4$, $A^1 = x^2$. Row $3$ is $1, 3, 3, 1$.
\begin{align*}
  (x^2 + 3)^3 &= x^6 + 3x^4(3) + 3x^2(9) + 27
\end{align*}
\ans{x^6 + 9x^4 + 27x^2 + 27}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{(a) Expand $(1 + x)^4$. (b) Use it to evaluate $3^4$. (c) Find $n$ with coefficient sum $512$, and explain.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Row $4$ is $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$, and every power of $1$ is $1$:
\ans{x^4 + 4x^3 + 6x^2 + 4x + 1}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $3^4 = (1 + 2)^4$, so substitute $x = 2$:
\[ 16 + 4(8) + 6(4) + 4(2) + 1 = 16 + 32 + 24 + 8 + 1 \]
\ans{81}

\textbf{Solution (c):} Substituting $x = 1$ makes every power of $x$ equal to $1$, so the
expansion collapses to the sum of its coefficients --- and the left side becomes $2^n$.
\begin{align*}
  2^n &= 512 = 2^9
\end{align*}
\ans{n = 9}

\vspace{0.1cm}
\textbf{The written half of (c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response says that
substituting $1$ for the letter turns every power into $1$, so each term is left as its own
coefficient and the total of the expansion is the total of the coefficients; since
$1 + 1 = 2$, that total is $2^n$. Full credit connects the substitution to the collapse of
the powers. Writing $2^n = 512$ and solving, with no account of \emph{why} the coefficient
sum is a power of $2$, answers only half the question.
