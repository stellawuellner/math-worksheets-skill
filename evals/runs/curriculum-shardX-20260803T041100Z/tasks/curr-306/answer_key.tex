\noindent\textbf{How to grade this.} The pair \emph{name} is half of each
answer. A correct measure with the wrong name (``vertical'' where the student
used corresponding angles) shows the arithmetic worked and the reasoning did
not; mark the name. Angle numbering follows the sheet's diagram: angles 1--4
around the upper intersection (1 upper right, 2 upper left, 3 lower left,
4 lower right) and 5--8 around the lower one, same order.

\problem{Name each pair: $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$; $\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$;
$\angle 2$ and $\angle 8$.

\textbf{Solution:} Read position, not size.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$ sit in the \emph{same} corner at their own
        intersections (upper right at each): \textbf{corresponding angles}.
  \item $\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$ are both between the parallel lines and on
        \emph{opposite} sides of $t$: \textbf{alternate interior angles}.
  \item $\angle 2$ and $\angle 8$ are both outside the parallel lines and on
        opposite sides of $t$: \textbf{alternate exterior angles}.
\end{itemize}
All three pairs are congruent pairs, because $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$.}

\problem{$m\angle 1 = 112^\circ$. Find $m\angle 5$, then $m\angle 6$.

\textbf{Solution:} $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$ are corresponding angles and the
lines are parallel, so they are congruent: $\ans{m\angle 5 = 112^\circ}$.

Next, $\angle 5$ and $\angle 6$ are adjacent along $\ell_2$, a linear pair, so
they are supplementary: $180^\circ - 112^\circ = \ans{m\angle 6 = 68^\circ}$.}

\problem{$m\angle 4 = 65^\circ$. Find $m\angle 6$, then $m\angle 5$.

\textbf{Solution:} $\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$ are alternate interior angles
(both between the lines, opposite sides of $t$), so they are congruent:
$\ans{m\angle 6 = 65^\circ}$.

$\angle 4$ and $\angle 5$ are same-side interior angles (both between the
lines, same side of $t$), so they are supplementary rather than congruent:
$180^\circ - 65^\circ = \ans{m\angle 5 = 115^\circ}$. The two names are what
decide congruent versus supplementary.}

\problem{Corresponding angles: $m\angle 2 = (3x+10)^\circ$,
$m\angle 6 = (5x-24)^\circ$.

\textbf{Solution:} Corresponding angles on parallel lines are congruent, so set
the expressions equal --- that is the equation the pair name justifies.
\begin{align*}
  3x + 10 &= 5x - 24 \\
  34 &= 2x \\
  x &= 17
\end{align*}
So $\ans{x = 17}$, and substituting back,
$m\angle 2 = 3(17) + 10 = \ans{61^\circ}$. Check the partner:
$5(17) - 24 = 61$ as well.}

\problem{$m\angle 7 = 74^\circ$. Find $m\angle 1$, then $m\angle 4$.

\textbf{Solution:} $\angle 7$ and $\angle 1$ are alternate exterior angles
(both outside the parallel lines, opposite sides of $t$), so they are
congruent: $\ans{m\angle 1 = 74^\circ}$.

$\angle 1$ and $\angle 4$ are adjacent at the upper intersection --- a linear
pair along $t$ --- so they are supplementary:
$180^\circ - 74^\circ = \ans{m\angle 4 = 106^\circ}$.}

\problem{Same-side interior: $m\angle 3 = (2x+15)^\circ$,
$m\angle 6 = (3x+40)^\circ$.

\textbf{Solution:} Same-side interior angles on parallel lines are
supplementary, so the expressions \emph{sum} to $180$; setting them equal is the
standard error here.
\begin{align*}
  (2x + 15) + (3x + 40) &= 180 \\
  5x + 55 &= 180 \\
  x &= 25
\end{align*}
So $\ans{x = 25}$ and $m\angle 3 = 2(25) + 15 = \ans{65^\circ}$. Check:
$m\angle 6 = 3(25) + 40 = 115$, and $65 + 115 = 180$.}

\problem{Alternate exterior: $m\angle 2 = (4x-9)^\circ$,
$m\angle 8 = (3x+21)^\circ$.

\textbf{Solution:} Alternate exterior angles are congruent, so
\begin{align*}
  4x - 9 &= 3x + 21 \\
  x &= 30
\end{align*}
giving $\ans{x = 30}$ and $m\angle 2 = 4(30) - 9 = \ans{111^\circ}$.
Second check: $m\angle 8 = 3(30) + 21 = 111$ too, and $\angle 7$ is the linear
pair partner of $\angle 8$ along $\ell_2$, so $m\angle 7 = 180 - 111 = 69$.}

\problem{Explain why $\angle 3$ and $\angle 6$ are supplementary when
$\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$. \emph{(Manual review --- accept any correct chain.)}

\textbf{Model answer:} $\angle 3$ and $\angle 5$ are alternate interior angles,
so with $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$ they are congruent:
$m\angle 3 = m\angle 5$. Angles $5$ and $6$ are adjacent along $\ell_2$, a
linear pair, so $m\angle 5 + m\angle 6 = 180$. Substituting the first statement
into the second gives $m\angle 3 + m\angle 6 = 180$, which is what
supplementary means.

An equally good chain runs through corresponding angles: $\angle 3$ and
$\angle 7$ are corresponding, hence congruent, and $\angle 7$ and $\angle 6$
form a linear pair. What must be present for full credit is a named congruent
pair, a named supplementary pair, and the substitution that joins them ---
``they look supplementary'' is not a reason.}
