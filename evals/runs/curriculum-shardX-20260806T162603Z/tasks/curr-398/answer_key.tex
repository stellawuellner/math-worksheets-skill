\problem{Whispering gallery floor $\frac{x^2}{169} + \frac{y^2}{144} = 1$ (metres). (a) Major axis. (b) Minor axis.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The denominators are $a^2$ and $b^2$. The larger, $169$, sits under
$x^2$, so the long axis is horizontal with half-length $\sqrt{169} = 13$; the full major axis
is twice that. \ans{26 \text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The half-length of the short axis is $\sqrt{144} = 12$, so the minor
axis is $24$. \ans{24 \text{ m}}
The room is $26$ metres across at its widest and $24$ metres across at its narrowest.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Distance between the two foci of $\frac{x^2}{169} + \frac{y^2}{144} = 1$ (metres).}

\textbf{Solution:} For an ellipse the foci sit inside, so $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ --- subtract.
\begin{align*}
  c &= \sqrt{169 - 144} = \sqrt{25} = 5
\end{align*}
The two listening spots are $2c$ apart. \ans{10 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Height of the arch $\frac{x^2}{400} + \frac{y^2}{144} = 1$ at $x = 10$ (feet).}

\textbf{Solution:} Solve the equation for $y$ and substitute $x = 10$; the arch is the upper
half, so take the positive root.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{y^2}{144} &= 1 - \frac{100}{400} = \frac{3}{4} \\
  y^2 &= 108 \\
  y &= \sqrt{108} = 6\sqrt{3}
\end{align*}
\ans{6\sqrt{3} \text{ ft}}
That is about $10.4$ feet, comfortably below the $12$-foot centre height, as it must be.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Cooling tower $\frac{x^2}{64} - \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$ (metres). (a) Vertex separation. (b) Focal distance.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The $x^2$ term is the positive one, so the branches open left and
right and the vertices sit at $\pm\sqrt{64} = \pm 8$. The distance across the waist is twice
that. \ans{16 \text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} For a hyperbola $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ --- \emph{add}, the opposite of the
ellipse rule, because the foci sit beyond the vertices.
\[ c = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} \]
\ans{10 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Asymptotes of $\frac{x^2}{64} - \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$. (a) Rising slope. (b) What they describe.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} For this orientation the asymptotes are $y = \pm\frac{b}{a}x$ with
$a = \sqrt{64} = 8$ and $b = \sqrt{36} = 6$.
\[ \frac{6}{8} \]
The rising asymptote has slope \ans{\dfrac{3}{4}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} High above the waist the tower wall follows the
asymptotes almost exactly, so the silhouette there is effectively two straight lines of slope
$\pm\frac{3}{4}$ --- but the wall never actually reaches them; the gap keeps shrinking without
ever closing. Full credit says both things: the wall gets arbitrarily close, and it never
touches. Describing the asymptotes as the edges of the tower, or as a line the wall meets,
is the misconception this part exists to catch.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Planetarium dome $\frac{x^2}{100} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$ (metres). (a) Focal distance. (b) Cable length.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ for an ellipse:
\[ c = \sqrt{100 - 36} = \sqrt{64} \]
\ans{8 \text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The defining property of an ellipse is that the two focal distances
add to the same total everywhere on the curve, and that total is the major axis $2a$.
Checking at a vertex: the near focus is $a - c = 2$ away and the far one $a + c = 18$,
totalling $20$. \ans{20 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Hyperbolic mirror $\frac{y^2}{9} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1$ (centimetres). (a) Vertex distance. (b) Focal distance.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The $y^2$ term is positive, so the branches open up and down and the
vertices lie $\sqrt{9}$ from the centre. \ans{3 \text{ cm}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Adding, as a hyperbola requires:
\[ c = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} \]
\ans{5 \text{ cm}}
The focus is further from the centre than the vertex, which is always true for a hyperbola
and never for an ellipse.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Shadow line $\frac{y^2}{25} - \frac{x^2}{144} = 1$ (centimetres). (a) Rising asymptote slope. (b) Vertex distance.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With the $y^2$ term positive the asymptotes are
$y = \pm\frac{a}{b}x$, where $a = \sqrt{25} = 5$ sits over $y^2$ and $b = \sqrt{144} = 12$
over $x^2$. The rising slope is \ans{\dfrac{5}{12}}
Note the orientation: for an up-down hyperbola the slope is $a/b$, not $b/a$.

\textbf{Solution (b):} The vertices are $\sqrt{25}$ from the centre. \ans{5 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Garden bed $30$ ft by $18$ ft; distance between the foci.}

\textbf{Solution:} The stated measurements are the full axes, so halve them first:
$a = 15$ and $b = 9$.
\begin{align*}
  c &= \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144} = 12
\end{align*}
The sprinklers are $2c$ apart. \ans{24 \text{ ft}}
Halving first is the whole problem here: using $30$ and $18$ directly gives a nonsense
answer larger than the bed.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Gallery $40$ m wide with foci $32$ m apart. (a) Ceiling height. (b) Why wider foci means a lower ceiling.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Halve both given measurements: the semi-major axis is $a = 20$ and the
focal distance is $c = 16$. For an ellipse $b^2 = a^2 - c^2$, and $b$ is the height at the
centre.
\begin{align*}
  b &= \sqrt{20^2 - 16^2} = \sqrt{400 - 256} = \sqrt{144}
\end{align*}
\ans{12 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The width is fixed, so $a = 20$ does not move,
and the height comes from $b = \sqrt{a^2 - c^2}$. Pushing the listening spots apart increases
$c$, which increases the amount being subtracted, so $b$ falls. Geometrically: as the foci
slide out toward the ends of the ellipse, the curve has to flatten, and in the limit it
collapses onto the segment joining them. Full credit connects a larger focal separation to a
smaller height with a reason --- either the algebraic chain or the flattening argument ---
rather than asserting it.
