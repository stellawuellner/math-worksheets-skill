Here are three PDFs on normal-model z-scores and percentiles, for a precalculus / advanced statistics student.

**Worksheet (`ws_normal_curr447.pdf`, 5 pages, 12 problems)**
A twelve-entry standard normal table is printed at the top of page 1 and every problem uses it, so no calculator or external table is needed and the numbers cannot drift. The sequence runs the loop in both directions and then makes the student choose which direction to use: standardise a value (1, 2, 5), turn a z-score into a left-tail area and a percentile (3, 4, 6), reverse it to find the value at a given percentile or above a given cut-off (7, 8), compare two results measured on different scales (9), turn a proportion into a probability in lowest terms (10), estimate $\mu$ and $\sigma$ from raw data and then standardise inside it (11), and close with a four-part quality-control problem where the machine's mean has to be reset to hit a target underfill rate (12). Difficulty runs 1 to 5 with no dips.

**Answer key (`ak_normal_curr447.pdf`, 3 pages)**
Every problem restated and worked, with the reasoning that transfers rather than the answer alone — the left-tail convention stated each time it matters, right-tail statements converted before the table is read, and the $n$ versus $n-1$ decision spelled out in problem 11. Problem 12(c) also flags something a student usually misses: relaxing the accepted underfill rate from 0.0228 to 0.1003 *lowers* the required mean rather than raising it. The key opens with a Quick Answers strip, a Curriculum block (level, HSS-ID.A–HSS-ID.C and HSS-IC.A–HSS-IC.B with the problems each covers, difficulty range), and a Common wrong answers list generated from the three declared traps — 0.7 on problem 2 means the subtraction was done mean-minus-value; 0.9332 on problem 4 means the table entry was reported instead of the complement; 171.28 on problem 7 means $z$ was added to the mean without multiplying by $\sigma$.

**Study guide (`ss_normal_curr447.pdf`, 2 pages)**
Four sections, each with a rule box, a worked example whose first step names why that tool applies, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box: standardising, z to percentile (the same table is reprinted here so the guide is usable on its own), percentile back to a value, and comparing across distributions. The reverse-direction box states the two rules that cause most of the lost marks — multiply by $\sigma$ before adding $\mu$, and convert "top $p\%$" into a left-tail area first.

**What was verified.** 23 of the 25 checks were recomputed independently: every z-score, every table lookup (the printed table and the checks read the same numbers, so the sheet and the key cannot disagree), the complement and between-two-values subtractions, both reverse conversions, the probability in lowest terms, and the mean and standard deviation of the seven-athlete squad. All 14 study-guide values were verified the same way. Two items are flagged for manual review, correctly: the written interpretation in problem 9 and the normality-assumption explanation in problem 12(d). The key gives a model answer for both.
