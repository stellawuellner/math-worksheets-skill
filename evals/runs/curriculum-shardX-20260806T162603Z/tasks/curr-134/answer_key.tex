\problem{Multiply: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{4}{5}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply straight across --- numerator times numerator,
denominator times denominator:
\[ \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 5} = \frac{8}{15} \]
Nothing cancels, so the product is already in simplest form. \ans{\tfrac{8}{15}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Multiply, simplest form: $\dfrac{5}{6} \times \dfrac{3}{10}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply straight across, then simplify (or cancel a top
number with a bottom number first --- $5$ with $10$, $3$ with $6$):
\[ \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{10} = \frac{5 \times 3}{6 \times 10}
   = \frac{15}{60} \]
Dividing top and bottom by $15$ gives the answer. \ans{\tfrac{1}{4}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Divide: $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Keep the first fraction, change $\div$ to $\times$, flip the
\emph{second} fraction (the divisor):
\[ \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{2}
   = \frac{15}{8} \]
As a mixed number that is $1\tfrac{7}{8}$. \ans{\tfrac{15}{8}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Jordan's division $\dfrac{5}{8} \div \dfrac{1}{2}$ (find and fix)}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) Jordan flipped the \emph{first} fraction: $\dfrac{5}{8}$ became
$\dfrac{8}{5}$. The keep--change--flip rule flips only the \emph{divisor}, the
fraction you are dividing by ($\dfrac{1}{2}$). A correct response must say that
the wrong fraction was inverted.

(b) Done correctly:
\[ \frac{5}{8} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{2}{1} = \frac{10}{8} \]
which reduces to the answer. \ans{\tfrac{5}{4}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Flour: $6$ cups, each loaf uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup}

\textbf{Solution:} ``How many $\tfrac{3}{4}$s fit in $6$?'' is a division:
\[ 6 \div \frac{3}{4} = 6 \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{24}{3} \]
So Mr.\ Diaz can make \ans{8} loaves.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ava's product $\dfrac{2}{9} \times \dfrac{3}{4}$ (find and fix)}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) Ava \emph{cross}-multiplied ($2 \times 4$ over $9 \times 3$) as if she were
solving a proportion. Multiplying fractions goes straight across: top times
top over bottom times bottom. A correct response must name the cross-multiply
(or ``multiplied diagonally'') mistake.

(b) Done correctly:
\[ \frac{2}{9} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 3}{9 \times 4}
   = \frac{6}{36} \]
which simplifies to the answer. \ans{\tfrac{1}{6}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ribbon: $\dfrac{5}{6}$ m cut into $\dfrac{1}{12}$-m pieces}

\textbf{Solution:} ``How many $\tfrac{1}{12}$s fit in $\tfrac{5}{6}$?'' is a
division:
\[ \frac{5}{6} \div \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{12}{1}
   = \frac{60}{6} \]
So there are \ans{10} pieces.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: $2\tfrac{1}{2} \div \dfrac{5}{8}$}

\textbf{Solution:} First rewrite the mixed number as an improper fraction:
$2\tfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2}$. Then keep--change--flip:
\[ \frac{5}{2} \div \frac{5}{8} = \frac{5}{2} \times \frac{8}{5}
   = \frac{40}{10} \]
which is the whole number \ans{4}.
