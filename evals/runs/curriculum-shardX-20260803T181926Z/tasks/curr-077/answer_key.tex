\problem{Ana already knows that $4 \times 3 = 12$. Use the turnaround fact to find $3 \times 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Swapping the two factors does not change the product --- four rows of three counters and three rows of four counters hold the same number. So $3 \times 4$ has the same answer as $4 \times 3$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{12}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ben already knows that $8 \times 2 = 16$. Use the turnaround fact to find $2 \times 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same turnaround: $2 \times 8$ and $8 \times 2$ are the same fact written the other way round.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{16}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Break apart to multiply: \quad $6 \times 7 = 6 \times (5 + 2)$}

\textbf{Solution:} The $7$ has been split into $5 + 2$, so multiply $6$ by each piece and add:
\[ 6 \times 5 = 30, \qquad 6 \times 2 = 12, \qquad 30 + 12 = 42 \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{42}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Break apart to multiply: \quad $7 \times 8 = (7 \times 4) + (7 \times 4)$}

\textbf{Solution:} The $8$ has been split into two fours, so work out one half and double it:
\[ 7 \times 4 = 28, \qquad 28 + 28 = 56 \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{56}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find both products: \quad $9 \times 5$ \quad and \quad $5 \times 9$}

\textbf{Solution:} Both are the same fact turned around, so both give the same product. Counting by fives nine times, or by nines five times, lands on the same number.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{45}

Noticing this halves how many facts have to be memorised: learn one of the pair and the other comes free.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Use a missing-factor fact to divide: \quad $24 \div 6$}

\textbf{Solution:} Dividing asks how many groups of $6$ make $24$. Think through the sixes: $6, 12, 18, 24$ --- that is four of them, so $6 \times 4 = 24$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{n = 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Nines are easier from tens: \quad $9 \times 6 = (10 \times 6) - (1 \times 6)$}

\textbf{Solution:} Nine groups of six is one group short of ten groups of six:
\[ 10 \times 6 = 60, \qquad 1 \times 6 = 6, \qquad 60 - 6 = 54 \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{54}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Break $8 \times 9$ apart into two easier facts, then write the product.}

\textbf{Solution:} Any split of one factor works. Splitting the $9$ into $5 + 4$ gives two facts most students already know:
\[ 8 \times 5 = 40, \qquad 8 \times 4 = 32, \qquad 40 + 32 = 72 \]
Other splits reach the same product --- for example $8 \times 9 = (8 \times 10) - 8$. Accept any correct pair of facts with the right total.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{72}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Malik says $7 \times 9$ gives the same answer as $7 \times 10$ with one group of $7$ taken away. Use his strategy.}

\textbf{Solution:} Malik is right. Nine groups of seven is ten groups of seven with one whole group removed:
\[ 7 \times 10 = 70, \qquad 70 - 7 = 63 \]
The number taken away is a whole group of $7$, not $1$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{63}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{There are $35$ stickers shared equally among $5$ friends. Write the missing-factor fact, then find how many stickers each friend gets.}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing $35$ into $5$ equal groups asks: five times what makes thirty-five? The matching fact is $5 \times n = 35$, and the fives run $5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35$ --- seven of them. Each friend gets seven stickers.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{n = 7}
