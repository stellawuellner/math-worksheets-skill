\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt{144}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A square root asks for the factor used \emph{twice}, so search
the square row of the table rather than dividing.

\textbf{Step 2.} $12 \times 12 = 144$, so the root is exact.
\ans{12}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $72$ --- the radicand was halved. Halving is not
rooting: $72 \times 72$ is nowhere near $144$.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{27}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A cube root asks for the factor used \emph{three} times.

\textbf{Step 2.} $27 = 3 \times 3 \times 3$, so the cube root is $3$.
\ans{3}

\textit{Why not $9$:} $9$ is $27 \div 3$, and $9 \times 9 \times 9 = 729$, not
$27$. Dividing by three and taking a cube root are different operations.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt{225}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $225$ is past most memorised tables, so factor it into pieces
that are perfect squares: $225 = 9 \times 25$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The square root of a product is the product of the roots:
$\sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{25} = 3 \times 5$.
\ans{15}

\textit{Check:} $15 \times 15 = 225$.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt{\frac{49}{64}}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The root of a quotient is the quotient of the roots, so the
numerator and denominator can be handled separately:
$\sqrt{\frac{49}{64}} = \frac{\sqrt{49}}{\sqrt{64}}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Both are perfect squares: $\sqrt{49} = 7$ and $\sqrt{64} = 8$.
\ans{\tfrac{7}{8}}

\textit{Check:} $\frac{7}{8} \times \frac{7}{8} = \frac{49}{64}$. The answer
stays a fraction --- there is nothing to round.}

\problem{\textbf{Locate and approximate $\sqrt{60}$.}

\textbf{(a)} $60$ is not in the square row, so trap it between the two nearest
entries: $49 < 60 < 64$, which gives $7 < \sqrt{60} < 8$.

\textbf{(b)} $\sqrt{60} = 7.74596\ldots$, which rounds to
\ans{7.75}

\textbf{(c)} The rounded value terminates, so $7.75 = \frac{31}{4}$ is rational,
while $\sqrt{60}$ is irrational --- its decimal never ends and never repeats.
$7.75$ is a stand-in, not an equality. (Accept any sentence making that
distinction; $7.75^2 = 60.0625 \neq 60$ is also a complete answer.)

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $30$ --- the radicand was halved.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{343}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The index is $3$, so the cube row is the right table --- the
square row would answer a different question.

\textbf{Step 2.} $343 = 7 \times 7 \times 7$, and checking, $7^2 = 49$ then
$49 \times 7 = 343$.
\ans{7}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} about $18.5$ --- $\sqrt{343}$ instead of
$\sqrt[3]{343}$. The index tells you how many equal factors to look for.}

\problem{\textbf{Square patio of area $169$ square feet.}

\textbf{Step 1.} For a square, $\text{area} = s^2$, so $s^2 = 169$ and the side
is the square root of the area --- the exponent $2$ in the area formula is what
makes it a square root.

\textbf{Step 2.} $13 \times 13 = 169$, so $s = 13$.
\ans{13\ \text{ft}}

\textit{Units:} the area was in square feet, and a length is in feet --- taking
the square root of an area returns a plain length unit.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{\frac{8}{125}}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Handle the numerator and denominator separately, exactly as for
a square root: $\sqrt[3]{\frac{8}{125}} = \frac{\sqrt[3]{8}}{\sqrt[3]{125}}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $8 = 2 \times 2 \times 2$ and $125 = 5 \times 5 \times 5$, so
the roots are $2$ and $5$.
\ans{\tfrac{2}{5}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $0.016$ --- the cube root was taken on top only,
leaving $\frac{2}{125}$. Whatever you do to the numerator you must do to the
denominator.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare $\sqrt{50}$ with $7.1$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Comparing a root with a decimal is easiest as a comparison of
squares, because squaring removes the radical exactly:
$(\sqrt{50})^2 = 50$ and $7.1^2 = 50.41$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $50 < 50.41$, and both numbers are positive, so the same order
holds before squaring.
\[ \boxed{\sqrt{50} \;<\; 7.1} \]

\textit{Worth saying out loud:} $\sqrt{50} = 7.0710\ldots$, so a student who
rounds to $7.1$ first will conclude the two are equal. They are not --- rounding
early destroys the comparison.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate $\sqrt{0.36}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Decimals hide their perfect squares, so rewrite as a fraction:
$0.36 = \frac{36}{100}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Both parts are perfect squares:
$\frac{\sqrt{36}}{\sqrt{100}} = \frac{6}{10}$.
\ans{0.6}

\textit{Check:} $0.6 \times 0.6 = 0.36$.
\textit{Common wrong answer:} $0.06$ --- the decimal point was shifted instead
of a root taken. $0.06 \times 0.06 = 0.0036$, which is not $0.36$.}

\problem{\textbf{Storage cube of volume $216$ cubic centimetres.}

\textbf{Step 1.} For a cube, $\text{volume} = e^3$, so $e^3 = 216$ and the edge
is the \emph{cube} root --- the exponent in the formula sets the index of the
root.

\textbf{Step 2.} From the cube row, $6 \times 6 \times 6 = 216$.
\ans{6\ \text{cm}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} about $14.7$ --- $\sqrt{216}$ was used. An area
formula squares one length, a volume formula cubes it, so a volume undoes with a
cube root.}

\problem{\textbf{Locate and approximate $\sqrt[3]{100}$.}

\textbf{(a)} Use the cube row: $64 < 100 < 125$, so
$4 < \sqrt[3]{100} < 5$.

\textbf{(b)} $\sqrt[3]{100} = 4.6415\ldots$, which rounds to
\ans{4.64}

\textbf{(c)} The claim is \textbf{false}. $\sqrt{100} = 10$ while
$\sqrt[3]{100} \approx 4.64$, so the cube root is the \emph{smaller} one.
Splitting $100$ into three equal factors gives smaller factors than splitting it
into two: for any $n > 1$, the higher the index, the closer the root sits to
$1$. (Accept any answer that identifies the claim as false and compares $10$
with $4.64$.)

\textit{Common wrong answer:} about $33.3$ --- $100 \div 3$ instead of a cube
root.}
