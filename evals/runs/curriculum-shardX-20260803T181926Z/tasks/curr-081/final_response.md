# Unit Fractions in Area Models — Grades 2–3

Three PDFs are ready: the student worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key,
and a two-page study guide.

## What the worksheet covers

Eight problems, all of them built on pictures your child can point at. Every
problem shows a shape divided into parts with one part shaded, and asks for the
fraction that names it. The ideas ramp in this order:

1. **Naming one shaded part** (problems 1, 2, 3) — a rectangle in fourths, a
   circle in thirds, a square in eighths. Same idea, three different shapes, so
   the rule attaches to *equal parts* rather than to "rectangles".
2. **Checking the parts are equal first** (problem 4) — two bars cut into six
   pieces, only one of them fairly. Your child names the fair one and crosses
   out the other. This is the idea the whole page rests on: a fraction name
   only fits when the parts match.
3. **A part moved off the whole** (problem 5) — a strip drawn beside the bar it
   came from. The bottom number still counts the parts the *whole* was cut
   into, which is where children most often slip.
4. **Comparing unit fractions** (6, 7) — same-size shapes, one part shaded
   each, so the comparison is purely "how big is one piece". Problem 7 orders
   three of them.
5. **Explaining the rule** (problem 8) — a classmate calls an unequal piece
   "one third". Your child writes a sentence saying why that name does not fit.

## What was verified

Seven of the eight answers were recomputed independently before anything was
printed, and the answer key was machine-checked against them problem by
problem, so a printed answer cannot disagree with the picture it belongs to.
Two problems carry declared *misconception traps* (counting cut lines instead
of parts; renaming a piece by how many are left in the bar); each trap was
confirmed to give a visibly different answer, so the problem really can tell
those mistakes apart. The answer key prints them as "common wrong answer" notes
for whoever is grading.

Problem 8 is an open written explanation, so it is **flagged for manual
review** — there is no way to machine-check a sentence. The key gives a model
answer and says exactly what full credit needs (the words *equal parts*).

## The study guide

Two pages, three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example with its own
picture, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down so your child tries
first and checks second. The closing watch-out box covers the two mistakes this
topic reliably produces: counting cut lines instead of pieces, and assuming a
bigger bottom number means a bigger piece.

## Notes

The grade level is not printed on the worksheet or the study guide by design —
it appears on the answer key, with a generated curriculum section listing the
standard covered (3.NF.A) and the difficulty range.
