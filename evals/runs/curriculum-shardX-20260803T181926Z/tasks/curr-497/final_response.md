Here are three PDFs on convergence tests for infinite series, aimed at an AP Calculus BC student building procedural fluency.

**Worksheet (`ws_series_curr497.pdf`, 5 pages, 10 problems)**
The sheet rotates deliberately among the four tests rather than blocking them, so the student has to *choose* the test rather than repeat one: direct comparison (1, 6), ratio (2, 5, 9), integral (3, 7), alternating series with error bounds (4, 8, 10). Difficulty climbs 1 → 5: it opens with a geometric sum and a one-line ratio, and finishes with a radius-of-convergence problem and a conditional-convergence argument that needs two different tests in one answer. Each problem asks for the quantity the test actually depends on — the limit, the improper integral, the bounding series — not just the word "converges", and every answer is exact (fractions, $1/\ln 2$).

**Answer key (`ak_series_curr497.pdf`, 5 pages)**
Every problem is restated and worked in full: hypotheses checked before each test is applied, ratios simplified before the limit is taken, the $u = \ln x$ substitution shown, and the telescoping partial sum written out. It opens with a Quick Answers strip, a Curriculum block (AP Calculus BC, LIM-7 / LIM-8, difficulty range), and a Common wrong answers list generated from the four misconception traps built into problems 1, 4 and 8 — for example, a student who writes 1/2 on problem 1 took the $n=1$ term as the first term of the geometric series, and a student who bounds the error by $1/100$ on problem 8 used the last term kept instead of the first term dropped.

**Study guide (`ss_series_curr497.pdf`, 2 pages)**
Four sections — ratio, integral, direct comparison, alternating series — each with a "when to reach for it" rule box, a worked example whose first step says *why* that test is the right one for that term, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down inside the box. The comparison section lists the two benchmark families ($\sum r^n$ and $\sum 1/n^p$) that every comparison problem on the worksheet leans on.

**What was verified.** 11 of the 14 checks were recomputed independently by SymPy: every ratio-test limit, both improper integrals (including $\int_2^\infty dx/(x\ln^2 x) = 1/\ln 2$), both partial sums, both error bounds, and the telescoping sum $11/18$. All 10 study-guide values were verified the same way. Three items are flagged for manual review because they are genuinely written arguments and cannot be machine-checked: the comparison justifications in problems 1(b) and 6(a), and the conditional-convergence argument in problem 10. The key gives a complete model answer for each of those.
