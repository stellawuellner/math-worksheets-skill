\problem{Round $2{,}963$ to the nearest hundred.}

\textbf{Solution:} The hundreds digit is 9; the tens digit is $6 \geq 5$, so
round up. 29 hundreds become 30 hundreds — the carry crosses into the
thousands: $2{,}963 \rightarrow 3{,}000$. \ans{3000}

\problem{Round $39{,}725$ to the nearest thousand.}

\textbf{Solution:} The thousands digit is 9; the hundreds digit is $7 \geq 5$,
so round up. 39 thousands become 40 thousands: $39{,}725 \rightarrow 40{,}000$.
\ans{40000}

\problem{Leo rounds $4{,}972$ to the nearest hundred and writes $4{,}900$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The tens digit of $4{,}972$ is 7, which says round the
hundreds \emph{up}. Rounding 49 hundreds up gives 50 hundreds, so the correct
answer is $5{,}000$. \ans{5000}

Full-credit explanation: Leo rounded down (kept 49 hundreds) even though the
tens digit 7 demands rounding up, and he missed that the carry crosses the
thousands boundary.

\problem{Mia multiplies $30 \times 40$ and writes $120$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $30 \times 40 = (3 \times 10) \times (4 \times 10) =
12 \times 100 = 1{,}200$. \ans{1200}

Full-credit explanation: there are \emph{two} factors of ten — one from 30 and
one from 40 — so the basic fact $3 \times 4 = 12$ needs \emph{two} placeholder
zeros. Mia attached only one.

\problem{Round $9{,}984$ to the nearest hundred.}

\textbf{Solution:} The tens digit is $8 \geq 5$, so the 99 hundreds round up to
100 hundreds: $9{,}984 \rightarrow 10{,}000$. Every digit changes — that is
what rounding across a 9 does. \ans{10000}

\problem{Jon writes $50{,}000 + 300 + 7 = 5{,}307$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Fill the places: 5 ten-thousands, 0 thousands, 3 hundreds,
0 tens, 7 ones. The empty thousands and tens places need placeholder zeros:
$50{,}000 + 300 + 7 = 50{,}307$. \ans{50307}

Full-credit explanation: Jon pushed the nonzero digits together and dropped the
zeros, which moved the 5 from the ten-thousands place to the thousands place.

\problem{Circle all that round to 700 (nearest hundred): 748, 651, 762, 685.}

\textbf{Solution:} Round each one. $748$: tens digit $4 < 5$, rounds down to
\ans{700}. \ $651$: tens digit $5 \geq 5$... careful, 651 is \emph{above} 650,
between 600 and 700 and past the midpoint, so it rounds up to \ans{700}. \
$762$: tens digit $6 \geq 5$, rounds up to \ans{800}. \ $685$: tens digit
$8 \geq 5$, rounds up to \ans{700}.

Circle $748$, $651$, and $685$. Do \emph{not} circle 762 — it rounds to 800.

\problem{Ava: ``452 and 448 round to the same ten, so they are equal.''}

\textbf{Solution:} $452$: ones digit $2 < 5$, rounds down to \ans{450}. \
$448$: ones digit $8 \geq 5$, rounds up to \ans{450}.

Full-credit explanation: rounding replaces each number with the \emph{nearest}
ten, so two different numbers can land on the same rounded value. Sharing a
rounded value does not make them equal: $452 \neq 448$ — they just both live
closest to 450.
