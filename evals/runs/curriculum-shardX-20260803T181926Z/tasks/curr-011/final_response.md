# More, Fewer, and the Same — three documents

Here are the three PDFs for comparing groups of objects (kindergarten/grade 1,
CCSS K.CC.A.1–K.CC.C.7).

**Study guide** (`ss_compare_curr011.pdf`, 2 pages) — read this first. Three
skill sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a try-it your child
attempts before the worksheet (the answer is printed upside down inside the
box):

1. *Which group has more, fewer, or the same?* — the one-to-one matching model,
   and how it becomes the symbols `>`, `<`, `=`.
2. *How many more? How many fewer?* — why both questions are answered by the
   same subtraction, and why it is the leftovers that get counted.
3. *Making two groups the same* — counting up from the smaller number, then
   checking by adding.

**Worksheet** (`ws_compare_curr011.pdf`) — 8 problems, all on the requested
focus. Problems 1, 2, 4 and 6 compare object sets and write the notation
(including one ordering item with three cups of marbles); 3 and 5 are
how-many-more / how-many-fewer stories; 7 and 8 are make-them-equal problems
with a missing addend. Each problem shows the objects to count or asks the child
to draw them, and every problem has an answer blank. Difficulty ramps 1 → 4.

**Answer key** (`ak_compare_curr011.pdf`) — a quick-answer bank at the top, then
a full worked explanation for each problem in the language of matching rather
than just a number. The key also carries the curriculum section (grade level,
standard code, difficulty range); the grade level deliberately does not appear
on the worksheet the child holds.

**What was machine-verified:** all 8 worksheet answers and all 6 study-guide
answers were recomputed with SymPy — the three comparisons, the ordering, both
differences, and both missing addends. Nothing is flagged for manual review.

**Two misconception traps** were designed in and are printed in the key's
"common wrong answers" block: an answer of 11 on problem 3 means the two rows
were added instead of compared, and an answer of 9 on problem 5 means the whole
bigger row was counted instead of just the extras. Both were checked
mechanically to be distinguishable from the right answer.

One note on problem 8: the number is verified, but the "explain how the picture
shows it" sentence is a human judgement call — the key tells you what to accept.
