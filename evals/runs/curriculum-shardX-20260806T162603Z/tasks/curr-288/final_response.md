# Factoring Special Forms — worksheet, answer key, study guide

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide (`ss_specialfactoring_curr288.pdf`, 2 pages)** — read this first. Three
  sections, each with a rule box, a worked example that opens by saying *why* that pattern
  applies, and a "try it" whose answer is printed upside down inside the box: (1) difference
  of two squares, (2) perfect-square trinomials, (3) taking the common factor out first and
  then applying a pattern. Two watch-out boxes flag the two errors this topic produces most:
  trying to factor a *sum* of squares, and accepting a trinomial as a perfect square without
  running the middle-term test.
- **Worksheet (`ws_specialfactoring_curr288.pdf`, 4 pages)** — 10 problems, each one an area
  or volume model with its units stated, so the factors mean something (a side length, a
  crate dimension). The ramp runs from $x^2-36$ to $x^3-16x$ and a two-part synthesis item.
  Every problem has 5–7 cm of work space and its own answer line.
- **Answer key (`ak_specialfactoring_curr288.pdf`, 3 pages)** — a Quick Answers bank for fast
  grading, a "What is verified" note, a generated Curriculum section (standards codes and
  difficulty range), and a full worked solution for every problem showing the reasoning, not
  just the result.

## What is verified, honestly

The worksheet asks for **11 responses** across its 10 problems.

- **10 are machine-checked.** Every factoring and the one expansion was recomputed
  independently with SymPy before the PDFs were allowed to compile, and a separate gate
  confirms each printed boxed answer matches the value that was verified. Nine of the ten
  problems are therefore fully machine-checked.
- **1 is instructor-judged: problem 10(b)**, "explain how the numbers 16, 24 and 9 tell you
  this is a perfect-square trinomial rather than a difference of squares." That is a written
  explanation, and no computer algebra system can grade it. It is marked `---` in the Quick
  Answers bank, named in the key's "What is verified" note, and the answer key states exactly
  what a full-credit response must contain (three terms rather than two, square ends, and —
  the point of the item — the middle-term check $2\cdot 4x\cdot 3 = 24x$).

Nothing on this sheet is marked `[unchecked]`: there is no printed answer without either a
machine check or a written grading rubric.

## Notes for the adult

- The grade level prints on the answer key only (in the Curriculum block), never on the
  worksheet or study guide.
- Problems 5, 8 and 9 are the ones that catch students who reach for a pattern before pulling
  out the common factor — 9 in particular is not finished at $x(x^2-16)$.
- Problem 6 runs the process backwards (multiply out rather than factor). It is there because
  students who can only factor in one direction usually cannot check their own work.
