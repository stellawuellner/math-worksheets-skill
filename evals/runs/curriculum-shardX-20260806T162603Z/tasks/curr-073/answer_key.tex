\problem{Find the missing factor: $5 \times n = 20$}

\textbf{Solution:} Ask: 5 times what makes 20? From the fives table, $5 \times 4 = 20$. \ans{n = 4}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$18 \div 3 = ?$ \quad Think: $3 \times \textbf{?} = 18$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Division asks for the unknown factor: 3 times what makes 18? Since $3 \times 6 = 18$, we get $18 \div 3 = 6$. \ans{6}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{24 stickers placed equally onto 4 pages. How many on each page?}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing 24 into 4 equal groups asks $4 \times \textbf{?} = 24$. The related fact is $4 \times 6 = 24$, so each page gets 6 stickers. \ans{6~\text{stickers}}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor: $n \times 7 = 42$}

\textbf{Solution:} What number of 7s makes 42? From the sevens table, $6 \times 7 = 42$. \ans{n = 6}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$35 \div 5 = ?$ \quad Think: $5 \times \textbf{?} = 35$.}

\textbf{Solution:} 5 times what makes 35? $5 \times 7 = 35$, so $35 \div 5 = 7$. \ans{7}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{30 markers, 6 in each box. How many boxes?}

\textbf{Solution:} Making groups of 6 from 30 asks $6 \times \textbf{?} = 30$. Since $6 \times 5 = 30$, she fills 5 boxes. (Subtracting, $30 - 6 = 24$, only removes one box's markers --- it does not count boxes.) \ans{5~\text{boxes}}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor: $9 \times n = 54$}

\textbf{Solution:} 9 times what makes 54? From the nines table, $9 \times 6 = 54$. \ans{n = 6}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$48 \div 6 = ?$ \quad Think: $6 \times \textbf{?} = 48$.}

\textbf{Solution:} 6 times what makes 48? $6 \times 8 = 48$, so $48 \div 6 = 8$. \ans{8}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{40 chairs, 8 chairs in each row.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The story fixes the group size (8) and hides the number of rows, so the fact is $8 \times \textbf{?} = 40$ (also accepted: $\textbf{?} \times 8 = 40$ or $8 \times n = 40$).

\textbf{Solution (b):} 8 times what makes 40? $8 \times 5 = 40$, so she makes 5 rows. \ans{5~\text{rows}}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Challenge: 63 apples in 7 equal bags. How does $7 \times 9 = 63$ help?}

\textbf{Solution:} Splitting 63 apples equally into 7 bags asks the missing-factor question ``7 times what makes 63?'' The known fact $7 \times 9 = 63$ answers it directly: the missing factor is 9, so $63 \div 7 = 9$ and each bag holds 9 apples. A correct explanation must connect the division to the known multiplication fact, not just state the answer. \ans{9~\text{apples}}
