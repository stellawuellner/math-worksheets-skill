\wstitleblock{Solving Quadratic Equations --- Diagnosing Incomplete Root Sets and Formula Sign Errors}{Algebra 1}{}

\noindent\small Most wrong answers on this topic are not arithmetic slips. They
are one of two habits:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{An incomplete root set} --- a square root taken without $\pm$, a
        factor divided away, or only the ``nice'' solution reported. A quadratic
        has two solutions unless the discriminant is zero.
  \item \textbf{A sign error in the formula} --- $-b$ written as $b$ when $b$ is
        already negative, or $-4ac$ subtracted when $c$ is negative and it should
        be added.
  \item In every problem, work in this order: name the mistake in words, then
        show the corrected work, then state the complete solution set.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Warm-up.} Solve $x^2 = 49$.
\par\smallskip
Toby answers $x = 7$. Is his solution set complete? Write the complete set.}

\problem[5.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Jae solves $x^2 = 3x$ by dividing both
sides by $x$, and reports the single solution $x = 3$.
\par\smallskip
Dividing by $x$ assumes something about $x$ that the equation never promised.
Say what that assumption is, then solve the equation correctly by factoring and
give both solutions.}

\problem[5.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Rio computes the discriminant of
$3x^2 - 7x - 6 = 0$ like this:
\par\smallskip
\centerline{$b^2 - 4ac = (-7)^2 - 4(3)(6) = -23$, \quad so ``no real solutions''.}
\par\smallskip
Rio's substitution changed one of the three coefficients. Name which one and how,
then compute the correct discriminant.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Mia uses the quadratic formula on
$2x^2 - 4x - 3 = 0$. She reasons: ``$b$ is already negative, so the numerator
starts with $-4$,'' and reports the larger root as
$x = \dfrac{-4 + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2(2)} \approx 0.58$.
\par\smallskip
State the rule Mia broke in one sentence, then compute the correct larger root.
Leave it in exact form.}

\problem[5.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Sam solves $(x - 3)^2 = 25$ by taking the
square root of both sides. Because he takes only the positive square root, he
reports $x = 8$ as the answer.
\par\smallskip
Which solution did Sam lose, and what should he have written on the line where he
took the square root? Give the complete solution set.}

\problem[5.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Kai solves $(2x + 3)(x - 4) = 0$ by
reading the numbers straight out of the parentheses and answering $x = 3$ and
$x = -4$.
\par\smallskip
Kai made two different mistakes at once --- a sign and an unfinished step.
Describe both, then give the correct solution set.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} A student solves $4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0$ and
reports \emph{two} solutions, $x = \frac{3}{2}$ and $x = -\frac{3}{2}$, reasoning
that ``every quadratic has two roots, one positive and one negative''.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Compute the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ for this equation.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Use its value to say how many distinct real solutions there are, and
give the correct solution set. Explain what the student's rule gets wrong.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Explain.}
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why taking the square root of both sides of an equation
loses a solution unless you write $\pm$. Use the fact that two different numbers
can have the same square.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Point to where the same $\pm$ lives inside the quadratic formula,
and say what it produces.
\par
\textbf{(c)} Write one checking habit that would catch a missing root before you
hand the work in.
\par\noansline}
