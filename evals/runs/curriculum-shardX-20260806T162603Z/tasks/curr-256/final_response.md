# Explaining legal moves in equations and inequalities — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_legalmoves_curr256.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: naming the property
  behind each move (with the balance-scale image made explicit), solving when the variable sits on
  both sides, and the single move that flips an inequality. The first rule box separates the two
  kinds of legal move that students blur together: doing the same thing to *both* sides (a property
  of equality) versus rewriting *one* side into an equal expression (distributing, collecting like
  terms). Each section has a worked example that names its moves as it goes, plus a try-it.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_legalmoves_curr256.pdf`, 5 pages) — 10 problems, difficulty 1 → 5. It opens
  with a balance-pan table so the "same to both sides" rule has a picture attached, then moves to
  reading a finished solution and naming each move, to solving, to inequalities, to an item where
  one step is illegal (a mis-distribution that is not a both-sides error at all), and finishes with
  a fraction equation the student must solve *and* justify line by line.
- **Answer key** (`ak_legalmoves_curr256.pdf`, 4 pages) — every problem worked with the property
  named at each line, a Quick Answers bank, and a Curriculum block (HSA-REI.A.1 for the justifying
  items, HSA-REI.B.3 for the solving ones; difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **The other 5 problems (1, 3, 4, 6, 9) are fully machine-checked, and 10 checks in all are verified** by SymPy — every solution value and every inequality solution
  set, including the direction of the sign, recomputed independently of the answer key.
- **5 of the 10 problems (2, 5, 7, 8, 10) carry an instructor-judged part**, and that is the nature
  of this topic: "name the move", "explain what rule it breaks", "say where the sign flips" are
  prose, and no program grades prose. Every one of those problems also has a machine-checked
  computational part, and the answer key prints a rubric for each written part — what a full-credit
  response must name, and what earns half credit. The Quick Answers bank marks them `—`.
- **2 blanks are marked as working space**: the two balance-model cells in problem 1. The graded
  answer for that problem is the single blank marked **Answer**, and it is verified.

The grade level prints on the answer key only.
