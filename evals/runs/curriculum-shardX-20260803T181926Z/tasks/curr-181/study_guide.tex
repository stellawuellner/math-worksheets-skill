\sstitleblock{Writing Inequalities from Verbal Constraints}

\skillheading{1. Turn a phrase into an inequality symbol}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{The boundary question:} is the number in the sentence itself allowed?
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=0pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{more than, over, greater than} $\rightarrow$ $>$ \quad (boundary
  not allowed --- open circle)
  \item \emph{less than, under, fewer than} $\rightarrow$ $<$ \quad (open
  circle)
  \item \emph{at least, no less than, minimum of} $\rightarrow$ $\ge$ \quad
  (boundary allowed --- closed circle)
  \item \emph{at most, no more than, maximum of} $\rightarrow$ $\le$ \quad
  (closed circle)
\end{itemize}
Say what the variable counts \emph{in words} first --- that sentence is part of
the answer.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A theatre seats no more than $250$ people. Write an
inequality for the size $s$ of an audience.
\step{``No more than'' asks whether $250$ itself is allowed --- it is, since a
full house of $250$ is legal, so this is the closed direction.}
\step{The audience size is the quantity being limited, and the limit is above
it, so $s$ sits on the small side of $250$.}

$\ans{s \le 250}$ \quad (closed circle at $250$, shaded left)
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad You must be at least $16$ years old to get a learner's
permit. Write an inequality for the ages $a$ that qualify.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{a \ge 16}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Build the inequality from a story}

\begin{formulabox}
Most story constraints have the same three pieces:\\[2pt]
\textbf{(one-time amount)} $+$ \textbf{(amount each time)} $\times$
\textbf{(how many)} \ \ compared with \ \ \textbf{(the limit)}\\[3pt]
The number attached to the variable is the one that happens \emph{again and
again}; the number standing alone happens \emph{once}. Getting those two the
right way round is most of the work.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A $\$48$ gift card, a $\$12$ snack, and rentals at
$\$9$ each. How many rentals $r$?
\step{The snack is bought once and the rentals repeat, so $9$ is the number
that multiplies $r$ and $12$ stands alone.}
\step{Spending cannot pass the card's value: $12 + 9r \le 48$, so $9r \le 36$
and dividing by $9$ gives the count.}

$\ans{r \le 4}$ \quad (check: $12 + 9 \cdot 4 = 48$ exactly)
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad A fair charges $\$8$ to get in and $\$3$ per ride. You
have at most $\$35$. Write and solve an inequality for the number of rides
$n$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{n \le 9}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Solve, flip when you must, and read it back}

\begin{formulabox}
Solving an inequality works exactly like solving an equation, with \emph{one}
extra rule:\\[3pt]
\textbf{Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the
symbol.} Adding and subtracting never do.\\[3pt]
Then \textbf{check one value} from your answer in the original sentence, and
finally say what the answer means for the situation --- ``at most $4$ rentals''
rather than just ``$r \le 4$''.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A tank holds $300$ litres and drains $25$ litres a
minute. When is more than $100$ litres left?
\step{The amount left shrinks, so it is $300 - 25t$; ``more than'' is strict,
which makes this a $>$ problem with a negative coefficient waiting.}
\step{$300 - 25t > 100$ gives $-25t > -200$, and dividing by $-25$ flips the
symbol.}

$\ans{t < 8}$ \quad (check $t = 4$: $200$ litres left, which is more than
$100$)
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad A balloon is at $180$ metres and descends $20$ metres a
minute. For which times $t$ is it above $60$ metres?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{t < 6}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} the flip rule is about \emph{multiplying or dividing}
by a negative, never about subtracting one. And a solved inequality is not yet
an answer to a story: if the variable counts objects, finish by saying how many
whole objects are possible.
\end{watchoutbox}
