Here are the three PDFs for the Grades 6–7 error-analysis practice on the two
classic geometry mix-ups: radius versus diameter, and area versus perimeter.

**Student worksheet (8 problems).** Every stem forces the diagnostic decision
the sheet is about — is the question asking for the distance *around* or the
surface *covered*, and were you given the radius or the diameter? The numbers
are chosen so each canonical wrong method lands visibly off the correct answer
(a diameter-for-radius area comes out four times too big; a perimeter-for-area
answer is a different number entirely). Two find-and-fix items show a student's
incorrect work to diagnose (Mara's diameter-in-the-area-formula; Jon buying
paint by perimeter), and the challenge problem asks for a circumference and an
area from the same circle, with different units on the two answer lines.

**Answer key.** Step-by-step reasoning for every problem, a quick-answers bank
with a "common wrong answers" note under each trap (so a grader can recognize
*which* error produced 314.16 or 30), and a generated Curriculum section
(standards 6.G.A and 7.G.B, difficulty range 1–5).

**Study guide (2 pages).** Three sections — circle formulas (with the halve-
the-diameter rule), perimeter-or-area decision making, and a checklist for
catching radius/diameter mistakes — each with a rule box, worked example, and
try-it.

**What is verified.** All 9 numeric answers are machine-checked with SymPy, and
every planted wrong result is declared as a misconception trap: 9 traps on the
worksheet (every problem carries one) plus 1 in the study guide, each
machine-proven distinguishable from the correct answer, and the unit on every
answer line is machine-bound to the key. The two "name the error" explanations
(problems 6a and 7a) are instructor-judged, with grading guidance in the key;
everything else is machine-verified.
