\wstitleblock{Triangle Similarity --- Diagnosing Reversed Correspondence and Scale-Factor Errors}{High School Geometry}{}

\noindent\small In a similarity statement the \textbf{order of the letters is the
correspondence}: $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ says $A \leftrightarrow D$,
$B \leftrightarrow E$, $C \leftrightarrow F$, so $\overline{AB}$ pairs with
$\overline{DE}$ and $\overline{BC}$ with $\overline{EF}$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item A \textbf{reversed correspondence} pairs a side with the wrong partner.
  \item A \textbf{reversed scale factor} uses large-over-small where
        small-over-large belongs (or the reverse), so a side that should grow
        shrinks instead.
  \item Before computing, decide which triangle is larger; then say whether your
        answer should be bigger or smaller than the side you started from.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\problem[5cm]{Warm up. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ with $AB = 6$,
$BC = 8$ and $DE = 9$. Find $EF$.\par
Write the matching pairs first, then set up one proportion and solve it.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} $\triangle PQR \sim \triangle STU$ with
$PQ = 10$, $QR = 14$ and $ST = 15$. Kai writes
\[ \frac{ST}{PQ} = \frac{QR}{TU}, \qquad \frac{15}{10} = \frac{14}{TU},
   \qquad TU \approx 9.33 \]
Kai's two ratios do not run in the same direction. Name the error, then find the
correct length of $TU$.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle FED$ ---
read that statement carefully. $AB = 12$, $BC = 9$ and $FE = 8$.\par
Rho pairs $\overline{BC}$ with $\overline{ED}$ correctly but then multiplies by
$\frac{12}{8}$ and reports $ED = 13.5$. Explain why a factor greater than $1$
cannot be right here, then find $ED$.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio
$5 : 8$. The smaller triangle has area $50$ square centimetres.\par
Rae multiplies by $\frac{8}{5}$ and reports an area of $80$ square centimetres.
Name what Rae scaled incorrectly, then find the correct area of the larger
triangle, in square centimetres.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} A person $5$ ft tall casts a shadow $4$ ft
long. At the same moment a flagpole casts a shadow $22$ ft long. The person and
the flagpole with their shadows form similar right triangles.\par
Ines writes $\dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{22}{h}$ and reports $h \approx 17.6$ ft.\par
State which two lengths Ines paired that do not correspond, then find the correct
height $h$ of the flagpole, in feet.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Challenge.} $\triangle JKL \sim \triangle MNP$ with
$JK = 15$, $MN = 9$ and $KL = 20$. Tomas writes: ``the scale factor is
$\frac{15}{9}$, so $NP = 20 \times \frac{15}{9} \approx 33.33$.''\par
\textbf{(a)} Say which triangle is larger and explain why Tomas's answer is
impossible before any arithmetic is checked.\par
\textbf{(b)} Write the rule you would give Tomas for deciding which way up to
write the scale factor.\par
\textbf{(c)} Find the correct length of $NP$.}
