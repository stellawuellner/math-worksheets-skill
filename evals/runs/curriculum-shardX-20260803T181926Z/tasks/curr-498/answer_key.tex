\problem{\textbf{Sensor model $C(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (x-4)^n / (n \cdot 2^n)$:
ratio test and radius.}
\par\smallskip
With $a_n = (x-4)^n / (n \cdot 2^n)$,
\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
= \left|\frac{(x-4)^{n+1}}{(n+1)\,2^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n\,2^n}{(x-4)^n}\right|
= |x-4| \cdot \frac{n}{2(n+1)} .
\]
The $x$-free factor is what the ratio test needs in the limit. Dividing
numerator and denominator by $n$ gives $\dfrac{1}{2(1 + 1/n)}$, and $1/n \to 0$.

The limit is \ans{L = \tfrac{1}{2}}

The ratio test demands $|x-4| \cdot L < 1$, i.e. $|x-4| < 1/L$, so the radius is
$R = 1/L$ bars.

\ans{R = 2 \text{ bars}}}

\problem{\textbf{Endpoints from the center and radius.}
\par\smallskip
The series is centered at $c = 4$ bars with $R = 2$ bars, and the ratio test
guarantees convergence exactly on $|x - 4| < 2$. The boundary of that set is
where $|x - 4| = 2$, which is the two points $c - R$ and $c + R$.
\par\smallskip
Left endpoint: $c - R = 4 - 2$.
\ans{x = 2 \text{ bars}}

Right endpoint: $c + R = 4 + 2$.
\ans{x = 6 \text{ bars}}

\emph{Note:} the ratio test says nothing at either point --- there
$|x-4| \cdot L = 1$, and the test is inconclusive. That is why problems 3 and 4
exist.}

\problem{\textbf{Left endpoint $x = 2$ bars.}
\par\smallskip
Substitute $x = 2$, so $x - 4 = -2$:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-2)^n}{n \cdot 2^n}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n 2^n}{n \cdot 2^n}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}.
\]
This is the alternating harmonic series. \textbf{Test:} the alternating series
test --- the terms $1/n$ are positive, decreasing, and tend to $0$ --- so it
converges. (It converges conditionally: $\sum 1/n$ itself diverges.)
\par\smallskip
Its exact sum is the value of the Maclaurin series
$\ln(1+u) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (-1)^{n-1} u^n / n$ at $u = 1$, with the sign
flipped because our numerator is $(-1)^n$, not $(-1)^{n-1}$.

\ans{-\ln 2}

So $x = 2$ bars \emph{belongs} to the interval, and it earns a square bracket.}

\problem{\textbf{Right endpoint $x = 6$ bars, and the full interval.}
\par\smallskip
Substitute $x = 6$, so $x - 4 = 2$:
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{n \cdot 2^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n},
\]
the harmonic series. \textbf{Test:} the $p$-series test with $p = 1$; a
$p$-series diverges for $p \le 1$, so this endpoint diverges and is
\emph{excluded}. (The $n$th-term test is useless here: $1/n \to 0$, yet the
series still diverges.)
\par\smallskip
Combining problems 1--4: the ratio test gives convergence on $2 < x < 6$, the
left endpoint converges and the right endpoint diverges.

\ans{2 \le x < 6}

The model is usable for pressures from $2$ bars up to but not including $6$
bars.}

\problem{\textbf{Gain model $G(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (x+3)^n / (n^2 \cdot 5^n)$:
ratio test and radius.}
\par\smallskip
With $a_n = (x+3)^n / (n^2 \cdot 5^n)$,
\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
= |x+3| \cdot \frac{n^2}{5\,(n+1)^2}.
\]
Divide numerator and denominator by $n^2$: the factor becomes
$\dfrac{1}{5(1 + 1/n)^2} \to \dfrac{1}{5}$.

The limit is \ans{L = \tfrac{1}{5}}

Then $|x+3| < 1/L$, so the radius is $R = 1/L$ volts.

\ans{R = 5 \text{ volts}}}

\problem{\textbf{Both endpoints of the gain model, and the full interval.}
\par\smallskip
The center is $c = -3$ volts and $R = 5$ volts, so the endpoints are
$-3 + 5 = 2$ and $-3 - 5 = -8$ volts.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{At $x = 2$ volts} ($x + 3 = 5$):
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{5^n}{n^2 \cdot 5^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2},
\]
a $p$-series with $p = 2 > 1$, so it converges. Its exact sum is the Basel
value.

\ans{\dfrac{\pi^2}{6}}

\textbf{At $x = -8$ volts} ($x + 3 = -5$):
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-5)^n}{n^2 \cdot 5^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2},
\]
which converges absolutely by comparison with $\sum 1/n^2$ (the alternating
series test also settles it). Its exact sum is the negative of the alternating
Basel value, $-\eta(2) = -\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{\pi^2}{6}$.

\ans{-\dfrac{\pi^2}{12}}

Both endpoints converge, so both earn square brackets and the interval is
$-8 \le x \le 2$ volts.}

\problem{\textbf{Reaction-rate model $R(x) = \sum_{n \ge 0} (x-2)^n / n!$.}
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} With $a_n = (x-2)^n / n!$,
\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
= |x-2| \cdot \frac{n!}{(n+1)!} = |x-2| \cdot \frac{1}{n+1},
\]
and $1/(n+1) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

The limit is \ans{L = 0}

\textbf{(b)} Because $L = 0$, the product $|x-2| \cdot L = 0 < 1$ for
\emph{every} real $x$, so the radius is $R = \infty$ and the interval of
convergence is all real numbers, written $(-\infty, \infty)$. There are no
endpoints to test: an infinite radius means the boundary set $|x - 2| = R$ is
empty, so the inconclusive case of the ratio test never arises.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} Setting $x = 3$ gives $x - 2 = 1$, so
$R(3) = \sum_{n \ge 0} 1/n!$, which is the Maclaurin series for $e^u$ at
$u = 1$.

\ans{R(3) = e}}

\problem{\textbf{Energy model $E(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} n\,(x-1)^n / 3^n$.}
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} With $a_n = n\,(x-1)^n / 3^n$,
\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
= |x-1| \cdot \frac{n+1}{3n},
\]
and dividing through by $n$ gives $\dfrac{1 + 1/n}{3} \to \dfrac{1}{3}$.

The limit is \ans{L = \tfrac{1}{3}}

So $|x-1| < 3$, the radius is $R = 3$, and the endpoints are $1 - 3 = -2$ and
$1 + 3 = 4$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} \emph{(Open reasoning --- flagged for manual review.)}
\textbf{Model answer.} At $x = 4$ the terms become $n \cdot 3^n / 3^n = n$, so
the series is $\sum n$, whose terms grow without bound; since the terms do not
approach $0$, the $n$th-term test for divergence applies and the series
diverges. At $x = -2$ the terms become $n(-3)^n / 3^n = (-1)^n n$, whose
absolute value $n$ again does not approach $0$; the $n$th-term test applies
here too and the series diverges. The alternating series test does \emph{not}
rescue this endpoint, because that test requires terms decreasing to $0$ and
these increase without bound.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} Both endpoints diverge, so both are excluded and both brackets are
round.

\ans{-2 < x < 4}

\emph{Grading note:} full credit requires (i) the correct simplification at each
endpoint, (ii) the $n$th-term test named at both --- not the alternating series
test at $x = -2$ --- and (iii) round brackets justified by the divergence, not
asserted. A student who writes $[-2, 4)$ has tested only one endpoint.}
