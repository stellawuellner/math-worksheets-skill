\problem{\textbf{$f(x) = 3 \cdot 2^{x}$, find $f(4)$.}
Substitute $x = 4$. The power comes first, the multiplication second:
$2^{4} = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 16$, and then $3 \cdot 16 = 48$.
$\ans{f(4) = 48}$}

\problem{\textbf{$g(x) = 100 \cdot (0.8)^{x}$, find $g(2)$.}
Here the base is less than $1$, so this function decays.
$(0.8)^{2} = 0.8 \cdot 0.8 = 0.64$, and $100 \cdot 0.64 = 64$.
$\ans{g(2) = 64}$}

\problem{\textbf{Table and graph of $h(x) = 2^{x}$.}
Each value of $x$ is substituted into the same power. Reading left to right, a
step of $1$ in $x$ doubles the output --- that constant ratio is what makes the
curve exponential.
\par\smallskip
$h(-1) = 2^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2^{1}}$, so $\ans{h(-1) = \frac{1}{2}}$
\par\smallskip
$h(0) = 2^{0}$, and any nonzero base to the power $0$ is one, so
$\ans{h(0) = 1}$
\par\smallskip
$h(1) = 2^{1}$, so $\ans{h(1) = 2}$
\par\smallskip
$h(2) = 2 \cdot 2$, so $\ans{h(2) = 4}$
\par\smallskip
$h(3) = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2$, so $\ans{h(3) = 8}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Graph:} the five plotted points are
$(-1, \frac{1}{2})$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 2)$, $(2, 4)$, $(3, 8)$. The curve rises
slowly on the left, passes through the $y$-axis at height one, and climbs
steeply on the right; it never touches the $x$-axis. Accept any smooth curve
through the five correct points.}

\problem{\textbf{$p(x) = 2 \cdot 3^{x}$, find $p(-2)$.}
A negative exponent means a reciprocal, not a negative answer:
$3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{3^{2}} = \dfrac{1}{9}$. Then
$2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{9}$ gives the exact value.
$\ans{p(-2) = \frac{2}{9}}$}

\problem{\textbf{$P(t) = 400 \cdot 2^{\,t}$ bacteria, find $P(5)$.}
The starting count $400$ is the value at $t = 0$; each hour multiplies by the
base $2$. After $5$ hours the multiplier is $2^{5} = 32$, so
$400 \cdot 32 = 12800$ bacteria.
$\ans{P(5) = 12800}$}

\problem{\textbf{$k(x) = 16 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$ litres --- graph,
then read off when $2$ litres remain.}
The table halves each time:
$k(0) = 16$, $k(1) = 8$, $k(2) = 4$, $k(3) = 2$, $k(4) = 1$.
Plotting those five points and joining them gives a curve falling toward the
$x$-axis without ever reaching it. Reading across from height $2$ meets the
curve above $x = 3$, and the equation confirms it:
$16 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} = 2$ means
$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} = \dfrac{1}{8} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}$.
$\ans{x = 3}$}

\problem{\textbf{$V(t) = 24000 \cdot (0.85)^{t}$ dollars, find $V(4)$.}
Keeping $0.85$ of the value each year means multiplying by $0.85$ four times:
$(0.85)^{4} = 0.52200625$. Then
$24000 \cdot 0.52200625 = 12528.15$ dollars.
$\ans{V(4) = 12528.15}$}

\problem{\textbf{$f(x) = 6 \cdot (0.5)^{x}$, find $f(-1)$ and $f(3)$.}
A negative exponent flips the base: $(0.5)^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{0.5} = 2$, so
$6 \cdot 2$ gives $\ans{f(-1) = 12}$
\par\smallskip
Going the other way, $(0.5)^{3} = 0.125 = \dfrac{1}{8}$, so
$6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{6}{8}$, which reduces.
$\ans{f(3) = \frac{3}{4}}$
\par\smallskip
Notice the pattern: moving one step left multiplies by $2$, moving one step
right multiplies by $0.5$.}

\problem{\textbf{Graph $y = 2^{x}$ and $y = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$; find
where they are equal.}
The two curves are mirror images across the $y$-axis, because
$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} = 2^{-x}$. They can only meet where the exponent
and its negative agree, and both functions pass through height one at the
$y$-axis.
Solving $2^{x} = 2^{-x}$ needs $x = -x$, which happens only at the origin's
$x$-value.
$\ans{x = 0}$}

\problem{\textbf{Town of $8500$ growing $3$ percent per year, population after
$12$ years.}
Growing by $3$ percent multiplies by $1.03$ once per year, so the model is
$8500 \cdot (1.03)^{t}$ and $t = 12$ gives
$(1.03)^{12} = 1.42576$ to five decimal places, and
$8500 \cdot 1.42576 = 12118.97$, which rounds to a whole person.
$\ans{12119}$}

\problem{\textbf{$y = a \cdot 2^{x}$ passes through $(3, 40)$; find $a$.}
The point says that when $x = 3$ the output is $40$, so substitute both:
$a \cdot 2^{3} = 40$. Since $2^{3} = 8$, this is $8a = 40$.
$\ans{a = 5}$
\par\smallskip
The completed function is $y = 5 \cdot 2^{x}$, so the sketch passes through
$(0, 5)$, $(1, 10)$, $(2, 20)$ and the given point $(3, 40)$.}

\problem{\textbf{Account A: $1200 \cdot (1.05)^{t}$; Account B:
$900 \cdot (1.09)^{t}$; compare after $10$ years.}
Each rate becomes a multiplier: $5$ percent growth is $1.05$ per year and $9$
percent growth is $1.09$ per year.
\begin{align*}
1200 \cdot (1.05)^{10} &= 1200 \cdot 1.628894627 = 1954.67 \\
900 \cdot (1.09)^{10} &= 900 \cdot 2.367363674 = 2130.63
\end{align*}
$\ans{A = 1954.67}$
\par\smallskip
$\ans{B = 2130.63}$
\par\smallskip
Account B is worth more after $10$ years even though it started with less: the
larger base beats the larger starting value once enough years have passed.}
