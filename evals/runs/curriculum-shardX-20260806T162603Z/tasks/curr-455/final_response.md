Three PDFs — study guide, worksheet, answer key.

**Study guide (`ss_evidence_curr455.pdf`, 2 pages).** Opens with the organising
idea: a graph shows the shape, a table shows the trend, algebra settles the
value, and when two disagree the algebra wins. Then one section per kind of
evidence — reading a limit off a graph (open circle counts, solid dot does not),
reading one off a table (both sides, and a table can only ever predict), and
proving it with algebra (the three rewrites: factor, conjugate, split the
absolute value). Each section has a worked example and a distinct try-it with its
answer printed upside down inside the box.

**Worksheet (`ws_evidence_curr455.pdf`, 5 pages).** Ten problems. A single
labelled **Graph A** sits at the top of page 1 and is used by problems 1, 2, 5
and 8; the other problems carry their own table or expression. After a two-problem
graph warm-up the three evidence types interleave — table, algebra, graph, table,
algebra, graph, algebra — so the student has to decide which tool the problem is
actually handing them. Problem 10 is the synthesis challenge: one function,
$r(x)=(x^2-9)/|x-3|$, attacked with a table, then with algebra, then with a
sketch. Difficulty ramps 1 → 4.

Graph A is drawn from the same branch formulas the answer key uses
($y=x+2$, $y=6-2x$, $y=2/(x-4)$), so the picture and the verified answers cannot
disagree. It shows all three things worth seeing at once: a jump at $x=1$ with a
solid dot at $(1,1)$ that sits on neither branch, an ordinary continuous point at
$x=3$, and a vertical asymptote at $x=4$ where one side is finite and the other
is not.

**Answer key (`ak_evidence_curr455.pdf`, 4 pages).** Full reasoning, with a short
note at the top stating exactly how Graph A was built so you can check any graph
reading against a formula. Quick Answers bank with each part labelled, plus a
generated Curriculum block (LIM-1, LIM-2; difficulty 1–4).

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 15 graded responses. **Thirteen are
machine-checked** with SymPy — every limit, including the graph readings, because
each graph answer is the limit of the branch formula the curve was plotted from.
**Two are instructor-judged: problem 5 and problem 10(c).** Problem 5 asks for a
written comparison of $\lim_{x\to1^-}f$, $\lim_{x\to1^+}f$ and $f(1)$ — the one
place all three differ — and problem 10(c) asks for a sketch plus a conclusion.
Both print as `---` in the Quick Answers bank and both carry a written rubric in
the key, including what earns nothing (for 10(c), a single connected curve
through $x=3$, which contradicts the table the student just read).

No misconception traps are declared here: this is a review sheet where the
failure mode is trusting the wrong kind of evidence rather than mis-executing one
computation, and problems 6 and 10 are built to expose exactly that.
