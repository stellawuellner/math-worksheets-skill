\noindent\textbf{How to use this key.} On this sheet the number is rarely the
point. Credit the two moves that are: simplifying the difference quotient
\emph{before} taking the limit (so the $0/0$ form is resolved rather than
evaluated), and stating what the resulting number measures, with its units.
Problem 8 is open reasoning and is flagged for manual review --- a model
response is given below to compare against, not a key to match word for word.

\problem{$f(x) = x^2$; complete the table and find $f'(4)$.

\textbf{Solution:} The difference quotient simplifies once and for all:
\[
\frac{(4+h)^2 - 4^2}{h} = \frac{16 + 8h + h^2 - 16}{h} = \frac{8h + h^2}{h}
= 8 + h \quad (h \neq 0).
\]
So the table entries are $9$, $8.5$, $8.1$, $8.01$ --- each is $8 + h$, and the
column is visibly closing on 8 from above. Because the simplified form is
$8 + h$, the limit is immediate: $\ans{8}$. Note that cancelling the $h$ is
legitimate only because the limit never uses $h = 0$ itself, only values near
it.}

\problem{$s(t) = t^2 + 2t$; average velocities from $t = 2$, then the
instantaneous velocity there.

\textbf{Solution:} $s(2) = 8$, and
\[
\frac{s(2+h) - s(2)}{h} = \frac{(2+h)^2 + 2(2+h) - 8}{h}
= \frac{6h + h^2}{h} = 6 + h.
\]
The table reads $7$, $6.5$, $6.1$, $6.01$. Each of those is a genuine average
velocity over a real window, and none of them is the instantaneous velocity;
the instantaneous velocity is what they approach:
$\ans{6\text{ meters per second}}$. A student who stops at the first column and
reports 7 has reported the average velocity from $t = 2$ to $t = 3$, which is
the classic confusion this problem is built to expose.}

\problem{$V(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$; find $V'(t)$ and say what it measures.

\textbf{Solution:} Differentiating term by term,
$\ans{3t^2-12t+9}$.

Meaning: $V'(t)$ is the instantaneous rate at which the volume in the tank is
changing at time $t$ --- how fast gallons are being gained (positive) or lost
(negative) at that instant, not over any interval. It is a function of $t$, so
the tank's rate is different at different moments.}

\problem{Find the instantaneous rate at $t = 5$.

\textbf{Solution:} $V'(5) = 3(25) - 12(5) + 9 = 75 - 60 + 9 = 24$, so the rate
is $\ans{24\text{ gallons per minute}}$. It is positive, so at that instant the
volume is increasing --- the tank is filling. Substituting into $V$ instead of
$V'$ gives $125 - 150 + 45 = 20$ gallons, which is the amount of water present,
not a rate; the units are the tell.}

\problem{Recognise $\displaystyle \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(5+h)^3 - 125}{h}$.

\textbf{Solution:} The quotient has the shape
$\frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$. The $(5+h)$ shows $a = 5$, and cubing it shows
$f(x) = x^3$; the subtracted constant confirms it, since $f(5) = 125$. So the
limit is $f'(5)$ for $f(x) = x^3$. Since $f'(x) = 3x^2$, the value is
$3(25) = \ans{75}$. Reading a limit this way turns a hard algebraic
simplification into a one-line derivative evaluation, which is exactly why the
pattern is worth recognising.}

\problem{$f(x) = 1/x$; find $f'(2)$ from the definition.

\textbf{Solution:}
\[
\frac{\frac{1}{2+h} - \frac{1}{2}}{h}
= \frac{\frac{2 - (2+h)}{2(2+h)}}{h}
= \frac{-h}{2h(2+h)}
= \frac{-1}{2(2+h)}.
\]
Now the $h \to 0$ substitution is safe: the expression becomes
$\frac{-1}{4}$, that is $\ans{-0.25}$. The sign is meaningful --- $1/x$ is
decreasing at $x = 2$, so its instantaneous rate there must be negative.}

\problem{Table of distances; estimate the velocity at $t = 3$.

\textbf{Solution:} With no formula there is no limit to take, so the best
available estimate is the average rate over the smallest window we have that is
centred on $t = 3$: from $t = 2$ to $t = 4$,
\[
\frac{41 - 29}{4 - 2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6,
\]
so about $\ans{6\text{ meters per second}}$. The centred window is better
because the one-sided estimates ($35 - 29 = 6$ over one second forward from 2,
and $41 - 35 = 6$ over one second forward from 3) each lean to one side of
$t = 3$, while the centred window averages the behaviour just before and just
after, cancelling most of the first-order error. Forgetting to divide by the
2-second window gives 12, a distance rather than a rate.}

\problem{Open response --- flagged for manual review.

\textbf{Model response:} For $f(x) = mx + k$ the difference quotient is
\[
\frac{m(a+h) + k - (ma + k)}{h} = \frac{mh}{h} = m,
\]
which contains no $h$ at all. It is already equal to $m$ for every nonzero $h$
and every $a$, so the limit is $m$ regardless of where you stand: a line has
one slope. That is also why a table of average rates for a linear function
needs no limit --- every entry in the table is already the answer, so nothing
is being approached. For a curve the quotient still depends on $h$, and the
limit is what removes that dependence.

Units: in $\dfrac{V(t+h) - V(t)}{h}$ the numerator is a difference of volumes,
so it carries gallons, and the denominator is a difference of times, so it
carries minutes. The quotient therefore has units of gallons per minute, and
taking a limit does not change units --- every term in the sequence already had
them. This is the general rule: a derivative's units are always the output
units divided by the input units.

\textbf{What to look for when grading:} the algebra showing the $h$ cancels and
nothing survives; the explicit statement that the result is independent of $a$;
and units derived from the quotient rather than asserted.
