# Comparing Groups with Two-Way Tables — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_twoway_curr248.pdf`, 5 pages) — 10 problems over five real situations
  (sport and walking to school, cats and dogs by age, homework club and test results,
  travel to school by year group, a new school lunch). The table is printed on the page
  with every problem that uses it, so nothing has to be looked up elsewhere. The ramp
  goes from reading a single cell, through row fractions and row percentages, to two
  problems that ask whether the two variables are associated.
- **Answer key** (`ak_twoway_curr248.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is verified"
  note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem that names which total is
  the denominator before doing the arithmetic.
- **Study guide** (`ss_twoway_curr248.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections: reading a cell and a
  total, turning a count into a row percentage, comparing two groups fairly, and deciding
  whether there is an association from the gap between row percentages. Each has a rule
  box, a worked example and a try-it.

## What is verified

**15 of the 17 answers on this sheet are machine-checked** by SymPy, across all 10
problems. Eight of the ten problems are fully machine-checked; the two that are not are
**partly instructor-judged**:

- **Problem 6** — the non-attenders' pass rate (60 percent) is machine-checked; the
  description of whether attending the club and passing are associated is judged by you.
- **Problem 10** — both year-group percentages (75 and 40) are machine-checked; the
  judgement about association, and what the percentages show that the raw counts do not,
  is judged by you.

That matches the key's own "What is verified" note. Both written halves have a grading
rubric in the key.

## Notes for you

- The central idea of the sheet is the denominator. Problems 4 and 8 are built so that
  the group with the larger raw count does *not* have the larger share (45 out of 80 is a
  smaller fraction than 35 out of 60). If your student compares counts instead of
  fractions there, they will get it backwards — which is the point.
- Problem 9 is the deliberate contrast: it asks about *all* students, so the grand total
  120 really is the right denominator.
- Standard covered: 8.SP.A.4 across all ten problems.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
