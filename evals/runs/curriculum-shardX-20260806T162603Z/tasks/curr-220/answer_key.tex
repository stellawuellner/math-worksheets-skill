\problem{Gym table: months 0, 2, 4, 6 against total cost \$30, \$54, \$78,
\$102. (a) Change in cost from month 0 to month 6. (b) Rate of change in
dollars per month.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Read the two end rows: 102 dollars at month 6 against
30 dollars at month 0, so the change is $102 - 30$. \ans{72}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The change in cost goes on top and the change in months
underneath: $\dfrac{72}{6 - 0}$. \ans{m = 12}

\problem{Second gym: $C = 9x + 45$ dollars for $x$ months. Rate of change?}

\textbf{Solution:} Increase $x$ by one month. The cost goes from $9x + 45$ to
$9(x + 1) + 45$, and the difference is $9$ dollars --- the number multiplying
$x$. The \$45 joining fee never changes, so it does not affect the rate.
\ans{9}

\problem{Line $P$ through $(0, 2)$ and $(4, 14)$. Rate of change?}

\textbf{Solution:} From the first marked point to the second, $y$ rises
$14 - 2 = 12$ while $x$ runs $4 - 0 = 4$, so the rate is $12/4$.
\ans{m = 3}

\problem{Ana: 0 minutes / 0 metres and 9 minutes / 1350 metres. Ben:
$d = 160t$. (a) Ana's rate. (b) Compare.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{1350 - 0}{9 - 0}$ metres per minute.
\ans{m = 150}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Ben's equation has 160 multiplying $t$, so his rate is
160 metres per minute. Comparing Ana's rate with Ben's: \ans{150 < 160}

\problem{Battery: $B = 45 - 6t$ percent after $t$ hours. Rate of change?}

\textbf{Solution:} Each extra hour subtracts another 6 percent, so the rate is
negative: the charge falls by 6 percentage points per hour. \ans{-6}

\problem{Line $Q$ through $(0, 10)$ and $(5, 0)$. Rate of change?}

\textbf{Solution:} $y$ falls $0 - 10 = -10$ while $x$ runs $5 - 0 = 5$, so the
rate is $-10/5$. The line falls from left to right, which agrees with the
negative sign. \ans{m = -2}

\problem{Line $R$ through $(0, 1)$ and $(6, 13)$; second line $y = 3x$.
(a) Rate of $R$. (b) Compare.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{13 - 1}{6 - 0} = \dfrac{12}{6}$. \ans{m = 2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The second line's equation has 3 multiplying $x$, so
its rate is 3. Comparing the rate of $R$ with it: \ans{2 < 3}

\problem{Reading log: days 0, 3, 6, 9 against pages left 200, 155, 110, 65.
(a) Change in pages left. (b) Rate of change in pages per day.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $65 - 200$ pages. \ans{-135}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\dfrac{-135}{9 - 0}$ pages per day; the book is
getting shorter, so the rate is negative. \ans{m = -15}

\problem{Machine A makes 3 items per minute (line $P$). Machine B: 0 minutes /
5 items and 8 minutes / 45 items. (a) B's rate. (b) Compare.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{45 - 5}{8 - 0}$ items per minute. \ans{m = 5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Comparing machine B's rate with machine A's rate of 3:
\ans{5 > 3}

\problem{Sapling: $y = \frac{3}{4}x + 2$ metres after $x$ months. Rate of
change?}

\textbf{Solution:} The number multiplying $x$ is $\frac{3}{4}$, so each extra
month adds three quarters of a metre. The starting height of 2 metres does not
change the rate. \ans{m = \tfrac{3}{4}}

\problem{Pool 1: 0 minutes / 20 litres and 6 minutes / 92 litres. Pool 2:
$L = 14t + 20$. (a) Pool 1's rate. (b) Compare. (c) Explain from a graph.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{92 - 20}{6 - 0}$ litres per minute.
\ans{m = 12}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Pool 2's equation has 14 multiplying $t$, so its rate
is 14 litres per minute. Comparing pool 1 with pool 2: \ans{12 < 14}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct explanation says the
steeper line is the faster pool, and connects steepness to the rate: pool 2
climbs 14 litres for every minute across while pool 1 climbs only 12, so pool
2's line is steeper. Both pools start at the same 20 litres, so pool 2's line
is above pool 1's from the first minute onward. ``Look at the graph'' with no
statement of what to look at does not earn credit.

\problem{Service X: 0 hours / 0 km and 5 hours / 275 km. Service Y:
$d = 60t$. (a) X's rate. (b) Compare. (c) Explain from a graph.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{275 - 0}{5 - 0}$ kilometres per hour.
\ans{m = 55}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Service Y's equation has 60 multiplying $t$, so its
rate is 60 km per hour. Comparing X with Y: \ans{55 < 60}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct explanation says both
services start at zero kilometres, so their graphs share the same starting
point and the faster one is simply whichever line is steeper --- equivalently,
whichever line is higher at any chosen time. Reading both heights at one
common hour and comparing them earns full credit. Restating ``Y is faster''
with no graphical reason does not.
