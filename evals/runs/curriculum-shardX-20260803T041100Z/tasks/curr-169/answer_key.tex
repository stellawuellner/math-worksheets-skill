\problem{\textbf{Evaluate} $-8 - (-3)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Add the opposite. The second number is $-3$, and its opposite
is $+3$, so $-8 - (-3) = -8 + 3$. Starting at $-8$ and moving 3 units to the
right lands on $-5$. \ans{-5}

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} an answer of $-11$ means the two negatives were
merged into one and the student computed $-8 + (-3)$. The minus sign in front of
the parenthesis is an operation; the minus inside is part of the number.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate} $5 - 12$.

\textbf{Solution:} Add the opposite: $5 - 12 = 5 + (-12)$. The two numbers pull
in opposite directions, so subtract distances from zero, $12 - 5 = 7$, and take
the sign of the number farther from zero, which is $-12$. The result is
therefore negative. \ans{-7}

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} an answer of $7$ means the student computed
$12 - 5$ to avoid the negative. Which number is written first is what decides
the sign.}

\problem{\textbf{Evaluate} $-3.5 - 2.25$.

\textbf{Solution:} Add the opposite: $-3.5 - 2.25 = -3.5 + (-2.25)$. Both terms
are now negative, so they accumulate in the same direction: the distances add,
$3.5 + 2.25 = 5.75$, and the common sign is negative. \ans{-5.75}

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} an answer of $-1.25$ means the subtraction was
rewritten as an addition without changing the sign of the second number.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare} $-8 - (-3)$ and $-8 + 3$.

\textbf{Solution:} Rewrite the left side by adding the opposite:
$-8 - (-3) = -8 + 3$. The two expressions are now identical character for
character, so no arithmetic is needed --- they must be equal. (Both do evaluate
to $-5$.) The symbol that belongs in the blank is \ans{=}

\noindent Completed sentence: subtracting $-3$ has the same effect as
\emph{adding} $3$, because the opposite of $-3$ is $3$.}

\problem{\textbf{Order} $-2 - 7$, \ $2 - 7$, \ $-2 + 7$ \textbf{from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Evaluate each one first, adding the opposite where needed.
$-2 - 7 = -2 + (-7) = -9$. \ $2 - 7 = 2 + (-7) = -5$. \ $-2 + 7 = 5$.
On the number line $-9$ sits farthest left, then $-5$, then $5$ to the right of
zero. \ans{-9 < -5 < 5}

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} ordering by digits alone puts $-9$ last. Among
negatives, the larger the digit, the smaller the number.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} Dana wrote $-4 - (-9) = -13$.

\textbf{Solution:} Dana added the two negatives instead of subtracting a
negative --- she computed $-4 + (-9)$. The rule she broke is ``subtract by adding
the opposite'': the opposite of $-9$ is $+9$, not $-9$. Corrected,
$-4 - (-9) = -4 + 9$, and moving 9 units right from $-4$ lands 5 units past
zero. \ans{5}

\noindent A useful size check: subtracting a negative always makes the result
\emph{larger} than the number you started with, so any answer below $-4$ is
wrong before the arithmetic is even checked.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} Marcus wrote $-7 - 4 = -3$.

\textbf{Solution:} Marcus treated $-7 - 4$ as $-7 + 4$; he used the minus as a
sign on the 4 and then added it anyway. Subtracting a positive means adding a
negative: $-7 - 4 = -7 + (-4)$. Both terms are negative, so the distances add,
$7 + 4 = 11$, with a negative sign. \ans{-11}

\noindent Size check: subtracting a positive must move the result \emph{left},
so the answer has to be less than $-7$; $-3$ is to the right of $-7$ and cannot
be correct.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge --- find and fix:} a student got $-12.75$ for
$-2.5 - (-6) - 4.25$.

\textbf{Solution:} The mishandled sign is the one in the middle term. The
student read $- (-6)$ as $-6$, computing $-2.5 - 6 - 4.25 = -12.75$; the double
negative should have become $+6$. Rewrite every subtraction as an addition
first, then work left to right:
\begin{align*}
  -2.5 - (-6) - 4.25 &= -2.5 + 6 + (-4.25) \\
  -2.5 + 6 &= 3.5 \\
  3.5 + (-4.25) &= -0.75
\end{align*}
The correct value is \ans{-0.75}

\noindent The corrected answer is 12 units to the right of the student's, which
is exactly twice the 6 whose sign was flipped --- the standard signature of a
single dropped double negative.}
