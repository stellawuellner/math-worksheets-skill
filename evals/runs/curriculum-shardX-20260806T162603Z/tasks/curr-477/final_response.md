# Integration by Parts: Choosing the Pieces — three PDFs

**Course:** AP Calculus AB/BC · **Topic:** substitution, parts, partial
fractions and improper integrals, focused on using integration by parts
strategically · **10 problems**

## What you get

- **`ws_parts_curr477.pdf` — the student worksheet.**
  A clean ramp from a worked pattern to independent fluency, and **no two
  problems share a skeleton**: $xe^x$, then a bare $\ln x$ (where $dv = dx$ is
  the whole trick), then $x\sin x$, $x\ln x$, a definite exponential, a
  double-pass $x^2e^x$, $\arctan x$, the cyclic $e^x\sin x$ that comes back and
  has to be solved for algebraically, a definite trig integral, and finally an
  improper integral finished with a limit. The three strategy families are
  interleaved rather than blocked, so the choice of $u$ is a decision on every
  problem instead of a habit.
- **`ak_parts_curr477.pdf` — the full answer key.**
  Every problem restated and worked with the $u$/$dv$ choice named before the
  algebra. It opens with a generated Quick Answers bank, a "What is verified"
  note, and a Curriculum block.
- **`ss_parts_curr477.pdf` — the study guide (2 pages).**
  Three sections: one pass and how to choose $u$ and $dv$; why logarithms and
  inverse trig functions are always $u$; and the two ways a second pass ends
  the problem (degree reduction, or the integral returning so you can solve for
  it). Each has a rule box, a worked example that opens by naming the choice,
  and a try-it with the answer printed upside down.

## Verification, stated honestly

The ten problems ask for **13 responses**.

- **11 of 13 are machine-checked** with SymPy. Every antiderivative is checked
  by differentiating it back to the integrand — the strongest check available
  here, since a dropped coefficient or a sign slip cannot survive it — and both
  definite integrals and the improper integral's limit are recomputed exactly.
- **2 of 13 are instructor-judged**: 6(b), why parts must be applied twice, and
  10(c), why $u = x$ rather than $u = e^{-x}$ is the strategic choice. They
  print as `---` in the Quick Answers bank, and the key states what a correct
  answer must contain — the degree-lowering idea in 6(b), the reason and not
  just the assignment in 10(c). Those two are the "strategically" in the task,
  so they are worth reading rather than skimming.

The study guide's six results are all machine-checked.

## Suggested order

Study guide first, then the worksheet, then the answer key.
