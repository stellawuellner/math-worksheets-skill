\problem{The blocks show $47$. Cross out $23$. What is left?}

\textbf{Solution:} There are enough ones to take from, so no rod needs opening. Cross out three of the seven single squares, leaving four ones, then cross out two of the four rods, leaving two rods. Two tens and four ones. \ans{24}

\textbf{Blocks left:} two rods and four squares.

\problem{The blocks show $68$. Cross out $35$. What is left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Ones first: cross out five of the eight squares, leaving three. Then three of the six rods, leaving three rods. Three tens and three ones. \ans{33}

\problem{The blocks show $52$. Cross out $27$.}

\textbf{Solution:} There are only two single squares and seven must go, so open one rod into ten ones. The picture now shows four rods and twelve ones --- still fifty-two, only shown differently. Cross out seven of the twelve ones, leaving five; cross out two of the four rods, leaving two. Two tens and five ones. \ans{25}

\problem{The blocks show $64$. Cross out $28$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Four ones cannot give up eight, so open a rod: five rods and fourteen ones. Take eight ones, leaving six; take two rods, leaving three. Three tens and six ones. \ans{36}

\problem{The blocks show $71$. (a) Estimate $71 - 38$ to the nearest ten. (b) Find the exact answer.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $71$ rounds down to $70$ and $38$ rounds up to $40$. Subtracting the rounded numbers gives $70 - 40$. \ans{30}

\textbf{Solution (b):} One single square cannot give up eight, so open a rod: six rods and eleven ones. Take eight ones, leaving three; take three rods, leaving three. The exact answer sits three above the estimate, so the estimate did its job. \ans{33}

\problem{A jar held $82$ marbles and $45$ were taken out. How many are left?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Taken out'' means subtract. Two ones cannot give up five, so open a rod: seven rods and twelve ones. Take five ones, leaving seven; take four rods, leaving three. \ans{37 \text{ marbles}}

\problem{Sam worked out $63 - 27$ and wrote $44$. (a) Work it out correctly. (b) Explain what Sam did wrong.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Three ones cannot give up seven, so open a rod: five rods and thirteen ones. Take seven ones, leaving six; take two rods, leaving three. \ans{36}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response names the flipped column. Full credit for the idea that there were only three ones to take seven from, and instead of opening a rod the ones column was worked backwards --- the smaller digit taken from the larger. Accept an answer that says a ten should have been opened into ten ones first, leaving five rods and thirteen ones. Saying only that the answer is too big, or that regrouping was needed, without naming the reversed column, earns half credit. Restating the correct difference earns no credit, since part (a) already asks for it.

\problem{The blocks show $94$. (a) Estimate $94 - 47$ to the nearest ten. (b) Find the exact answer. (c) Explain why the estimate landed below it.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $94$ rounds down to $90$ and $47$ rounds up to $50$, so the estimate is $90 - 50$. \ans{40}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Four ones cannot give up seven, so open a rod: eight rods and fourteen ones. Take seven ones, leaving seven; take four rods, leaving four. \ans{47}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response tracks the direction of each rounding. Full credit for the idea that the number being taken away was rounded \emph{up} while the number in front was rounded \emph{down}, so the estimate takes away more from less and must come out low. Accept an answer that follows just one of the two roundings correctly and says which way it moved the result. Noticing only that the estimate is smaller, with no reason, earns half credit. Claiming that an estimate must always match the exact answer earns no credit.
