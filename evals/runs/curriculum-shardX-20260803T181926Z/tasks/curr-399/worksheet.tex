\wstitleblock{Conic Sections --- Finding and Fixing Sign and Denominator Errors}{Algebra 2}{}

\noindent\small Almost every mistake made with a conic equation is one of two
mistakes. Read each equation twice before you compute.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Signs.} The standard forms are written with $(x-h)$ and $(y-k)$,
        so the centre coordinate is the value that makes the binomial
        \emph{zero}: $(x+4)$ means $h=-4$, not $h=4$.
  \item \textbf{Denominators.} Every denominator is a \emph{square}. The
        semi-axis is its square root, so a denominator of $25$ gives a
        semi-axis of $5$; and for a circle the right-hand side is $r^2$, not $r$.
  \item Show the step where you set the binomial to zero and the step where you
        take the square root. Those are the two steps these errors hide in.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Warm-up.} Write the centre and the radius of the circle
\[ (x+4)^2 + (y-7)^2 = 36. \]}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Dev looks at
$(x-5)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 49$ and writes: ``the centre is $(-5,\ 2)$ and the radius
is $49$.''
\par\smallskip
Dev made \emph{two} separate errors. Name each one, then write the correct
centre and the correct radius.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Semi-axes of an ellipse.} For
$\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$, write the length of the semi-major axis
and the length of the semi-minor axis.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Back to standard form.} The circle
$x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 10y + 18 = 0$ is written in general form. Complete the square
in $x$ and in $y$, then write the centre and the radius. Watch the sign of each
coefficient and the side of the equation the constant $18$ belongs on.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Rae studies
$\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{49} = 1$ and reports: ``the major axis is
horizontal and its length is $4$.''
\par\smallskip
Rae used the wrong denominator \emph{and} skipped a step. State which
denominator decides the major axis and why, then give the correct direction and
the correct full length of the major axis.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Shifted ellipse.} For
$\dfrac{(x+3)^2}{16} + \dfrac{(y-1)^2}{4} = 1$, write the centre and the length
of the semi-major axis.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Sam writes
$\dfrac{y^2}{9} - \dfrac{x^2}{16} = 1$ and concludes: ``it opens left and right,
with vertices $4$ units either side of the centre.''
\par\smallskip
Which term carries the plus sign, and what does that tell you about the
direction the branches open? Correct both the direction and the vertices.}

\problem{\textbf{Show what you know.} Choose one centre other than the origin
and use it for all three parts.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Write an equation of a circle with that centre.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Write an equation of an ellipse with that centre whose major axis
is vertical.
\par
\textbf{(c)} Write an equation of a hyperbola with that centre whose branches
open left and right.
\par
\textbf{(d)} Explain, in two or three sentences, how a reader who sees only your
three equations can tell them apart --- and how they can read your centre back
out of the signs.
