\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Sam, $6$ stickers and $3$ more.}\\
Step 1: name the mistake. Sam said ``six'' as his first hop. But the six
stickers are already there before he moves --- saying the number he starts from
costs no hop at all, so he only took two real hops instead of three.\\
Step 2: count on the right way. Hold $6$ in your head, then hop:
seven (one), eight (two), nine (three).\\
Step 3: Sam has $\ans{9}$ stickers.\\
Step 4: check it a different way. Count every dot in the picture from the
beginning: six filled dots and then three more open ones is nine dots
$\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $8$. A count-on answer that is exactly one too small is nearly
always this mistake.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Mia, $4$ blocks and $2$ more.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: Mia is adding two blocks, so she should take $2$ hops. She
said ``four, five'' --- only one of those was a real hop, because ``four'' is the
number she started from.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: hold $4$ in your head and hop twice: five (one),
six (two).\\
Step 3: Mia has $\ans{6}$ blocks.\\
Step 4: check with fingers --- hold up four fingers, then put up two more and
count them all: six $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $5$. Again exactly one too small.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Ravi, $3$ cars and $5$ more.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: Ravi started at $3$, the smaller number, which left him five
hops to keep track of. Starting at $5$, the bigger number, leaves only three hops
--- and the total is the same either way, so it is always allowed to start from
the bigger one.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: hold $5$ in your head and hop three times:
six (one), seven (two), eight (three).\\
Step 3: Ravi has $\ans{8}$ cars.\\
Step 4: check by counting the picture straight through: three filled dots and
five open dots make eight $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $7$. Fewer hops means fewer chances to drop one, which is the
whole reason for starting big.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Jo has $5$ shells and wants $9$.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: name the mistake. Jo counted the \emph{words} she said, and
the first word, ``five'', was not a hop --- it is where she started. The hops are
six, seven, eight, nine, so she really took $4$ hops.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: each hop is one more shell, so the number of hops is the
number of shells she still needs.\\
Step 3: Jo needs $\ans{4}$ more shells.\\
Step 4: check by adding it back: $5 + 4 = 9$ $\checkmark$. Jo's answer would give
$5 + 5 = 10$, which is too many.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $5$, the number of words said out loud. Counting words instead
of hops is the same slip as counting the start number as a hop.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Ana, $2$ marbles and $7$ more.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: Ana started at $2$ and had seven hops to track, and one got
lost. Starting at $7$ leaves only two hops.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: hold $7$ in your head and hop twice: eight (one),
nine (two).\\
Step 3: Ana has $\ans{9}$ marbles.\\
Step 4: check the other way round --- starting at $2$ and hopping seven times
also lands on nine, but you have to say seven numbers without a slip
$\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $8$. When the added number is big, always turn the problem
around first.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Ben's two problems.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: name the mistake. Both times Ben said his starting number as
hop number one. That always makes the total exactly one too small, which is why
both of his answers are off by the same amount.\\
Step 2: pencils --- hold $6$ in your head and hop four times: seven, eight, nine,
ten.\\
Step 3: Ben has $\ans{10}$ pencils.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 4: crayons --- turn it around and start from the bigger number.
Hold $6$ in your head and hop twice: seven, eight.\\
Step 5: Ben has $\ans{8}$ crayons.\\
Step 6: check both by counting the picture dots straight through: the top row
holds ten dots, the bottom row eight $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $9$ and $7$, each exactly one too small. When a child's answers
are all one below the right answer, the fix is the start number, not more
practice at counting.}
