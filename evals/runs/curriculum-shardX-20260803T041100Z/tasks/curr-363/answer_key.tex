\problem{Factor $f(x) = x^3 - 4x$ and list its zeros. \\
Pull the greatest common factor first --- skipping it is what loses a zero:
$x^3 - 4x = x(x^2 - 4)$. The second factor is a difference of squares,
$x^2 - 4 = (x-2)(x+2)$. \ans{x(x-2)(x+2)}
\par\noindent Each factor is linear and appears once, so the zeros are
$x = 0$, $x = 2$, $x = -2$, each of multiplicity $1$: the graph crosses the
axis at all three.}

\problem{$g(x) = (x+1)(x-3)^2$: zeros, multiplicity, and behavior. \\
The polynomial is already factored, so read the zeros straight off: setting each
factor to zero gives $x = -1$ from $(x+1)$ and $x = 3$ from $(x-3)^2$.
\par\noindent $x = -1$ has multiplicity $1$ (odd) --- the graph \emph{crosses}
there. $x = 3$ has multiplicity $2$ (even) --- the graph \emph{touches} the axis
and turns back, without changing sign. \ans{x = -1, \ x = 3}
\par\noindent Degree check: $1 + 2 = 3$, so $g$ is cubic with a positive leading
coefficient.}

\problem{Factor $h(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 10x$ and list its zeros. \\
GCF first: $x^3 - 3x^2 - 10x = x(x^2 - 3x - 10)$. Then factor the trinomial ---
two numbers multiplying to $-10$ and adding to $-3$ are $-5$ and $2$:
$x^2 - 3x - 10 = (x-5)(x+2)$. \ans{x(x-5)(x+2)}
\par\noindent Zeros $x = 0$, $x = 5$, $x = -2$, all of multiplicity $1$, so the
graph crosses at each.}

\problem{End behavior of $f(x) = -2x^3 + 5x - 1$. \\
Only the leading term matters as $|x|$ grows, because $-2x^3$ outgrows $5x$ and
the constant. The degree is $3$ (odd), so the two ends go opposite ways, and the
leading coefficient $-2$ is negative, so the right end falls.
\[ \text{as } x \to \infty,\ f(x) \to \boxed{-\infty}
   \qquad\qquad
   \text{as } x \to -\infty,\ f(x) \to \boxed{+\infty} \]
\par\noindent Expected sentence: ``The odd degree makes the ends opposite; the
negative leading coefficient decides which end goes down.''}

\problem{$p(x) = x^2(x-4)^3$: degree, zeros, multiplicity, behavior. \\
Adding the exponents gives the degree: $2 + 3 = 5$, so $p$ is quintic with a
positive leading coefficient.
\par\noindent $x = 0$ has multiplicity $2$ (even) --- the graph \emph{touches}
the axis and turns back. $x = 4$ has multiplicity $3$ (odd and greater than one)
--- the graph \emph{crosses}, but flattens against the axis as it passes.
\ans{x = 0, \ x = 4}}

\problem{Least-degree polynomial crossing at $x = -2$ and bouncing at $x = 1$. \\
Crossing means an odd multiplicity, and the least odd multiplicity is $1$, so
$(x+2)$ appears once. Bouncing means an even multiplicity, least is $2$, so
$(x-1)^2$. With leading coefficient $1$:
\[ (x+2)(x-1)^2 = (x+2)(x^2 - 2x + 1) = x^3 - 2x^2 + x + 2x^2 - 4x + 2 \]
Collecting like terms gives the expanded form. \ans{x^3 - 3x + 2}
\par\noindent Check: substituting $x = 1$ gives $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$ $\checkmark$ and
$x = -2$ gives $-8 + 6 + 2 = 0$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{Solve $x^4 - 5x^2 + 4 = 0$. \\
Treat it as a quadratic in $x^2$: two numbers multiplying to $4$ and adding to
$-5$ are $-1$ and $-4$, so
$x^4 - 5x^2 + 4 = (x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4)$. Each factor is a difference of squares:
\[ (x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4) = (x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x+2) \]
Four distinct linear factors, each of multiplicity $1$, so the graph crosses at
all four. \ans{x = -2, -1, 1, 2}}

\problem{End behavior of $g(x) = 3x^4 - x^3$. \\
The leading term is $3x^4$. The degree is $4$ (even), so both ends travel the
same way, and the leading coefficient $3$ is positive, so both rise.
\[ \text{as } x \to \infty,\ g(x) \to \boxed{+\infty}
   \qquad\qquad
   \text{as } x \to -\infty,\ g(x) \to \boxed{+\infty} \]
\par\noindent Expected sentence: ``Problem 4 had odd degree, so its ends split;
here the degree is even, so a negative input raised to an even power is still
positive and both ends rise.''}

\problem{Least-degree polynomial crossing at $x = -3$ and touching at $x = 2$. \\
Crossing at $x = -3$ gives the factor $(x+3)$ once; touching at $x = 2$ gives
$(x-2)^2$. With leading coefficient $1$:
\[ (x+3)(x-2)^2 = (x+3)(x^2 - 4x + 4) = x^3 - 4x^2 + 4x + 3x^2 - 12x + 12 \]
Collecting like terms gives the expanded form. \ans{x^3 - x^2 - 8x + 12}
\par\noindent Check the sketch: degree $3$ is odd with a positive leading
coefficient, so the curve falls on the left and rises on the right, bouncing off
the axis at $x = 2$ on the way up.}

\problem{Sketch $y = x(x+2)^2(x-3)$. \\
\textbf{Zeros and behavior.} $x = 0$ (multiplicity $1$, crosses),
$x = -2$ (multiplicity $2$, touches), $x = 3$ (multiplicity $1$, crosses).
\ans{x = -2, \ x = 0, \ x = 3}
\par\noindent\textbf{End behavior.} Degree $1 + 2 + 1 = 4$ (even) with a positive
leading coefficient, so both ends rise.
\par\noindent\textbf{Model sketch (manual review --- compare the student's
curve to this description).} Coming down from the upper left, the curve touches
the axis at $x = -2$ without crossing, turns back up, then comes down through
$x = 0$, dips to a minimum between $x = 0$ and $x = 3$ (the low point is about
$y = -32$, at $x = 2$), crosses at $x = 3$, and rises steeply.
\emph{Credit the sketch when the three intercepts are marked, the bounce at
$x = -2$ is visible, and both ends point up; the vertical scale is the
student's own choice.}}
