Here are the three PDFs for a 10-problem precalculus/statistics set on **summarizing
distributions with center, spread and shape**.

**Worksheet — `ws_distributions_curr446.pdf` (4 pages).** The sheet works with two real
data sets printed at the top — Class A (10 students, one clear outlier at 85 minutes) and
Class B (7 students, evenly spread) — so every idea is computed on data the student can see
rather than on a new story each time. The design is deliberate: Class A and Class B differ
in exactly the way the topic is about, so problem 6 finds Class A's outlier and calls the
shape right-skewed, problem 7 finds Class B's mean and median agreeing and calls it
symmetric, and the closing synthesis problem corrects the 85 to 45 and has the student watch
the mean fall by 4 while the median does not move at all. That is the resistance of the
median shown rather than asserted.

Problem 4 uses a small frequency table so the student practises turning a display into
notation, and problem 9 asks for a probability read straight off the sorted list.

Every answer that is a duration is answered on a line that prints its unit, and the verify
data declares that unit, so an answer in the wrong unit cannot pass.

**Answer key — `ak_distributions_curr446.pdf` (3 pages).** Every problem restated and worked
— including the full deviation-squared table for the standard deviation — with a
quick-answer bank, a curriculum block, and a "common wrong answers" line generated from the
declared trap (taking the 5th value of a ten-value list as the median).

**Study guide — `ss_distributions_curr446.pdf` (2 pages).** Four sections: measures of
center, measures of spread, shape and outliers and which center to trust, and reading a
display. Each has a rule box, a worked example that opens by naming why that measure is the
right one, and a try-it.

**What was verified, honestly.** The sheet asks for **18 separate responses**. **15 are
machine-checked** — every mean, median, range, quartile, IQR and standard deviation
recomputed by SymPy from the same data list printed on the sheet, plus both table readings
and the probability. **3 are instructor-judged** and marked `---` in the quick-answer bank:
the shape description in 6(b), the symmetry comparison in 7(c), and the resistance
explanation in 10(c). Those are the interpretive claims that make this topic more than
arithmetic, and no computer algebra system can grade them; the key prints a model answer and
an explicit full-credit criterion for each. The eight study-guide examples and try-its are
machine-checked.

No response is marked `[unchecked]`.
