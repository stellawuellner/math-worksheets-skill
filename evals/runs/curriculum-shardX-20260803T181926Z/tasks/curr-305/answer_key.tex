\problem{\textbf{Solve $3x + 7 = 22$ with a reason on every line.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A justified solution is a proof: each line must be the
previous line plus one named property, so undo the operations one at a time.

\textbf{Step 2.} $3x + 7 = 22$ \quad (Given)

\textbf{Step 3.} $3x = 15$ \quad (Subtraction Property of Equality --- subtract
$7$ from both sides)

\textbf{Step 4.} $x = 5$ \quad (Division Property of Equality --- divide both
sides by $3$) \ans{x = 5}

\textit{Manual-review item:} the reason column is the graded part here. Accept
Subtraction then Division; ``inverse operations'' alone is not a named
property.}

\problem{\textbf{$B$ between $A$ and $C$; $AB = 2x+3$, $BC = 3x-1$, $AC = 27$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Betweenness is what licenses adding the parts: the Segment
Addition Postulate says $AB + BC = AC$. Nothing in the givens says the two
parts are equal, so a sum --- not an equality between the parts --- is the
only equation available.

\textbf{Step 2.} Substitute the given expressions:
$(2x + 3) + (3x - 1) = 27$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Combine like terms: $5x + 2 = 27$, so $5x = 25$ and
\ans{x = 5}

\textbf{Step 4.} Substitute back into the expression for $AB$:
$AB = 2(5) + 3 = 13$. \ans{AB = 13}

\textit{Check:} $BC = 3(5) - 1 = 14$, and $13 + 14 = 27$, the given whole.}

\problem{\textbf{$\overrightarrow{BD}$ bisects $\angle ABC$;
$m\angle ABD = (2x+5)^\circ$, $m\angle DBC = (4x-19)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The definition of an angle bisector says the two halves are
congruent, and congruent angles have equal measures --- that equality is the
equation.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2x + 5 = 4x - 19$, so $24 = 2x$ and \ans{x = 12}

\textbf{Step 3.} Each half measures $2(12) + 5 = 29$ degrees.

\textbf{Step 4.} The whole angle is the sum of the two congruent halves
(Angle Addition Postulate), so $m\angle ABC = 2(29)$.
\ans{m\angle ABC = 58^\circ}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $29^\circ$ --- stopping at the half. The question
asks for $\angle ABC$, which the bisector splits.}

\problem{\textbf{Solve $5(x-2) = 3x + 8$ with a reason on every line.}

\textbf{Step 1.} There are parentheses before there is anything to undo, so the
first move is not a property of equality at all.

\textbf{Step 2.} $5(x - 2) = 3x + 8$ \quad (Given)

\textbf{Step 3.} $5x - 10 = 3x + 8$ \quad (\textbf{Distributive Property} ---
this is the line that is not a property of equality; it rewrites one side
rather than doing the same thing to both)

\textbf{Step 4.} $2x - 10 = 8$ \quad (Subtraction Property of Equality)

\textbf{Step 5.} $2x = 18$ \quad (Addition Property of Equality)

\textbf{Step 6.} $x = 9$ \quad (Division Property of Equality) \ans{x = 9}

\textit{Manual-review item:} the student must name Step 3 as the Distributive
Property and say that it changes only one side.}

\problem{\textbf{Given: $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$, $N$ is the
midpoint of $\overline{AM}$. Prove: $AN = \frac{1}{4}AB$.}

\textbf{Strategy.} Each midpoint gives one halving. Apply the definition
twice, then substitute one result into the other --- that substitution is the
step that turns two local facts into a single conclusion.

\modelproof{
1.\ $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$; $N$ is the midpoint of
$\overline{AM}$ & 1.\ Given \\ \hline
2.\ $AM = \frac{1}{2}AB$ & 2.\ Definition of midpoint \\ \hline
3.\ $AN = \frac{1}{2}AM$ & 3.\ Definition of midpoint \\ \hline
4.\ $AN = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}AB\right)$ & 4.\ Substitution Property
of Equality \\ \hline
5.\ $AN = \frac{1}{4}AB$ & 5.\ Simplify (Multiplication Property of Equality)
\\ \hline}

\textit{Manual-review item.} Accept any proof that uses the midpoint
definition twice and substitutes. A proof that jumps straight from the givens
to $\frac{1}{4}$ has skipped the step that does the work.}

\problem{\textbf{Linear pair: $m\angle AEC = (3x+10)^\circ$,
$m\angle CEB = (x+30)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The Linear Pair Postulate is what supplies a number: two
angles forming a linear pair are supplementary, so their measures sum to
$180$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(3x + 10) + (x + 30) = 180$, so $4x + 40 = 180$ and
$4x = 140$. \ans{x = 35}

\textbf{Step 3.} Substitute into the asked-for angle:
$m\angle AEC = 3(35) + 10$. \ans{m\angle AEC = 115^\circ}

\textit{Check:} $m\angle CEB = 35 + 30 = 65$, and $115 + 65 = 180$.}

\problem{\textbf{$P$ between $Q$ and $R$; $QP = 4x-5$, $PR = 2x+7$, $QR = 50$.
Dana wrote $4x - 5 = 2x + 7$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Dana's equation says the two parts are equal. Nothing
given says that --- no congruence marks, no midpoint, no bisector. The only
relationship the givens establish is that $P$ is between $Q$ and $R$, and
betweenness gives a \emph{sum}, not an equality of the parts.

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} The Segment Addition Postulate gives
$QP + PR = QR$, so $(4x - 5) + (2x + 7) = 50$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Combine: $6x + 2 = 50$, so $6x = 48$. \ans{x = 8}

\textit{Check:} $QP = 27$, $PR = 23$, and $27 + 23 = 50$. Notice the two parts
are genuinely unequal --- which is exactly why Dana's equation could not have
followed from the givens.

\textit{Manual-review item:} part (a) is the graded sentence. Look for
``nothing states the parts are congruent'' or ``betweenness gives a sum''.}

\problem{\textbf{Parallel lines: $m\angle p = (5x-12)^\circ$,
$m\angle q = (3x+16)^\circ$, same-side interior.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Same-side interior angles on parallel lines are supplementary
(Same-Side Interior Angles Theorem, which needs $m \parallel n$ --- without
the parallel givens, no relationship exists at all).

\textbf{Step 2.} $(5x - 12) + (3x + 16) = 180$, so $8x + 4 = 180$ and
$8x = 176$. \ans{x = 22}

\textbf{Step 3.} $m\angle p = 5(22) - 12$. \ans{m\angle p = 98^\circ}

\textbf{Step 4.} $m\angle q = 3(22) + 16$. \ans{m\angle q = 82^\circ}

\textit{Check:} $98 + 82 = 180$, as supplementary angles must.}

\problem{\textbf{Given: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{DE}$,
$\overline{BC} \cong \overline{EF}$, $\angle B \cong \angle E$. Prove:
$\overline{AC} \cong \overline{DF}$.}

\textbf{Strategy.} $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{DF}$ are not given directly,
so they have to arrive as corresponding parts --- which means proving the
whole triangles congruent first. The given angle sits between the two given
sides, so SAS is the postulate that fits.

\modelproof{
1.\ $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{DE}$ & 1.\ Given \\ \hline
2.\ $\angle B \cong \angle E$ & 2.\ Given \\ \hline
3.\ $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{EF}$ & 3.\ Given \\ \hline
4.\ $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ & 4.\ SAS Congruence Postulate \\ \hline
5.\ $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{DF}$ & 5.\ CPCTC \\ \hline}

\textit{Manual-review item.} The order of statements 1--3 does not matter, but
the angle must be identified as the \emph{included} angle for SAS to apply,
and step 5 must cite CPCTC rather than restating the given.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: $\angle ABD \cong \angle DBC$,
$m\angle ABC = (7x-4)^\circ$, $m\angle ABD = (2x+10)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a). Model argument.} $\overrightarrow{BD}$ lies inside
$\angle ABC$, so by the Angle Addition Postulate
$m\angle ABD + m\angle DBC = m\angle ABC$. It is given that
$\angle ABD \cong \angle DBC$, and congruent angles have equal measures, so
$m\angle DBC = m\angle ABD$. Substituting that into the first equation gives
$m\angle ABD + m\angle ABD = m\angle ABC$, that is,
$2 \cdot m\angle ABD = m\angle ABC$. Replacing each measure by its given
expression yields $7x - 4 = 2(2x + 10)$.

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} Distribute the right side: $7x - 4 = 4x + 20$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Subtract $4x$ and add $4$ to both sides: $3x = 24$.
\ans{x = 8}

\textbf{Step 4 (b).} Substitute into the whole angle:
$m\angle ABC = 7(8) - 4$. \ans{m\angle ABC = 52^\circ}

\textit{Check:} each half measures $2(8) + 10 = 26$ degrees, and
$26 + 26 = 52$.

\textit{Manual-review item:} part (a) is graded on the chain, not the answer.
The three named moves --- Angle Addition Postulate, congruent angles have
equal measures, Substitution --- must appear in an order that actually
reaches the equation.}
