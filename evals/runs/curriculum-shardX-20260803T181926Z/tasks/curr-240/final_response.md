# Congruent or Similar? Evidence from Transformations — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready.

**Study guide (2 pages)** — start here. A top box gives the two tools (the
distance formula and slope) and states plainly what each one proves: equal
corresponding lengths mean congruent, proportional lengths with the same factor
mean similar, and *slopes* are what tell you which transformation was used. Then
four sections, each with the coordinate rule, a two-step worked example, and a
try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the box:

1. translation — $(x,y) \to (x+a, y+b)$
2. reflection — across the $x$-axis, the $y$-axis, and the line $y = x$
3. rotation — quarter turns and the half turn
4. dilation — $(x,y) \to (kx, ky)$, the one that changes size

**Worksheet (8 pages, 12 problems)** — the four transformation types are
interleaved so no two adjacent problems use the same one, and the student has to
decide what the numbers prove rather than repeat a formula. Problems 1–4 are the
warm-up (one length pair each). From problem 5 on, the evidence gets harder to
read: problem 5 uses slope rather than length, problem 6 needs *two* ratios
before similarity can be claimed, problem 7 collects full SSS evidence (six
lengths), and problem 8 asks why a half turn is the one rotation that leaves a
slope alone. Problem 9 is an error analysis — a student claims congruence from
two right triangles, and the lengths 4 and 8 show why that is wrong and what
relationship holds instead. Problems 10 and 12 each carry a blank coordinate
grid for plotting and ask the student to *name* the rigid motion and justify it;
the synthesis challenge (12) turns on the reciprocal slopes $4/3$ and $3/4$ that
are the fingerprint of a reflection across $y = x$, then asks why lengths alone
could never have identified the motion.

Work space runs 5–8 cm per problem on top of the two grids.

**Answer key (4 pages)** — quick answers at the top, a generated Curriculum
section, then a stepped solution for every problem showing the substitution into
the distance or slope formula, not just the result. Each solution closes by
saying what the numbers establish.

**Verification.** All 12 problems were machine-checked with SymPy — 43 checks in
total, since most problems compare several corresponding parts. 40 passed
automatically: every distance was recomputed from the raw coordinates, every
slope was recomputed (including the two undefined ones), and the answer key's
boxed values were bound back to those results.

**Flagged for manual review — three items**, all genuinely open writing:
problem 9(b)–(c) (why Ana is wrong and what relationship does hold),
problem 10(c) (name and justify the rigid motion), and problem 12(c)–(d) (name
the motion, and explain why equal lengths cannot identify it). The key states
what to accept for each rather than claiming they were verified.

Standard: 8.G.A throughout. Difficulty ramps 1 → 5 across the sheet.
