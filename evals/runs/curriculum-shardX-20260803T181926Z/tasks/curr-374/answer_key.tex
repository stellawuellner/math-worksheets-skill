\problem{\textbf{Compute $\sqrt{9+16}$, and compare it with $\sqrt{9}+\sqrt{16}=7$.}

Step 1. The radical sign is a grouping symbol, so everything under it is done
first: $9+16=25$.

Step 2. Take one square root of that single number: $\sqrt{25}=5$.

Step 3. Compare with the split version: $\sqrt{9}+\sqrt{16}=3+4=7$. The two
results differ by $2$, so $\sqrt{a+b}$ and $\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}$ are not the same
expression --- one counterexample is enough to retire the rule for good.
$\ans{5}$

\emph{Teaching point:} the split is tempting because it looks like the
distributive property. Distribution belongs to multiplication over addition;
a radical is a power, and powers do not distribute over sums.}

\problem{\textbf{Priya: $\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{36}+\sqrt{64}=14$.}

Step 1. Locate the illegal step. It is the very first one: replacing
$\sqrt{36+64}$ with $\sqrt{36}+\sqrt{64}$. Nothing after it can repair the
answer, because the wrong quantity is already being evaluated.

Step 2. Add under the radical first: $36+64=100$.

Step 3. Take the root: $\sqrt{100}=10$. $\ans{10}$

Step 4. Check the size of the error. Priya's $14$ is $40\%$ too large, and her
numbers were chosen to be perfect squares, which is exactly what makes the split
look safe.}

\problem{\textbf{Bo: $\sqrt{5+7}$ reported as $4.88$.}

Step 1. Bo replaced $\sqrt{5+7}$ with $\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{7}$ --- the same split as
in problem 2, hidden by decimals.

Step 2. Add inside first: $5+7=12$.

Step 3. $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}=3.4641\ldots$, which rounds to $3.46$.
$\ans{3.46}$

Step 4. Compare: $3.46$ against Bo's $4.88$. When nothing comes out whole, the
only defence is the rule itself, not the look of the answer.}

\problem{\textbf{Dana: $\sqrt{50}+\sqrt{18}$ written as $\sqrt{68}\approx 8.25$.}

Step 1. Dana ran the split backwards: two radicals were merged into one radical
of a sum. It is illegal in that direction too.

Step 2. Simplify each radical with the product rule, which IS legal because
each radicand is a product:
$\sqrt{50}=\sqrt{25\cdot 2}=5\sqrt{2}$ and
$\sqrt{18}=\sqrt{9\cdot 2}=3\sqrt{2}$.

Step 3. Now the two terms are like radicals --- both are multiples of
$\sqrt{2}$ --- so they add like $5x+3x$:
$5\sqrt{2}+3\sqrt{2}=8\sqrt{2}$.

Step 4. As a decimal, $8\sqrt{2}=11.3137\ldots \approx 11.31$, well clear of
Dana's $8.25$. $\ans{8\sqrt{2} \approx 11.31}$}

\problem{\textbf{Kofi: $(25+144)^{1/2}$ evaluated as $25^{1/2}+144^{1/2}=17$.}

Step 1. A rational exponent is a radical in other clothing:
$(25+144)^{1/2}=\sqrt{25+144}$. Kofi's step is therefore the same split, and
$(u+v)^{1/2} \ne u^{1/2}+v^{1/2}$ for the same reason that
$(u+v)^2 \ne u^2+v^2$.

Step 2. Add inside the parentheses first: $25+144=169$.

Step 3. Apply the one-half power to that single number:
$169^{1/2}=\sqrt{169}=13$. $\ans{13}$

Step 4. Kofi's $17$ is what $5+12$ gives. Note that $5$, $12$, $13$ is a right
triangle: the legs never add to the hypotenuse, which is the same fact in
geometric form.}

\problem{\textbf{Jamal: from $\sqrt{x+9}=5$ to $\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{9}=5$, then $x=4$.}

Step 1. Test Jamal's answer in the ORIGINAL equation:
$\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}=3.6055\ldots$, which is not $5$. So $x=4$ is not a
solution, and the fault is the split in his first line.

Step 2. Solve correctly. Square the whole left side at once --- squaring undoes
the radical only when the radical is alone on that side, which it already is:
$\bigl(\sqrt{x+9}\bigr)^2=5^2$, so $x+9=25$.

Step 3. Subtract: $x=25-9=16$. $\ans{x = 16}$

Step 4. Check in the original equation: $\sqrt{16+9}=\sqrt{25}=5$
$\checkmark$. Radical equations always get this check, because squaring can
introduce extraneous roots even when every step is legal.}

\problem{\textbf{Patio diagonal: $\sqrt{20^2+21^2}$ feet, with Sam's $20+21=41$.}

Step 1. Sam's answer is the split again: $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ became $a+b$. Reject
it on size alone --- the diagonal of a patio cannot equal the two sides walked
end to end.

Step 2. Square each leg first: $20^2=400$ and $21^2=441$.

Step 3. Add under the radical: $400+441=841$.

Step 4. Take one root of the total: $\sqrt{841}=29$, and $29$ is exact, since
$29^2=841$. $\ans{29\text{ ft}}$

Step 5. Sanity check: $29$ is longer than either side and shorter than
$20+21=41$, which is exactly where a triangle's third side must land.}

\problem{\textbf{(a) Why the split fails, and (b) the one value of $x$ that
makes $\sqrt{x+25}=\sqrt{x}+5$ true.}

\textbf{(a)} \emph{Open response --- flagged for manual review. Model answer
below; accept any equivalent reasoning.}\\
Square the right-hand side:
\[ \bigl(\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}\bigr)^2 = a + 2\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b} + b = a+b+2\sqrt{ab}. \]
Squaring the left-hand side $\sqrt{a+b}$ gives just $a+b$. The two agree only
when the extra cross term $2\sqrt{ab}$ is zero --- that is, only when $a=0$ or
$b=0$. For every other pair the split leaves out a whole positive term, which
is why $\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}$ is always the larger of the two.\\
\emph{What a full-credit answer must contain:} the squared form
$a+b+2\sqrt{ab}$, and the observation that the cross term $2\sqrt{ab}$ is what
the split throws away.

\textbf{(b)} Part (a) already predicts the answer: the two sides can agree only
when the cross term vanishes. Here $b=25$ is fixed and not zero, so the only
candidate is $x=0$.

Step 1. Square both sides:
$x+25 = x + 10\sqrt{x} + 25$.

Step 2. Subtract $x+25$ from both sides: $10\sqrt{x}=0$, so $\sqrt{x}=0$.

Step 3. Therefore $x=0$, and the check confirms it:
$\sqrt{0+25}=5$ and $\sqrt{0}+5=5$ $\checkmark$. $\ans{x = 0}$}
