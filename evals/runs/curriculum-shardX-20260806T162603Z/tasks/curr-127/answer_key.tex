\problem{Fill in the missing numerators: $\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{\square}{6}$
and $\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{\square}{6}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by the same
number so the denominator becomes 6.
For $\tfrac{1}{2}$: multiply by 3 over 3, so
$\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 3}$, and the numerator is \ans{3}
For $\tfrac{1}{3}$: multiply by 2 over 2, so
$\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{1 \times 2}{3 \times 2}$, and the numerator is \ans{2}

\problem{What is the smallest common denominator you can use for
$\tfrac{1}{4}$ and $\tfrac{5}{12}$?}

\textbf{Solution:} List multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, \ldots\ The number 12 is
already a multiple of 4, because $3 \times 4 = 12$. So twelfths work for both
fractions, and nothing smaller does, since the denominator must be a multiple
of 12. Smallest common denominator: \ans{12}

\problem{Add: $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The smallest common denominator of 2 and 6 is 6.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{3}{6}$. Then
$\tfrac{3}{6} + \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{4}{6}$, which simplifies by dividing top
and bottom by 2. Final answer: $\ans{\tfrac{2}{3}}$

\problem{Rewrite both fractions with denominator 20: $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and
$\tfrac{2}{5}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} For $\tfrac{3}{4}$: since $4 \times 5 = 20$, multiply top
and bottom by 5: $\tfrac{3 \times 5}{4 \times 5}$, numerator \ans{15}
For $\tfrac{2}{5}$: since $5 \times 4 = 20$, multiply top and bottom by 4:
$\tfrac{2 \times 4}{5 \times 4}$, numerator \ans{8}

\problem{Subtract: $\tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{1}{3}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The smallest common denominator of 6 and 3 is 6.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{2}{6}$. Then
$\tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{2}{6} = \tfrac{3}{6}$, which simplifies to
$\ans{\tfrac{1}{2}}$

\problem{Add: $\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The denominators 3 and 4 share no common factor, so the
smallest common denominator is $3 \times 4 = 12$.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{8}{12}$ and $\tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{3}{12}$.
Then $\tfrac{8}{12} + \tfrac{3}{12} = \ans{\tfrac{11}{12}}$

\problem{Which of 18, 40, or 80 is the smallest common denominator that works
for $\tfrac{5}{8}$ and $\tfrac{3}{10}$?}

\textbf{Solution:} A common denominator must be a multiple of 8 \emph{and} of
10. Check 18: not a multiple of 8, so it fails. Check 40: $8 \times 5 = 40$
and $10 \times 4 = 40$, so 40 works. Check 80: also works, but it is larger.
Smallest working choice: \ans{40}

\problem{Subtract: $\tfrac{7}{10} - \tfrac{1}{4}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The smallest common denominator of 10 and 4 is 20.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{7}{10} = \tfrac{14}{20}$ and $\tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{5}{20}$.
Then $\tfrac{14}{20} - \tfrac{5}{20} = \ans{\tfrac{9}{20}}$

\problem{Ana ties a ribbon $\tfrac{3}{8}$ m long to a ribbon $\tfrac{1}{6}$ m
long. How long are the two ribbons together?}

\textbf{Solution:} We need $\tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{1}{6}$. The smallest common
denominator of 8 and 6 is 24.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{9}{24}$ and $\tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{4}{24}$.
Then $\tfrac{9}{24} + \tfrac{4}{24} = \tfrac{13}{24}$. Total length:
$\ans{\tfrac{13}{24}\ \text{m}}$

\problem{A bag holds $\tfrac{7}{8}$ kg of flour. Leo uses $\tfrac{2}{3}$ kg.
How much flour is left?}

\textbf{Solution:} We need $\tfrac{7}{8} - \tfrac{2}{3}$. The smallest common
denominator of 8 and 3 is 24.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{7}{8} = \tfrac{21}{24}$ and $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{16}{24}$.
Then $\tfrac{21}{24} - \tfrac{16}{24} = \tfrac{5}{24}$. Flour left:
$\ans{\tfrac{5}{24}\ \text{kg}}$

\problem{Find the missing fraction: $\tfrac{5}{12} + \square = \tfrac{3}{4}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Rewrite $\tfrac{3}{4}$ in twelfths: $\tfrac{3}{4} =
\tfrac{9}{12}$. The missing fraction is
$\tfrac{9}{12} - \tfrac{5}{12} = \tfrac{4}{12}$, which simplifies to
$\ans{\tfrac{1}{3}}$

\problem{Challenge. Add all three: $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3} +
\tfrac{1}{12}$}

\textbf{Solution:} One common denominator works for all three fractions:
12 is a multiple of 2, of 3, and of 12.
Rewrite: $\tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{6}{12}$, $\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{4}{12}$,
$\tfrac{1}{12}$ stays as it is. Then
$\tfrac{6}{12} + \tfrac{4}{12} + \tfrac{1}{12} = \ans{\tfrac{11}{12}}$
