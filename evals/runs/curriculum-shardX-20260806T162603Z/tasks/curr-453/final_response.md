Three PDFs — study guide, worksheet, answer key.

**Study guide (`ss_pwcont_curr453.pdf`, 2 pages).** Opens with the one idea the
whole topic rests on: continuity at a junction $x=c$ means the left-hand limit,
the right-hand limit, and $f(c)$ are all the same number. Then three sections,
each a method box + worked example + try-it with the answer upside down inside
the box — matching two pieces at one junction, filling a removable hole with the
limit value, and a three-piece model with two junctions and two unknowns.

**Worksheet (`ws_pwcont_curr453.pdf`, 4 pages).** Eight problems, every one of
them a parameter chosen to make a piecewise model continuous. Each is a real
model with its units stated in the stem and no decorative story detail: an oven
temperature in degrees Celsius, a tank in litres, a pressure sensor, a drone's
height in metres, a calibration curve needing the conjugate, a three-stage
conveyor, a phone plan in dollars, and a challenge that requires all three
continuity conditions at once. Difficulty ramps 1 → 4. Problems 1 and 4 carry
labelled answer lines (degrees, metres) so the constant's unit is written down,
not just implied.

**Answer key (`ak_pwcont_curr453.pdf`, 3 pages).** Every solution writes the two
one-sided limits out before setting them equal — the step students skip — and
several close with a check that the junction really closes. Quick Answers at the
top, with the two-unknown problems labelled `a` and `b` so you can see which is
which, plus a generated Curriculum block (LIM-2, difficulty 1–4).

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 11 graded responses. **Ten are
machine-checked** with SymPy: each continuity equation was solved independently,
and each removable-hole value was computed as an actual limit rather than assumed
from the key. **One is instructor-judged: problem 7(b)**, where the student has
to say what publishing $a=45$ would do to a bill near 4 GB. It prints as `---` in
the Quick Answers bank, and the key gives the rubric — full credit needs the
5-dollar downward jump at 4 GB *and* the consequence (a heavier user billed less
than a lighter one). A bare "it wouldn't be continuous" does not earn it.

No misconception traps are declared on this sheet; the errors here are structural
(matching the wrong pieces, or filling a hole with $f$'s formula instead of its
limit) rather than a single wrong number worth planting.
