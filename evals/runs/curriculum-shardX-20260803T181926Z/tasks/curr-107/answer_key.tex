\problem{\textbf{Multiply $214 \times 3$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Multiply the ones: $4 \times 3 = 12$. Write the $2$ in the
ones place and regroup the $1$ into the tens column.

\textbf{Step 2.} Multiply the tens: $1 \times 3 = 3$, then add the regrouped
$1$ to get $4$ tens.

\textbf{Step 3.} Multiply the hundreds: $2 \times 3 = 6$ hundreds. Nothing to
regroup. \ans{642}

\textit{Listen for:} a student who adds the regrouped digit before
multiplying. Multiply first, add the carry second.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $132 \times 4$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $2 \times 4 = 8$. Write $8$; there is nothing to
regroup.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: $3 \times 4 = 12$. Write the $2$ in the tens place and
regroup the $1$ into the hundreds column.

\textbf{Step 3.} Hundreds: $1 \times 4 = 4$, plus the regrouped $1$ makes $5$
hundreds. \ans{528}

\textit{Teaching note:} the regroup out of the tens is the one students most
often drop, because the ones column did not need one.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $476 \times 5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $6 \times 5 = 30$. Write $0$, regroup $3$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: $7 \times 5 = 35$, plus the regrouped $3$ is $38$.
Write $8$ in the tens place, regroup $3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Hundreds: $4 \times 5 = 20$, plus the regrouped $3$ is $23$.
That is $23$ hundreds, so both digits are written and the product grows to
four digits. \ans{2380}

\textit{Teaching note:} a three-digit number times five is often a four-digit
product --- an answer with only three digits is worth a second look.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $1243 \times 3$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $3 \times 3 = 9$. Tens: $4 \times 3 = 12$, so write $2$
and regroup $1$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Hundreds: $2 \times 3 = 6$, plus the regrouped $1$ is $7$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Thousands: $1 \times 3 = 3$. Reading the columns back
together gives the product. \ans{3729}

\textit{Check it:} the factor is a bit over $1200$ and $1200 \times 3 = 3600$,
so an answer near $3700$ is sensible.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $4187 \times 6$ by rounding, then find the exact
product.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $4187$ is between $4000$ and $5000$, and the hundreds digit
$1$ is less than $5$, so to the nearest thousand it rounds down to $4000$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Estimate with the rounded factor: $4000 \times 6$ is $4 \times 6 = 24$
thousands. \ans{24000}

\textbf{Step 3.} Exact product. Ones: $7 \times 6 = 42$, write $2$ regroup $4$.
Tens: $8 \times 6 = 48$, plus $4$ is $52$ --- write $2$, regroup $5$.
Hundreds: $1 \times 6 = 6$, plus $5$ is $11$ --- write $1$, regroup $1$.
Thousands: $4 \times 6 = 24$, plus $1$ is $25$. \ans{25122}

\textit{Compare:} the estimate is low by about $1100$, which is what rounding
a factor down by $187$ costs.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $3608 \times 5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $8 \times 5 = 40$. Write $0$, regroup $4$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: the digit is $0$, and $0 \times 5 = 0$ --- but the
regrouped $4$ still has to be added, so the tens digit is $4$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Hundreds: $6 \times 5 = 30$. Write $0$, regroup $3$.
Thousands: $3 \times 5 = 15$, plus $3$ is $18$. \ans{18040}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $1840$ --- the zero column was skipped
altogether and $368 \times 5$ was computed instead. A zero digit still holds a
place.}

\problem{\textbf{A bakery loads $1275$ rolls into each of $6$ vans.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Six vans each holding the same number of rolls is six equal
groups, so the total is $1275 \times 6$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Ones: $5 \times 6 = 30$, write $0$ regroup $3$. Tens:
$7 \times 6 = 42$, plus $3$ is $45$ --- write $5$, regroup $4$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Hundreds: $2 \times 6 = 12$, plus $4$ is $16$ --- write $6$,
regroup $1$. Thousands: $1 \times 6 = 6$, plus $1$ is $7$. \ans{7650}

\textit{Check it:} $1275$ is close to $1300$, and $1300 \times 6 = 7800$, so a
total near $7650$ is reasonable.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $2749 \times 8$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $9 \times 8 = 72$. Write $2$, regroup $7$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: $4 \times 8 = 32$, plus $7$ is $39$ --- write $9$,
regroup $3$. Hundreds: $7 \times 8 = 56$, plus $3$ is $59$ --- write $9$,
regroup $5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Thousands: $2 \times 8 = 16$, plus $5$ is $21$, and both
digits are written. \ans{21992}

\textit{Listen for:} regroups larger than one digit surprise students here.
The carry can be as big as $8$; it is still a single digit.}

\problem{\textbf{Estimate $5812 \times 7$ by rounding, then find the exact
product.}

\textbf{Step 1.} To the nearest hundred, $5812$ rounds to $5800$: the tens
digit $1$ is less than $5$, so the hundreds digit stays put.

\textbf{Step 2.} Estimate: $5800 \times 7$ is $58 \times 7 = 406$ hundreds.
\ans{40600}

\textbf{Step 3.} Exact product. Ones: $2 \times 7 = 14$, write $4$ regroup $1$.
Tens: $1 \times 7 = 7$, plus $1$ is $8$. Hundreds: $8 \times 7 = 56$, write
$6$ regroup $5$. Thousands: $5 \times 7 = 35$, plus $5$ is $40$. \ans{40684}

\textit{Compare:} rounding to the nearest hundred left the estimate only $84$
away, while rounding to the nearest thousand would have missed by $1284$.}

\problem{\textbf{Dana wrote $1806 \times 5 = 9000$. Find the mistake and the
correct product.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $6 \times 5 = 30$, so the ones digit is $0$ and $3$ is
regrouped into the tens column.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: the digit is $0$ and $0 \times 5 = 0$, but the
regrouped $3$ must still be added, so the tens digit is $3$, not $0$. This is
Dana's mistake: she wrote the zero and dropped the carry.

\textbf{Step 3.} Hundreds: $8 \times 5 = 40$ --- write $0$, regroup $4$.
Thousands: $1 \times 5 = 5$, plus $4$ is $9$. \ans{9030}

\textit{Open response (manual review):} the written explanation is the
student's own. Accept any answer that says the regrouped $3$ from the ones
column was never added into the tens column. \textit{Common wrong answer:}
$9000$ --- exactly Dana's slip.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $4975 \times 9$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ones: $5 \times 9 = 45$. Write $5$, regroup $4$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Tens: $7 \times 9 = 63$, plus $4$ is $67$ --- write $7$,
regroup $6$. Hundreds: $9 \times 9 = 81$, plus $6$ is $87$ --- write $7$,
regroup $8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Thousands: $4 \times 9 = 36$, plus $8$ is $44$. \ans{44775}

\textit{Check it:} $4975$ is just under $5000$ and $5000 \times 9 = 45000$, so
a product a little below $45000$ is what we expect.}

\problem{\textbf{$8$ cartons of $2350$ sheets, with $4000$ sheets used.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Eight equal cartons make this a multiplication first: the
order holds $2350 \times 8$ sheets. The sheets already used are then taken
away, so the question is a two-step problem.

\textbf{Step 2.} Ones: $0 \times 8 = 0$. Tens: $5 \times 8 = 40$ --- write $0$,
regroup $4$. Hundreds: $3 \times 8 = 24$, plus $4$ is $28$ --- write $8$,
regroup $2$. Thousands: $2 \times 8 = 16$, plus $2$ is $18$. The order is
$18800$ sheets.

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract the sheets used: $18800 - 4000 = 14800$. \ans{14800}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $18800$ --- the multiplication was done and the
subtraction forgotten. Re-reading the final sentence catches it.}
