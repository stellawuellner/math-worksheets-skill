\noindent\small\textbf{How to read this key.} Every problem here needs two pieces of
information joined together, so each solution names the two pieces first and only then
computes. The recurring student error is using one piece and guessing the other: reading a
line plot but forgetting that each $\times$ is a student rather than a book, or pairing two
patterns one step out of step. Both are shown below where they occur.\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{Two patterns, one ordered pair.}\par
Both patterns start at zero, so matching terms are the 1st with the 1st, the 2nd with the
2nd, and so on. Pattern A grows by 2 a step and Pattern B by 8 a step, so after the same
number of steps B has grown $8 \div 2 = 4$ times as much as A. Every ordered pair therefore
satisfies $B = 4 \times A$.
\[ B = \frac{8}{2} \times 10 = 4 \times 10 \]
The ordered pair is $(10, 40)$, so the $B$-value is $\ans{40}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Working the same relationship backwards.}\par
The multiplier is the one found above: each step Pattern B gains 8 while Pattern A gains
2, so $B = 4 \times A$, i.e.\ the \textbf{multiplier is 4}.\par
Going from a $B$-value back to its $A$-value undoes that multiplication, so divide:
\[ A = 72 \div \frac{8}{2} = 72 \div 4 \]
The $A$-value is $\ans{18}$, and the ordered pair is $(18, 72)$.\par
\textit{Check:} $18 \times 4 = 72$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Reading one column of the line plot.}\par
Find 5 on the number line and count the $\times$ marks stacked above it --- each $\times$
is one student, not one book. There are two marks above 5.\par
So $\ans{2}$ students read exactly 5 books, and as an ordered pair
(books read, number of students) that column is $(5, 2)$.}

\problem{\textbf{The mean of the whole plot.}\par
A mean needs two numbers: the total of all the values, and how many values there are.
Read the total straight off the plot by multiplying each book count by the size of its
stack, then add:
\[ 2(2) + 3(3) + 3(4) + 2(5) + 1(6) + 1(7) = 4 + 9 + 12 + 10 + 6 + 7 = 48 \]
Twelve students are on the plot, so divide the total by the number of students:
\[ \text{mean} = \frac{48}{12} \]
The mean is $\ans{4}$ books per student.\par
\textit{Watch for:} dividing by the number of \emph{columns} on the plot instead of the
number of students, which gives $48 \div 6 = 8$ --- a mean twice as large as the biggest
stack on the plot, and impossible.}

\problem{\textbf{A rule applied to a value read off the plot.}\par
Two pieces: the plot supplies the number of books, the club rule supplies the points per
book. The rightmost mark on the plot sits above 7, so the student who read the most books
read 7 of them. The rule gives 5 points a book:
\[ 7 \times 5 = 35 \]
That student earned $\ans{35}$ points.\par
\textit{Watch for:} using the \emph{tallest stack} instead of the \emph{rightmost} mark.
The tallest stacks are at 3 and 4 books, which is where most students are --- not where
the biggest reader is. Reading 3 books would give only $3 \times 5 = 15$ points.}

\problem{\textbf{Two patterns, plotted.}\par
List each pattern first, then pair matching terms:
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Pattern P: 1, 4, 7, 10 (start at 1, add 3)
  \item Pattern Q: 3, 12, 21, 30 (start at 3, add 9)
\end{itemize}
Ordered pairs: $(1, 3)$, $(4, 12)$, $(7, 21)$, $(10, 30)$. Plotted on Grid A those four
points climb steeply and lie on a straight line through the origin.\par
The relationship in words: \emph{each $Q$-value is 3 times its $P$-value.} Q grows 9 a
step while P grows 3 a step, and $9 \div 3 = 3$, which is exactly the multiplier the
plotted points show.\par
Plot-and-describe task: $\ans{\text{open response}}$ --- check the four plotted
points and the wording of the description.}

\problem{\textbf{The median of the plot.}\par
The median is the middle value when all twelve values are in order, so count $\times$
marks from the left rather than reading the middle of the number line:
\[ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 3,\ 3,\ \underline{4,\ 4},\ 4,\ 5,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7 \]
With an even number of students the median is the mean of the 6th and 7th values, both 4,
so the median is $\ans{4}$ books.\par
It is \textbf{equal} to the mean found earlier. A plot whose mean and median agree is
roughly balanced: the long thin tail out at 6 and 7 books is offset by the pile of
students at 2 and 3.}

\problem{\textbf{Shifting every value by the same amount.}\par
Every one of the twelve values goes up by the same 2 books, so the total goes up by
$12 \times 2 = 24$ and the mean goes up by exactly 2 --- the whole plot slides two spaces
right without changing shape.
\[ \text{new mean} = 4 + 2 \]
The new mean is $\ans{6}$ books.\par
That is the reason no re-adding is needed: adding a constant to every value adds the same
constant to the mean. (The long way confirms it: the new total is $48 + 24 = 72$, and
$72 \div 12 = 6$.)}

\problem{\textbf{Find Sara's mistake.}\par
Pattern S: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, \dots \quad Pattern T: 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, \dots\par
T grows 15 a step while S grows 5 a step, so $T = 3 \times S$ for matching terms:
\[ T = \frac{15}{5} \times 20 = 3 \times 20 \]
The correct pair is $(20, 60)$, so the $T$-value is $\ans{60}$.\par
\textbf{Sara's mistake:} she lined the two lists up one step out of step. The value 20 is
the 5th term of Pattern S, but 45 is only the 4th term of Pattern T. Pairing term 5 with
term 4 gives $(20, 45)$. Matching terms must sit in the \emph{same position} in both
patterns.}

\problem{\textbf{The class total.}\par
Each $\times$ is one student, and the number under it is how many books that student read,
so the total is a sum of stack-size times book-count:
\[ 2(2) + 3(3) + 3(4) + 2(5) + 1(6) + 1(7) = 4 + 9 + 12 + 10 + 6 + 7 \]
The class read $\ans{48}$ books.\par
As an ordered pair, (number of students, total books read) is $(12, 48)$.\par
\textit{Watch for:} answering 12. That is the number of $\times$ marks --- the students,
not the books. The plot's marks count people; the labels underneath count books.}

\problem{\textbf{A rule applied to the class total.}\par
The bonus rule needs the class total, which came from the plot: 48 books. Bonus points are
awarded only for books \emph{beyond} 40, so subtract rather than divide:
\[ 48 - 40 = 8 \]
The class earns $\ans{8}$ bonus points.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: a pattern built on top of the data.}\par
Month one is the class total already found, 48 books, and each later month adds 6:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
Month & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
Class total & 48 & 54 & 60 & 66 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
As ordered pairs (month number, class total): $(1, 48)$, $(2, 54)$, $(3, 60)$,
$(4, 66)$.\par
\textbf{The pattern in words:} the first number of each pair goes up by 1 while the second
goes up by 6, so every step right on a grid is six steps up. Plotted, the four points
would lie on a straight line climbing to the right --- and, unlike the patterns in
problems 1 and 9, that line would \emph{not} pass through the origin, because month one
already starts at 48 books rather than at zero.\par
Accept any description naming the constant increase of 6 and a straight line of points.
Open synthesis task: $\ans{\text{open response}}$ --- check the table, the ordered pairs,
and the description.}
