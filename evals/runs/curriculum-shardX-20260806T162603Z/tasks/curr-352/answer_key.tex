\problem{Simplify $(8 + 3i) + (2 + 9i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Adding complex numbers means adding the real parts and the
imaginary parts separately, because $i$ behaves like a variable that is being
collected: $(8 + 2) + (3 + 9)i$. \ans{10 + 12i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(7 - 4i) - (2 + 6i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the subtraction over both parts of the second
number first: $7 - 4i - 2 - 6i$. Now collect: $(7 - 2) + (-4 - 6)i$. \ans{5 - 10i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(-3 + 5i) + (9 - 11i) - (4 + 2i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the subtraction over the last pair, then collect
real with real and imaginary with imaginary.
\begin{align*}
  &(-3 + 5i) + (9 - 11i) - (4 + 2i) \\
  &= (-3 + 9 - 4) + (5 - 11 - 2)i
\end{align*}
The real parts give $2$ and the imaginary parts give $-8$. \ans{2 - 8i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply and write in standard form: $3i(4 - 5i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the monomial, then replace $i^2$ by $-1$.
\begin{align*}
  3i(4 - 5i) &= 12i - 15i^2 \\
             &= 12i - 15(-1) \\
             &= 15 + 12i
\end{align*}
Notice that multiplying by $i$ moved the real given into the imaginary part and
the imaginary given into the real part. \ans{15 + 12i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply and write in standard form: $(2 + 3i)(4 - i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply out all four products the way you would with two
binomials, then use $i^2 = -1$.
\begin{align*}
  (2 + 3i)(4 - i) &= 8 - 2i + 12i - 3i^2 \\
                  &= 8 + 10i - 3(-1) \\
                  &= 11 + 10i
\end{align*}
\ans{11 + 10i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(10 - 2i) - (-3 + 7i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Subtracting a negative real part adds: $10 - (-3) = 13$, and
$-2 - 7 = -9$ for the imaginary part. \ans{13 - 9i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply $(5 - 2i)(5 + 2i)$, then describe what kind of number the
product is and why the $i$ terms cancelled.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply out all four products.
\begin{align*}
  (5 - 2i)(5 + 2i) &= 25 + 10i - 10i - 4i^2 \\
                   &= 25 - 4(-1) \\
                   &= 29
\end{align*}
\ans{29}

\textbf{Written part (instructor-judged).} A correct description says the
product is a \emph{real} number, and explains why: the two middle terms
$+10i$ and $-10i$ are opposites, so they add to zero, and the remaining
$-4i^2$ becomes $+4$. In general $(a - bi)(a + bi) = a^2 + b^2$, which is
always real. Award no credit for repeating the number without saying why the
$i$ terms disappear.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Expand and simplify $(3 + 4i)^2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A square is still a product of two binomials --- the middle
term is what students most often lose.
\begin{align*}
  (3 + 4i)^2 &= 9 + 12i + 12i + 16i^2 \\
             &= 9 + 24i + 16(-1) \\
             &= -7 + 24i
\end{align*}
\ans{-7 + 24i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply and write in standard form: $(-6 + i)(2 - 5i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The second factor of the first number is $1i$, so the
products are $-6 \cdot 2$, $-6 \cdot (-5i)$, $i \cdot 2$ and $i \cdot (-5i)$.
\begin{align*}
  (-6 + i)(2 - 5i) &= -12 + 30i + 2i - 5i^2 \\
                   &= -12 + 32i + 5 \\
                   &= -7 + 32i
\end{align*}
\ans{-7 + 32i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $2(4 - 3i) - 3(1 + 5i)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute each real multiplier first; no $i^2$ appears
because neither factor is imaginary.
\begin{align*}
  2(4 - 3i) - 3(1 + 5i) &= 8 - 6i - 3 - 15i \\
                        &= 5 - 21i
\end{align*}
\ans{5 - 21i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(2 - 3i)^2 + (2 + 3i)^2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Expand each square separately, keeping the middle terms.
\begin{align*}
  (2 - 3i)^2 &= 4 - 12i + 9i^2 = -5 - 12i \\
  (2 + 3i)^2 &= 4 + 12i + 9i^2 = -5 + 12i
\end{align*}
The imaginary parts are opposites, so they cancel when the two squares are
added: $-5 + (-5) = -10$. \ans{-10}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Let $z = 3 - 2i$ and $w = -1 + 5i$. (a) Compute $zw - 2z$. (b) Explain
why the product of two numbers that each carry an imaginary part can end up with
a real part neither factor had.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Multiply first, then subtract $2z$.
\begin{align*}
  zw &= (3 - 2i)(-1 + 5i) = -3 + 15i + 2i - 10i^2 = 7 + 17i \\
  2z &= 6 - 4i \\
  zw - 2z &= (7 - 6) + (17 + 4)i
\end{align*}
\ans{1 + 21i}

\textbf{Written part (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer names two
moves: the two imaginary terms multiply to give a term containing $i^2$, and
replacing $i^2$ with $-1$ turns that term into a real number, which then
combines with the product of the two real parts. Here $(-2i)(5i) = -10i^2 =
+10$, and $-3 + 10 = 7$ --- a real part larger than either factor's. Half credit
for naming only one of the two moves.
