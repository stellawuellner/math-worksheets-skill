# Radical Equations, Domains, and Decimals — Algebra 2 (interleaved review)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_raddom_curr375.pdf`) — 12 problems combining radical
  equations, domains, and rounded decimal answers.
- **Answer key** (`ak_raddom_curr375.pdf`) — quick-answer bank, a "Common wrong
  answers" block, and a worked solution per problem.
- **Study guide** (`ss_raddom_curr375.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections: the domain
  of a radical; isolate then undo; rejecting the extraneous candidate; and
  reporting a rounded decimal. Each has a rule, a worked example that names its
  strategy first, and a try-it.

## How it is sequenced

Problems 1–3 are the warm-up, one subskill each. From problem 4 on all four
subskills are interleaved with no two consecutive problems using the same one, so
the student must decide each time whether the question wants a domain, an exact
solution, a check, or a rounded decimal. Problem 12 is the synthesis challenge:
one equation, four parts, needing the domain, the solve, the substitution check
and a written justification.

Problem 9 deliberately contrasts with problem 1: a radical in a denominator needs
a *strict* inequality, which is the distinction students most often flatten.
Three extraneous-root traps (problems 4, 8, 12) are declared and machine-checked
as distinguishable, and print in the key.
