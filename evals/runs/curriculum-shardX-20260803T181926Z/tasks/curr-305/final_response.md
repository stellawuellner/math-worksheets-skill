Here are the three PDFs for the geometry proof-logic review.

**Worksheet — `ws_proofsteps_curr305.pdf` (7 pages, 10 problems).**
Every problem asks for the same thing in a different costume: start from what
is *stated*, chain definitions and postulates, and name each move. Two problems
are algebraic solutions where the reason column is the graded part; three build
an equation out of a segment or angle relationship and require the student to
name the postulate that licensed it; two are full two-column proofs with a
blank statement/reason table to fill in; one is an error-analysis problem where
a classmate set two segment parts equal with nothing in the givens allowing it.
Problem 10 is the synthesis: write the argument that gets from a given angle
congruence to a specific equation, naming each step in order, then solve it.

After the first three problems the types are deliberately mixed, so the student
has to work out what kind of argument is called for rather than repeating a
pattern. The diagrams carry letters only — no measurements — because a diagram
that shows numbers invites reasoning from the picture instead of from the
givens, which is exactly the habit this topic is trying to break.

**Answer key — `ak_proofsteps_curr305.pdf` (3 pages).**
Each solution names the postulate or property behind every line, and the two
proof problems get complete model two-column proofs. The key also carries
explicit grading notes on the parts that are judgement calls: what to accept for
a "why is Dana's equation wrong" answer, and why an SAS proof has to identify
the angle as the *included* angle. A quick-answer strip and a Curriculum section
(standards covered, difficulty range) sit at the top.

**Study guide — `ss_proofsteps_curr305.pdf` (2 pages).**
Four sections: a reason for every line; from a segment picture to an equation;
from an angle relationship to an equation; and writing the two-column proof. The
second and third sections carry the small table that decides everything — which
relationship gives a *sum* and which gives an *equality*, which is the single
most common place these problems go wrong. Each section ends with a try-it whose
answer is printed upside down inside the box.

**What was verified.** Every numerical answer on the sheet — 14 checks across
the 10 problems — was recomputed independently, including the substitute-back
values (the angle measures, the segment length), so the key cannot disagree with
the worksheet.

**Six items are flagged for manual review, and that is the honest outcome for
this topic.** A proof is not machine-checkable: problems 5 and 9 are entirely
manual, and problems 1, 4, 7 and 10 have a computed half that is verified and a
written half (naming the properties, explaining the flawed step, writing the
argument) that a person has to read. The answer key gives a model answer and a
grading note for each of those six.
