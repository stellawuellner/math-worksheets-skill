\problem{A freight elevator's height is $h(t) = 2t^4 - 5t^3 + 7t$ metres,
$t$ in seconds. Find $h''(t)$.}

\textbf{Solution.} Differentiate twice with the power rule.
\begin{align*}
  h'(t)  &= 8t^3 - 15t^2 + 7 \\
  h''(t) &= 24t^2 - 30t
\end{align*}
The units of $h''$ are metres per second per second.
\[ \boxed{h''(t) = 24t^2 - 30t} \]

\problem{A test cart has position $s(t) = t^3 - 9t^2 + 24t$ metres, $t$ in
seconds. (a) Find $s''(t)$. (b) Find the acceleration at $t = 5$ seconds.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} Velocity is the first derivative, acceleration the second.
\begin{align*}
  s'(t)  &= 3t^2 - 18t + 24 \\
  s''(t) &= 6t - 18
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{s''(t) = 6t - 18} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} Substitute $t = 5$ into the acceleration function:
$s''(5) = 6(5) - 18 = 30 - 18$.
\[ \boxed{s''(5) = 12\ \text{m/s}^2} \]

\problem{A buoy's height is $y(t) = 3\sin(2t)$ metres, $t$ in seconds. Find
$y^{(4)}(t)$.}

\textbf{Solution.} Each differentiation brings down a factor of $2$ from the
chain rule and advances the sine through its four-step cycle.
\begin{align*}
  y'(t)   &= 6\cos(2t)   & y''(t)  &= -12\sin(2t) \\
  y'''(t) &= -24\cos(2t) & y^{(4)}(t) &= 48\sin(2t)
\end{align*}
After four derivatives the sine returns, multiplied by $2^4 = 16$.
\[ \boxed{y^{(4)}(t) = 48\sin(2t)} \]

\problem{A cooling tank holds $V(t) = 500e^{-t/5}$ litres, $t$ in minutes.
Find $V'''(t)$.}

\textbf{Solution.} Every derivative of $e^{-t/5}$ reproduces itself times
$-\tfrac{1}{5}$, so three derivatives multiply the coefficient by
$\left(-\tfrac{1}{5}\right)^3 = -\tfrac{1}{125}$.
\begin{align*}
  V'(t)   &= -100e^{-t/5} \\
  V''(t)  &= 20e^{-t/5} \\
  V'''(t) &= -4e^{-t/5}
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{V'''(t) = -4e^{-t/5}} \]

\problem{A drone's altitude is $A(t) = t^4 - 8t^3 + 18t^2 + 30$ metres,
$0 \le t \le 6$ seconds. (a) Find $A''(t)$. (b) Find every time at which the
vertical acceleration is zero.}

\textbf{Solution (a).}
\begin{align*}
  A'(t)  &= 4t^3 - 24t^2 + 36t \\
  A''(t) &= 12t^2 - 48t + 36
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{A''(t) = 12t^2 - 48t + 36} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} Set the acceleration to zero and factor:
$12t^2 - 48t + 36 = 12(t^2 - 4t + 3) = 12(t-1)(t-3)$. Both roots lie in
$[0, 6]$.
\[ \boxed{t = 1 \text{ or } t = 3} \]

\problem{A cable is modelled by $y^2 = 4x$ with $y > 0$. (a) Find
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ as a function of $x$. (b) Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ as a
function of $x$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating
$y$ as a function of $x$: $2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4$, so
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2}{y}$. On the branch $y > 0$ we have
$y = 2\sqrt{x}$, hence
\[ \boxed{\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2}{y} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} Differentiate $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x^{-1/2}$ once more:
\[ \boxed{\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\dfrac{1}{2x^{3/2}} = -\dfrac{4}{y^3}} \]
Either form is acceptable; the second is what implicit differentiation of
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2}{y}$ produces directly.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{The rim $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ (metres), upper half $y > 0$. (a) Find
$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ as a function of $x$. (b) Evaluate it at $(3, 4)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} Implicitly, $2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$ gives
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$. Differentiating that quotient and
substituting $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ gives $-\dfrac{25}{y^3}$. On the upper half
$y = \sqrt{25 - x^2}$, so
\[ \boxed{\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\dfrac{25}{(25 - x^2)^{3/2}} = -\dfrac{25}{y^3}} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} At $(3, 4)$ the denominator is $y^3 = 4^3$, so the
second derivative equals
\[ \boxed{-\dfrac{25}{64}} \]
The negative value says the upper rim is concave down, which matches the picture.

\problem{A culture holds $P(t) = t^3 - 12t^2 + 36t + 50$ thousand cells after
$t$ hours, $0 \le t \le 8$. (a) Find $P''(t)$. (b) Find the hour at which the
growth rate is smallest. (c) Explain how the sign of $P''$ settles it.}

\textbf{Solution (a).}
\begin{align*}
  P'(t)  &= 3t^2 - 24t + 36 \\
  P''(t) &= 6t - 24
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{P''(t) = 6t - 24} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} The growth rate $P'(t)$ has its own critical point where
its derivative $P''(t)$ is zero: $6t - 24 = 0$.
\[ \boxed{t = 4} \]
That is $4$ hours after the start, inside the interval.

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A complete answer must argue from
the sign of $P''$, not from a table of values: $P''(t) = 6t - 24$ is negative
for $t < 4$ and positive for $t > 4$, so the growth rate $P'$ decreases and then
increases, which makes $t = 4$ a minimum of $P'$. Award full credit only if the
response is about the growth rate $P'$ and not about the population $P$ itself;
$P$ is increasing on both sides of $t = 4$, so a student who argues about $P$
has answered a different question.
