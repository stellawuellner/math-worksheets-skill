Here are the three PDFs for a 12-problem precalculus set on **graphing basic polar
curves from equations**.

**Worksheet — `ws_polarcurves_curr432.pdf` (5 pages).** Twelve problems that build the
graph one decision at a time: substituting an angle to get a directed distance, the
sight-recognition shapes ($r = k$, $\theta = k$, $r = a\cos\theta$), limaçons and
cardioids, and rose curves. Difficulty ramps 1 → 5 and the four methods are interleaved
after the warm-up, so the student has to pick the method rather than repeat one. Two
problems (6 and 11) carry a blank polar grid to sketch on; the rest have open work space
and an answer line.

**Answer key — `ak_polarcurves_curr432.pdf` (3 pages).** Every problem restated with a
worked solution, not just a final value. It opens with a quick-answer bank for fast
grading, a curriculum block (level, standards codes, difficulty range), and a
"common wrong answers" list built from the three misconception traps I designed into
the givens: using sine for cosine on problem 1, taking $\cos(2\pi/3)$ as $+1/2$ on
problem 5, and forgetting to double the angle on problem 9. Each trap value is far
enough from the correct answer that the error is visible in the grading.

**Study guide — `ss_polarcurves_curr432.pdf` (2 pages).** Four sections, one per skill
the worksheet tests, each with a rule box, a worked example that opens by naming *why*
that method applies, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the box.

**What was verified, honestly.** The sheet asks for **19 separate responses** across the
12 problems. **14 of those are machine-checked** with SymPy — every substitution, every
angle solved on an interval, and the rounded value on problem 9 recomputed exactly and
compared to two decimal places. The remaining **5 are instructor-judged** and are marked
`---` in the answer key's quick-answer bank: the reasoning half of problems 4, 10 and 12,
and the two sketches (problems 6 and 11). That is deliberate — an "explain why" and a
freehand rose cannot be checked by a computer algebra system, and calling them verified
would be a false claim. For each of the five, the answer key prints the full model
response and states what a correct answer must contain, so grading them is a five-second
comparison rather than a judgement call. The eight study-guide examples and try-its are
machine-checked as well.

Nothing on the sheet prints an answer that carries no guarantee: there are no
`[unchecked]` marks in the bank.
