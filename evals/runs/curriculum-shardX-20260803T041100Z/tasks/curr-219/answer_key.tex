\problem{\textbf{Slope through $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$.}\\
Step 1: label the points in one fixed order: $(x_1, y_1) = (2, 3)$ and
$(x_2, y_2) = (6, 11)$.
Step 2: rise first (it belongs on top): $y_2 - y_1 = 11 - 3 = 8$.
Step 3: run underneath, subtracted in the SAME order: $x_2 - x_1 = 6 - 2 = 4$.
Step 4: $m = \dfrac{8}{4}$, so $\ans{m = 2}$. Sanity check: from $(2,3)$ the line
climbs $8$ while moving $4$ right, so a slope greater than $1$ is right.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $0.5$ --- the run divided by the rise.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Devi's slope through $(-3, 5)$ and $(2, -5)$.}\\
Step 1: read her set-up as an order question. On top she wrote
$5 - (-5)$: that is $y_1 - y_2$, first point minus second. Underneath she wrote
$2 - (-3)$: that is $x_2 - x_1$, second point minus first.
Step 2: name the error --- the two differences were taken in \emph{opposite}
orders, which multiplies the answer by $-1$. That is a sign reversal, not an
arithmetic slip; her $10$ and her $5$ are both correct numbers.
Step 3: redo it in one order, second minus first throughout:
$m = \dfrac{-5 - 5}{2 - (-3)} = \dfrac{-10}{5}$.
Step 4: $\ans{m = -2}$. Check the direction: $y$ falls from $5$ to $-5$ while $x$
increases, so the slope must be negative --- Devi's $+2$ could have been rejected
before any arithmetic.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Mika's slope through $(1, 4)$ and $(5, 6)$.}\\
Step 1: identify what each of his differences measures. His numerator $5 - 1$ is
the change in $x$, and his denominator $6 - 4$ is the change in $y$: the fraction
is upside down.
Step 2: name the error --- inverted ratio (run over rise). His answer is the
\emph{reciprocal} of the true slope, which is why it is $2$ instead of a number
below $1$.
Step 3: rebuild it as rise over run:
$m = \dfrac{6 - 4}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{2}{4}$.
Step 4: simplify: $\ans{m = \frac{1}{2}}$. Check: moving $4$ right lifts the line
only $2$, so the line is shallow and the slope must be less than $1$.}

\problem{\textbf{Change in the tank's level from hour $0$ to hour $6$.}\\
Step 1: read both values off the table: at hour $0$ the level is $46$ litres, and
at hour $6$ it is $28$ litres.
Step 2: a change is always \emph{later minus earlier} --- the order carries the
meaning, so it is $28 - 46$, not $46 - 28$.
Step 3: $28 - 46 = \ans{-18}$ litres.
Step 4: check the sign against the story: the tank is draining, so the change must
be negative.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $18$ --- the same size, but it describes a tank filling up.}

\problem{\textbf{Rate of change of the tank's level, hour $0$ to hour $6$.}\\
Step 1: a rate of change is the slope of the table: change in litres over change in
hours, both taken later-minus-earlier.
Step 2: numerator (from problem 4): $28 - 46 = -18$ litres.
Step 3: denominator, same order: $6 - 0 = 6$ hours.
Step 4: $\dfrac{-18}{6} = \ans{-3}$, that is, $3$ litres lost per hour.
Step 5: check with a single step of the table: from hour $0$ to hour $2$ the level
drops from $46$ to $40$, which is $6$ litres in $2$ hours --- the same $-3$ per
hour.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $3$ --- correct size, wrong sign, and it claims the tank is
filling.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Ty's slope through $(5, 9)$ and $(1, 1)$.}\\
Step 1: check the order of each difference. Ty's numerator $1 - 9$ runs from the
first point's $y$ to the second point's $y$; his denominator $5 - 1$ runs the other
way, second to first.
Step 2: name the error --- mixed order, so his answer carries the wrong sign.
Step 3: fix the order and keep it: taking $(5,9)$ as point $1$,
$m = \dfrac{1 - 9}{1 - 5} = \dfrac{-8}{-4}$.
Step 4: two negatives divide to a positive: $\ans{m = 2}$.
Step 5: direction --- a positive slope means the line \textbf{rises} from left to
right. Check it against the points: as $x$ grows from $1$ to $5$, $y$ grows from
$1$ to $9$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $-2$ --- Ty's answer; the size is right, so only the sign
diagnosis separates a correct paper from his.}

\problem{\textbf{Candle: $18$ cm at $3$ minutes, $6$ cm at $11$ minutes. Diagnose
Jo's rate and find the correct one.}\\
Step 1: read Jo's fraction. She put the time difference $11 - 3$ on top and the
height difference $6 - 18$ underneath --- minutes per centimetre, the reciprocal of
what was asked. Her orders were consistent, so her only error is the inversion.
Step 2: set it up as height change over time change, later minus earlier in both:
$\dfrac{6 - 18}{11 - 3}$.
Step 3: numerator $6 - 18 = -12$ cm; denominator $11 - 3 = 8$ minutes.
Step 4: $\dfrac{-12}{8} = \ans{-1.5}$, that is, the candle loses $1.5$ cm each
minute.
Step 5: check plausibility: in $8$ minutes it should lose $8 \times 1.5 = 12$ cm,
and it does ($18$ down to $6$).\\
\emph{Watch for:} $-0.67$ --- Jo's inverted value; note it is between $-1$ and $0$,
far too slow for a candle that lost $12$ cm in $8$ minutes.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: the line through $(a, 4)$ and $(7, -2)$ has slope $-3$.
Diagnose Priya's set-up and solve for $a$.}\\
Step 1: diagnose. Priya wrote $\dfrac{7 - a}{-2 - 4}$: the $x$-difference is on top
and the $y$-difference underneath, so her equation says
run over rise $= -3$. It is the inverted slope, set equal to the true slope.
Step 2: write the correct equation, rise over run, both differences second minus
first: $\dfrac{-2 - 4}{7 - a} = -3$.
Step 3: simplify the numerator: $\dfrac{-6}{7 - a} = -3$.
Step 4: multiply both sides by $(7 - a)$: $-6 = -3(7 - a) = -21 + 3a$.
Step 5: add $21$ to both sides: $15 = 3a$, so $\ans{a = 5}$.
Step 6: check by recomputing the slope with $a = 5$: through $(5, 4)$ and
$(7, -2)$, $m = \dfrac{-2 - 4}{7 - 5} = \dfrac{-6}{2} = -3$ $\checkmark$.
Priya's $-11$ fails this check: through $(-11, 4)$ and $(7, -2)$ the slope is
$\dfrac{-6}{18} = -\dfrac{1}{3}$, the reciprocal of $-3$ --- the signature of an
inverted set-up.}
