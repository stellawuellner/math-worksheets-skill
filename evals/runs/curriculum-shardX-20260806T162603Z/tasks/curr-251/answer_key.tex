\problem{$P(x) = 4x^3 - 7x^2 + x - 9$: complete the term/coefficient/degree table, then find $P(1)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Reading each term off in turn:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Coefficient} & \textbf{Degree of the term}\\
\hline
$4x^3$  & $4$  & $3$ \\\hline
$-7x^2$ & $-7$ & $2$ \\\hline
$x$     & $1$  & $1$ \\\hline
$-9$    & $-9$ & $0$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Two cells are the ones students miss: the $x$ term has coefficient $1$ (not
``none''), and the constant $-9$ has degree $0$, because $-9 = -9x^0$.
The degree of $P$ is the largest of those term degrees, $3$.
Substituting $x=1$ turns every power of $x$ into $1$, so $P(1)$ is just the sum of the
four coefficients: $4 + (-7) + 1 + (-9)$. \ans{P(1) = -11}

\problem{Area model for $(x+6)(x+2)$: fill in the four partial areas and write the total area in standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Each cell is (row label)$\times$(column label):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$ & $x$ & $+6$ \\
\hline
$x$  & $x^2$ & $6x$ \\\hline
$+2$ & $2x$  & $12$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The two shaded-diagonal cells $6x$ and $2x$ are like terms, so they combine:
$x^2 + 6x + 2x + 12 = x^2 + 8x + 12$. The four cells of the model are exactly the four
partial products, and the middle coefficient $8$ is the sum of the two numbers in the
binomials. \ans{x^2 + 8x + 12}

\problem{Write $18x^3 + 30x^2$ as (greatest common monomial factor)$\times$(remaining factor).}

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
  18 &= 2\cdot 3\cdot 3, \qquad 30 = 2\cdot 3\cdot 5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \gcd(18,30) = 6,\\
  \intertext{and the lowest power of $x$ present is $x^2$, so the common monomial factor is $6x^2$:}
  18x^3 + 30x^2 &= 6x^2\cdot 3x + 6x^2 \cdot 5 = 6x^2(3x+5).
\end{align*}
Check by re-distributing: $6x^2 \cdot 3x = 18x^3$ and $6x^2\cdot 5 = 30x^2$. \ans{6x^2(3x+5)}

\problem{$3x(2x-5)$ versus $6x^2 - 15x$: (a) confirm they are equivalent; (b) which form shows what?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Distribute $3x$ across the binomial:
$3x\cdot 2x = 6x^2$ and $3x\cdot(-5) = -15x$, so $3x(2x-5) = 6x^2 - 15x$. \ans{6x^2 - 15x}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The product form $3x(2x-5)$ shows the
\textit{factors}: it says at a glance that the polynomial is divisible by $3x$ and by $(2x-5)$,
which is what you need when hunting for zeros. The standard form $6x^2 - 15x$ shows the
\textit{degree} ($2$, from the leading term) and the \textit{coefficients} ($6$ and $-15$).
Full credit: names one thing each form makes visible. Half credit: describes only one form.

\problem{Multiply $(2x+3)(x-5)$ and write it in standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
  (2x+3)(x-5) &= 2x\cdot x + 2x\cdot(-5) + 3\cdot x + 3\cdot(-5)\\
              &= 2x^2 - 10x + 3x - 15\\
              &= 2x^2 - 7x - 15.
\end{align*}
Read off the parts: leading coefficient $2$, coefficient of $x$ equal to $-7$
(from $-10 + 3$), constant term $-15$, degree $2$. \ans{2x^2 - 7x - 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$Q(x) = (x+4)(x-1)(x+2)$: find the constant term using $Q(0)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Every term of the expanded product except the constant one carries at
least one factor of $x$, so substituting $x=0$ wipes them all out and leaves the constant term
alone. Substituting into the factored form is far less work than expanding:
$Q(0) = (0+4)(0-1)(0+2) = (4)(-1)(2)$. \ans{Q(0) = -8}

\noindent The constant term of $Q$ is therefore $-8$, and $Q$ has degree $3$ because three
linear factors are multiplied together.

\problem{$A(x) = (3x^2-1)(x+5)$, $B(x) = 2x^4 - x^2$: compare the degrees.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The degree of a product is the sum of the degrees of its factors, so
$A$ has degree $2 + 1 = 3$; there is no need to expand it. For $B$, the degree is the largest
exponent that appears, which is $4$. Since $3$ is less than $4$: \ans{<}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Factor $x^2 - 3x - 28$ into two binomials.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Look for two numbers whose product is the constant term $-28$ and whose
sum is the coefficient of $x$, namely $-3$. The pair $-7$ and $+4$ works:
$(-7)(4) = -28$ and $(-7) + 4 = -3$. Therefore
$x^2 - 3x - 28 = (x-7)(x+4)$. \ans{(x-7)(x+4)}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$(x+3)^2 - (x-3)^2$: (a) expand and simplify; (b) explain the drop in degree.}

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\begin{align*}
  (x+3)^2 - (x-3)^2 &= (x^2 + 6x + 9) - (x^2 - 6x + 9)\\
                    &= x^2 + 6x + 9 - x^2 + 6x - 9\\
                    &= 12x.
\end{align*}
\ans{12x}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Each square contributes an $x^2$ term with
coefficient $+1$, so the subtraction cancels them exactly; the constants $+9$ cancel too. What
survives is the difference of the linear terms, $6x - (-6x) = 12x$, of degree $1$. Full credit
must point at the cancelling $x^2$ terms, not merely restate the answer.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Area $6x^2 + 19x + 10$ with one side $(2x+5)$: (a) find the other side; (b) why do the degrees add?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The other side is the factor that pairs with $(2x+5)$. Split the middle
term using two numbers with product $6\cdot 10 = 60$ and sum $19$, namely $4$ and $15$:
\begin{align*}
  6x^2 + 19x + 10 &= 6x^2 + 4x + 15x + 10\\
                  &= 2x(3x+2) + 5(3x+2)\\
                  &= (2x+5)(3x+2).
\end{align*}
Dividing the area by the known side leaves the other side. \ans{3x + 2}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Multiplying two polynomials multiplies
their leading terms, so the degrees add: $1 + 1 = 2$. A degree-$2$ area built from a degree-$1$
side therefore has to leave a degree-$1$ side. Full credit states that add-the-degrees rule
rather than only reciting the numbers.
