\problem{$r = 6\cos\theta$ at $\theta = 60^\circ$. Substitute the angle and use the
exact cosine value: $\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}$, so $r = 6 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}$.
$\ans{r = 3}$}

\problem{$r = 4\sin\theta$ at $\theta = 30^\circ$. Since $\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}$,
$r = 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}$. $\ans{r = 2}$}

\problem{$r = 1 + \cos\theta$ at $\theta = 90^\circ$. Here $\cos 90^\circ = 0$, so
$r = 1 + 0$. The cardioid therefore reaches one unit straight up the $90^\circ$ ray.
$\ans{r = 1}$}

\problem{A rose returns to the pole exactly where $r = 0$:
\[ 4\sin(2\theta) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin(2\theta) = 0 \]
Sine vanishes at multiples of $180^\circ$, so $2\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ,
360^\circ, 540^\circ$ --- the doubled window is what produces four answers, not two.
Halving each gives
\[ \boxed{\theta = 0^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 270^\circ} \]
Between consecutive zeros the curve traces one petal, which is why $r = 4\sin(2\theta)$
has four petals rather than two.}

\problem{$r = 2 + 4\cos\theta$ at $\theta = 180^\circ$. Since $\cos 180^\circ = -1$,
$r = 2 + 4(-1) = 2 - 4$. $\ans{r = -2}$

\par A negative $r$ is not an error: the point is plotted 2 units from the pole in
the direction \emph{opposite} $180^\circ$, i.e. along the $0^\circ$ ray. A student who
takes $\cos 180^\circ$ as $+1$ gets 6 and misses the inner loop entirely.}

\problem{Set the cardioid's radius to zero:
\[ 1 + \cos\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos\theta = -1 \]
On $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$ the cosine equals $-1$ at exactly one angle.
$\ans{\theta = 180^\circ}$ \ This lone zero is the cardioid's cusp, which is what
distinguishes a cardioid from a limacon with an inner loop (two zeros) or one without
(none).}

\problem{The petal tips are where the radius attains its maximum 2:
\[ 2\sin(3\theta) = 2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin(3\theta) = 1 \]
Sine equals 1 at $90^\circ$ plus multiples of $360^\circ$, so
$3\theta = 90^\circ,\ 450^\circ,\ 810^\circ$ (the next value, $1170^\circ$, would put
$\theta$ outside the window). Dividing by 3:
\[ \boxed{\theta = 30^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 270^\circ} \]
Three tips, $120^\circ$ apart --- the three-petal rose you expect from an odd
coefficient.}

\problem{$r = 3 - 2\cos\theta$ at $\theta = 120^\circ$. Since
$\cos 120^\circ = -\tfrac{1}{2}$, $r = 3 - 2\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 3 + 1$.
Subtracting a negative increases $r$, which is why this limacon bulges toward
$180^\circ$. $\ans{r = 4}$}

\problem{The inner loop begins and ends where the curve passes through the pole:
\[ 2 + 4\cos\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos\theta = -\tfrac{1}{2} \]
Cosine is $-\tfrac{1}{2}$ in the second and third quadrants, at
\[ \boxed{\theta = 120^\circ,\ 240^\circ} \]
Between those two angles $r$ is negative, and those points plot on the opposite side
of the pole --- that is the inner loop.}

\problem{The circle's maximum radius occurs where
\[ 4\cos\theta = 4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos\theta = 1 \]
On $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$ this happens once. $\ans{\theta = 0^\circ}$

\par So $r = 4\cos\theta$ is the circle of diameter 4 sitting on the polar axis, with
centre $(2, 0^\circ)$ --- the maximum-$r$ direction is exactly the direction of the
offset.}

\problem{\textbf{Manual review --- hand-drawn sketch. A correct graph shows:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item The four quadrantal points: $r = 2$ at $\theta = 0^\circ$, $r = 1$ at
        $90^\circ$, $r = 0$ at $180^\circ$, $r = 1$ at $270^\circ$.
  \item A smooth heart shape symmetric about the horizontal polar axis (because
        $\cos$ is even, $r(-\theta) = r(\theta)$).
  \item The cusp labelled at the pole, reached along the $180^\circ$ direction.
  \item No part of the curve outside the ring $r = 2$, and no negative $r$: the
        radius $1 + \cos\theta$ never drops below 0.
\end{itemize}}

\problem{\textbf{Manual review --- identification, sketch, and justification.
A complete answer contains:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Family:} a limacon of the form $r = a + b\cos\theta$, specifically a
        limacon with an inner loop.
  \item \emph{Test:} compare $a$ with $b$. Here $a = 2$ and $b = 4$, so
        $a/b = 1/2 < 1$; the radius must change sign, and a polar curve crosses the
        pole exactly where it does. For $r = 4 + 2\cos\theta$ the ratio is $2 > 1$,
        the radius stays positive, and no loop can form.
  \item \emph{Evidence:} the pole crossings found in problem 9 are
        $\theta = 120^\circ$ and $240^\circ$; between them $r < 0$ (for instance
        $r = -2$ at $180^\circ$, from problem 5), and those negative radii plot back
        through the pole to form the loop.
  \item \emph{Sketch:} outer curve reaching $r = 6$ at $\theta = 0^\circ$, passing
        through the pole at $120^\circ$ and $240^\circ$, with the small loop lying on
        the $0^\circ$ side of the pole and extending to a distance 2. Symmetric about
        the polar axis.
\end{itemize}
Accept any justification that compares the constant with the coefficient and links
the sign change of $r$ to the pole crossings.}
