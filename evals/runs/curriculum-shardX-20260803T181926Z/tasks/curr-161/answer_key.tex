\problem{\textbf{Bar model: } $10\%$ of $80$ books.
The bar stands for the whole collection, which is $100\%$, and it is cut into ten
equal parts, so each part is $\frac{100}{10} = 10\%$ of the books.
Each part must therefore hold $\frac{80}{10}$ books.
Dividing the whole by ten is exactly what taking $10\%$ does, which is why
$10\%$ is the easiest benchmark to find.
So $10\%$ of $80 = \ans{8}$ books.}

\problem{\textbf{Bar model: } $50\%$ of $46$ marbles.
The bar is cut into two equal parts, and two equal parts of $100\%$ are $50\%$ each,
so one part is half the marbles.
Half of $46$ is $\frac{46}{2}$.
Splitting $46$ into $40 + 6$ makes the halving easy: $20 + 3$.
So $50\%$ of $46 = \ans{23}$ marbles.}

\problem{\textbf{Ratio table: } $30\%$ of $70$.
The table starts from $100\% \to 70$. Dividing both columns by ten gives
$10\% \to 7$. Multiplying both columns by three gives $30\% \to 21$.
In one step the same reasoning is a multiplication:
$30\% = \frac{30}{100} = 0.3$, and $0.3 \times 70 = 21$.
Both routes agree, so $30\%$ of $70 = \ans{21}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Fraction shortcut: } $25\%$ of $64$ songs.
$25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}$, so a quarter of the playlist is wanted and
the quickest route is a division: $\frac{64}{4} = 16$.
Check with the decimal route: $25\% = 0.25$ and $0.25 \times 64 = 16$, the same value.
So $25\%$ of $64 = \ans{16}$ songs.}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake: } $5\%$ of $120$.
Moving the decimal point one place to the left divides by ten, and dividing by ten
is taking $\frac{1}{10}$, which is $10\%$ --- not $5\%$.
That is why Dana got $12$: she found $10\%$ of $120$.
Since $5\%$ is half of $10\%$, halve her result: $\frac{12}{2} = 6$.
Check with the decimal: $5\% = 0.05$, and $0.05 \times 120 = 6$.
The correct value is $\ans{6}$, not $12$.}

\problem{\textbf{Restaurant tip: } $15\%$ of $48$ dollars.
Split the benchmark: $10\%$ of $48$ is $4.8$, and $5\%$ is half of that, $2.4$.
Adding the two parts gives $4.8 + 2.4 = 7.2$.
The one-step route agrees: $15\% = 0.15$ and $0.15 \times 48 = 7.2$.
To the nearest cent the tip is $\ans{7.20}$ dollars.}

\problem{\textbf{Concert hall: } $8\%$ of $250$ seats.
Write the percent as a decimal: $8\% = \frac{8}{100} = 0.08$.
Then $0.08 \times 250 = 20$.
Benchmark check: $10\%$ of $250$ is $25$, and $8\%$ must be a little less than
that, which $20$ is.
So the number of empty seats is $\ans{20 \text{ seats}}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Jacket on sale: } $35$ dollars, $20\%$ off.
The discount is a percent \emph{of the price}, so the equation is
$d = \frac{20}{100} \times 35$.
Solving: $0.2 \times 35 = 7$, so the discount is $\ans{7}$ dollars.

The sale price is what is left of the price after the discount comes off:
$35 - 7 = 28$.
Equivalently, paying $80\%$ of the price gives $0.8 \times 35 = 28$, the same value.
The sale price is $\ans{28}$ dollars.
(A student who wrote $15$ took twenty \emph{dollars} off rather than twenty percent.)}

\problem{\textbf{An awkward percent: } $12.5\%$ of $64$ grams.
$12.5\% = \frac{12.5}{100} = \frac{1}{8}$, so the recipe wants an eighth of the block.
An eighth is found by halving three times: $64 \to 32 \to 16 \to 8$.
The decimal route needs $0.125 \times 64$, a three-place decimal multiplication,
which is more work by hand for the same answer.
The recipe calls for $\ans{8}$ grams.}

\problem{\textbf{Two percents in a row: } $60$ dollars, $25\%$ off, then $8\%$ tax.
The discount is $\frac{25}{100} \times 60 = 15$ dollars, so the equation for the
sale price is $s = 60 - 15$, giving $s = \ans{45}$ dollars.

Tax is charged on what the shop actually sells the backpack for, which is the sale
price, so the tax is $\frac{8}{100} \times 45 = 3.6$ dollars.
Final price: $45 + 3.6 = 48.6$.
To the nearest cent the final price is $\ans{48.60}$ dollars.

Taking the tax on the original price would give $60 + 4.8 = 64.8$ dollars, which
overcharges by more than the discount was worth --- the tax rate applies to the
amount being paid, not to a price nobody pays.}
