\problem{Kim knows $3 \times 7 = 21$. What is $7 \times 3$?}

\textbf{Solution:} Turning the factors around never changes the product --- a 3-by-7 array turned sideways is a 7-by-3 array with the same 21 things. So $7 \times 3 = 21$. \ans{21}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$6 \times 4 = 24$, so what is $4 \times 6$?}

\textbf{Solution:} Same two factors, other order: the product stays 24. \ans{24}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$7 \times 6 = 7 \times 5 + 7 \times 1$}

\textbf{Solution:} Six 7s is five 7s plus one more 7: $35 + 7 = 42$. \ans{42}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$8 \times 7 = 8 \times 5 + 8 \times 2$}

\textbf{Solution:} Split the 7 into $5 + 2$. Then $8 \times 5 = 40$ and $8 \times 2 = 16$, and the parts add back together: $40 + 16 = 56$.
\[ \boxed{8 \times 7 = 40 + 16 = 56} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Turn-around fact for $9 \times 2$, and the product.}

\textbf{Solution:} The turn-around fact swaps the factors: $2 \times 9 = 18$. Both facts name the same double: $9 + 9 = 18$. \ans{18}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$6 \times 9 = 6 \times 10 - 6 \times 1$}

\textbf{Solution:} Nine 6s is ten 6s minus one 6: $60 - 6 = 54$. \ans{54}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Which order is easier to skip count for the 8-and-5 fact?}

\textbf{Solution:} Counting by 5s eight times ($8 \times 5$) is the friendlier count for most students --- fives land on tidy numbers. Counting by 8s five times is also correct if that is what the student ran. Either way the product is $8 \times 5 = 40$. \ans{40}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$4 \times 12 = 4 \times 10 + 4 \times 2$}

\textbf{Solution:} Split the 12 into $10 + 2$: $4 \times 10 = 40$ and $4 \times 2 = 8$, so $40 + 8 = 48$. \ans{48}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $6 \times 7$ two ways, then say why they agree.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Break the 7: $6 \times 5 + 6 \times 2 = 30 + 12 = 42$. \ans{42}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Break the 6: $7 \times 5 + 7 \times 1 = 35 + 7 = 42$. \ans{42}

\textbf{Solution (c):} Both ways count the same 6-by-7 array of 42 things --- breaking a factor apart only cuts the array into pieces, it never adds or removes anything, so every correct split must land on the same product.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Challenge: any strategy for $9 \times 7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Example strategies: break the 9 into $5 + 4$ ($35 + 28 = 63$); break the 7 into $5 + 2$ ($45 + 18 = 63$); or use ten 7s minus one 7 ($70 - 7 = 63$). Credit any turn-around or break-apart move that is written out and used correctly. The product is $9 \times 7 = 63$. \ans{63}
