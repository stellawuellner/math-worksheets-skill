\wstitleblock{Comparing Categorical Frequencies in a Two-Way Table}{Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra}{}

\noindent\small
Reading a two-way table, row and column relative frequencies, and comparing them
to judge association.
\par\smallskip
All ten problems use the survey table below. Two hundred eighth graders were
asked two yes-or-no questions: \emph{Do you play a school sport?} and
\emph{Do you get 8 or more hours of sleep on a school night?} Every number in
the table is a count of students.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item A \textbf{row relative frequency} divides a cell by \emph{its own row}
        total. A \textbf{column relative frequency} divides the same cell by
        \emph{its own column} total. They answer different questions, so always
        say which total you used.
  \item Give every percent to the nearest whole percent.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
 & \textbf{8 or more hours} & \textbf{Under 8 hours} & \textbf{Row total} \\
\hline
\textbf{Plays a school sport} & 30 & 20 & 50 \\
\hline
\textbf{Does not play a sport} & 45 & 105 & 150 \\
\hline
\textbf{Column total} & 75 & 125 & 200 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem[5cm]{How many of the 200 students both play a school sport and get
8 or more hours of sleep? Name the row and the column you used to find the
cell.}

\problem[5cm]{Add the four inner cells of the table --- 30, 20, 45 and 105 ---
to check the grand total. How many students were surveyed altogether?}

\problem[6.5cm]{Of the 50 students who play a school sport, 30 get 8 or more
hours of sleep. What percent of the sport players get 8 or more hours? Write the
row relative frequency as a percent.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Find how many more of the students who do not play a sport get
under 8 hours of sleep (105 students) than the students who do play a sport and
get under 8 hours (20 students).}

\problem[6.5cm]{Of the 150 students who do not play a school sport, 45 get
8 or more hours of sleep. What percent of the non-players get 8 or more hours?
Write the row relative frequency as a percent.}

\problem[7cm]{Now switch to a column. Of the 75 students who get 8 or more hours
of sleep, 30 play a school sport. What percent of the well-rested students play
a sport? Write the column relative frequency as a percent, and state clearly
which total you divided by.}

\problem[7cm]{Stay in the columns. Of the 125 students who get under 8 hours of
sleep, 20 play a school sport. What percent of the short-sleep students play a
sport? Write the column relative frequency as a percent, and state which total
you divided by.}

\problem[6.5cm]{Problem 3 gave the percent of sport players who get 8 or more
hours of sleep (60 percent) and problem 5 gave the same percent for non-players
(30 percent). Subtract to find the gap between the two row relative frequencies,
in percentage points.}

\problem[6.5cm]{Five other eighth-grade classes ran the same survey and built
their own two-way tables. The percent of sport players getting 8 or more hours
of sleep came out as 55, 70, 45, 60 and 60. Find the median of these five
percents.}

\problem{Use your answers from problems 3 and 5. Is there an association between
playing a school sport and getting 8 or more hours of sleep? Say yes or no, and
then explain in complete sentences why comparing the two row percentages is
better evidence than comparing the raw counts 30 and 45.
