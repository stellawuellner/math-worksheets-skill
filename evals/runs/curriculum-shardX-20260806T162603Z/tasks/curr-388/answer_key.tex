\problem{$M = \begin{bmatrix} 24 & 18 \\ 31 & 12 \end{bmatrix}$, $T = \begin{bmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 27 & 15 \end{bmatrix}$. Find $M + T$ in bikes.}

\textbf{Solution:} Matrices of the same shape add entry by entry; each sum is the two-day
rental total for one depot and one bike type.
\[ M + T = \begin{bmatrix} 24 + 19 & 18 + 22 \\ 31 + 27 & 12 + 15 \end{bmatrix}
        = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{43} & \ans{40} \\ \ans{58} & \ans{27} \end{bmatrix} \text{ bikes} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$P = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 10 \\ 5 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$ dollars per rental. Find $1.25P$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A scalar multiplies every entry, so the shape does not change and no entry
interacts with any other.
\[ 1.25P = \begin{bmatrix} 1.25(6) & 1.25(10) \\ 1.25(5) & 1.25(9) \end{bmatrix}
        = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{7.5} & \ans{12.5} \\ \ans{6.25} & \ans{11.25} \end{bmatrix} \text{ dollars per rental} \]
A $25\%$ rise is the scalar $1.25$ because $100\% + 25\% = 125\%$.

\problem{$U = \begin{bmatrix} 52 & 34 \\ 47 & 29 \end{bmatrix}$ out, $V = \begin{bmatrix} 45 & 30 \\ 41 & 26 \end{bmatrix}$ returned. Find $U - V$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Subtract entry by entry; each difference is the number of bikes of that
type still out at that depot.
\[ U - V = \begin{bmatrix} 52 - 45 & 34 - 30 \\ 47 - 41 & 29 - 26 \end{bmatrix}
        = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{7} & \ans{4} \\ \ans{6} & \ans{3} \end{bmatrix} \text{ bikes} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$W = \begin{bmatrix} 30 & 14 \\ 22 & 20 \end{bmatrix}$ bikes, $c = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}$ dollars per rental. Find $Wc$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A $2 \times 2$ times a $2 \times 1$ gives a $2 \times 1$. Each entry pairs
one row of $W$ with the single column of $c$.
\begin{align*}
  \text{North} &= 30(6) + 14(10) = 180 + 140 \\
  \text{South} &= 22(6) + 20(10) = 132 + 200
\end{align*}
\[ Wc = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{320} \\ \ans{332} \end{bmatrix} \text{ dollars} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ added, $B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 6 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ retired. Find $3A - 2B$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Scale first, then subtract entry by entry.
\[ 3A = \begin{bmatrix} 12 & 21 \\ 6 & 15 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   2B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 12 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \]
\[ 3A - 2B = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{10} & \ans{15} \\ \ans{-6} & \ans{11} \end{bmatrix} \text{ bikes} \]
The negative entry is real information: the South depot loses $6$ standard bikes over the
five months.

\problem{$D = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, $E = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$. Find $DE$ in parts.}

\textbf{Solution:} Row of $D$ against column of $E$, one product per entry.
\begin{align*}
  (1,1) &= 5(2) + 2(3) = 10 + 6 &  (1,2) &= 5(1) + 2(5) = 5 + 10 \\
  (2,1) &= 3(2) + 4(3) = 6 + 12 &  (2,2) &= 3(1) + 4(5) = 3 + 20
\end{align*}
\[ DE = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{16} & \ans{15} \\ \ans{18} & \ans{23} \end{bmatrix} \text{ parts} \]
The inner dimensions match ($2 = 2$), and the answer inherits the outer ones: $2 \times 2$.

\problem{Two standard and three electric passes cost $22$ dollars; four standard and one electric cost $24$ dollars. Write a matrix equation and solve.}

\textbf{Solution:} The conditions are $2x + 3y = 22$ and $4x + y = 24$, which is
\[ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
   = \begin{bmatrix} 22 \\ 24 \end{bmatrix} \]
Solving by elimination: multiply the second equation by $3$ to get $12x + 3y = 72$, then
subtract the first.
\begin{align*}
  12x + 3y - (2x + 3y) &= 72 - 22 \\
  10x &= 50
\end{align*}
So $x = 5$, and $4(5) + y = 24$ gives $y = 4$. A standard pass costs \ans{x = 5} dollars and
an electric pass costs \ans{y = 4} dollars.

\problem{$F = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 3 \\ 6 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$, $G = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 4 \\ 12 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$. (a) Entry $(1,2)$ of $FG$. (b) What it measures.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Row $1$ of $F$ against column $2$ of $G$:
\[ 8(4) + 3(9) = 32 + 27 \]
so the entry is \ans{59}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Row $1$ is Saturday and column $2$ is staff
minutes, so the entry is the total staff time in minutes that Saturday's rentals require:
$8$ standard rentals at $4$ minutes each plus $3$ electric rentals at $9$ minutes each.
Full credit names the row, names the column, and says the entry totals across the two bike
types. ``It is $59$ minutes'' alone does not show the student can read a product matrix.

\problem{$H = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 6 & 4 \\ 8 & 9 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$, $k = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 10 \\ 14 \end{bmatrix}$. (a) Find $Hk$. (b) Why $kH$ does not exist.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $H$ is $2 \times 3$ and $k$ is $3 \times 1$; the inner dimensions
agree, so the product is $2 \times 1$.
\begin{align*}
  \text{North} &= 10(6) + 6(10) + 4(14) = 60 + 60 + 56 \\
  \text{South} &= 8(6) + 9(10) + 3(14) = 48 + 90 + 42
\end{align*}
\[ Hk = \begin{bmatrix} \ans{176} \\ \ans{180} \end{bmatrix} \text{ dollars} \]

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} In the order $kH$ the shapes are $3 \times 1$
and $2 \times 3$: the inner dimensions are $1$ and $2$, which do not agree, so no row of $k$
can be paired with a column of $H$ and the product is undefined. Full credit states the
inner-dimension rule and applies it to these shapes. ``Matrices do not commute'' is not
enough here --- the issue is not a different answer but no answer at all.

\problem{$100$ bikes in three classes; $20$ more electric than standard; a full round of rentals at $6$, $10$ and $14$ dollars takes $960$ dollars. (a) Solve. (b) Interpret the matrix equation.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The three conditions are
\[ x + y + z = 100, \qquad -x + y = 20, \qquad 6x + 10y + 14z = 960 \]
\[ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 6 & 10 & 14 \end{bmatrix}
   \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}
   = \begin{bmatrix} 100 \\ 20 \\ 960 \end{bmatrix} \]
From the second row $y = x + 20$. Substituting into the first gives $z = 80 - 2x$.
Substituting both into the third:
\begin{align*}
  6x + 10(x + 20) + 14(80 - 2x) &= 960 \\
  6x + 10x + 200 + 1120 - 28x &= 960 \\
  -12x &= -360
\end{align*}
So $x = 30$, then $y = 50$ and $z = 20$: \ans{x = 30} standard, \ans{y = 50} electric,
\ans{z = 20} cargo bikes.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The coefficient matrix holds one row per
condition (total count, the electric-versus-standard difference, total revenue) and one
column per bike class. The variable column holds the three unknown counts. The constant
column holds the three totals the conditions demand: $100$ bikes, a difference of $20$ bikes,
and $960$ dollars. Full credit ties rows to conditions and columns to unknowns; restating
the three equations in words is not an interpretation.
