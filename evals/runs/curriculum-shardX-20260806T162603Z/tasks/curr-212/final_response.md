# Equations with Brackets and Fractions — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_distfrac_curr212.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) distributing over a bracket, (2) clearing a
  denominator by multiplying *both whole sides* by the least common denominator, (3) the
  harder case where a bracket sits over a fraction bar. A watch-out box names the two slips
  this whole topic turns on — both are "something on the page was left untouched".
- **`ws_distfrac_curr212.pdf` — Worksheet (4 pages, 12 problems).** A clean fluency ramp
  with no repeated skeleton: single brackets, then simple denominators, then a grouped
  numerator, then two fractions with different denominators, then brackets on both sides,
  then a fraction equation with variables on both sides, and finally a bracket over a
  denominator. It ends with a find-the-error challenge on a student's written work.
- **`ak_distfrac_curr212.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with each
  move named and the multi-step ones shown line by line, plus a Quick Answers strip and a
  Curriculum block (standard 8.EE.C.7, difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **11 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked.** SymPy solved every equation
  independently and compared its full solution set with the printed answer, so a wrong or
  incomplete answer would have blocked the build before any PDF was produced.
- **Problem 12 is split.** Part (a), the correct solution, is machine-checked. Part (b) —
  explaining which step Nia got wrong and what rule she broke — is **instructor-judged**,
  because it is written reasoning. It prints as `---` in the Quick Answers strip, and the
  answer key gives you the rubric: the answer must name the "every term inside the bracket"
  rule, and a substitution check is strong supporting evidence.
- **One misconception trap was declared and proved distinguishable:** distributing to the
  first term inside the bracket only. That is exactly where Nia's $\tfrac{22}{3}$ comes
  from, and the build confirmed it could not be mistaken for the correct answer.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same
  way (6 of 6 machine-checked).

Every answer on the sheet is a whole number, which makes checking quick: if your child lands
on a fraction anywhere except Nia's wrong work, a term was almost certainly left unmultiplied.
