# Polynomial structure: terms, factors, coefficients, degree — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_polystructure_curr251.pdf`, 2 pages) — three skill sections, each with a
  rule box, a worked example that opens by saying *why* that method was chosen, and a try-it
  whose answer is printed upside down inside the box: (1) reading terms, coefficients and degree,
  with the "P(1) is the sum of the coefficients" check; (2) turning a product into a sum with a
  2×2 area model; (3) turning a sum back into a product (common factor first, then the
  product-and-sum trinomial rule). A watch-out box covers the two classic slips: forgetting that
  the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees, and reading a missing numeral as a missing
  coefficient.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_polystructure_curr251.pdf`, 5 pages) — 10 problems, difficulty ramped 1 → 5.
  It opens with two filled-in models (a term/coefficient/degree table and an area-model grid) so
  the notation is attached to a picture before the sheet asks for it, then interleaves the three
  methods so the student has to choose. The last two problems are the stretch: a difference of
  two squares whose degree collapses from 2 to 1, and a rectangle where the area and one side are
  given and the other side must be recovered.
- **Answer key** (`ak_polystructure_curr251.pdf`, 3 pages) — full worked reasoning for every
  problem (not answers only), a Quick Answers bank for fast grading, and a generated Curriculum
  block listing the standards codes (HSA-APR.A.1, HSA-SSE.B.3) with the problems that hit them.

## What is verified, honestly

- **7 of the 10 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy: every expansion, factorisation,
  equivalence, substitution and the degree comparison were recomputed independently of the
  answer key before anything compiled.
- **3 problems (4, 9, 10) are part machine-checked and part instructor-judged.** Each of those
  has an (a) that is a value — verified — and a (b) that asks the student to explain something
  ("which form shows the factors?", "why did the degree drop?", "why do the degrees add?"). No
  program can grade a sentence, so those parts are flagged for you, and the answer key prints
  the grading rubric: what a full-credit response has to say, and what earns half credit. The
  Quick Answers bank shows those as `—` rather than pretending they were checked.
- **12 blanks are marked as working space**, not answers: the cells of the two model tables in
  problems 1 and 2. The final answer for each of those problems is the single blank marked
  **Answer**, and that one is verified. The key says so too.

Nothing on the worksheet or the study guide prints a grade level; it appears on the answer key
only, where it is useful to you.
