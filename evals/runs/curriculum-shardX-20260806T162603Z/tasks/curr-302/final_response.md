# Postulates, Theorems, Converses, and Biconditionals — high-school geometry (10 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_proof_logic_curr302.pdf`, 6 pages) — 10 problems that build from a
  worked pattern to independent fluency. It opens with a four-row classification table
  (postulate or theorem?), then alternates: use a postulate to set up and solve an
  equation, write and test a converse, decide when a biconditional is allowed. No two
  problems share a skeleton — the addition postulates, the linear-pair postulate, the
  converse of the corresponding-angles postulate, and the perpendicular biconditional
  each appear once. Diagrams carry labels only; all measurements are in the text.
- **Answer key** (`ak_proof_logic_curr302.pdf`, 4 pages) — every equation worked line by
  line with a substitution check, and for each written item a model answer plus a
  grading note saying which part of the sentence has to be there.
- **Study guide** (`ss_proof_logic_curr302.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: postulate
  versus theorem (and the addition postulates), writing and testing a converse, and when
  a biconditional is allowed. Rule box, worked example, and try-it in each.

## What is verified, and what is not

**6 of the 16 responses are machine-checked with SymPy** — every algebraic answer:
$x = 6$ (segment addition), $x = 13$ and $m\angle DBC = 21^\circ$ (angle addition),
$x = 22$ (linear pair), $x = 20$ (converse of corresponding angles), and $x = 12$
(perpendicular biconditional). The study guide's three worked examples and three
try-it answers are verified the same way.

**10 responses are flagged for your judgement** — the classification reasoning in
problem 1, both parts of problems 3, 5, 6 and 8, and part (a) of problem 10. Writing a
converse, judging whether it is true, and explaining why a biconditional is or is not
available are all prose answers; no computer algebra system can grade them, and they are
the substance of this topic rather than an add-on. The answer key gives a model response
for each and a grading note naming the required element (for a converse: the exact swap;
for a false converse: a case meeting the new hypothesis plus the definition condition it
breaks). The Quick Answers bank marks these `---`. No `[unchecked]` marks appear.

## Notes

Standards: HSG-CO.A (definitions, biconditionals, the addition postulates) and
HSG-CO.C (vertical angles, linear pairs, parallel-line converses). Difficulty ramps 1
to 5; problem 8(b) deliberately asks the student to look back across the sheet and pick
a statement whose converse they have already disproved, and problem 10 closes by tying
the two directions of a biconditional to the two ways it gets used in a proof.
