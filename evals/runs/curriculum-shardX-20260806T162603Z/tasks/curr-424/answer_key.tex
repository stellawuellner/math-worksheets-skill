\problem{$2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin\theta$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student divided by $\sin\theta$ and got $60^\circ, 300^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} Move everything to one side and factor --- never divide by something that can be zero.
\[ 2\sin\theta\cos\theta - \sin\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin\theta\,(2\cos\theta - 1) = 0 \]
$\sin\theta = 0$ gives $\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ$; \ $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$. \ans{\theta = 0^\circ,\ 60^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 300^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say that dividing by $\sin\theta$ is legal only where $\sin\theta \ne 0$, and that the angles where it \emph{is} zero are precisely the two solutions destroyed. Full credit also states the repair: collect on one side and factor, so the sine survives as a factor. Noting only that two answers are missing is partial credit.

\problem{$4\sin^2\theta - 1 = 0$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student took one square root and got $30^\circ, 150^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} Factor as a difference of squares, which cannot lose a branch:
\[ (2\sin\theta - 1)(2\sin\theta + 1) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{2} \ \text{ or } \ \sin\theta = -\tfrac{1}{2}. \]
The positive case gives $30^\circ, 150^\circ$; the negative case gives $210^\circ, 330^\circ$. \ans{\theta = 30^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 210^\circ,\ 330^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say a square equal to a positive number has \emph{two} square roots, so $\sin\theta = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, and only the positive branch was kept. Each branch supplies two angles per turn, so four solutions exist. Recommending the difference-of-squares factorisation earns full credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$2\cos^2\theta = \cos\theta$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student divided by $\cos\theta$ and got $60^\circ, 300^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} \[ 2\cos^2\theta - \cos\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos\theta\,(2\cos\theta - 1) = 0 \]
$\cos\theta = 0$ gives $\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ$; \ $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$. \ans{\theta = 60^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 270^\circ,\ 300^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must identify the cancelled cosine and say that cancelling silently assumes $\cos\theta \ne 0$ --- an assumption the problem never grants. Factoring keeps both cases. Full credit requires naming the assumption, not just listing the two missing right angles.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$\sin\theta + 1 = \cos\theta$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student squared and reported $0^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} The squared equation does give those three candidates, but squaring is not reversible, so each must be tested in the \emph{original} equation:
\begin{align*}
  \theta = 0^\circ:\quad & 0 + 1 = 1 \ \text{ and } \ \cos 0^\circ = 1 && \checkmark \\
  \theta = 180^\circ:\quad & 0 + 1 = 1 \ \text{ but } \ \cos 180^\circ = -1 && \times \\
  \theta = 270^\circ:\quad & -1 + 1 = 0 \ \text{ and } \ \cos 270^\circ = 0 && \checkmark
\end{align*}
\ans{\theta = 0^\circ,\ 270^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say squaring can create roots the original never had, so every candidate has to be substituted back, and must carry out that test on $180^\circ$ to show the two sides disagree. Saying only that one answer is wrong, without the reason squaring produces it, is partial credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$2\sin 2\theta = 1$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student reported $15^\circ, 75^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} Put $u = 2\theta$. As $\theta$ runs over $[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$, $u$ runs over $[0^\circ, 720^\circ)$:
\[ \sin u = \tfrac{1}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u = 30^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 390^\circ,\ 510^\circ. \]
Halving each: $\theta = 15^\circ, 75^\circ, 195^\circ, 255^\circ$. \ans{\theta = 15^\circ,\ 75^\circ,\ 195^\circ,\ 255^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say the doubled angle sweeps twice as far, so its interval ends at $720^\circ$, and stopping at $360^\circ$ discards the second pass. Full credit states the repair: substitute for the inside angle, multiply both endpoints by the coefficient, solve, then divide every answer back.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{\sin\theta + \sin 2\theta}{\sin\theta}$. Student cancelled every $\sin\theta$ and wrote $3$.}

\textbf{(a)} Expand the double angle first, then factor the numerator before dividing:
\[ \frac{\sin\theta + 2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \frac{\sin\theta\,(1 + 2\cos\theta)}{\sin\theta} = 1 + 2\cos\theta \quad (\sin\theta \ne 0). \]
\ans{1 + 2\cos\theta}

\textbf{(b)} At $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the expression is $1 + 2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$. \ans{2}

\textbf{(c)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say a common factor may only be cancelled from a whole term: the second term is $2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, so removing its $\sin\theta$ leaves $2\cos\theta$, not a bare $2$. Full credit requires naming the dropped cosine factor; a correct answer shows the numerator written as $\sin\theta(1 + 2\cos\theta)$ before dividing.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$\cos 2\theta = \cos\theta$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student reported $120^\circ, 240^\circ$ and discarded $\cos\theta = 1$.}

\textbf{(a)} The factoring is correct: $(2\cos\theta + 1)(\cos\theta - 1) = 0$. Both factors matter.
\[ \cos\theta = -\tfrac{1}{2} \ \Longrightarrow\ \theta = 120^\circ,\ 240^\circ; \qquad \cos\theta = 1 \ \Longrightarrow\ \theta = 0^\circ. \]
\ans{\theta = 0^\circ,\ 120^\circ,\ 240^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The response must say that $\cos\theta = 1$ is perfectly attainable --- it happens at $0^\circ$, which the half-open interval includes --- so that factor contributes a solution. Full credit notes that $360^\circ$ is excluded, which is why the angle appears once rather than twice. Supplying the missing angle without saying why the student wrongly rejected it is partial credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$2\sin\theta\cos 2\theta = \sin\theta$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Student cancelled $\sin\theta$, used $2\theta \in [0^\circ, 360^\circ)$, and reported $30^\circ, 150^\circ$.}

\textbf{(a)} Factor rather than cancel, then widen the interval for the doubled angle:
\[ \sin\theta\,(2\cos 2\theta - 1) = 0 \]
$\sin\theta = 0$ gives $\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ$. For the other factor, $u = 2\theta$ runs over $[0^\circ, 720^\circ)$ and $\cos u = \dfrac{1}{2}$ at $u = 60^\circ, 300^\circ, 420^\circ, 660^\circ$, so $\theta = 30^\circ, 150^\circ, 210^\circ, 330^\circ$. \ans{\theta = 0^\circ,\ 30^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 210^\circ,\ 330^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} Two independent faults must be named, with the losses attributed. Dividing by $\sin\theta$ cost $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Solving the doubled angle over the un-widened interval cost $210^\circ$ and $330^\circ$. Naming one fault only is half credit; a correct response says which missing solutions came from which fault.
