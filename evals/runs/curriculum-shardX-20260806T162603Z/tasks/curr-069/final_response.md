# Multiplication: Rows, Arrays, and the Total (Grades 2–3)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_multarrays_curr069.pdf`) — 8 problems on the meaning of multiplication, all aimed at one specific mix-up: confusing the *rows* (or the row-plus-group sum) with the *total*. Problems 1–3 and 6 are straight practice with arrays and equal-group stories; problems 4, 5, 7, and 8 are find-and-fix items where a student (Maya, Leo, Kai, Rosa) has added the two numbers — e.g. wrote 4 + 8 = 12 for a 4-by-8 sticker array — and your child must say what went wrong and compute the real total.
- **Answer key** (`ak_multarrays_curr069.pdf`) — full reasoning for every problem, a quick-answers bank for fast grading, a Curriculum section (standards 3.OA.A.1/3/4, difficulty ramp 1–4), and a "Common wrong answers" note per trap so a wrong answer like 12 immediately tells you *which* mistake was made.
- **Study guide** (`ss_multarrays_curr069.pdf`) — 2 pages, three skills (array totals, equal-group stories, fixing the rows-versus-total mix-up), each with a rule box, a short worked example, and an upside-down-answer try-it.

**What is verified:** every numeric answer — 8 of the 12 graded responses — was machine-checked with SymPy (the correction half of each find-and-fix item included), and all 7 planted wrong results are declared as misconception traps that the checker proved are distinguishably wrong (e.g. 4 + 8 = 12 can never be accepted where 32 is required). The 4 explanation parts — "what did Maya/Leo/Kai/Rosa do wrong?" — are genuinely open responses, so they are flagged for instructor judgment; the answer key marks them with `---` in the quick-answers bank and states what a correct explanation must contain. The key's "What is verified" note reflects exactly this split.
