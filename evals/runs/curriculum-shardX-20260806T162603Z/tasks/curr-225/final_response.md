Here are the three PDFs for a Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra interleaved review set on
**linear representations, focused on matching contexts, tables, equations and
graphs to one another**.

**Skills Summary (study guide)** — `ss_matchrep_curr225.pdf`, 2 pages.
Four sections, one per direction of translation: table to equation, context to
equation, graph to equation (with a small worked grid), and deciding whether
two forms actually match. Each has a rule box, a two-step worked example whose
first step says *why* that method applies, and a try-it with the answer printed
upside down inside the box. The fourth section states the rule the sheet turns
on: two representations match only when *both* numbers agree, because two
different lines can share one value.

**Worksheet** — `ws_matchrep_curr225.pdf`, 7 pages, 12 problems.
A shared **Graph Bank** at the top carries three labelled lines with two marked
points each; its caption says that only the problems naming a line by letter
use it, so no figure can be misread as belonging to a neighbouring problem.
Two warm-ups pull an equation out of a table and out of a story; after that the
four translation directions rotate so no method repeats more than twice in a
row. Four problems put two or three representations side by side and ask
whether they describe the same line — one of them deliberately does *not*
match, and the constants agree while the slopes do not, which is exactly the
case a single-value check would pass. The closing problem asks for a value at
$x = 20$, beyond both the table and the drawn grid, so only the equation can
answer it.

**Answer key** — `ak_matchrep_curr225.pdf`, 3 pages.
Quick Answers bank labelled by response, then a worked solution per problem
that shows which number became the slope and which became the intercept.
Curriculum block: 8.EE.B.6 and 8.F.B, difficulty 1–4.

**What is verified, honestly.** Twenty-six checks were declared across the
twelve problems, and **twenty-two are machine-checked with SymPy**: every slope
from points or from a table, every table reading, every substitution into an
equation, and the one solve-for-the-input problem. Worksheet prose numbers
match the verification data 68 out of 68, so every number a student reads on
the sheet is a number the checker used.

**Four responses are instructor-judged, not machine-checked**: the "explain how
you decided" part of problems 4, 8, 11 and 12. That matches the key's own
generated note — 8 of 12 problems fully machine-checked, with those four each
carrying one instructor-judged part marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank. The
worked solution for each states what a correct justification must contain; in
particular, the key is explicit that agreeing on a single value is not
sufficient evidence and should not earn full credit.
