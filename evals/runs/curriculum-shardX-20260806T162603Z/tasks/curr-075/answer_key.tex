\problem{Two friends share 10 pretzels equally.}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing 10 into 2 equal groups is the equation $10 \div 2 = 5$. Each friend gets 5 pretzels, because $2 \times 5 = 10$. \ans{5}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{18 flowers, 6 flowers in each vase.}

\textbf{Solution:} Making groups of 6 from 18 is $18 \div 6 = 3$. The florist fills 3 vases, because $6 \times 3 = 18$. \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution:} Making groups of 2 from 16 is $16 \div 2 = 8$. Sam makes 8 pairs, because $2 \times 8 = 16$. \ans{8}

\problem{Drawing: 15 dots in 3 equal groups.}

\textbf{Solution:} The drawing splits 15 into 3 equal groups, which is $15 \div 3 = 5$. Each group holds 5 dots --- count one group to check. \ans{5}

\problem{A division story for $12 \div 4$, then solve.}

\textbf{Solution:} Any story with 12 things and 4 groups works, e.g. ``12 muffins are shared equally onto 4 plates. How many muffins on each plate?'' (Groups of 4 also work: ``12 muffins packed 4 to a box --- how many boxes?'') The quotient: $12 \div 4 = 3$, because $4 \times 3 = 12$. \ans{3}

\problem{Challenge: Maya shares 24 berries among 4 bowls; the drawing shows 4 equal groups.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The matching equation is $24 \div 4$ --- 24 berries split into 4 equal bowls, exactly what the drawing shows. $4 \div 24$ is backwards (it would split 4 things among 24 bowls), and $24 - 4$ takes 4 berries away instead of sharing them all.

\textbf{Solution (b):} $24 \div 4 = 6$ berries in each bowl, because $4 \times 6 = 24$. Subtracting instead ($24 - 4 = 20$) does not share anything. \ans{6}
