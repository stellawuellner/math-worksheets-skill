# Polygon Interior and Exterior Angle Sums — 10 problems

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ws_polygonangles_curr332.pdf`** — the student worksheet (4 pages, plenty of
  writing room under each problem).

- **`ss_polygonangles_curr332.pdf`** — a 2-page skills summary to keep beside the
  worksheet.

## What the worksheet covers

Ten problems on the two polygon angle rules, ramped from recall to synthesis and
mixed so your student has to *choose* a rule rather than repeat one:

1–2. interior angle sum from the number of sides, $(n-2)\cdot 180^\circ$
3. each exterior angle of a regular 9-gon
4, 6. one missing interior angle in a quadrilateral and in a pentagon
5. number of sides from a given exterior angle
7. interior angle → exterior angle → number of sides (two parts)
8. number of sides from a given interior angle *sum*
9. a find-and-fix item: Marcus uses $5 \cdot 180^\circ$ for a pentagon
10. the challenge — each interior angle is 5 times each exterior angle

No two problems share a skeleton, and after the first two warm-ups no method
repeats more than twice in a row.

## What is verified, and what is not

The sheet asks for **13 separate responses** across those 10 problems.

- **12 are machine-checked.** Every numeric answer was recomputed independently
  with SymPy before the PDFs were built, and the answer key's printed boxes were
  then bound back to those same values, so a typo in the key cannot survive.
- **1 is instructor-judged: problem 9(b)**, the written explanation of Marcus's
  error. No computer can grade a sentence, so the key prints `---` for it in the
  Quick Answers bank and gives you a three-level rubric (full / half / no credit)
  next to a model answer. The key's own "What is verified" note says the same
  thing — 12 of 13.

There are no `[unchecked]` marks: every blank the student is asked to fill either
has a machine-checked value or a written rubric.

## Extra grading help

Problem 9 has a declared misconception trap, so the key also prints a **Common
wrong answers** line: if the student writes 900, they multiplied the number of
sides by 180 without subtracting 2 first. That tells you what to reteach rather
than just what to mark wrong.

## The study guide

Four sections, each with a rule box, a worked example whose first step says *why*
that rule applies, and a try-it problem with the answer printed upside down in
the box. It closes with a watch-out box on the two mistakes this topic produces
most: dividing (or not dividing) by $n$, and thinking the $360^\circ$ exterior
total changes with the number of sides.
