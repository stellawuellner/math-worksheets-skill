\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{50}$ to the nearest hundredth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A root is placed by trapping it between perfect squares, so
find the two that surround $50$: $49=7^2$ and $64=8^2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} So $7<\sqrt{50}<8$, and since $50$ is barely above $49$ the
value must sit just above $7$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Refining, $7.07^2=49.98$ and $7.08^2=50.13$, so to the nearest
hundredth $\sqrt{50}=\ans{7.07}$.

\textit{Teaching note:} the trapping step is what makes the calculator value
checkable. A student who writes $25$ here has not been stopped by anything.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{9}$ against $2.9$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Not every square root is irrational --- check for a perfect
square before reaching for a decimal approximation.

\textbf{Step 2.} $9$ is a perfect square, so $\sqrt{9}=3$ exactly, and $3$
against $2.9$ is an ordinary decimal comparison.

\textbf{Step 3.} Three is greater, so $\sqrt{9}\ \ans{>}\ 2.9$.

\textit{Listen for:} ``a square root is always irrational.'' It is irrational
only when the radicand is not a perfect square.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $3.5$, $\sqrt{10}$, $\dfrac{10}{3}$ from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The three are written in three different notations, so nothing
can be ordered until they share one --- convert all to decimals.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\sqrt{10}\approx 3.162$ (since $3.16^2=9.99$),
$\dfrac{10}{3}=3.333\ldots$, and $3.5$ is already a decimal.

\textbf{Step 3.} Reading the decimals in order gives
$\ans{\sqrt{10} < \tfrac{10}{3} < 3.5}$.

\textit{Note:} the answer is written with the original symbols, not the
decimals --- the decimals were the tool, not the answer.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{20}$ to the nearest hundredth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Trap it: $16=4^2$ and $25=5^2$ surround $20$, so
$4<\sqrt{20}<5$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $20$ sits near the middle of that gap, so try $4.5$:
$4.5^2=20.25$, slightly too big. Try $4.47$: $4.47^2=19.98$.

\textbf{Step 3.} To the nearest hundredth, $\sqrt{20}=\ans{4.47}$, lying
between $4$ and $5$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $10$ --- the radicand was halved instead of
square-rooted. Halving and square-rooting agree only at $4$.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{2}$ against $1.5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both values are positive, and squaring preserves order for
positives, so squaring removes the radical without any estimation.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(\sqrt{2})^2=2$ and $1.5^2=2.25$. Since $2<2.25$, the
original order is the same.

\textbf{Step 3.} So $\sqrt{2}\ \ans{<}\ 1.5$.

\textit{Check:} $\sqrt2\approx1.414$, which is indeed below $1.5$.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $\sqrt{30}$, $5.5$, $\dfrac{27}{5}$ from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Convert to decimals, and keep three places --- these three
values differ only in the first two.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{27}{5}=5.4$; $\sqrt{30}\approx 5.477$ (since
$5.48^2=30.03$); and $5.5$ is given.

\textbf{Step 3.} In order: $\ans{\tfrac{27}{5} < \sqrt{30} < 5.5}$.

\textit{Teaching note:} the irrational value lands between the two rationals.
That is the shape of most of these problems.}

\problem{\textbf{$\pi$ against $\dfrac{22}{7}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\dfrac{22}{7}$ is a rational \emph{approximation} of $\pi$,
never equal to it, so the comparison turns on decimal places past the third.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\pi=3.14159\ldots$ and $\dfrac{22}{7}=3.142857\ldots$. They
agree to two places and separate at the third.

\textbf{Step 3.} So $\pi\ \ans{<}\ \dfrac{22}{7}$.

\textit{Listen for:} ``they are equal.'' $\pi$ is irrational, so no fraction of
whole numbers can equal it --- an equals sign here is always wrong.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{85}$ to the nearest hundredth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Trap it: $81=9^2$ and $100=10^2$, so $9<\sqrt{85}<10$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $85$ is only $4$ above $81$ but $15$ below $100$, so the value
is much nearer $9$ than $10$ --- not half-way, because squaring stretches the
gaps unevenly.

\textbf{Step 3.} $9.22^2=85.01$, so $\sqrt{85}=\ans{9.22}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $8.5$ --- the decimal point was moved one place
instead of taking a root.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $\sqrt{45}$, $6.7$, $\dfrac{20}{3}$ from greatest to
least.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The direction is reversed here, so decide the decimal order
first and reverse it once at the end rather than trying to think backwards
throughout.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{20}{3}=6.667$; $\sqrt{45}\approx 6.708$ (since
$6.71^2=45.02$); $6.7$ is given. Least to greatest that is
$6.667$, $6.7$, $6.708$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Reversing, greatest to least is
$\ans{\sqrt{45} > 6.7 > \tfrac{20}{3}}$.

\textit{Common error:} answering in the wrong direction. Two decimal places
would also have made $6.7$ and $6.708$ look equal.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{18}$ against $\dfrac{17}{4}$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Dividing is the conversion step: $17\div 4$, so
$\dfrac{17}{4}=\ans{4.25}$ exactly.

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} Now estimate the root: $16<18<25$, so $\sqrt{18}$ is just
above $4$. More precisely $4.24^2=17.98$ and $4.25^2=18.0625$, so
$\sqrt{18}\approx 4.243$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Since $4.243<4.25$, the answer is
$\sqrt{18}\ \ans{<}\ \dfrac{17}{4}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $17.4$ for part (a) --- the fraction bar was read
as a decimal point. Also note $4.25^2=18.0625$ is above $18$, which by itself
settles the comparison without any decimals.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $\sqrt{2}$, $1.42$, $\dfrac{7}{5}$, $1.41$ from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Step 1.} All four values live inside a range of about $0.02$, so two
decimal places are not enough to separate them --- carry at least four.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{7}{5}=1.4000$; $1.41$ is $1.4100$; $\sqrt2=1.4142$;
$1.42$ is $1.4200$.

\textbf{Step 3.} In order:
$\ans{\tfrac{7}{5} < 1.41 < \sqrt{2} < 1.42}$.

\textit{Teaching note:} rounding $\sqrt2$ to $1.41$ would have made two of
these equal. How many places you carry is part of the method, not a detail.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: order $-\sqrt{5}$, $-2.3$, $-\dfrac{9}{4}$,
$\sqrt{6}$, $2.4$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Split by sign first: three negatives and two positives, and
every negative is less than every positive, so the two groups can be ordered
separately.

\textbf{Step 2.} Negatives as decimals: $-2.3$, $-\dfrac{9}{4}=-2.25$,
$-\sqrt5\approx-2.236$. Further from zero means smaller, so the order runs
$-2.3$, then $-2.25$, then $-2.236$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Positives: $2.4$ and $\sqrt6\approx 2.449$ (since
$2.45^2=6.0025$), so $2.4$ comes first.

\textbf{Step 4.} Putting the two groups together:
$\ans{-2.3 < -\tfrac{9}{4} < -\sqrt{5} < 2.4 < \sqrt{6}}$.

\textit{Listen for:} $-2.3$ placed last among the negatives because ``$2.3$ is
the smallest number''. On the negative side the order reverses, and this is the
one problem on the sheet that tests it.}
