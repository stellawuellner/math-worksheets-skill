\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$: \quad 372 vs.\ 327}

\textbf{Solution:} Hundreds tie (3 and 3), so compare tens: 7 tens beat
2 tens. \ans{372 > 327}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$: \quad 458 vs.\ 485}

\textbf{Solution:} Hundreds tie (4 and 4); tens decide: 5 tens are fewer
than 8 tens. \ans{458 < 485}

\problem{Compare the expanded forms $300 + 40 + 2$ and $300 + 20 + 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The hundreds parts match (300 and 300). The tens parts
decide: 40 is greater than 20, and the ones cannot overturn a tens
difference. \ans{300 + 40 + 2 > 300 + 20 + 4}

\problem{Maya read 246 pages; Leo read 264 pages. Compare, and who read more?}

\textbf{Solution:} Hundreds tie (2 and 2); compare tens: 4 tens versus
6 tens, so \ans{246 < 264}

The greater count, 264, belongs to Leo, so \emph{Leo} read more pages.

\problem{Order least to greatest: 512, 251, 521}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare hundreds first: 251 has only 2 hundreds, so it is
least. 512 and 521 tie at 5 hundreds; tens decide: 1 ten beats nothing ---
512 has 1 ten, 521 has 2 tens, so 512 comes before 521.
\ans{251,\ 512,\ 521}

\problem{Green Hill 730 m, Stone Hill 703 m: compare the heights.}

\textbf{Solution:} Hundreds tie (7 and 7). Tens decide: 730 has 3 tens while
703 has 0 tens --- the zero holds the tens place. So Green Hill is taller:
\ans{730 > 703}

\problem{Greatest three-digit number from the digits 2, 5, 8 (used once each).}

\textbf{Solution:} The biggest digit must take the biggest place: 8 in the
hundreds, then 5 in the tens, then 2 in the ones: $800 + 50 + 2$.
\ans{852}

\problem{Order the can counts 318, 381, 313 from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} All three have 3 hundreds, so compare tens: 313 and 318
have 1 ten; 381 has 8 tens, so 381 is greatest. Between 313 and 318 the ones
decide: 3 ones are fewer than 8 ones. \ans{313,\ 318,\ 381}

\problem{Sam says 476 is greater than 512 because 7 tens beat 1 ten. True comparison, and why is he wrong?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare hundreds \emph{before} tens: 476 has 4 hundreds,
512 has 5 hundreds, so \ans{476 < 512}

\emph{What a good explanation contains:} the hundreds place is compared
first, and 5 hundreds beat 4 hundreds no matter what the tens digits are;
tens only matter when the hundreds tie.

\problem{Challenge: order 907, 790, 970, 709 from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare hundreds: 709 and 790 have 7 hundreds; 907 and
970 have 9 hundreds, so the 7-hundreds pair comes first. Within each pair the
tens decide: 709 (0 tens) before 790 (9 tens), and 907 (0 tens) before 970
(7 tens). \ans{709,\ 790,\ 907,\ 970}
