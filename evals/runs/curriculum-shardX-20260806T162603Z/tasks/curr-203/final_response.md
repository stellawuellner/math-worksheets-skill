# Approximating Irrational Roots on a Number Line — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_estimate_curr203.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) bracketing a root between the two whole numbers
  whose squares straddle the radicand, (2) squeezing that bracket to a decimal by squaring
  candidates, (3) using the estimate in a real measurement, including when to round *up*
  rather than to the nearest. A watch-out box names the single most common wrong move on
  this topic — halving the number under the radical.
- **`ws_estimate_curr203.pdf` — Worksheet (4 pages, 10 problems).** Bracketing, decimal
  squeezing, and measurement applications are interleaved, so your child has to decide each
  time whether the answer wanted is a pair of whole numbers, a rounded decimal, or a
  practical size. Three problems are applications with stated units (a rug, a vegetable
  bed, a tarp); one uses a number line to place the value. The last problem is a
  find-the-error challenge.
- **`ak_estimate_curr203.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with the
  full squeeze shown — the squared candidates, not just the answer — plus a Quick Answers
  strip, a Curriculum block (standard 8.NS.A), and a "Common wrong answers" note.

## What is verified, honestly

- **8 of the 10 problems are fully machine-checked.** Every bracket and every rounded
  decimal was recomputed independently with SymPy before the PDFs were allowed to compile,
  and each rounded answer had to agree at the precision it is printed with.
- **Two problems are part machine-checked and part instructor-judged.** In problem 7 the
  decimal estimate is checked but the *dot marked on the number line* is not — that is a
  drawn response. In problem 10 the estimate is checked but the written explanation of
  Ravi's error is not. Both print as `---` in the Quick Answers strip, and the answer key
  gives you the grading rubric for each: where the dot must sit, and what the explanation
  must name.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same
  way (8 checks, all machine-checked).
- One misconception trap was declared and proved distinguishable: halving the radicand
  instead of rooting it. The wrong value it produces, 25, is the number planted in Ravi's
  work in problem 10.

No calculator is needed. Every estimate on the sheet is reachable by squaring candidate
decimals, and the answer key shows those squares so you can follow the reasoning rather
than just check a number.
