Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 6 statistics concept practice.

- **Worksheet** (`ws_statq_spread_curr196.pdf`) — 10 problems built around real
  data tables the student reads directly, alternating between the two halves of
  the focus. **Is it a statistical question?** — two piano-club questions to tell
  apart, a data set to invent a question for, and a family-ages question that
  looks statistical and is not. **How spread out is the data?** — range of
  reading times, lowest/highest/range of six temperatures, quartiles and the
  interquartile range of twelve plant heights, and a week of library counts.
  Two closing items put centre and spread together: two teams with the *same
  mean* and very different ranges, and nine clinic waits where one 45-minute
  wait drags the mean away from the median.
- **Answer key** (`ak_statq_spread_curr196.pdf`) — Quick Answers bank, a
  generated Curriculum section (6.SP.A and 6.SP.B.5, difficulty 1–5), and worked
  solutions that show the ordering, the halves, and the subtraction rather than
  just the number. The six instructor-judged responses each carry a rubric
  saying what a correct answer must contain.
- **Study guide** (`ss_statq_spread_curr196.pdf`) — three sections: telling a
  statistical question apart (with the "could two honest answers differ?" test),
  measuring spread with range and IQR, and describing a set with centre *and*
  spread. Each has a worked example and a try-it with the answer upside down
  inside the box; the watch-out is the two-teams case, where equal means hide
  unequal data.

**What is verified.** The sheet asks for 25 responses. **19 are machine-checked**
with SymPy — every range, median, mean, quartile, interquartile range, minimum,
maximum, total and busiest-day lookup, each recomputed from the same data array
that is printed in the problem's table, so the table and the answer cannot
disagree. **6 are instructor-judged**, and they are the ones that have to be:
choosing which question is statistical and saying why (problems 1 and 7),
writing a statistical question of one's own (3a), describing a data set in a
sentence (5c), saying which team is more consistent and why (8c), and choosing
between mean and median for a typical wait (10d). The key's note reads "4 of 10
problems fully machine-checked" — that counts *problems*, and six of them mix a
computed answer with a written one. All nine study-guide checks are
machine-verified.
