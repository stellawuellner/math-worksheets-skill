\wstitleblock{Interpreting the Solution of a Linear System on a Graph}{Algebra 1}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Reading the solution point off a graph, graphing a
system to find its solution, and classifying a system as one solution, no
solution, or infinitely many}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The big idea, and how to use it.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item A point lies on a line exactly when its coordinates make that line's
        equation true. A point on \emph{both} lines makes \emph{both} equations
        true --- that is what ``solution of the system'' means.
  \item To read a solution off a graph, find where the lines cross and write the
        crossing as an ordered pair $(x, y)$ --- across first, then up. Then
        \textbf{check it in both equations}: a graph read by eye can be a square off.
  \item To solve by graphing, put each equation in slope-intercept form, plot the
        $y$-intercept, step off the slope to a second point, draw the line, and read
        the crossing.
  \item Two lines can cross once (one solution), never (parallel: no solution), or
        everywhere (the same line: infinitely many). Equal slopes with different
        intercepts means parallel; equal slopes \emph{and} equal intercepts means one
        line drawn twice.
\end{itemize}
