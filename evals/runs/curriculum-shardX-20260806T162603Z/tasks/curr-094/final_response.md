# Area or Perimeter? Fix the Mix-Up (Grade 3) — what you received

Three PDFs:

- **Student worksheet** (`ws_areaperim_curr094.pdf`) — 8 problems on
  3.MD.C.7 / 3.MD.D.8 aimed at the single most common measurement mix-up:
  using perimeter arithmetic for area or vice versa. Every rectangle is drawn
  to scale with its labeled dimensions. Two find-and-fix items (Mateo's
  "area by adding sides" and Jaya's "fence by multiplying sides") plant the
  canonical wrong answers; a third problem (the ribbon) is built so the
  area-instead-of-perimeter error lands visibly off. The challenge problem
  computes both measures for one rectangle and compares them.
- **Step-by-step answer key** (`ak_areaperim_curr094.pdf`) — worked reasoning
  for every part, a quick-answers bank, "Common wrong answers" notes generated
  from the three declared traps (so you can diagnose 28, 72, or 36 at a
  glance), and model answers for the explanation parts.
- **Study guide** (`ss_areaperim_curr094.pdf`) — three sections: find area,
  find perimeter, and choose which one a story needs, each with a rule box, a
  worked example, a try-it with the answer upside down, plus a watch-out box
  about units.

**What is verified:** the worksheet's 13 responses split 10 machine-checked and
3 instructor-judged. SymPy recomputed every area, perimeter, and the
comparison from the printed dimensions; the three planted wrong methods were
machine-checked to give answers the correct check rejects (so each trap is
genuinely distinguishable); and the unit gate confirmed every answer line and
boxed answer carries its declared unit (cm^2 vs cm and so on). The
instructor-judged items are the circled word in problem 3(a) and the written
explanations in problems 4(b) and 7(b) — the key prints a rubric for each,
matching its own "What is verified" note. All 6 study-guide examples
and try-its are machine-verified.
