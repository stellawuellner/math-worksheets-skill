\problem{Sport and walking to school: how many play basketball and do not walk?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Find the Basketball row, then run across to the ``Does not walk'' column.
The cell where they meet counts the students in both categories at once. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Same survey: how many students walk to school altogether?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The ``Walks'' column holds $18$ footballers and $10$ basketball players.
Adding down the column gives the column total: $18 + 10$. It matches the total already printed in
the bottom row, which is a useful check. \ans{28}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Cats and dogs: what fraction of the under-30 group prefers cats?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The question is about the under-30 group only, so the denominator is that
row's total, $80$, not the grand total of $140$. The numerator is the cell where the row meets the
cats column: $\dfrac{35}{80}$. Dividing both parts by $5$ simplifies it. \ans{\tfrac{7}{16}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Cats and dogs: compare the two groups' dog-preferring fractions.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Each fraction uses its own row total.
\[ \text{under 30}: \ \frac{45}{80} = \frac{9}{16} = 0.5625, \qquad
   \text{30 and over}: \ \frac{35}{60} = \frac{7}{12} = 0.5833\ldots \]
The second is larger, even though the first came from more people --- which is exactly why the
raw counts $45$ and $35$ cannot be compared directly.
\[ \boxed{\tfrac{9}{16} \quad \text{and} \quad \tfrac{7}{12}, \quad \text{so} \ \tfrac{9}{16} < \tfrac{7}{12}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Homework club: what percent of the club attenders passed?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The attenders row totals $50$ and $42$ of them passed, so the row
percentage is $\dfrac{42}{50} \times 100 = 0.84 \times 100$. \ans{84}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Homework club: the non-attenders' pass percentage, and whether there is an association.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad $\dfrac{30}{50} \times 100$. \ans{60}

\textbf{(b) Description (instructor-judged).} A correct response compares the two row percentages:
$84$ percent of the club attenders passed against $60$ percent of the non-attenders, a gap of $24$
percentage points, so attending the club and passing do appear to be associated in this school.
Comparing the raw counts $42$ and $30$ without noticing that each row holds $50$ students earns
partial credit only --- here the rows happen to be equal, but that is not something to assume.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Travel survey: which way of travelling is used by the most students overall?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The bottom row holds the column totals: $42$ by bus, $54$ walking, $24$
cycling. The largest is walking. \ans{\text{walk}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Travel survey: compare the cycling fractions of the two year groups.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Each year group has a row total of $60$.
\[ \text{Year 8}: \ \frac{18}{60} = \frac{3}{10}, \qquad \text{Year 7}: \ \frac{6}{60} = \frac{1}{10} \]
Year 8 cycles three times as often as Year 7.
\[ \boxed{\tfrac{3}{10} \quad \text{and} \quad \tfrac{1}{10}, \quad \text{so} \ \tfrac{3}{10} > \tfrac{1}{10}} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{School lunch: what percent of all students surveyed liked it?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad ``All students'' means the grand total, $120$, and $69$ of them liked it:
$\dfrac{69}{120} \times 100 = 0.575 \times 100$. \ans{57.5}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{School lunch: the two year groups' percentages, and whether opinion is associated with
year group.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Each year group has $60$ students, so divide by $60$ and multiply by
$100$: $\dfrac{45}{60} \times 100$ and $\dfrac{24}{60} \times 100$.
\[ \boxed{\text{Year 7} = 75 \qquad \text{Year 9} = 40} \]

\textbf{(c) Judgement (instructor-judged).} A correct response uses the two percentages: $75$
percent of Year 7 liked the lunch against $40$ percent of Year 9, a gap far too large to dismiss,
so year group and opinion do appear associated in this survey. A full answer also explains what
the percentages add: they express each group's opinion as a share of its own row, which is what
makes two groups comparable even when the rows hold different numbers of students. A response
built on the raw counts alone earns partial credit.
