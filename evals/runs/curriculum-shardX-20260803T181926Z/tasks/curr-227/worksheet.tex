\wstitleblock{Solving Systems of Two Linear Equations by Graphing}{Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra}{}

\noindent\small
Graphing two lines from slope-intercept form, graphing from standard form,
recognizing parallel and identical lines, and reading a real-world intersection.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Graph \emph{both} lines on the same grid. The solution of the system is
        the point where they cross, written as an ordered pair $(x, y)$.
  \item From $y = mx + b$: plot the $y$-intercept $b$ first, then use the slope
        $m$ as rise over run to step to a second point.
  \item From standard form $Ax + By = C$: the two intercepts are quickest. Set
        $x = 0$ to find one, then $y = 0$ to find the other.
  \item Always check your point by substituting it back into \emph{both}
        equations.
  \item Two lines that never cross have \emph{no solution}; two lines that lie
        on top of each other have \emph{infinitely many}.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\stdgrid}

\problem[2.5cm]{Two clubs charge a joining fee plus a monthly fee. Club A costs

its total is $y = 10x + 10$. Graph both totals and write the ordered pair where
the clubs cost the same.

\problem[2.5cm]{Two bean plants are measured each week. Plant A is $6$ cm tall
and grows $4$ cm a week, so its height after $x$ weeks is $y = 4x + 6$. Plant B
is $2$ cm tall and grows $6$ cm a week, so its height is $y = 6x + 2$. Graph
both heights and write the ordered pair where the plants are the same height.
\wsgrid{xmin=0,xmax=6,ymin=0,ymax=24,xtick={1,2,3,4,5,6},
  minor ytick={2,6,10,14,18,22}, ytick={4,8,12,16,20,24}, xlabel={weeks}, ylabel={cm}}}

\problem[2.5cm]{Rearrange each equation into slope-intercept form first, then

\[ 3x - y = 5 \qquad\text{and}\qquad x + y = 7 \]
\stdgrid}

\problem[2.5cm]{Graph both lines. Then answer: how many ordered pairs solve this

\[ 2x + 2y = 8 \qquad\text{and}\qquad y = -x + 4 \]
\stdgrid}

\problem[2.6cm]{Two candles are lit at the same time. The tall candle is $20$ cm

$y = -x + 12$. Graph both heights, write the ordered pair where the candles are
the same height, and state which candle is taller before that time.
