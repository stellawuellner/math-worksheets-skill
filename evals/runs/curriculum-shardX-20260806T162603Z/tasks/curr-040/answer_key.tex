\problem{$9 + 4 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} Make ten: 9 and 1 is 10, and 3 more is 13. \ans{13}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$6 + 6 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} A double: 6 and 6 is 12. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$7 + 8 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} Near double: 7 and 7 is 14, one more is 15. \ans{15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$14 - 6 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} Take away through ten: $14 - 4 = 10$, then two more,
$10 - 2 = 8$. \ans{8}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$5 + \_\_ = 12$}

\textbf{Solution:} A missing part --- count on from 5 to 12: 6, 7, 8, 9, 10,
11, 12 is seven jumps. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ken's tower: 8 red blocks and 7 blue blocks. How many blocks?}

\textbf{Solution:} Both parts are known, so add: 8 and 2 make 10, and the
leftover 5 makes 15 blocks. \ans{15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$17 - 9 = \_\_$}

\textbf{Solution:} Count up from 9: 9 to 10 is 1, 10 to 17 is 7, so 8 in all.
\ans{8}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{$\_\_ - 4 = 9$}

\textbf{Solution:} The whole is missing, not a part! Put the parts back
together: $9 + 4 = 13$. Check: $13 - 4 = 9$. \ans{13}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{16 apples, the kids ate 7. How many left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Taking away, so subtract: $16 - 7$. Count up from 7 to 16:
that is 9 apples left. \ans{9}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: $8 + \_\_ = 15$ and $15 - \_\_ = 8$. Tell how you know.}

\textbf{Solution:} Find the missing part first: count on from 8 to 15, which
is 7 jumps. The same 7 fits the subtraction, because both equations use the
same fact family. \ans{7}

\emph{Tell how you know (instructor judges):} accept any wording that
connects the two facts --- for example, ``8 and 7 make 15, so taking the 7
back out of 15 leaves 8. It is the same three numbers both times.''
