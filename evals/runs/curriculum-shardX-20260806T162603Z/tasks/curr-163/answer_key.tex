\problem{Book at 24 dollars, sales tax $5\%$. Find the tax and the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The tax is a percent of the marked price:
$\dfrac{5}{100} \times 24 = 1.20$ dollars. \ans{1.20}

(b) Adding the tax on multiplies the price by $1.05$:
$24 \times 1.05 = 25.20$ dollars, which is also $24 + 1.20$. \ans{25.20}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Meal at 40 dollars, $18\%$ tip. Find the tip and the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $\dfrac{18}{100} \times 40 = 7.20$ dollars. \ans{7.20}

(b) $40 \times 1.18 = 47.20$ dollars. \ans{47.20}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Jacket at 60 dollars, $25\%$ off. Find the discount and the sale
price.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $\dfrac{25}{100} \times 60 = 15$ dollars off. \ans{15}

(b) Taking $25\%$ off leaves $75\%$, so $60 \times 0.75 = 45$ dollars, which is
also $60 - 15$. \ans{45}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Repair billed at 132 dollars, tax $6.5\%$. Find the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} A $6.5\%$ tax multiplies the bill by $1.065$:
$132 \times 1.065 = 140.58$ dollars. The tax alone is $8.58$
dollars. \ans{140.58}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Lamp cost 35 dollars, marked up $40\%$. Find the markup and the selling
price.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The markup is a percent of what the shop \emph{paid}:
$\dfrac{40}{100} \times 35 = 14$ dollars. \ans{14}

(b) $35 \times 1.40 = 49$ dollars, which is also $35 + 14$. \ans{49}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Game was 48 dollars, now 36 dollars. What percent was taken off?}

\textbf{Solution:} The amount taken off is $48 - 36 = 12$ dollars, and a
discount percent is always measured against the \emph{original} price:
$\dfrac{12}{48} \times 100 = 25$ percent. \ans{25}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} measuring the 12 dollars against the sale price
gives $\dfrac{12}{36} \times 100 = 33.3$ percent.

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Bill of 86 dollars, $15\%$ tip. How much is the tip?}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{15}{100} \times 86 = 12.90$ dollars. A quick check:
$10\%$ is $8.60$ and half of that is $4.30$, and $8.60 + 4.30 = 12.90$. \ans{12.90}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Chair sells for 84 dollars after a $20\%$ markup. What did the shop
pay?}

\textbf{Solution:} The selling price is the cost multiplied by $1.20$, so the
cost is the selling price divided by $1.20$:
$84 \div 1.20 = 70$ dollars. Check: $20\%$ of 70 is 14, and $70 + 14 = 84$. \ans{70}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} taking $20\%$ off the 84 dollars gives $67.20$
dollars, and marking that up again would not return to 84.

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Coat on sale for 68 dollars after $15\%$ off. What was the original
price?}

\textbf{Solution:} A $15\%$ discount leaves $85\%$ of the original, so
$0.85\,x = 68$ and $x = 68 \div 0.85 = 80$ dollars. Check: $15\%$ of 80 is 12,
and $80 - 12 = 68$. \ans{80}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} adding $15\%$ of the sale price back on gives
$68 \times 1.15 = 78.20$ dollars, which is not the original.

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Tent marked 120 dollars, $30\%$ off, then $8\%$ tax on the sale price.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Taking $30\%$ off leaves $70\%$:
$120 \times 0.70 = 84$ dollars. \ans{84}

(b) The tax is charged on the sale price, not the marked price:
$\dfrac{8}{100} \times 84 = 6.72$ dollars. \ans{6.72}

(c) $84 + 6.72 = 90.72$ dollars, which is also
$120 \times 0.70 \times 1.08$. \ans{90.72}

(d) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} No --- the total does not change. Taking the
discount multiplies by $0.70$ and adding the tax multiplies by $1.08$, and
multiplying by two factors gives the same product in either order. Taxing first
gives $120 \times 1.08 = 129.60$ dollars, and $30\%$ off that is
$129.60 \times 0.70 = 90.72$ dollars, the same total. Full credit needs the
reason to be about multiplying by the two factors --- either named as a property
or shown by working the second order through.
