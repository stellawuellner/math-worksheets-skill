\begin{center}
\textit{Expanded-form models, the product law, and the quotient law}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The model behind both laws}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.9cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item An exponent counts repeated factors. Writing a power out in expanded form is
        always allowed, and it is what makes each law obvious.
  \item \textbf{Multiplying} puts two groups of factors end to end, so the counts add:
        $x^m \cdot x^n = x^{m+n}$.
  \item \textbf{Dividing} cancels matching factors top and bottom, so the counts subtract:
        $\dfrac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$.
  \item Leave answers as a single power of the given base, with no negative exponent in
        the final line unless a problem asks for one.
\end{itemize}
