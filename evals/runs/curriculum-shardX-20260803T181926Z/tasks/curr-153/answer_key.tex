\problem{\textbf{Punch: juice to soda is $2 : 3$, with $10$ cups of juice.}
\par
The juice tape is $2$ boxes long and covers $10$ cups, so one box is worth
$10 \div 2$:
\par
$\ans{5}$ cups per box.
\par
The soda tape is $3$ boxes long, so it is $3 \times 5$.
\par
$\ans{15\ \text{cups}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $10 + 3 = 13$ or ``add one box'' means the student added
instead of scaling. Every box on a tape diagram is worth the same amount, and
that amount has to be found first.}

\problem{\textbf{Swimming: $4$ laps in $6$ minutes.}
\par
Both lines are marked in equal steps, so each step forward on the top line is
one step forward on the bottom line: $4$ laps and $6$ minutes per step.
\par
At $8$ laps (two steps): $\ans{12}$ minutes. \quad At $12$ laps (three steps):
$\ans{18}$ minutes.
\par
For $10$ laps, scale from the first pair: one lap takes
$6 \div 4 = 1.5$ minutes, so $10$ laps take $10 \times 1.5$.
\par
$\ans{15\ \text{minutes}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $10$ laps sits between $8$ and $12$ laps, and $15$ minutes sits
between $12$ and $18$ minutes $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{A $48$ cm ribbon cut $3 : 5$ red to blue.}
\par
The whole ribbon is $3 + 5 = 8$ equal boxes, so one box is $48 \div 8$:
\par
$\ans{6}$ cm per box.
\par
Red is $3$ boxes: $3 \times 6$.
\par
$\ans{18\ \text{cm}}$
\par
Blue is $5$ boxes: $5 \times 6$.
\par
$\ans{30\ \text{cm}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $18 + 30 = 48$ cm, the whole ribbon $\checkmark$
\emph{Grading note:} dividing $48$ by $3$ or by $5$ is the classic error --- the
known total covers \emph{all} the boxes, not one colour's boxes.}

\problem{\textbf{Apples: $3$ kilograms cost $12$ dollars.}
\par
To reach the $1$ kilogram tick, divide the $3$ kilogram pair by $3$ --- both
lines by the same number:
\par
$\ans{4}$ dollars for one kilogram (the unit rate).
\par
With the unit rate known, every other amount is one multiplication:
$7 \times 4$.
\par
$\ans{28}$ dollars for $7$ kilograms.}

\problem{\textbf{Pens: Brand A $5$ for $4$ dollars, Brand B $8$ for $6$
dollars.}
\par
The brands cannot be compared at $5$ pens against $8$ pens, so move both lines
to the same place: one pen.
\par
Brand A: $4 \div 5$ gives $\ans{0.80}$ dollars per pen.
\par
Brand B: $6 \div 8$ gives $\ans{0.75}$ dollars per pen.
\par
$\ans{0.80 > 0.75}$
\par
Brand B costs less per pen, so \textbf{Brand B} is the cheaper pen.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} ``B is cheaper because $6$ is less than $8$'' compares the
wrong things --- a price is only comparable once both are for the same number
of pens.}

\problem{\textbf{Walk to ride is $4 : 7$, with $12$ more riders.}
\par
The ride tape is $7$ boxes and the walk tape is $4$ boxes, so the extra riders
are the $7 - 4 = 3$ boxes that stick out. Those $3$ boxes are the $12$ extra
students, so with $x$ students per box:
\[ 3x = 12 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 12 \div 3 \]
$\ans{x = 4}$ students per box.
\par
Walkers: $4 \times 4$.
\par
$\ans{16}$ students walk.
\par
Riders: $7 \times 4$.
\par
$\ans{28}$ students ride.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $28 - 16 = 12$ more riders $\checkmark$ (and $16 : 28$ reduces to
$4 : 7$).}

\problem{\textbf{Car: $150$ kilometres in $2$ hours.}
\par
\textbf{(a)} Each step on the line is $2$ hours and $150$ kilometres. Five
hours is $2.5$ steps, so scale the pair by $2.5$: $150 \times 2.5$. Equivalently
the unit rate is $150 \div 2 = 75$ per hour, and $5 \times 75$ gives the same
thing.
\par
$\ans{375}$ kilometres in five hours.
\par
\textbf{(b)} $300$ kilometres is exactly twice $150$ kilometres, so it takes
twice as long as $2$ hours.
\par
$\ans{4}$ hours for $300$ kilometres.}

\problem{\textbf{Printer: $24$ pages in $3$ minutes.}
\par
\textbf{(a)} Divide both lines by $3$ to land on the $1$ minute tick:
$24 \div 3$.
\par
$\ans{8}$ pages per minute --- the unit rate.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Now divide the page count by the unit rate: $60 \div 8$.
\par
$\ans{7.5\ \text{minutes}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $7.5 \times 8 = 60$ pages $\checkmark$. Half a minute is a
legitimate answer here --- the rate is steady, so the line has points between
the labelled ticks.}

\problem{\textbf{Which cookie recipe is sweeter?}
\par
Recipe X is $2$ boxes of sugar to $5$ of flour; recipe Y is $3$ to $7$. Write
each as sugar per part of flour:
\par
X: $\ans{0.4}$, since $2 \div 5 = 0.4$. \quad Y: $3 \div 7 \approx 0.43$.
\par
$\ans{\frac{2}{5} < \frac{3}{7}}$
\par
Recipe \textbf{Y} is sweeter.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} the common denominator route is just as good:
$\frac{2}{5} = \frac{14}{35}$ and $\frac{3}{7} = \frac{15}{35}$. Counting boxes
alone fails here --- Y has more sugar boxes \emph{and} more flour boxes.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: $15$ kilograms feeds $24$ people.}
\par
\textbf{(a)} The two pairs sit on the same double number line, so they form a
proportion:
\[ \frac{x}{8} = \frac{15}{24} \;\Longrightarrow\; 24x = 8 \times 15 = 120
   \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 120 \div 24 \]
$\ans{x = 5}$ kilograms feeds $8$ people.
\par
\textbf{(b)} The unit rate is the kilograms above $1$ person: $15 \div 24$.
\par
$\ans{0.625}$ kilograms per person.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $0.625 \times 8 = 5$ and $0.625 \times 24 = 15$ $\checkmark$
Both pairs really do lie on one line.}
