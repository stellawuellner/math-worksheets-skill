# Missing and Mixed-Up Numbers — Kindergarten / Grade 1

Three PDFs are ready. The worksheet and the study guide are set in large type
(14pt, extra leading and wide answer lines) so a child who is still forming
numerals has room to write.

**Study guide (`ss_counting_curr004.pdf`, 2 pages)** — three short sections, each
with a rule, a worked example and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down in
the box:

1. **Counting forward one at a time** — the next number is one more; check the
   other way too, since the missing number is one less than the number after the
   blank. The 1–20 counting row is printed for reference.
2. **Finding a number that was skipped** — count the row aloud, one finger per
   numeral, and stop at the first word that does not match. It also names the
   harder case: a number can be *misplaced* rather than missing, so nothing is
   absent and the row is still wrong.
3. **Which number is bigger** — the number you say later is the bigger one, with
   the flipped pair 12 / 21 called out by name.

**Worksheet (`ws_counting_curr004.pdf`, 3 pages)** — 6 problems, all on finding
missing or misplaced numbers in a counting sequence. Problems 1 and 2 are single
and double gaps (2 lands in the teens, where counting usually slips). Problem 3
asks which of 21 and 12 is greater. Problems 4 and 5 are the find-and-fix items:
Ben's count skips 8, and Mia's cards have 15 and 16 traded — one missing number
and one misplaced number, which are different errors and are diagnosed
differently. Problem 6 asks the child to show how they know, by drawing a number
line or counters.

**Answer key (`ak_counting_curr004.pdf`, 2 pages)** — a quick-answer bank at the
top, then a worked explanation of each item written for the adult helping, with
coaching notes: what the common wrong answer is and what to say when it appears.
For example, the key flags that a child skip-counting by twos will accept 9 as
the number after 7, and that a child matching digit shapes rather than counting
will pick 12 over 21. The grade level and the standard (K.CC.A.1–K.CC.C.7) appear
on the key only, never on the child's page.

**What was machine-verified.** All eight machine checks passed: every missing
number was recomputed as "one more than the number before" (and problem 1 also as
"one less than the number after"), the 21-vs-12 comparison, and the full
re-ordering of Mia's five cards into counting order. Two misconception traps were
declared and confirmed *distinguishable* — counting by twos from 7 gives 9, and
writing 16 straight after 14 gives 16, and in both cases the problem's own check
rejects the wrong result, which is what makes the item able to teach the error.

**Flagged for manual review.** Problem 6 is an open response — the child draws or
explains — so it is labelled manual rather than claimed as verified. The expected
answer is 19; the key describes what a good explanation looks like and what to
listen for: counting forward one at a time from a number before the gap, rather
than guessing.
