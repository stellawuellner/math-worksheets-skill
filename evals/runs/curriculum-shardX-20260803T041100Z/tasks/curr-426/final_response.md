# Solving AAS and ASA Triangles with the Law of Sines (Precalculus)

- **Worksheet** (`ws_lawsines_curr426.pdf`) — 10 problems, 6 pages. **Every problem
  carries its own to-scale triangle figure**, generated by the pipeline's renderer
  directly from the same `given` data the verifier checks, so no drawing can disagree
  with the answer key. Each figure labels only the given values and marks the requested
  quantity `?`. The sequence: 2 third-angle warm-ups, then AAS and ASA alternating so
  the student must decide the case before reaching for a formula (problems 3–6), two
  surveying applications in metres, a two-step AAS problem, and a triangulation problem
  whose part (b) asks *why* AAS/ASA determines a unique triangle when SSA may not.
  3.5–5 cm of work space per problem plus the figure.
- **Answer key** (`ak_lawsines_curr426.pdf`) — 4 pages. Every solution begins by saying
  which pairing is complete (and therefore whether the third angle is needed first),
  shows the proportion before the arithmetic, prints the intermediate sine values, and
  ends with a plausibility check (bigger angle faces longer side). Units carry into the
  boxed answers for the three metre problems. The generated quick-answer bank prints
  the three declared traps, so 7.99, 40.42 or 88.36 on a paper immediately names the
  error.
- **Study guide** (`ss_lawsines_curr426.pdf`) — 2 pages, three sections matching the
  three worksheet skills: name the case and find the third angle · AAS · ASA. Each has
  a rule box, a two-step worked example, and a distinct try-it with the answer upside
  down; the watch-out box targets the upside-down ratio, which is the dominant error.

## Verification

10 of the 11 verify entries are machine-verified with SymPy's `triangle` solver, which
solves each triangle independently from its three givens and compares the requested
part. Three declared misconception traps were confirmed distinguishable. All 6
study-guide answers are verified the same way. Three problems declare `answer_unit: m`,
and the unit is bound in both directions (worksheet `\answerline{m}` ↔ key `\text{m}`).

**One entry is flagged `manual`, correctly:** problem 10(b), the explanation of why
AAS/ASA pins down exactly one triangle while SSA can be ambiguous. That is an argument,
not a computation, so it is encoded `{"type": "manual", ...}`; the key carries a model
answer and a look-for list (angle sum forces the third angle; one side sets the scale;
sin θ = k has two supplementary solutions). Build ends at exit 2 for that reason, which
is the correct outcome.

Standard `HSG-SRT.D.10, HSG-SRT.D.11` is taken verbatim from
`references/standards-map.md` ("Law of sines/cosines"). Facet plan (`third-angle`,
`aas-side`, `asa-side`) declared, every entry tagged, subtitle bound, each facet taught
by its own study-guide example. Difficulty ramps 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4.

## Gates that failed on the first attempt

1. **`compile-ws`: 7 pages against a 6-page budget.** The page budget is computed from
   the problem set, and renderer figures are not part of that estimate — 10 figures of
   ~4 cm each is roughly 1.5 pages the budget never saw. Fixed by tightening the intro
   block and trimming work space from 4–7 cm down to 3.5–5 cm per problem (still above
   the 2.5 cm floor and appropriate for a two-line law-of-sines computation), not by
   dropping a problem.
2. **`compile-ss`: overfull hbox by 124 pt.** A long inline `\dfrac` describing the
   general side formula in words could not break. Fixed by promoting it to a displayed
   equation with an explicit line break.
3. **`layout-ws` (self-inflicted, worth reporting).** The prose checker strips
   `\vspace{4.5cm}` but not `\vspace*{4.5cm}` (its strip regex does not allow the star),
   so starred workspace inside a `\problem` stem leaks 4.5 into the prose scan — the
   worksheet report reads 0/4. Switching to the unstarred form silences the prose noise
   but immediately fails `check_layout`, which computes minipage depth from the document
   text only and cannot see the minipage inside the `\problem` macro, so anything in a
   stem reads as depth 0. The two checkers therefore pull in opposite directions for
   the one legitimate pattern of "figure, then work space, then a unit answer line"
   inside a single stem. I kept `\vspace*` (correct layout) and accepted the prose
   flags. Worth fixing in the skill: either strip `\vspace*?` in
   `check_prose_consistency._prose_stripped`, or treat `\problem` regions as
   minipage-interior in `check_layout.stranded_workspace`.
