\wstitleblock{Inequalities: When Does the Symbol Flip?}{Grades 6--7 \quad Expressions and Equations}{}

\noindent\textbf{The one rule this page is about.} \small Multiplying or dividing
\emph{both sides} of an inequality by a \emph{negative} number reverses the
inequality symbol. Adding or subtracting a negative number does \emph{not}.
Every problem below is a place where that rule is either used or forgotten.
\normalsize

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Directions.} Show each step. When you divide or multiply by a
negative, write ``flip'' beside that step so a reader can see where the symbol
turned around. For the find-and-fix problems, name the error in words before you
give the correct answer.

\problem[5cm]{The statement $6 > -2$ is true. Multiply \emph{both} sides by $-4$.

\smallskip
Left side: \quad $-4 \times 6 = -24$. \qquad
Right side: \quad $-4 \times (-2) = $ \underline{\hspace{2cm}}

\smallskip
Now compare your two results and circle the symbol that makes the new statement
true: \quad $-24$ \ \fbox{$>$} \ \fbox{$<$} \ (your answer). What did the
original ``$>$'' become?}

\problem[5.5cm]{Solve for $x$: \quad $-4x > 12$

\smallskip
Write the solution as an inequality, and state which step forced the flip.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Priya solved $-7x \le 21$ like this:
\begin{center}
$-7x \le 21 \;\longrightarrow\; \dfrac{-7x}{-7} \le \dfrac{21}{-7}
\;\longrightarrow\; x \le -3$
\end{center}
Her arithmetic is correct, but her answer is wrong. \textbf{(a)} Test $x = -10$
in the \emph{original} inequality to show her answer cannot be right.
\textbf{(b)} Name her error in one sentence. \textbf{(c)} Write the correct
solution.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Solve for $x$: \quad $-\dfrac{x}{3} < 2$

\smallskip
Dividing by $3$ and multiplying by $-1$ are two different moves --- only one of
them flips the symbol.}

\problem[5cm]{Solving $-5x - 2 \le 18$ gives the boundary value $x = -4$. Check
that boundary: substitute $x = -4$ into the expression $-5x - 2$ and evaluate it.

\smallskip
If your result equals $18$, the boundary is correct. (A student who gets $-22$
here has broken the negative-times-negative rule --- check your signs.)}

\problem[6cm]{Solve for $x$: \quad $4 - 3x > 19$

\smallskip
Undo the $+4$ first. Only then do you meet the negative coefficient.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Dev solved $8 - 2x \ge 14$ like this:
\begin{center}
$8 - 2x \ge 14 \;\longrightarrow\; -2x \ge 6
\;\longrightarrow\; x \ge -3$
\end{center}
\textbf{(a)} Which of Dev's two steps is wrong, and why is the other one fine?
\textbf{(b)} Show that $x = 0$ satisfies Dev's answer but not the original
inequality. \textbf{(c)} Write the correct solution.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Explain.} Adding $-5$ to both sides of an inequality never
changes the symbol, but multiplying both sides by $-5$ always does. Using a
number line (or a pair of specific numbers of your own) explain why those two
moves behave differently. Your explanation should say what multiplying by a
negative does to the \emph{positions} of two numbers on the line.
\noansline}
