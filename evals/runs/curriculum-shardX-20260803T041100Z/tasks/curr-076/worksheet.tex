\wstitleblock{Using Twos, Fives, and Tens as Anchor Facts}{Grades 2--3 \quad Operations and Algebraic Thinking}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Skip counting by twos, fives, and tens, doubling a two-fact, building
from a five-fact, taking one group away from a ten-fact, and using anchor facts
to divide}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The one idea behind every problem.} Some facts are easy to
count: twos, fives, and tens. Those are your \textbf{anchor facts}. Every harder
fact sits right next door to one of them, so you never have to guess --- you
start at an anchor and take one small step.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\centering
\textbf{One more group} than the five-fact \quad$\rightarrow$\quad add the group
size once
\\[3pt]
\textbf{Double} the two-fact \quad$\rightarrow$\quad the four-fact
\\[3pt]
\textbf{One group less} than the ten-fact \quad$\rightarrow$\quad the nine-fact}}

\smallskip
\small\textit{Reference card. No numbers are filled in --- use the numbers given
in each problem.}\normalsize
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Show the anchor fact you started from. That step is part of the answer
here, not something to skip.
\item You may draw dots, circles, or rows of counters in the work space ---
pictures count as work.
\item Write your final answer on the answer line.
\end{itemize}

\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}, \underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}

\problem[6.5cm]{Nia already knows her five-facts by counting nickels, so she
knows $8 \times 5 = 40$ without thinking.

\smallskip
Now she needs $8 \times 6$. Only one thing changed: there is \emph{one more
group of} 8.

\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Draw 5 groups of 8 dots, then draw the one extra group in a
different colour or circle it.

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Start at the anchor fact 40 and take that one step. What is
$8 \times 6$?}

\problem[6.5cm]{Ravi knows the two-fact $6 \times 2 = 12$.

\smallskip
A four-fact is always the two-fact \emph{doubled}, because 4 is two 2s.

\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Draw 6 rows with 2 dots in each row. Then draw the whole picture
again right beside it.

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Use your picture to explain in one sentence why doubling works.

\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} What is $6 \times 4$?}

\problem[7cm]{Tens are the easiest anchor of all, and 9 is just one group short
of 10.

\smallskip
Priya needs $7 \times 9$. She starts at the ten-fact $7 \times 10 = 70$.

\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} How many groups of 7 does she have too many? Write that number.

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Take that many away from 70 and write the answer to $7 \times 9$.

\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} Careful: taking away one \emph{group} is not the same as taking
away one \emph{counter}. Say in one sentence what the difference is.}

\problem[6.5cm]{A craft box holds 45 beads. Each bracelet uses 5 beads.

\smallskip
Anchor facts work backwards too: instead of dividing, count \emph{up} by fives
and keep track of how many jumps it takes.

\par\smallskip
5, 10, \underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}, \underline{\hspace{1.0cm}},
\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}, \underline{\hspace{1.0cm}},
\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}, \underline{\hspace{1.0cm}},
\underline{\hspace{1.0cm}}

\smallskip
How many bracelets can be made?}

\problem[7.5cm]{Sevens have no anchor of their own --- so build one. A seven-fact
can be split into a five-fact plus a two-fact, because $5 + 2 = 7$.

\smallskip
Tomas needs $7 \times 7$.

\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Draw 7 rows of 7 dots. Draw a line that cuts every row into a part
with 5 dots and a part with 2 dots.

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Write the five-fact for the left part and the two-fact for the
right part.

\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} Add them. What is $7 \times 7$?}

\problem[8cm]{\textbf{Challenge.} Marco has 56 stickers. He puts 7 stickers in
each row.

\smallskip
He does not know his seven-facts yet, but he does know the anchor fact
$7 \times 5 = 35$.

\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} How many stickers are left over after those first five rows?

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Keep counting on by sevens from 35 and record each jump until you
reach 56. How many extra rows did that take?

\smallskip
\textbf{(c)} How many rows does Marco fill altogether?}
