\problem{Dog run $(2x+9)$ m by $4$ m: perimeter in metres, no brackets.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Perimeter is two long sides plus two short sides, so
$2(2x + 9) + 2(4)$. Distribute the $2$ across both terms of the bracket:
$2\cdot 2x = 4x$ and $2\cdot 9 = 18$, and $2\cdot 4 = 8$. Then $18 + 8 = 26$. \ans{4x + 26\ \text{m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$15$ notebooks at $(x+3)$ dollars each: total cost in dollars, no brackets.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Fifteen copies of the same price is $15(x + 3)$. The $15$ multiplies both
parts of the price --- the variable part and the fixed part:
$15\cdot x = 15x$ and $15\cdot 3 = 45$. \ans{15x + 45}

\noindent Read the answer back: the cost rises by $15$ dollars for each extra dollar of notebook
price, on top of a fixed $45$ dollars.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Hallway of area $(6x+30)$ square metres and width $6$ m: find the length in metres.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Area $=$ width $\times$ length, so length $=$ area $\div$ width. Taking the
common factor $6$ out of the area shows the division without long working:
$6x + 30 = 6(x + 5)$, so the length is the second factor. \ans{x + 5\ \text{m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Plan A $8(x+5)$ dollars, Plan B $8x + 40$ dollars: (a) confirm they agree; (b) what does each form show?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Distribute the $8$: \ $8\cdot x = 8x$ and $8\cdot 5 = 40$, so
Plan A charges $8x + 40$ dollars, the same as Plan B for every $x$. \ans{8x + 40}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The factored form $8(x + 5)$ shows the price
per book, $8$ dollars, and the $5$ spare books included in the order. The expanded form
$8x + 40$ shows the flat $40$-dollar charge for those spares as a separate term, which is what you
want when comparing against a plan with a different fixed fee. Full credit connects each form to
the quantity it makes visible; describing only one form earns half credit.

\problem{Patio $(x+6)$ m by $(x+2)$ m: area in square metres, standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
  (x+6)(x+2) &= x\cdot x + x\cdot 2 + 6\cdot x + 6\cdot 2\\
             &= x^2 + 2x + 6x + 12 \;=\; x^2 + 8x + 12.
\end{align*}
Each of the four products is one rectangle of the area model. \ans{x^2 + 8x + 12\ \text{m}^2}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $(9x^2 + 15x)$ square metres as a product of the greatest common monomial factor and one remaining factor.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The largest number dividing both $9$ and $15$ is $3$, and the lowest power
of $x$ present in both terms is $x$, so the greatest common monomial factor is $3x$:
$9x^2 + 15x = 3x\cdot 3x + 3x\cdot 5 = 3x(3x + 5)$. Re-distributing gives back
$9x^2 + 15x$, which is the check. \ans{3x(3x+5)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Income $40x$ dollars, costs $5(3x+20)$ dollars: profit in dollars, no brackets.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Profit $= 40x - 5(3x + 20)$. Distribute the $5$ first, and keep the minus
sign with \emph{both} products: $5(3x + 20) = 15x + 100$, so
$40x - 15x - 100 = 25x - 100$. Subtracting only the $15x$ and forgetting the $100$ is the usual
slip here. \ans{25x - 100}

\problem{Field $(3x+12)$ m in $3$ equal strips: (a) length of one strip; (b) why the factored form shows it.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad One strip is a third of the length. Factoring first,
$3x + 12 = 3(x + 4)$, so dividing by $3$ leaves the second factor. \ans{x + 4\ \text{m}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} In the form $3(x + 4)$ the field is literally
written as $3$ copies of a length $x + 4$, so the number of strips is one of the factors on the
page; finding one strip is reading off the other factor, not computing a division. Full credit
refers to the factor $3$ standing for the number of strips; a response that only recomputes the
division earns half credit.

\problem{Edging: $3(2x+5) + 4(x-2) - 2(x+1)$ metres. Total length, no brackets.}

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
  3(2x+5) &= 6x + 15,\\
  4(x-2)  &= 4x - 8,\\
  2(x+1)  &= 2x + 2 \quad\text{(this whole amount is removed)},\\
  \intertext{so the total is}
  6x + 15 + 4x - 8 - 2x - 2 &= (6x + 4x - 2x) + (15 - 8 - 2) \;=\; 8x + 5.
\end{align*}
\ans{8x + 5\ \text{m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Sheet $(3x+6)$ cm by $(x+4)$ cm: (a) standard form; (b) factored form; (c) which form shows divisibility by $3$?}

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\begin{align*}
  (3x+6)(x+4) &= 3x\cdot x + 3x\cdot 4 + 6\cdot x + 6\cdot 4\\
              &= 3x^2 + 12x + 6x + 24 \;=\; 3x^2 + 18x + 24.
\end{align*}
\ans{3x^2 + 18x + 24\ \text{cm}^2}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Every coefficient is divisible by $3$, so take $3$ out first:
$3x^2 + 18x + 24 = 3(x^2 + 6x + 8)$. Then $x^2 + 6x + 8$ needs two numbers with product $8$ and
sum $6$, namely $2$ and $4$. \ans{3(x+2)(x+4)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} The factored form. The leading factor $3$
multiplies the whole product, so for any whole-number $x$ the area is $3$ times a whole number and
is therefore divisible by $3$. The standard form hides this: you would have to check each
coefficient. Full credit names the visible factor $3$ as the reason; answering only that both
forms give the same number earns half credit.
