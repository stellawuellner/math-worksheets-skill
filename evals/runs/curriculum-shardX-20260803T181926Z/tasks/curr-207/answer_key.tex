\problem{Evaluate $7^0$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Use the quotient law on a power divided by itself:
$\dfrac{7^3}{7^3} = 7^{3-3} = 7^0$, and that quotient is plainly $1$.

\textbf{Step 2.} So $7^0 = 1$ --- and the same argument works for every
nonzero base. \quad $\ans{1}$}

\problem{Evaluate $5^{-2}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} By definition $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^{n}}$, so
$5^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{5^{2}}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $5^2 = 25$, so the value is one twenty-fifth.

$\ans{\dfrac{1}{25}}$

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $-25$ --- the exponent's sign was moved onto the
value. The exponent tells you to \emph{reciprocate}, never to negate.}

\problem{Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} A negative exponent on a fraction flips the fraction:
$\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^{2}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Square the numerator and the denominator: $3^2 = 9$ and
$2^2 = 4$.

$\ans{\dfrac{9}{4}}$}

\problem{Rewrite $3x^{-2}$ with positive exponents only.

\textbf{Step 1.} The exponent $-2$ belongs to $x$ alone --- the coefficient
$3$ has no negative exponent, so it does not move.

\textbf{Step 2.} Send only $x^{-2}$ to the denominator.

$\ans{\dfrac{3}{x^{2}}}$

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $\dfrac{1}{3x^{2}}$ --- the coefficient was
dragged down with the variable.}

\problem{Simplify $x^{5} \cdot x^{-8}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Same base multiplied, so the product law applies: add the
exponents.

\textbf{Step 2.} $5 + (-8) = -3$, so the result is $x^{-3}$, which must be
rewritten with a positive exponent.

$\ans{\dfrac{1}{x^{3}}}$}

\problem{Compare $2^{-3}$ and $3^{-2}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Neither power can be compared while it is still written with
a negative exponent, so evaluate both: $2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$ and
$3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Both are unit fractions, and the one with the \emph{smaller}
denominator is the larger number: eighths are bigger pieces than ninths.

$\ans{\dfrac{1}{8} > \dfrac{1}{9}}$

\textit{Teaching note:} students who compare the exponents ($-3 < -2$) and
stop get this backwards. Evaluate first.}

\problem{Write $4.5 \times 10^{-3}$ grams as a decimal.

\textbf{Step 1.} $10^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{1000}$, so the number is
$4.5 \div 1000$ --- a division, which makes the number \emph{smaller}.

\textbf{Step 2.} Dividing by $1000$ moves the decimal point three places to
the left: $4.5 \rightarrow 0.45 \rightarrow 0.045 \rightarrow 0.0045$.

$\ans{0.0045}$

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $4500$ --- the point was moved the wrong way. A
negative power of ten always shrinks the number.}

\problem{Simplify $\left(x^{-2}\right)^{3} \cdot x^{4}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Power of a power first: multiply the exponents,
$(-2)(3) = -6$, giving $x^{-6}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Now the product law: $-6 + 4 = -2$, so the result is
$x^{-2}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Rewrite with a positive exponent.

$\ans{\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}}$}

\problem{Rewrite $\dfrac{2x^{-3}}{y^{-2}}$ with positive exponents.

\textbf{Step 1.} A factor with a negative exponent changes level: $x^{-3}$ in
the numerator moves \emph{down}, and $y^{-2}$ in the denominator moves
\emph{up}.

\textbf{Step 2.} Each becomes positive on arrival:
$\dfrac{2x^{-3}}{y^{-2}} = \dfrac{2y^{2}}{x^{3}}$.

$\ans{\dfrac{2y^{2}}{x^{3}}}$}

\problem{Order $5^{0}$, $5^{-1}$ and $5^{1}$ from least to greatest.

\textbf{Step 1.} Evaluate each power: $5^{0} = 1$, $5^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{5}$,
and $5^{1} = 5$.

\textbf{Step 2.} One fifth is less than one, and one is less than five.

$\ans{5^{-1} < 5^{0} < 5^{1}}$

\textit{Teaching note:} with a base larger than $1$, making the exponent
smaller makes the value smaller --- the negative exponent lands below $1$, the
zero exponent lands exactly at $1$.}

\problem{Find and fix Dana's mistake: Dana wrote $4^{-2} = -16$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Trace the wrong move. Dana computed $4^2 = 16$ and then
attached the minus sign from the exponent to the answer, as though $4^{-2}$
meant $-(4^2)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The definition says otherwise: the negative exponent sends
the power to the denominator, $4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{4^{2}}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $4^2 = 16$, so the correct value is one sixteenth --- a
\emph{positive} number smaller than $1$, which is the quickest way to see that
$-16$ could not be right.

$\ans{\dfrac{1}{16}}$}

\problem{Simplify $\dfrac{3x^{-2}y^{4}}{6x^{3}y^{-1}}$.

\textbf{Step 1.} Handle the three parts separately. Coefficients:
$\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The $x$'s: subtract the exponents, $-2 - 3 = -5$, giving
$x^{-5}$, which belongs in the denominator.

\textbf{Step 3.} The $y$'s: $4 - (-1) = 5$, giving $y^{5}$ in the numerator.

\textbf{Step 4.} Put the three results together.

$\ans{\dfrac{y^{5}}{2x^{5}}}$}
