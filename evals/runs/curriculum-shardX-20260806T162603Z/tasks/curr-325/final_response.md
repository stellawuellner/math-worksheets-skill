Here are the three PDFs for the right-triangle method-selection set:

- **Worksheet** — `ws_rtmethods_curr325.pdf` (6 pages)
- **Answer key** — `ak_rtmethods_curr325.pdf` (3 pages)
- **Study guide** — `ss_rtmethods_curr325.pdf` (2 pages)

**What the worksheet covers.** Ten problems whose real difficulty is deciding
*which tool the givens allow* before computing anything. The four methods are
deliberately interleaved rather than blocked — Pythagoras, Pythagoras, special
triangle, side-from-angle, angle-from-sides, special triangle, Pythagoras,
side-from-angle, angle-from-sides, special triangle — so the student never gets
to run the same method twice down the page. Every problem carries its own
diagram, generated from the same givens the answer is checked against, and a
value-free reference triangle at the top fixes the labelling convention.

The ramp runs from a 9-12-15 hypotenuse to a closing synthesis item: a square
tile cut along its 24 cm diagonal, where the student computes a leg and then
explains how the 45-45-90 relationship reaches the same value without a
calculator.

**What is verified, honestly.** The verification file holds 11 entries, one per
response.

- **10 entries are machine-checked** with SymPy: 15 cm, 24 in, 9.90 cm, 10.83 m,
  28.07°, 19.05 ft, 15 ft, 44.04 m, 33.37° and 16.97 cm. **9 of the 10 problems
  are fully machine-checked end to end.**
- **1 entry is instructor-judged**: problem 10(b), the written explanation of the
  special-triangle route. The key prints a grading rubric for it instead of an
  answer, and the "What is verified" note names problem 10 as the only one with
  an instructor-judged part.

Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`. Five problems (1, 2, 4, 6, 9) carry declared
misconception traps, so the key's "Common wrong answers" block tells you what a
student who added the legs (21 cm), added instead of subtracting the squares
(27.86 in), used cosine for an opposite side (14.38 m), multiplied by a tangent
instead of dividing (6.35 ft), or reached for the inverse tangent when the
hypotenuse was known (28.81°) would have written.

**Study guide.** A short chooser box up front — two sides and no angle means
Pythagoras; 45°, 30° or 60° means the exact ratios; anything else means
trigonometry — then one section per method, each with its rule, a worked example
opening with the reasoning that selects the tool, and a try-it whose answer is
printed upside down inside the box.
