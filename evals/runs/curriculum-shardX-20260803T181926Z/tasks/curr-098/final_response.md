Here are the three documents for the grades 2–3 shape-sort workshop — ten
problems on classifying four-sided shapes by the attributes they share.

**Worksheet (`ws_quads_curr098.pdf`, 5 pages).** Every problem shows its own
shape cards, drawn true to size so the child can test them with the corner of a
sheet of paper and compare side lengths by eye or with a ruler. The sheet
rotates three sorting tests plus reading the class's data:

- square corners (problems 1, 5, 9)
- side lengths (problems 2, 7)
- parallel sides (problems 3, 8, 10)
- reading the class's shape-sort graph and line plot (problems 4, 6)

It builds from single-attribute sorts to shapes that belong to two groups at
once: problem 5 asks how one card can be a rectangle and a square at the same
time, problem 8 is an error-analysis task (Kim counted a trapezoid into the
"two pairs of parallel sides" box), problem 9 asks for the cards that pass both
the corner test and the equal-sides test — including a square standing on its
point — and problem 10 asks the child to draw two shapes on dot paper and name
something they share. The directions define "square corner" and "parallel" in
child's words before the first problem.

**Answer key (`ak_quads_curr098.pdf`, 3 pages).** A quick-answer bank for
grading, then a card-by-card walkthrough for every problem: which test was
applied, what each card did under that test, and why. The three open-response
items carry a model answer plus a "look for" line naming what a correct
explanation has to contain. The grade level and the curriculum summary
(standards 3.G.A and 3.MD, difficulty range) print here only.

**Study guide (`ss_quads_curr098.pdf`, 2 pages).** Four short sections — square
corners, side lengths, parallel sides, and reading a shape graph — each with a
rule in plain words, a worked example on real drawn cards, and a try-it whose
answer is printed upside down inside the box.

**Verification.** All ten machine checks pass: every count on the sheet was
recomputed from the attribute table that also decided which shape is drawn on
each card, so the pictures cannot disagree with the answers, and the graph and
line-plot questions are computed from the same data arrays the figures plot.
Problem 8's trap (counting the trapezoid, which gives 4 instead of 3) was
machine-checked as distinguishably wrong. Three items are flagged for manual
review because they are genuinely open: problem 5's explanation, problem 6(c)'s
"why can't a four-sided shape have exactly three square corners", and problem
10's drawing task.
