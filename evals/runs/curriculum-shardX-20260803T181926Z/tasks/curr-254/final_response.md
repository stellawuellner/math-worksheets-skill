# Sign and Like-Term Errors in Polynomial Operations — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready.

**Study guide (`ss_poly_curr254.pdf`, 2 pages)** — start here. Three sections,
each with a rule box, a worked example and a try-it problem whose answer is
printed upside down in the box:

1. **Subtracting and combining like terms** — subtraction is adding the
   opposite, so every term inside the parentheses flips sign; terms combine only
   when the variable parts match exactly.
2. **Distributing and expanding** — every term, every time; two negatives make a
   positive; a binomial square is a product, so the middle term survives.
3. **Factoring with signs** — the product/sum rule for $x^2 + bx + c$, plus the
   substitution check that catches a swapped pair immediately.

**Worksheet (`ws_poly_curr254.pdf`, 3 pages)** — 8 problems, all on the same
focus: spotting and repairing sign and like-term errors. Problems 1–3 are the
clean mechanics (combine, subtract, distribute). Problems 4, 5 and 7 are
find-and-fix items: each shows a plausible wrong line, asks the student to name
the step that failed, redo it, and confirm the repair by substituting a value.
Problem 6 pairs a factorisation with its substitution check. Problem 8 is a
short written explanation. Difficulty ramps 1 → 4.

**Answer key (`ak_poly_curr254.pdf`, 3 pages)** — a quick-answer bank at the top
(generated from the verification data, not retyped), then full step-by-step
solutions. Each find-and-fix solution names the specific error, not just the
right answer, and the key prints a "common wrong answers" line for every
planted mistake — for example, *if they got $-8$: distributed the subtraction
sign to the first term only*. It also carries the curriculum section with the
standard (HSA-APR.A.1) and the difficulty range; the grade level appears only
here, never on the student's pages.

**What was machine-verified.** Every algebraic answer was recomputed with SymPy:
all eight expansions, factorisations, the equivalence check on $(2x-3)^2$, and
each substitution value. Four misconception traps were declared and checked to
be *distinguishable* — the wrong method has to land on a visibly different
number, or the problem cannot teach the error it targets. All eleven machine
checks passed.

**Flagged for manual review.** Problem 8 is an open written response, so it is
labelled manual rather than claimed as verified. The key includes a model answer
and a short note on what to look for when reading a student's version: the
subtraction rule stated as multiplying every term by $-1$, a substitution that
actually separates the two expressions (note that $x = 1$ does not), and a
definition of like terms in terms of the variable part.
