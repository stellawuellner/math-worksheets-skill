\problem{Differentiate $y = 4x^5 - 3x^2 + 7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Power rule term by term, $\frac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}$; the
constant $7$ has slope $0$.
\[
  y' = 4(5)x^4 - 3(2)x^1 + 0 = 20x^4 - 6x. \qquad \ans{20x^4 - 6x}
\]

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = 2x^3 + \dfrac{5}{x}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Rewrite $\frac{5}{x} = 5x^{-1}$ so the power rule applies:
\[
  y' = 6x^2 + 5(-1)x^{-2} = 6x^2 - \frac{5}{x^2}. \qquad \ans{6x^2 - \dfrac{5}{x^2}}
\]
No quotient rule is needed --- a constant over a power is just a power.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = \left(x^2+1\right)(3x-4)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Product rule, $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ with $u = x^2+1$ and
$v = 3x-4$:
\begin{align*}
  y' &= (2x)(3x - 4) + \left(x^2+1\right)(3) \\
     &= 6x^2 - 8x + 3x^2 + 3 = 9x^2 - 8x + 3.
\end{align*}
So the derivative is \ans{9x^2 - 8x + 3}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = \dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Quotient rule,
$\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$, with $u = 2x+1$,
$v = x-3$:
\begin{align*}
  y' &= \frac{2(x-3) - (2x+1)(1)}{(x-3)^2}
      = \frac{2x - 6 - 2x - 1}{(x-3)^2} = \frac{-7}{(x-3)^2}.
\end{align*}
So the derivative is \ans{\dfrac{-7}{(x-3)^2}}

The order $u'v - uv'$ matters: reversing it flips the sign to $+7$.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = \sqrt{x} + \dfrac{6}{x^3}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Rewrite both terms as powers: $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$ and
$\frac{6}{x^3} = 6x^{-3}$.
\[
  y' = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} + 6(-3)x^{-4}
     = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} - \frac{18}{x^4}.
\]
So the derivative is \ans{\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} - \dfrac{18}{x^4}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = \left(3x^2-1\right)\left(x^3+2x\right)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Product rule with $u = 3x^2-1$, $v = x^3+2x$:
\begin{align*}
  y' &= (6x)\left(x^3 + 2x\right) + \left(3x^2-1\right)\left(3x^2+2\right) \\
     &= 6x^4 + 12x^2 + \left(9x^4 + 6x^2 - 3x^2 - 2\right) \\
     &= 15x^4 + 15x^2 - 2 .
\end{align*}
So the derivative is \ans{15x^4 + 15x^2 - 2}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Differentiate $y = \dfrac{x^2}{x^2+1}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Quotient rule with $u = x^2$, $v = x^2+1$:
\begin{align*}
  y' &= \frac{(2x)\left(x^2+1\right) - x^2(2x)}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}
      = \frac{2x^3 + 2x - 2x^3}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}
      = \frac{2x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}.
\end{align*}
So the derivative is \ans{\dfrac{2x}{\left(x^2+1\right)^2}}

\problem{$f(x) = (x+2)\left(x^2-5\right)$. (a) $f'(x)$. (b) The slope at $x = 2$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Product rule with $u = x+2$, $v = x^2-5$:
\[
  f'(x) = (1)\left(x^2-5\right) + (x+2)(2x) = x^2 - 5 + 2x^2 + 4x = 3x^2 + 4x - 5,
\]
so \ans{3x^2 + 4x - 5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Substitute $x = 2$: $\ 3(4) + 4(2) - 5 = 12 + 8 - 5$.
\ans{15}

Multiplying the two derivatives instead ($1 \cdot 2x = 2x$, giving $4$ at
$x = 2$) is the standard product-rule error; the number to watch for is $4$.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{$g(x) = \dfrac{2x-1}{x^2+3}$. (a) $g'(x)$. (b) $g'(1)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Quotient rule with $u = 2x-1$, $v = x^2+3$:
\begin{align*}
  g'(x) &= \frac{2\left(x^2+3\right) - (2x-1)(2x)}{\left(x^2+3\right)^2}
        = \frac{2x^2 + 6 - 4x^2 + 2x}{\left(x^2+3\right)^2},
\end{align*}
which collects to \ans{\dfrac{-2x^2 + 2x + 6}{\left(x^2+3\right)^2}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} At $x = 1$ the numerator is $-2 + 2 + 6 = 6$ and the
denominator is $(1+3)^2 = 16$, so $g'(1) = \frac{6}{16}$, which reduces to
\ans{\tfrac{3}{8}}

Dividing derivative-by-derivative instead would give $\frac{2}{2x} = 1$ at
$x = 1$; the number to watch for is $1$.

\problem{Challenge. $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2+3x}{x-1}$. (a) $f'(x)$. (b) $f'(3)$.
(c) What does (b) say about the graph?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Quotient rule with $u = x^2+3x$, $v = x-1$:
\begin{align*}
  f'(x) &= \frac{(2x+3)(x-1) - \left(x^2+3x\right)(1)}{(x-1)^2}
         = \frac{2x^2 + x - 3 - x^2 - 3x}{(x-1)^2},
\end{align*}
so the simplified derivative is \ans{\dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 3}{(x-1)^2}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} At $x = 3$ the numerator is $9 - 6 - 3 = 0$ and the
denominator is $(3-1)^2 = 4$, so the slope is \ans{0}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} A zero derivative
means the tangent line at that point is \textbf{horizontal}, so the graph of $f$
levels off at $x = 3$. Full credit says horizontal tangent and ties it to the
derivative being zero there. A stronger answer notices that the numerator
factors as $(x-3)(x+1)$, so $x = -1$ is the only other point on the curve with a
horizontal tangent. Do not accept ``the function is zero there'': $f(3) = 9$, and
it is the \emph{slope} that vanishes, not the value.
