\sstitleblock{Finding Unknown Factors with Related Multiplication Facts}

\noindent\small\textbf{The one big idea:} every division question is a multiplication question
with a factor missing. ``Total, number of groups, size of group'' --- know any two, find the third.
\normalsize

\skillheading{1. Unknown factor from an array}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Model:}\\[4pt]
rows $\times$ (how many in each row) $=$ total.\par
Cover one of the three numbers and it becomes the unknown factor. If you know the total and the
number of rows, ask: \emph{how many rows of what make the total?}
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad An array has 3 equal rows and 18 counters in all.
How many counters are in each row?
\step{The number of rows and the total are known, so the missing number is a factor: write $3 \times ? = 18$.}
\step{Count up by threes until you reach the total: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18$ --- that took six threes.}
Each row holds $\ans{6}$ counters.
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad An array has 5 equal rows and 40 counters in all.
How many counters are in each row?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{8}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Unknown factor in an equation}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Rule:}\\[4pt]
To solve $a \times ? = c$ (or $? \times a = c$), read it as \emph{``$a$ times what gives $c$?''}
Order does not matter in multiplication, so a blank in front is solved exactly the same way.
Skip-count by $a$, or recall the times-table row for $a$.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad Find the unknown factor: $8 \times ? = 40$.
\step{One factor and the product are known, so this is an unknown-factor question: eight times what gives 40?}
\step{Skip-count by eights: $8, 16, 24, 32, 40$ --- five eights.}
The unknown factor is $\ans{5}$.
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad Find the unknown factor: $? \times 4 = 32$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{8}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Equal-group stories (unknown-factor-word-problem)}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{How to set it up:}\\[4pt]
Find the \textbf{total} and the \textbf{number of equal groups} in the story, then write
(number of groups) $\times$ (size of each group) $=$ total, leaving the unknown blank.
Words like \emph{shares equally, each, per, in all} tell you which number is which.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad 24 apples are packed equally into 3 baskets.
How many apples are in each basket?
\step{Three equal groups with a total of 24, and the group size is missing --- so write $3 \times ? = 24$.}
\step{Three of what makes 24? Count by threes to 24, or use the fact $3 \times 8 = 24$.}
Each basket holds $\ans{8}$ apples.
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad 35 marbles are packed equally into 5 bags.
How many marbles are in each bag?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{7}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{4. Using a related division fact (related-division-fact)}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Fact family:}\\[4pt]
One trio of numbers makes four facts. From $4 \times 9 = 36$ you also get
$9 \times 4 = 36$, \; $36 \div 4 = 9$, \; $36 \div 9 = 4$.\par
So a division you have never practised is just a multiplication fact you already know, read backwards.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad You already know $9 \times 4 = 36$. Use it to find $36 \div 9$.
\step{Dividing by 9 asks for the missing factor in $9 \times ? = 36$, and the known fact already fills that blank.}
\step{The fact says nine fours make 36, so the missing factor is the other number in the fact.}
Therefore $36 \div 9 = \ans{4}$.
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad You know $7 \times 8 = 56$. Use it to find $56 \div 8$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{7}$}\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}\textbf{(!) Watch out:} The blank is a \emph{factor}, never the total. In
$6 \times ? = 54$ the answer is not 54 and not 6 --- it is the number of sixes it takes to reach 54.
And in a two-step story, answer the question that was asked: the mystery number may only be step one.
\end{watchoutbox}
