\problem{\textbf{$g(x)=f(x-2)+5$ for $f(x)=x^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Read the two changes by position: the $-2$ is inside $f$, so it
is horizontal and moves \emph{opposite} to its sign; the $+5$ is outside, so it
is vertical and moves as written.

\textbf{Step 2.} So the graph shifts right $2$ and up $5$, and the rule is
$g(x)=(x-2)^2+5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Expanding, $(x-2)^2+5=x^2-4x+4+5$, so
$g(x)=\ans{x^2-4x+9}$.

\textit{Listen for:} ``left $2$''. Inside the function, the sign lies about the
direction --- $f(x-2)$ needs a larger $x$ to reach the same output, so the
graph moves right.}

\problem{\textbf{$(f\circ g)(2)$ for $f(x)=2x-3$, $g(x)=x^2+1$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Composition is evaluated inside out, so $g$ runs first and its
output becomes the input of $f$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $g(2)=2^2+1=5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Then $f(5)=2(5)-3$, so $(f\circ g)(2)=\ans{7}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $g(f(2))=g(1)=2$ --- the two functions were
applied in the wrong order. Composition is not commutative.}

\problem{\textbf{The inverse of $f(x)=2x-3$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} An inverse undoes $f$, so write $y=2x-3$, swap the roles
of the variables to get $x=2y-3$, and solve for $y$.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Adding $3$: $x+3=2y$. Dividing by $2$:
$\ans{f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+3}{2}}$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Check by composing:
$f\bigl(f^{-1}(x)\bigr)=2\cdot\dfrac{x+3}{2}-3=(x+3)-3=x$, which is what
``undoes'' means.

\textbf{Step 4 (b).} Evaluating, $f^{-1}(7)=\dfrac{7+3}{2}$, so
$f^{-1}(7)=\ans{5}$.

\textit{Check:} $f(5)=2(5)-3=7$, so the pair $(5,7)$ on $f$ becomes $(7,5)$ on
$f^{-1}$ --- the reflection across $y=x$, seen numerically.}

\problem{\textbf{$h(x)=-f(2x)$ for $f(x)=x^2-x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two changes again, and they are of different kinds: the $2x$ is
inside (horizontal), the leading minus is outside (vertical).

\textbf{Step 2.} Substituting $2x$ for $x$: $f(2x)=(2x)^2-(2x)=4x^2-2x$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Negating everything gives
$h(x)=\ans{-4x^2+2x}$: a horizontal compression by a factor of one half,
followed by a reflection in the $x$-axis.

\textit{Common error:} distributing the minus to only the first term. It is
outside the whole function, so every term flips.}

\problem{\textbf{$(g\circ f)(x)$ for $f(x)=x+4$, $g(x)=x^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $f$ runs first, so its whole output $x+4$ is what gets squared
--- not $x$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(g\circ f)(x)=g(x+4)=(x+4)^2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Expanding, $(g\circ f)(x)=\ans{x^2+8x+16}$.

\textit{What this shows:} composing with $x+4$ on the inside is exactly the
horizontal shift rule --- the graph of $g\circ f$ is the parabola $y=x^2$ moved
left $4$. Transformations are compositions with simple linear functions.}

\problem{\textbf{The inverse of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Same procedure as any inverse: $y=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$, swap
to get $x=\dfrac{2y+1}{y-3}$, then solve for $y$ --- but now the unknown sits in
both the numerator and the denominator, so clear the fraction first.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Multiplying: $x(y-3)=2y+1$, so $xy-3x=2y+1$.

\textbf{Step 3 (a).} Collect the $y$ terms: $xy-2y=3x+1$, so $y(x-2)=3x+1$ and
$\ans{f^{-1}(x)=\frac{3x+1}{x-2}}$.

\textbf{Step 4 (b).} Substituting $f^{-1}$ into $f$ and simplifying returns $x$,
which confirms the algebra. Evaluating,
$f^{-1}(4)=\dfrac{13}{2}$, so $f^{-1}(4)=\ans{\frac{13}{2}}$.

\textit{Note:} $f$ excludes $3$ from its domain and never outputs $2$; the
inverse excludes $2$ and never outputs $3$. Domain and range swap places, just
like the coordinates.}

\problem{\textbf{$h$ from $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$: stretch $3$, left $4$, down $1$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Sort the three changes by whether they touch $x$ or $y$: a
vertical stretch and a vertical shift are outside, and the horizontal shift is
inside with the opposite sign.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Left $4$ means $x+4$ inside the root; stretch by $3$ and
down $1$ are outside, so $h(x)=3\sqrt{x+4}-1$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} $h(5)=3\sqrt{9}-1=3(3)-1$, so $h(5)=\ans{8}$.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $2$ --- from $3\sqrt{5-4}-1$, shifting right
instead of left.}

\problem{\textbf{$(f\circ g)(-1)$ for $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x-2}$, $g(x)=x+5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Inside out again: find $g(-1)$ first, then feed it to $f$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $g(-1)=-1+5=4$, and $f(4)=\dfrac{1}{4-2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so
$(f\circ g)(-1)=\ans{\frac{1}{2}}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} For the domain: $f\circ g$ is $\dfrac{1}{x+3}$, which is
undefined when $x+3=0$. The excluded input is $x=-3$.

\textit{Teaching note:} the excluded value comes from the \emph{inner} output
hitting $f$'s bad input, which is why domains of compositions have to be
checked before simplifying, not after.}

\problem{\textbf{Solving $f^{-1}(x)=2$ for $f(x)=3x-7$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $f^{-1}(x)=2$ says exactly that $f(2)=x$ --- reading the
inverse statement backwards is the shortcut, and it avoids finding $f^{-1}$ at
all.

\textbf{Step 2 (shortcut).} $f(2)=3(2)-7=-1$, so $x=-1$.

\textbf{Step 3 (check with the formula).} $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x+7}{3}$, and
setting $\dfrac{x+7}{3}=2$ gives $x+7=6$, so $\ans{x=-1}$ --- the same answer.

\textit{What this shows:} an inverse equation is the original equation with the
roles of input and output swapped. Students who see that stop computing
inverses they do not need.}

\problem{\textbf{$(f\circ g)^{-1}$ against $g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}$, for $f(x)=2x$
and $g(x)=x+3$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Compose first: $(f\circ g)(x)=f(x+3)=2(x+3)=2x+6$.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} Inverting that: $y=2x+6$ swaps to $x=2y+6$, so
$y=\dfrac{x-6}{2}$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Separately, $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}$ and
$g^{-1}(x)=x-3$, so
$g^{-1}\bigl(f^{-1}(x)\bigr)=\ans{\frac{x}{2}-3}$.

\textbf{Step 4.} The two agree, since $\dfrac{x-6}{2}=\dfrac{x}{2}-3$.

\textit{Why the order reverses:} $f\circ g$ does $g$ first and $f$ second, so
undoing it must undo $f$ first and $g$ second --- the same reason you take your
shoes off before your socks.}

\problem{\textbf{$h(x)=-2f(x-1)+3$ with $f(1)=4$ and $f(3)=-2$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} $h(2)$ needs $f$ at $2-1=1$, and the table supplies that
value --- so the only table entry this part uses is $f(1)=4$.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} $h(2)=-2f(1)+3=-2(4)+3$, so $h(2)=\ans{-5}$.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} In order: shift right $1$ (inside, opposite sign);
then stretch vertically by $2$ and reflect in the $x$-axis (the factor $-2$
outside); then shift up $3$.

\textit{Grading note (manual):} part (b) needs the order as much as the list.
Applying the up-$3$ before the $-2$ would multiply it as well, giving $-2$
instead of $-5$ --- and that wrong value is the check on whether the order was
understood.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $-2$, from stretching after shifting up.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: $f(x)=(x-1)^2$ on $x\ge 1$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} On all real numbers $f$ fails the horizontal line test ---
$f(0)=f(2)=1$, so two inputs share an output and no rule could undo it.
Restricted to $x\ge 1$ the function is strictly increasing, so each output comes
from exactly one input and an inverse exists.

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} $y=(x-1)^2$ swaps to $x=(y-1)^2$. Taking the square root
and keeping the branch with $y\ge 1$ gives
$\ans{f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{x}+1}$ --- the positive root is chosen because of the
restriction, not by convention.

\textbf{Step 3 (b).} Check: $f\bigl(f^{-1}(x)\bigr)=(\sqrt{x}+1-1)^2=x$ for
$x\ge 0$, so the two really do undo each other.

\textbf{Step 4 (c).} $f(5)=(5-1)^2=16$ and $f^{-1}(16)=\sqrt{16}+1=4+1$, so
$f^{-1}\bigl(f(5)\bigr)=\ans{5}$.

\textit{What that shows:} the point $(5,16)$ on $f$ corresponds to $(16,5)$ on
$f^{-1}$, and going out and back returns the input. That round trip is the
reflection across $y=x$ done in numbers rather than in a picture.

\textit{Grading note (manual):} part (a) is the graded reasoning here. Either
the horizontal line test with a concrete pair of inputs, or ``$f$ is strictly
increasing on $x\ge 1$'', earns full credit; ``because we restricted it'' does
not.}
