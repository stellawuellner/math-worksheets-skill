\wstitleblock{Reading Line Plots Marked in Fractions}{Grade 4--5}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Reading stacks of X's off a line plot marked in eighths and
quarters, comparing the fractional values, and totalling or redistributing the whole
set}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{How to use this sheet.}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every \textsf{X} on a line plot stands for one measurement. The fraction written
        underneath says how big that measurement was.
  \item So a line plot holds two different kinds of number, and each question wants one of
        them. \emph{How many} questions are answered by counting \textsf{X}'s. \emph{How
        much} questions are answered by adding the fractions the \textsf{X}'s sit on.
  \item The marks along the bottom are evenly spaced, and equal spaces stand for equal
        differences in size. That is what lets you see the shape of the data at a glance.
  \item When you add fractions off a plot, rewrite them all with the same denominator first
        --- eighths on the first plot, quarters on the second.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
