\problem{A sticker sheet has $4$ equal rows and $24$ stickers in all. How many stickers are in each row? \quad $4 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 24$}

\textbf{Solution:} The $24$ stickers are shared equally among $4$ rows, so the missing factor is the
size of one row: $24 \div 4$. Skip-count by $4$ until you reach $24$: $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24$ --- that is
six jumps, so $4 \times 6 = 24$. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A garden has $5$ equal rows of seedlings and $35$ seedlings in all. How many seedlings are in each row? \quad $5 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 35$}

\textbf{Solution:} One row is $35 \div 5$. Skip-count by $5$: $5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35$ --- seven jumps.
Check with the multiplication fact: $5 \times 7 = 35$. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Tiles are laid in equal rows of $3$ tiles, $18$ tiles in all. How many rows are there? \quad $\underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} \times 3 = 18$}

\textbf{Solution:} Here the missing factor is the NUMBER OF ROWS, not the row size. Each row uses
$3$ tiles, so ask how many $3$s are in $18$: $18 \div 3$. Count by threes: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18$ --- six
threes, so $6 \times 3 = 18$. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor. \quad $6 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 42$}

\textbf{Solution:} Ask ``six times what gives $42$?'' The related division fact is $42 \div 6$.
From the sixes: $6 \times 6 = 36$ and $6 \times 7 = 42$. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor. \quad $\underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} \times 9 = 54$}

\textbf{Solution:} The missing factor is the first one this time, but multiplication can be read in
either order: what times $9$ makes $54$? The related division fact is $54 \div 9$. From the nines:
$9 \times 6 = 54$. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A craft box holds exactly $8$ crayons. A classroom has $56$ crayons in full boxes. How many boxes? \quad $8 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 56$}

\textbf{Solution:} Each box is a group of $8$, so the missing factor counts the groups: $56 \div 8$.
From the eights: $8 \times 7 = 56$. The unit is boxes, because the missing factor counts boxes, not
crayons. \ans{7\ \text{boxes}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor. \quad $7 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 63$}

\textbf{Solution:} Seven times what makes $63$? The related division fact is $63 \div 7$. From the
sevens: $7 \times 9 = 63$. \ans{9}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A baking tray holds exactly $5$ muffins. A bakery has $45$ muffins on full trays. How many trays? \quad $5 \times \underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} = 45$}

\textbf{Solution:} Each tray is a group of $5$, so the missing factor counts the trays: $45 \div 5$.
Count by fives to $45$ --- nine fives --- or use $5 \times 9 = 45$. \ans{9\ \text{trays}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the missing factor. \quad $\underline{\hspace{1.1cm}} \times 8 = 72$}

\textbf{Solution:} What times $8$ makes $72$? The related division fact is $72 \div 8$. The eights run
$8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64, 72$ --- nine eights, and $9 \times 8 = 72$. \ans{9}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A class sets up $6$ equal rows of chairs, $48$ chairs in all. (a) How many chairs are in each row? (b) Explain how a multiplication fact you already know helps you find that missing number.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The $48$ chairs are shared equally among $6$ rows, so one row is $48 \div 6$.
From the sixes: $6 \times 8 = 48$. \ans{8}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer names a fact the student already
knows and says how it produces the missing number. Model answer: ``I know $6 \times 8 = 48$, and the
equation is $6 \times \square = 48$, so the missing number must be $8$.'' Skip-counting is equally
acceptable: ``I counted by sixes --- $6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48$ --- and that took $8$ sixes.''
Answers that give only the number $8$ with no fact and no count do not earn full credit; answers that
say ``I divided $48$ by $6$'' earn full credit if the student also states the fact or the count that
gave the quotient.
