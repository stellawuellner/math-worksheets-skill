\problem{Colony table $60, 90, 135, 202.5$. (a) The hourly multiplier. (b) Predict the count at $t = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Divide consecutive counts: $90 \div 60$. The same ratio appears again ($135 \div 90$), so the model is exponential. \ans{r = 1.5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The model is $60 \cdot 1.5^{\,t}$. At $t = 5$: $1.5^5 = 7.59375$, so $60 \times 7.59375$. \ans{455.63}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Yearly table $800, 640, 512, 409.6$. (a) The multiplier. (b) Percent lost each year.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $640 \div 800$. \ans{r = 0.8}

\textbf{Solution (b):} A multiplier of $0.8$ keeps $80\%$ and so loses the rest: $100(1 - 0.8) = 100(0.2)$. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Culture of $250$ growing $8\%$ an hour. (a) The multiplier. (b) Count after $6$ hours.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Growth of $8\%$ keeps the whole and adds $8$ hundredths: $1 + \frac{8}{100}$. \ans{r = 1.08}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $1.08^6 = 1.58687432\ldots$, so $250 \times 1.58687432 = 396.71858\ldots$, rounded to two decimal places. \ans{396.72}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Car worth \$18{,}000 losing $15\%$ a year. (a) The multiplier. (b) Value after $4$ years.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Losing $15\%$ leaves $85\%$: $1 - \frac{15}{100}$. \ans{r = 0.85}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $0.85^4 = 0.52200625$, so $18000 \times 0.52200625$. \ans{9396.11}

\textbf{Common wrong answer:} \$7200 comes from subtracting $15\%$ of the \emph{original} price four times. Each year's loss is taken from the value at the start of that year, which is smaller every time.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Sample: $800$ mg at $t = 0$, $100$ mg at $t = 3$ hours. (a) The hourly multiplier. (b) Mass after $5$ hours.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Three hours of multiplying give $800 r^3 = 100$, so $r^3 = \frac{100}{800} = \frac{1}{8}$. The number whose cube is $\frac{1}{8}$ is $\frac{1}{2}$. \ans{r = 0.5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $800 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5} = \frac{800}{32}$ --- halve five times: $400, 200, 100, 50$. \ans{25}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Population $300 \cdot 2^{\,t/5}$. When does it reach $4800$?}

\textbf{Solution:} Divide by $300$: $2^{\,t/5} = 16 = 2^4$, so $\frac{t}{5} = 4$. Check: $300 \cdot 2^4 = 300 \cdot 16 = 4800$ $\checkmark$ \ans{t = 20}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Bacteria: $40$ on day $0$, $120$ on day $1$. (a) The daily multiplier. (b) Count on day $4$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} One day of multiplying takes $40$ to $120$, so $r = 120 \div 40$. \ans{r = 3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The model is $40 \cdot 3^{\,t}$, and $3^4 = 81$, so $40 \times 81$. \ans{3240}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Drug: $640 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t}$ mg. When is $20$ mg left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Divide by $640$: $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t} = \frac{20}{640} = \frac{1}{32}$, and $\frac{1}{32} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5}$. Check: halving $640$ five times gives $320, 160, 80, 40, 20$ $\checkmark$ \ans{t = 5\ \text{hr}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Town of $12{,}000$ losing $3\%$ a year. (a) The multiplier. (b) Population after $10$ years.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $1 - \frac{3}{100}$. \ans{r = 0.97}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $0.97^{10} = 0.73742412\ldots$, so $12000 \times 0.73742412 = 8849.0895\ldots$, rounded to two decimal places. \ans{8849.09}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Radioactive sample: $1200 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/8}$ grams, half-life $8$ days. (a) Mass at $24$ days. (b) When is $37.5$ g left? (c) Why halving is not subtracting.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $24$ days is three half-lives, so $\frac{t}{8} = 3$ and the mass halves three times: $1200 \to 600 \to 300 \to 150$. \ans{150\ \text{g}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Divide by $1200$: $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/8} = \frac{37.5}{1200} = \frac{1}{32} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5}$, so $\frac{t}{8} = 5$. Check: five half-lives from $1200$ give $600, 300, 150, 75, 37.5$ $\checkmark$ \ans{t = 40}

\textbf{(c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct answer says halving takes the same \emph{fraction} of whatever is present, so each successive loss is smaller in absolute terms and the mass approaches zero without reaching it; subtracting a fixed number of grams each period would remove the same amount every time and would drive the mass to zero and then below it, which is impossible. Full credit for contrasting a constant fraction with a constant amount and drawing the consequence; partial credit for naming the difference without the consequence.
