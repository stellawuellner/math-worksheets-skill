Here are the three documents for the grades 4–5 patterns workshop — ten problems
on generating two numerical patterns and comparing their matching terms.

**Worksheet (`ws_patterns_curr148.pdf`, 5 pages).** The sheet builds one idea in
order. Problems 1 and 2 generate two patterns from their rules. Problem 3 lines
them up and asks how many times one term is the other. Problem 4 turns the
corresponding terms into ordered pairs and plots them on a labelled grid.
Problems 5–7 do the same work in a savings context (Ana and Ben's jars),
finishing with the mean of each saver's five weekly totals and the question of
whether the relationship between matching terms survives averaging. Problem 8 is
the error-analysis problem the whole sheet points at: two patterns that start at
the same number but step by 2 and by 4, where "double the step" does *not* double
the terms, and a planted wrong answer of 18 to find. Problem 9 plots a second
pair of patterns, and problem 10 asks the student to invent two rules whose terms
stay in a 1-to-4 ratio and explain how the starting number and the step had to be
chosen.

Three skills alternate: building a pattern from a rule (1, 2, 5), comparing
corresponding terms (3, 6, 7, 8), and ordered pairs on the grid (4, 9, 10).
Difficulty ramps 1 to 5, and every problem gets 4–8 cm of writing room; the two
graphing problems have a full labelled grid plus 3.5 cm to write the pairs.

**Answer key (`ak_patterns_curr148.pdf`, 3 pages).** Quick-answer bank, then a
worked solution for every problem, with the two patterns printed as lined-up
tables where that is the point. It names the step-counting slip explicitly (term
6 takes five additions, not six), explains why the "times 3" relationship in
problems 1–4 holds — both patterns start at 0 *and* the steps are in that ratio
— and why Mia's claim in problem 8 fails at the very first term. The three open
items carry a model answer plus a "look for" line. Grade level and the curriculum
summary (5.OA.B.3, 5.G.A.2) print here only.

**Study guide (`ss_patterns_curr148.pdf`, 2 pages).** Three sections — building a
pattern from its rule, comparing corresponding terms, and turning two patterns
into ordered pairs — each with a rule box, a worked example, and a try-it with
the answer upside down inside the box, closing with a watch-out on the
off-by-one step count and on plotting the first number across.

**Verification.** All 12 machine checks pass: every requested term was recomputed
from start + step × (n−1), every "how many times" quotient from the two terms,
both means from the five printed weekly totals, and the graphing answers from the
term-to-term relationship. Problem 8's trap was machine-checked as distinguishably
wrong (18 against the correct 15), so the problem really does catch the
misconception it targets. Three items are flagged for manual review because they
are open by design: writing and plotting the ordered pairs in problems 4 and 9,
and inventing the pair of rules in problem 10.
