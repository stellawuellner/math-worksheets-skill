\problem{\textbf{Apples in bags of 6.}
Divide: $53 \div 6$. Since $6 \times 8 = 48$ and $53 - 48 = 5$, the quotient is 8
with remainder 5.\par
The stand sells only \emph{full} bags, and 5 apples are not enough for another
full bag --- the remainder is dropped.
\ans{8 \text{ full bags}}}

\problem{\textbf{Stickers shared among 5 friends.}
Divide: $47 \div 5$. Since $5 \times 9 = 45$ and $47 - 45 = 2$, each friend gets
9 whole stickers and the remainder is 2.\par
The question asks what is left over, so the remainder itself is the answer.
\ans{2 \text{ stickers left over}}}

\problem{\textbf{Vans for 128 students.}
Divide: $128 \div 9$. Since $9 \times 14 = 126$ and $128 - 126 = 2$, the quotient
is 14 with remainder 2.\par
Fourteen vans seat only 126 students; the 2 remaining students still need a seat,
so one more van is needed --- round the quotient up.
\ans{15 \text{ vans}}}

\problem{\textbf{Ribbon cut into 8-centimetre pieces.}
Divide: $275 \div 8$. Since $8 \times 34 = 272$ and $275 - 272 = 3$, the quotient
is 34 with remainder 3.\par
The leftover 3 centimetres is shorter than a whole piece, so it cannot count as
a piece --- drop the remainder.
\ans{34 \text{ whole pieces}}}

\problem{\textbf{Eggs in cartons of 6.}
Divide: $350 \div 6$. Since $6 \times 58 = 348$ and $350 - 348 = 2$, the quotient
is 58 with remainder 2.\par
Every egg must be in a carton, and 58 cartons hold only 348 eggs, so the last
2 eggs need one more carton --- round up.
\ans{59 \text{ cartons}}}

\problem{\textbf{Trading cards, 9 to a page.}
Divide: $623 \div 9$. Since $9 \times 69 = 621$ and $623 - 621 = 2$, the quotient
is 69 with remainder 2.\par
Rosa fills 69 full pages, and the 2 remaining cards go on the next page --- the
last page she uses is partly filled, and the remainder tells how many cards are on it.
\ans{2 \text{ cards on the last page}}}

\problem{\textbf{Buses for 237 students, 40 seats each.}
(a) Estimate: 237 rounds to 240 to the nearest ten, and $240 \div 40 = 6$, so about
6 buses. \ans{\text{about } 6 \text{ buses}}

(b) Exactly: $237 \div 40$ gives $40 \times 5 = 200$ with $237 - 200 = 37$ left, so
5 buses fill completely and the remainder rides on a sixth bus. The estimate of
6 buses was right, and the last bus is only partly full.
\ans{37 \text{ students on the last bus}}}

\problem{\textbf{Pencils in boxes of 9.}
Divide: $1248 \div 9$. Since $9 \times 138 = 1242$ and $1248 - 1242 = 6$, the
quotient is 138 with remainder 6.\par
Only completely filled boxes are counted, and the 6 leftover pencils do not fill
a box --- drop the remainder.
\ans{138 \text{ full boxes}}}

\problem{\textbf{Books on shelves of 8.}
Divide: $2455 \div 8$. Since $8 \times 306 = 2448$ and $2455 - 2448 = 7$, the
quotient is 306 with remainder 7.

(a) Every book must be on a shelf. 306 shelves hold 2448 books, so the 7 remaining
books need one more shelf --- round up. \ans{307 \text{ shelves}}

(b) The last shelf holds only those leftover books, which is the remainder.
\ans{7 \text{ books on the last shelf}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix the mistake --- marbles in boxes of 6.}
Maya's division is correct: $6 \times 52 = 312$ and $314 - 312 = 2$, so
$314 \div 6 = 52$ R $2$. Her \emph{interpretation} is what went wrong: she reported
the quotient, but 52 boxes hold only 312 marbles, which leaves 2 marbles with no box.

(a) The question asks how many boxes are needed so that every marble is boxed,
so the remainder forces one more box --- round up. \ans{53 \text{ boxes}}

(b) That last box is not full; it holds only the leftover marbles, the remainder.
\ans{2 \text{ leftover marbles}}}
