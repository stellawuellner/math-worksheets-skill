\sstitleblock{Graphs, Line Plots, and Shape Sorting}

\noindent\small Every question on the worksheet starts the same way: decide
what the display is showing you, then decide whether the question wants you to
\emph{read} one number, \emph{add} several, \emph{compare} two, or \emph{sort}
shapes first and count afterwards.
\normalsize

\skillheading{1. Reading a bar graph}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{How to read it:} each bar stands for one group. Slide from the top of
the bar across to the number scale --- that number is the count for that
group.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Read one bar} for ``how many did $\underline{\phantom{xx}}$ have?''.
\textbf{Add every bar} for ``altogether'' or ``in all''.
\textbf{Subtract two bars} for ``how many more'' or ``how many fewer''.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A bar graph shows fruit picked: apples 7, pears 4, plums 9.
How many pieces of fruit were picked in all?
\step{``In all'' means every group counts, so this is an adding question, not
a reading-one-bar question.}
\step{$7 + 4 + 9$: first $7 + 4 = 11$, then $11 + 9 = 20$.}
\par
$\ans{20}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A bar graph shows stickers: stars 6, moons 3, suns 8. How
many stickers in all?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{17}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Reading a line plot}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{How to read it:} the numbers along the bottom are \emph{measurements}.
Each X above a number stands for \emph{one object} of that size.\\[3pt]
So a stack of 5 X marks above the 4 means five objects, each 4 units long. To
find how many objects there are altogether, count every X on the whole plot.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A line plot of ribbon lengths has 3 X marks above 2, 4 X
marks above 3, and 2 X marks above 4. How many ribbons are 3 inches long?
\step{The question names one length, so find that number on the line and count
only the stack sitting above it.}
\step{Above the 3 there are 4 X marks, and each X is one ribbon.}
\par
$\ans{4}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A line plot of book widths has 2 X marks above 5, 6 X marks
above 6, and 3 X marks above 7. How many books are 6 cm wide?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{6}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Comparing and measuring a data set}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Most common (mode):} the measurement with the \emph{tallest} stack of
X marks. In the list $2, 3, 3, 3, 5$ the most common value is $3$.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Range:} greatest measurement $-$ least measurement. It tells you how
spread out the data is.\\[3pt]
\textbf{(!) Both answers are measurements, not stack heights.} Give the number
from the bottom of the plot, with its unit.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Ribbon lengths in inches: $4, 4, 6, 7, 7, 9$. Find the range.
\step{Range compares the two ends of the data, so I only need the greatest and
the least value --- the repeats in the middle do not matter.}
\step{Greatest is $9$, least is $4$, and $9 - 4 = 5$.}
\par
$\ans{5}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Pencil lengths in centimetres: $2, 5, 5, 6, 8$. Find the
range.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{6}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{4. Sorting shapes by sides and corners}
\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Count the sides; the corners always match.} 3 sides = triangle,
4 sides = quadrilateral, 5 sides = pentagon, 6 sides = hexagon.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Squares, rectangles, rhombuses and trapezoids are all quadrilaterals.}
A square is the special one that has 4 \emph{equal} sides \emph{and} 4 square
corners. So ``it has 4 sides'' never proves ``it is a square''.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A bag holds a triangle, a square, a rectangle, a trapezoid
and a hexagon. How many of them have exactly 4 sides?
\step{Sorting comes before counting: give every shape its side number first,
then count only the row I was asked about.}
\step{Triangle 3, square 4, rectangle 4, trapezoid 4, hexagon 6 --- so the
``4 sides'' row holds the square, the rectangle and the trapezoid.}
\par
$\ans{3}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A bag holds a rectangle, a rhombus, a pentagon and a
triangle. How many have exactly 4 sides?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{2}$}
\end{tryitbox}
