# Transformations of Absolute-Value Graphs — three PDFs

- **Study guide (`ss_absvaluegraph_curr297.pdf`, 2 pages)** — three sections: moving the V
  left/right/up/down (with the trick that never fails — *what makes the inside of the bars
  zero?*), flipping and steepening the arms with the multiplier out front, and a three-step
  routine for drawing the graph from the equation. Rule box, worked example with an explicit
  strategy step, and a try-it in each; a watch-out box on the direction of the horizontal move.
- **Worksheet (`ws_absvaluegraph_curr297.pdf`, 6 pages)** — 12 problems on a clean ramp from
  $|x|-3$ to $-\tfrac{1}{2}|x-2|+3$, with no two problems sharing a skeleton: shifts first,
  then reflections and stretches, then two graph-it problems with printed grids, a
  write-the-equation-from-the-corner problem, and a synthesis item that asks *why* a shallower
  slope moves the intercepts further out.
- **Answer key (`ak_absvaluegraph_curr297.pdf`, 3 pages)** — Quick Answers bank, a "What is
  verified" note, a "Common wrong answers" line, a generated Curriculum section, and worked
  solutions that show the two-case split every time and point out where the usual slip is
  (dividing before splitting on problem 10).

## What is verified

The sheet asks for **24 responses** across 12 problems.

- **19 are machine-checked.** Every intercept set and every substitution was recomputed with
  SymPy — including the fractional-coefficient synthesis problem — and each printed boxed
  answer in the key was separately confirmed against the verified value. Seven of the twelve
  problems (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10) are fully machine-checked.
- **5 are instructor-judged: 6(b), 8(a), 9(b), 11(a) and 12(c).** Three are **sketches** (a
  drawn graph cannot be checked by computer), one is a **description** of the two moves from
  the parent graph, and one is an **equation the student writes** from a corner and a slope.
  Each is marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank, listed in the key's "What is verified" note,
  and carries a specific rubric — the sketch rubrics name the exact corner coordinates and
  crossings a correct graph must show, so grading them is a matter of looking, not judging.

Nothing is marked `[unchecked]`.

## Notes for the adult

- One trap is declared and machine-checked: on problem 2, reading $|x-4|$ as a shift *left*
  gives $-4$. The key prints that under "Common wrong answers".
- Problem 10 is the sneaky one: $3|x-2| = 6$ must be divided by 3 before the absolute value is
  split. Students who split first get the wrong pair.
- The grade level prints on the answer key only.
