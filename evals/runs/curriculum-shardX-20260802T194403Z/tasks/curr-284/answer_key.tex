\noindent\small\textbf{Grading note.} On this sheet the sign reasoning is the skill, so a
correct number reached by the wrong substitution should not earn full credit. The
``common wrong answers'' list above tells you which misreading produced each wrong value.
\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{Minimum value of $f(x) = (x - 5)^2 + 2$.}\par
The vertex $x$ is whatever makes the squared factor zero: $x - 5 = 0$ gives $x = 5$, so the
vertex is at $x = 5$, \emph{not} $x = -5$. (The graph moved right, in the opposite
direction from the printed sign.)
\[ f(5) = (5 - 5)^2 + 2 = 0 + 2 \]
Because $(x-5)^2 \ge 0$ for every $x$, adding 2 can never produce anything smaller, so this
really is the minimum. $\ans{2}$\par
\textit{If they got 102:} they substituted $x = -5$, reading the printed sign literally.}

\problem{\textbf{$y = x^2$ shifted 4 left and 3 down; evaluate at $x = -4$.}\par
A shift left is written with a \emph{plus} inside the parentheses, because the graph moves
to where $x + 4 = 0$, i.e. $x = -4$:
\[ y = (x + 4)^2 - 3 \]
\[ y(-4) = (-4 + 4)^2 - 3 = 0 - 3 \]
The value at $x = -4$ is the vertex height, which is the whole point of the check.
$\ans{-3}$\par
\textit{If they got 61:} they wrote $(x - 4)^2 - 3$ for ``left 4'' and then evaluated it at
$x = -4$, so the two sign choices compounded.}

\problem{\textbf{$x$-intercepts of $p(x) = (x - 1)^2 - 16$.}\par
Set $p(x) = 0$ and undo the transformations in reverse order:
\begin{align*}
(x - 1)^2 - 16 &= 0\\
(x - 1)^2 &= 16\\
x - 1 &= \pm 4 \qquad \text{(both roots --- dropping $-4$ loses an intercept)}\\
x &= 1 + 4 = 5 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = 1 - 4 = -3
\end{align*}
Sign check: the axis of symmetry is $x = 1$, and the two intercepts sit 4 units on either
side of it $\checkmark$
\[ \boxed{x = -3 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 5} \]}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: maximum of $y = -(x - 6)^2 + 7$.}\par
\emph{The error:} Jamal read $(x - 6)$ as a vertex at $x = -6$. The vertex sits where the
squared factor is zero, so $x - 6 = 0$ gives $x = 6$. His arithmetic was fine; his $h$ was
not.\par
Correct substitution:
\[ y(6) = -(6 - 6)^2 + 7 = -0 + 7 \]
Since $a = -1 < 0$ the parabola opens downward, so this vertex value is a \emph{maximum}
--- 7 is the largest output the function ever reaches. $\ans{7}$\par
\textit{If they got $-137$:} they repeated Jamal's substitution $x = -6$ instead of fixing
it.}

\problem{\textbf{Reflect, stretch by 3, left 2, up 12; evaluate at $x = 0$.}\par
Build the equation one transformation at a time. Reflection and vertical stretch both live
in $a$: $a = -3$. Left 2 puts $+2$ inside the parentheses; up 12 adds 12 outside.
\[ q(x) = -3(x + 2)^2 + 12 \]
\[ q(0) = -3(0 + 2)^2 + 12 = -3(4) + 12 = -12 + 12 \]
So $x = 0$ is one of the $x$-intercepts of the transformed parabola. $\ans{0}$\par
\textit{If they got 24:} they kept $a = +3$ and lost the reflection. Note the vertex is at
$x = -2$ with value 12, so a positive $a$ would make 12 a minimum instead of a maximum ---
the shape of the answer changes, not just the number.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: minimum of $g(x) = (x + 7)^2 - 5$.}\par
\emph{The error:} Mia called $(x + 7)$ a shift 7 units \emph{right}. It is a shift 7 units
\emph{left}, because $x + 7 = 0$ at $x = -7$. Her ``down 5'' was correct.\par
Because she put the vertex at $x = 7$, she evaluated at the wrong place. Correct
substitution:
\[ g(-7) = (-7 + 7)^2 - 5 = 0 - 5 \]
$\ans{-5}$\par
\textit{If they got 191:} that is $g(7)$ --- Mia's value. It is a real point on the
parabola, just not the lowest one, which is exactly why a wrong $h$ can go unnoticed.}

\problem{\textbf{Reflect, right 2, up 25; find the $x$-intercepts.}\par
Reflection makes $a = -1$; right 2 gives $(x - 2)$; up 25 adds 25:
\[ y = -(x - 2)^2 + 25 \]
Set it to zero:
\begin{align*}
-(x - 2)^2 + 25 &= 0\\
(x - 2)^2 &= 25\\
x - 2 &= \pm 5\\
x &= 7 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = -3
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{x = -3 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 7} \]
\textit{Why the reflection does not move the axis:} reflecting over the $x$-axis replaces
each output $y$ with $-y$ and leaves every input untouched. The axis of symmetry is a
statement about inputs, so it stays at $x = 2$ --- and indeed $-3$ and $7$ are each 5 units
from 2.}

\problem{\textbf{Error analysis --- Ravi's study card.} \quad (open response --- read the
explanations)\par
\textbf{R1 is wrong.} The shift is not in the direction of the printed sign. The vertex
lands where the squared factor is zero, so $(x + 4)$ puts the vertex at $x = -4$: the graph
moves \emph{left} 4. \textit{Corrected:} ``In $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ the graph shifts to
$x = h$, the value that makes $x - h$ zero. So $(x + 4)$ means $h = -4$: left 4.''\par
\textbf{R2 is correct.} $k$ is added to every output after the squaring, so a positive $k$
raises the whole graph and a negative $k$ lowers it. Here the printed sign and the
direction agree, because nothing is subtracted \emph{from} $y$.\par
\textbf{R3 is wrong.} $a$ multiplies the outputs; it does nothing to the inputs. A negative
$a$ flips the parabola upside down about its own vertex, but the vertex --- and therefore
both shifts --- stays exactly where $h$ and $k$ put it. \textit{Corrected:} ``When $a < 0$
the parabola opens downward and its vertex becomes a maximum instead of a minimum; the
shifts are unchanged.''\par
\textit{Full credit} requires naming R1 and R3 as wrong, an explanation that appeals to
``what makes the parentheses zero'' (R1) and to ``$a$ scales outputs, not inputs'' (R3),
and a corrected wording for each. Naming the wrong rules with no reason is partial credit.
$\ans{\text{R1 wrong, R2 correct, R3 wrong}}$}
