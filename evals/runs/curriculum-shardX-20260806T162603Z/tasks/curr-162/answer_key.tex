\problem{40 seats, 14 taken. What percent are taken?}

\textbf{Solution:} The part is 14 and the whole is 40, so the rate is
$\dfrac{14}{40} = 0.35$, and $0.35 \times 100 = 35$ percent. \ans{35}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{24 questions, 18 correct. What percent is correct?}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{18}{24} = 0.75$, so the rate is
$0.75 \times 100 = 75$ percent. \ans{75}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{12 tickets is $25\%$ of the printing. How many were printed?}

\textbf{Solution:} Here the part is known and the whole is missing. Twenty-five
percent is $0.25$, so $0.25\,x = 12$ and $x = 12 \div 0.25 = 48$
tickets. \ans{48}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{45 seedlings, 27 flowered. What percent flowered?}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{27}{45} = 0.6$, and $0.6 \times 100 = 60$
percent. \ans{60}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{63 kilometres is $70\%$ of the trail. How long is the trail?}

\textbf{Solution:} The whole is missing again: $0.70\,x = 63$, so
$x = 63 \div 0.70 = 90$ kilometres. \ans{90}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{60}{100}\,x = 15$ for the class size.}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{60}{100} = 0.6$, so the equation is $0.6\,x = 15$.
Divide both sides by $0.6$: $x = 15 \div 0.6 = 25$ students. Check:
$60\%$ of 25 is 15. \ans{25}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{56 marbles, 7 green. What percent are green?}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{7}{56} = 0.125$, and $0.125 \times 100 = 12.5$
percent. A percent may be a decimal. \ans{12.5}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{45 books is $12.5\%$ of the cart. How many books are on the cart?}

\textbf{Solution:} $12.5\%$ is $0.125$, so $0.125\,x = 45$ and
$x = 45 \div 0.125 = 360$ books. \ans{360}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{35}{100}\,x = 91$ for the stock size.}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{35}{100} = 0.35$, so $0.35\,x = 91$ and
$x = 91 \div 0.35 = 260$ shirts. Check: $35\%$ of 260 is 91. \ans{260}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{60 birds counted, 17 finches. What percent, to the nearest tenth?}

\textbf{Solution:} $\dfrac{17}{60} = 0.28333\ldots$, so the rate is
$0.28333\ldots \times 100 = 28.333\ldots$ percent, which rounds to $28.3$
percent. \ans{28.3}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} dividing the total by the part instead of the part
by the total gives $\dfrac{60}{17} \times 100 \approx 352.9$ percent --- a
percent above 100 for a part of the flock, which is the tell.

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{An 84 dollar deposit is $20\%$ of a bicycle's price. Find the price and
the amount still owed.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $0.20\,x = 84$, so $x = 84 \div 0.20 = 420$
dollars. \ans{420}

(b) The deposit is already paid, so what remains is
$420 - 84 = 336$ dollars. \ans{336}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{45 students are $30\%$ of those asked. Solve for the number asked, then
explain the division shortcut.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $\dfrac{30}{100}\,x = 45$ becomes $0.30\,x = 45$, so
$x = 45 \div 0.30 = 150$ students. \ans{150}

(b) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The equation says the unknown whole was
multiplied by $0.30$ to produce 45. To undo a multiplication you divide by the
same factor, so $45 \div 0.30$ \emph{is} the solving step --- written without
bothering to name the unknown. Full credit needs that idea (dividing undoes the
multiplying), in the student's own words, not merely the observation that both
routes give 150.
