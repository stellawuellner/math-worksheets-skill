\problem{\textbf{$6 - 2$.}
Put a finger on square $6$. Hop left once to $5$, twice to $4$. Two hops taken,
so the finger stops on $4$.
$\ans{4}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Listen for:} a child who lands on $5$ counted the starting square as the
first hop. Say ``six'' on the square, then start counting at the first hop.}

\problem{\textbf{$8 - 3$.}
Start on $8$. Hop left: $7$, $6$, $5$. Three hops, so the answer is the square
under the finger.
$\ans{5}$}

\problem{\textbf{$10 - 4$.}
Start on $10$ and hop left four times: $9$, $8$, $7$, $6$.
$\ans{6}$}

\problem{\textbf{$9 - 7$.}
Counting back seven times is a long way. The two numbers are close, so count
\emph{up} instead: start on $7$, hop right to $8$, hop right to $9$. That is two
hops, and the number of hops is the difference.
$\ans{2}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Why it works:} $7$ and $2$ more make $9$, so $9$ take away $7$ leaves $2$.}

\problem{\textbf{$7 - 5$.}
Start on $7$ and hop back five times: $6$, $5$, $4$, $3$, $2$. The circled
square is the answer.
$\ans{2}$
\par\smallskip
Counting up from $5$ to $7$ gives the same $2$ --- a good chance to show the
child that both routes agree.}

\problem{\textbf{$9 - \underline{\hspace{0.8cm}} = 4$.}
Here the number of hops is missing, not the landing square. Put one finger on
$9$ and one on $4$, then count the hops between them: $8$, $7$, $6$, $5$, $4$.
That is five hops, so five is what was taken away.
$\ans{5}$
\par\smallskip
Check by hopping back: $9$ hop back $5$ lands on $4$. Correct.}

\problem{\textbf{$10 - 6$.}
Start on $6$ and hop right until the finger reaches $10$: $7$, $8$, $9$, $10$.
Four hops.
$\ans{4}$}

\problem{\textbf{Eight frogs, five hop away.}
The story says take away, so start on $8$ and hop back five squares: $7$, $6$,
$5$, $4$, $3$. Three frogs are still on the log.
$\ans{3}$
\par\smallskip
Accept a drawing of five crossed-out frogs as well --- it is the same idea.}

\problem{\textbf{$\underline{\hspace{0.8cm}} - 3 = 5$.}
This time the \emph{start} is missing. Undo the subtraction: begin on the
answer, $5$, and hop \emph{up} three squares: $6$, $7$, $8$. Starting on $8$ and
hopping back $3$ does land on $5$, so the start number is right.
$\ans{8}$}

\problem{\textbf{Mia had ten stickers and gave eight away.}
Counting back eight times invites a miscount. The two numbers are close, so
count up: start on $8$ and hop right to $9$, then to $10$. Two hops, so Mia has
two stickers left.
$\ans{2}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{The point of the problem:} choosing to count up instead of back is the
skill, not the answer. Praise the choice.}
