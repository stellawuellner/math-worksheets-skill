\problem{Flour to milk is 2 to 3. Complete the milk row for 4, 6, and 8 cups of
flour.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each column multiplies the base column $(2, 3)$ by the same
number: $4 = 2 \times 2$, $6 = 2 \times 3$, $8 = 2 \times 4$. Multiply the milk
value 3 by the same multipliers: $3 \times 2$, $3 \times 3$, $3 \times 4$, giving
\ans{6}, \ans{9}, \ans{12}

\problem{Wheels to cars is 4 to 1. Complete the wheels row for 2, 3, and 5 cars.}

\textbf{Solution:} One car has 4 wheels, so multiply the number of cars by 4:
$2 \times 4$, $3 \times 4$, $5 \times 4$, giving \ans{8}, \ans{12}, \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{3 notebooks cost 6 dollars. Find the cost of one notebook, then of 5.}

\textbf{Solution:} Divide both quantities by 3 to build the unit column:
$6 \div 3 = 2$ dollars for one notebook. \ans{2}

Then multiply the unit cost by 5: $2 \times 5 = 10$ dollars. \ans{10}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{4 baskets hold 10 melons. Complete the melon row for 2, 8, and 12
baskets.}

\textbf{Solution:} For 2 baskets, divide both quantities by 2: $10 \div 2 = 5$.
\ans{5}

For 8 baskets, multiply the base column by 2: $10 \times 2 = 20$. \ans{20}

For 12 baskets, multiply by 3: $10 \times 3 = 30$. \ans{30}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Find the missing value: $\dfrac{3}{7} = \dfrac{?}{28}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The denominator was multiplied by 4, because $7 \times 4 =
28$. Multiply the numerator by the same 4: $3 \times 4 = 12$. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{4 tickets cost 36 dollars. Find the cost for one person, then for 6.}

\textbf{Solution:} Unit column: divide both quantities by 4, so one ticket costs
$36 \div 4 = 9$ dollars. \ans{9}

Six tickets: $9 \times 6 = 54$ dollars. \ans{54}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Red to white is 6 to 8. Complete the white row for 9 and 15 parts red.}

\textbf{Solution:} From 6 to 9 the multiplier is $9 \div 6 = 1.5$, so white is
$8 \times 1.5 = 12$. \ans{12}

From 6 to 15 the multiplier is $15 \div 6 = 2.5$, so white is $8 \times 2.5 =
20$. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Find the missing value: $\dfrac{9}{12} = \dfrac{15}{?}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The numerator was scaled by $15 \div 9 = \frac{5}{3}$, so the
denominator is $12 \times \frac{5}{3} = 20$. Check: $\frac{15}{20}$ and
$\frac{9}{12}$ both simplify to $\frac{3}{4}$. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Water to rice is 10 to 4. Complete the water row for one cup and for 7
cups of rice.}

\textbf{Solution:} Unit column: divide both by 4, so one cup of rice takes
$10 \div 4 = 2.5$ cups of water. \ans{2.5}

Seven cups: $2.5 \times 7 = 17.5$ cups of water. \ans{17.5}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Every row shows the ratio 12 to 8. Fill in the missing first quantity
(paired with 10) and the missing second quantity (paired with 18).}

\textbf{Solution:} The ratio $\frac{12}{8}$ simplifies to $\frac{3}{2}$, so the
first quantity is always $\frac{3}{2}$ of the second.

Paired with 10: first quantity $= \frac{3}{2} \times 10 = 15$. \ans{15}

Paired with 18: second quantity $= 18 \div \frac{3}{2} = 12$. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Machine A: 180 pages in 4 minutes. Machine B: 250 pages in 5 minutes.
Build one-minute columns and decide which is faster.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Machine A: divide both by 4: $180 \div 4 = 45$ pages per
minute. \ans{45}

(b) Machine B: divide both by 5: $250 \div 5 = 50$ pages per minute. \ans{50}

(c) Comparing unit rates, $45 < 50$, so Machine B is
faster. \ans{45 < 50\text{, Machine B}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Juice to soda is 3 to 5. Complete the soda row for 6 and 9 cups of
juice, then find the juice needed for 24 cups of punch in total.}

\textbf{Solution:} For 6 cups of juice the multiplier is 2, so soda is
$5 \times 2 = 10$. \ans{10}

For 9 cups of juice the multiplier is 3, so soda is $5 \times 3 = 15$. \ans{15}

For the last question, one batch of $3 + 5 = 8$ total cups contains 3 cups of
juice. To reach 24 total cups, multiply the whole batch by $24 \div 8 = 3$:
juice $= 3 \times 3 = 9$ cups. \ans{9}
