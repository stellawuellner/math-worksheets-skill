\problem{Lift limit 450 kg, boxes of 25 kg. Inequality for $b$ and its
solution.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``No more than'' allows the limit itself, so the sign is
$\le$: \ $25b \le 450$. Dividing both sides by 25 keeps the direction because 25
is positive: $b \le 18$. The test table agrees --- 18 boxes weigh exactly 450 kg
and are allowed, 19 boxes weigh 475 kg and are not. \ans{b \leq 18}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{A film for people over 12. Inequality for the age $y$.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Over 12'' does not include 12 itself, so the sign is
strict: $y > 12$, which is already solved. A twelve-year-old may not
watch. \ans{y > 12}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Pass with at least 36 points, 4 points a question. Inequality for $q$.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``At least'' includes the boundary, so $4q \ge 36$. Divide
both sides by 4: $q \ge 9$. Nine correct questions score exactly 36 and
pass. \ans{q \geq 9}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Taxi: 4 dollars to start plus 3 dollars per km, at most 25 dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The distance charge is $3k$
and the start charge is paid once, and ``at most'' allows spending the whole 25
dollars: $3k + 4 \le 25$. Accept $4 + 3k \le 25$ or $25 \ge 3k + 4$. A strict
$<$ is wrong, and so is an equation with an equals sign.

(b) Subtract 4 from both sides: $3k \le 21$. Divide by 3:
$k \le 7$ kilometres. \ans{k \leq 7}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Parcel under 20 kg, 6 kg already packed. Inequality for $m$.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Less than'' excludes the boundary, so
$m + 6 < 20$. Subtract 6 from both sides: $m < 14$ kilograms. Exactly 14
kilograms of contents would make the parcel weigh 20 kg, which is not
allowed. \ans{m < 14}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Badge needs at least 60 km walked; 23 km already done.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``At least'' includes the boundary:
$d + 23 \ge 60$. Subtract 23: $d \ge 37$ kilometres. The table agrees --- 37
further kilometres reaches exactly 60 and earns the badge, 36 does not. \ans{d \geq 37}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Shelf holds at most 45 kg; bookends 3 kg, each book $\frac{3}{2}$ kg.}

\textbf{Solution:} The bookends are on the shelf once and each book repeats, so
$\frac{3}{2}n + 3 \le 45$. Subtract 3: $\frac{3}{2}n \le 42$. Multiply both
sides by $\frac{2}{3}$: $n \le 28$ books. \ans{n \leq 28}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Fewer than 24 players in 6 equal teams. Inequality for $p$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Six teams of $p$ players is $6p$ players in all, and
``fewer than'' is strict: $6p < 24$. Divide both sides by 6:
$p < 4$ players on a team. \ans{p < 4}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Pool under 900 litres; 150 already in, tap adds 30 litres a minute.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The tap contributes $30t$
litres and the 150 litres are already there, and ``fewer than'' is strict:
$30t + 150 < 900$. Accept $150 + 30t < 900$. A non-strict $\le$ is wrong, and
$30(t + 150)$ is wrong --- the 150 litres are not added every minute.

(b) Subtract 150: $30t < 750$. Divide by 30:
$t < 25$ minutes. \ans{t < 25}

\vspace{0.25cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem{Bus tour: 30 dollars for the driver plus 12 dollars a person, at most
210 dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The per-person charge is
$12n$ and the driver's 30 dollars is paid once, and ``at most'' allows spending
all 210 dollars: $12n + 30 \le 210$. Accept $30 + 12n \le 210$. A strict $<$ is
wrong.

(b) Subtract 30: $12n \le 180$. Divide by 12:
$n \le 15$ people. \ans{n \leq 15}

(c) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The solution set contains every real number up
to and including 15 --- including $7.4$ and $-3$ --- but a group size must be a
whole number and cannot be negative, so only the whole numbers from 0 (or 1) up
to 15 are actually possible. Full credit needs the whole-number point and the
observation that the solution set on its own does not carry that restriction;
the situation does.
