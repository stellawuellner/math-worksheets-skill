\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $6x^2 - 15x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Checklist step one: is there a common factor? The coefficients
$6$ and $15$ share $3$, and both terms carry an $x$, so the greatest common
factor is $3x$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6x^2 - 15x = 3x(2x - 5)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The bracket has two terms but no subtraction of two squares,
and it is degree one, so nothing factors further. Technique: GCF only.
\ans{3x(2x-5)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $x^2 + 9x + 20$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor. Three terms with leading coefficient $1$, so
look for a factor pair of $20$ that adds to $9$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4$ and $5$: $4 \cdot 5 = 20$ and $4 + 5 = 9$ $\checkmark$

\textbf{Step 3.} $x^2 + 9x + 20 = (x + 4)(x + 5)$. Technique: monic trinomial.
\ans{(x+4)(x+5)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $x^2 - 64$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor. Two terms with a subtraction sign between
them: test the difference of squares.

\textbf{Step 2.} $x^2 = (x)^2$ and $64 = (8)^2$, so $A = x$ and $B = 8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $A^2 - B^2 = (A - B)(A + B)$ gives $(x - 8)(x + 8)$. Expanding
back, the middle terms $+8x$ and $-8x$ cancel $\checkmark$ Technique:
difference of squares. \ans{(x-8)(x+8)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $3x^2 - 27$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} It looks like a difference of squares, but $3x^2$ is not a
perfect square, so the pattern does not apply yet. Checklist step one first:
both terms are divisible by $3$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $3x^2 - 27 = 3(x^2 - 9)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Now the bracket \emph{is} a difference of squares with $A = x$
and $B = 3$, so $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$ and the complete factorization is
$3(x - 3)(x + 3)$. Technique: GCF, then difference of squares.
\ans{3(x-3)(x+3)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $2x^2 + 7x + 3$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor ($3$ is odd), and the leading coefficient is
$2$, not $1$ --- so factor by grouping on the product of the outer
coefficients.

\textbf{Step 2.} That product is $2 \cdot 3 = 6$, and we need a factor pair of
$6$ adding to $7$: those are $6$ and $1$. Split the middle term:
$2x^2 + 6x + x + 3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Group: $2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (2x + 1)(x + 3)$. Technique:
non-monic trinomial by grouping. \ans{(2x+1)(x+3)}}

\problem{\textbf{Flower bed of area $x^2 - 3x - 28$ square feet.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A rectangle's area is length times width, so factoring the
area into two brackets is exactly what produces the two side lengths. No common
factor, leading coefficient $1$: look for a factor pair of $-28$ that adds to
$-3$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The constant is negative, so the pair has opposite signs and
the larger one is negative: $-7$ and $+4$ give $-7 \cdot 4 = -28$ and
$-7 + 4 = -3$ $\checkmark$

\textbf{Step 3.} $x^2 - 3x - 28 = (x - 7)(x + 4)$, so the bed measures $x - 7$
feet by $x + 4$ feet. Technique: monic trinomial. \ans{(x-7)(x+4)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $4x^2 - 12x + 9$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor ($9$ is odd). Three terms whose first and last
are perfect squares --- test the perfect-square pattern before trying pairs.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4x^2 = (2x)^2$ and $9 = (3)^2$, so $A = 2x$ and $B = 3$. The
pattern demands a middle term of $2AB = 2 \cdot 2x \cdot 3 = 12x$, and the
trinomial has $-12x$: the size matches and the sign is negative.

\textbf{Step 3.} A negative middle term means the bracket is a difference:
$4x^2 - 12x + 9 = (2x - 3)^2$. Technique: perfect-square trinomial.
\ans{(2x-3)^2}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $2x^3 - 18x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two terms, but neither is a perfect square as written --- and
both are divisible by $2$ and both contain $x$. The greatest common factor is
$2x$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2x^3 - 18x = 2x(x^2 - 9)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The bracket is a difference of squares with $A = x$, $B = 3$:
$x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$. Complete: $2x(x - 3)(x + 3)$. Technique: GCF, then
difference of squares. \ans{2x(x-3)(x+3)}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $6x^2 - 7x - 3$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor, leading coefficient $6$: grouping again.
The product of the outer coefficients is $6 \cdot (-3) = -18$.

\textbf{Step 2.} A factor pair of $-18$ adding to $-7$ is $-9$ and $+2$. Split
the middle term: $6x^2 - 9x + 2x - 3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Group: $3x(2x - 3) + 1(2x - 3) = (2x - 3)(3x + 1)$. Expanding
back gives $6x^2 + 2x - 9x - 3 = 6x^2 - 7x - 3$ $\checkmark$ Technique:
non-monic trinomial by grouping. \ans{(2x-3)(3x+1)}}

\problem{\textbf{Error analysis: is $2x^2 + 8x + 8 = (2x + 4)(x + 2)$ complete?}

\textbf{Step 1 (part a).} Multiply the student's answer out:
$(2x + 4)(x + 2) = 2x^2 + 4x + 4x + 8 = 2x^2 + 8x + 8$. It really does equal the
original, so expanding alone cannot expose the fault --- this is why
``completely'' matters.

\textbf{Step 2 (part b).} The first bracket still has a common factor:
$2x + 4 = 2(x + 2)$. The student skipped checklist step one, so a factor of $2$
was left buried inside a bracket instead of pulled to the front.

\textbf{Step 3 (part c).} Take the GCF out first: $2x^2 + 8x + 8 = 2(x^2 + 4x +
4)$, and $x^2 + 4x + 4$ is a perfect square, $(x + 2)^2$. So the complete
factorization is $2(x + 2)^2$.

\textit{Manual review (part b):} accept any sentence saying that $2x + 4$ still
contains a common factor of $2$, or equivalently that the answer is a correct
product but not fully factored. \ans{2x^2 + 8x + 8 \quad \text{and} \quad 2(x+2)^2}}

\problem{\textbf{Factor completely: $x^4 - 81$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No common factor. Two terms with a subtraction sign, and both
are squares: $x^4 = (x^2)^2$ and $81 = (9)^2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Difference of squares with $A = x^2$, $B = 9$:
$x^4 - 81 = (x^2 - 9)(x^2 + 9)$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check each bracket again. $x^2 - 9$ is itself a difference of
squares, $(x - 3)(x + 3)$; $x^2 + 9$ is a \emph{sum} of squares and does not
factor over the integers, so the work stops there. Technique: difference of
squares, twice. \ans{(x-3)(x+3)(x^2+9)}}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis challenge: factor completely $4x^3 + 4x^2 - 24x$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Checklist step one: the coefficients $4$, $4$ and $24$ all
share $4$, and every term carries an $x$, so the greatest common factor is
$4x$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4x^3 + 4x^2 - 24x = 4x(x^2 + x - 6)$. The bracket now has
leading coefficient $1$, which is the easy trinomial case --- taking the GCF out
first is what turned a messy cubic into it.

\textbf{Step 3.} A factor pair of $-6$ adding to $+1$ is $+3$ and $-2$, so
$x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$ and the complete factorization is
$4x(x + 3)(x - 2)$. Technique: GCF, then monic trinomial.
\ans{4x(x+3)(x-2)}}
