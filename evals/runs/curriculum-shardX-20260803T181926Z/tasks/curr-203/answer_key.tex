\problem{\textbf{Between which whole numbers is $\sqrt{47}$?}

\textbf{Step 1.} $47$ is not a perfect square, so the root is irrational and
has to be trapped between the two perfect squares on either side of it.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6^2 = 36$ and $7^2 = 49$. Since $36 < 47 < 49$, taking square
roots (which preserves order for positive numbers) gives
$6 < \sqrt{47} < 7$. \ans{6 < \sqrt{47} < 7}

\textbf{Step 3.} On the line, $47$ is much nearer $49$ than $36$, so the dot
$A$ belongs close to $7$ --- just left of it, around $6.9$.}

\problem{\textbf{Between which whole numbers is $\sqrt{150}$?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Same method, higher up the line: find the perfect squares that
bracket $150$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $12^2 = 144$ and $13^2 = 169$, and $144 < 150 < 169$, so
\ans{12 < \sqrt{150} < 13}

\textbf{Step 3.} $150$ sits only a little above $144$, so the dot $B$ belongs
just to the right of $12$ --- about a quarter of the way to $13$.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{30}$ to the nearest tenth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Bracket first: $5^2 = 25$ and $6^2 = 36$, so the root is
between $5$ and $6$. Now test tenths by squaring, since squaring is the only
tool available without a calculator.

\textbf{Step 2.} $5.4^2 = 29.16$ (too small) and $5.5^2 = 30.25$ (too big), so
$5.4 < \sqrt{30} < 5.5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $30$ is $0.84$ above $29.16$ but only $0.25$ below $30.25$, so
the root is nearer the upper tenth: \ans{\sqrt{30} \approx 5.5}

\textit{On the line:} the dot goes just left of the $5.5$ tick.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare $\sqrt{20}$ and $4.5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} One number is irrational and one is a terminating decimal, so
put them in the same form: square the decimal and compare with the radicand.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4.5^2 = 20.25$, and $20 < 20.25$. Taking square roots keeps
the order, so $\sqrt{20} < 4.5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} \ans{\sqrt{20} < 4.5}

\textit{On the line:} $\sqrt{20} \approx 4.47$, so its dot sits between the
$4.4$ and $4.5$ ticks --- close to $4.5$ but definitely to its left.
Squaring the decimal, rather than estimating the root, is what makes the
comparison exact.}

\problem{\textbf{$\sqrt{83}$ to the nearest tenth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $9^2 = 81$ and $10^2 = 100$, so $9 < \sqrt{83} < 10$, and $83$
is close to the bottom of that gap.

\textbf{Step 2.} $9.1^2 = 82.81$ (too small) and $9.2^2 = 84.64$ (too big), so
$9.1 < \sqrt{83} < 9.2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $83$ is only $0.19$ above $82.81$ but $1.64$ below $84.64$, so
the root rounds down: \ans{\sqrt{83} \approx 9.1}

\textit{On the line:} the dot sits just right of the $9.1$ tick.}

\problem{\textbf{Which of $\sqrt{55}$, $\sqrt{58}$, $\sqrt{62}$ belongs at $P$?}

\textbf{Step 1.} $P$ is the sixth tick past $7.0$, so $P$ is at $7.6$. Rather
than estimate three roots, square the position: whichever radicand is closest
to $7.6^2$ is the one that belongs there.

\textbf{Step 2.} $7.6^2 = 57.76$ and $7.7^2 = 59.29$. Only $58$ lies between
those, so $\sqrt{58}$ is the root whose value rounds to $7.6$.

\textbf{Step 3.} \ans{\sqrt{58} \approx 7.6}

\textit{Common wrong answers:} $7.4$ --- $\sqrt{55}$, which lands two ticks
left of $P$; and $7.9$ --- $\sqrt{62}$, which lands to the right of $P$.
Squaring the tick value tests all three candidates at once.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $2.7$, $\sqrt{8}$, $3.1$, $\sqrt{10}$ from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Mixed rationals and irrationals cannot be compared by
appearance, so estimate each root to a tenth first and read the order off the
line.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2.8^2 = 7.84$ and $2.9^2 = 8.41$, so
$\sqrt{8} \approx 2.8$. And $3.1^2 = 9.61$ while $3.2^2 = 10.24$, so
$\sqrt{10} \approx 3.2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Placing $2.7$, $2.8$, $3.1$ and $3.2$ on the line in that
order gives \ans{2.7 < \sqrt{8} < 3.1 < \sqrt{10}}

\textit{Watch the near miss:} $3.1$ and $\sqrt{10}$ look close, but
$3.1^2 = 9.61 < 10$, so $\sqrt{10}$ is genuinely the larger of the two.}

\problem{\textbf{Plot $\sqrt{41}$ and $\sqrt{45}$ to the nearest tenth.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both radicands lie between $36$ and $49$, so both roots lie
between $6$ and $7$ --- the whole number line printed for this problem.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6.4^2 = 40.96$ and $6.5^2 = 42.25$, so $41$ is barely above
$40.96$: \ans{\sqrt{41} \approx 6.4}

\textbf{Step 3.} $6.7^2 = 44.89$ and $6.8^2 = 46.24$, so $45$ is barely above
$44.89$: \ans{\sqrt{45} \approx 6.7}

\textit{On the line:} both dots sit just to the right of their ticks, which is
the visual signature of a radicand only slightly above a tenth's square.}

\problem{\textbf{Square patio of area $132$ square feet --- side to the nearest
tenth of a foot.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A square's side is the square root of its area, so the side
length in feet is $\sqrt{132}$, and $132$ is not a perfect square.

\textbf{Step 2.} $11^2 = 121$ and $12^2 = 144$, so the side is between $11$ and
$12$ feet. Test tenths: $11.4^2 = 129.96$ (too small) and $11.5^2 = 132.25$
(too big).

\textbf{Step 3.} $132$ is $2.04$ above $129.96$ but only $0.25$ below
$132.25$, so the side rounds up: \ans{\sqrt{132} \approx 11.5\ \text{ft}}

\textit{Sense check:} $11.5$ feet squared is about $132.25$ square feet, which
is within a quarter of a square foot of the real patio --- a sensible
carpentry-grade estimate.}

\problem{\textbf{Why Nadia's rounding rule fails for $\sqrt{50}$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Nadia rounded the \emph{radicand} to the nearest perfect
square and then took its root. That is not the same operation as rounding the
root, and the two disagree whenever the radicand sits near the low end of its
bracket --- which is exactly this case.

\textbf{Step 2.} Bracket properly: $7^2 = 49$ and $8^2 = 64$, so
$7 < \sqrt{50} < 8$. Now test tenths: $7.0^2 = 49$ (too small) and
$7.1^2 = 50.41$ (too big), so $7.0 < \sqrt{50} < 7.1$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $50$ is a full $1$ above $49$ but only $0.41$ below $50.41$,
so the root is nearer the upper tenth: \ans{\sqrt{50} \approx 7.1}

\textbf{Step 4.} The explanation: squaring stretches the gaps, so a radicand
that is only a little above a perfect square already has a root a noticeable
distance above that square's root. Nadia's rule throws away the size of the
gap, and the number line shows $\sqrt{50}$ sitting clearly right of the
$7.0$ tick.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $7.0$ --- the root of the nearer perfect square,
reported instead of an estimate between the brackets.}
