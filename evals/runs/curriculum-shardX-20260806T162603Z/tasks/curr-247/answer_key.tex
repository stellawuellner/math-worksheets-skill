\problem{Fundraiser plot: give the greatest number of donations, then draw a line of fit.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The topmost point is the week with $7$ posters. \ans{28}

\textbf{(b) Line of fit (instructor-judged).} A correct line is one straight ruler line running
from lower left to upper right through the middle of the cloud, with about as many points above
it as below and none far away from it. A line joining only the first and last points, a curve, or
a line drawn along one edge of the cloud does not earn full credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(1, 6)$ and $(5, 18)$. Find its slope.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Take the two points that lie exactly on the drawn line, not two data
points: $m = \dfrac{18 - 6}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{12}{4}$. \ans{m = 3}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(0, 5)$ and $(4, 17)$. Find its slope and predict at
$x = 7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $m = \dfrac{17 - 5}{4 - 0} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$. The line meets the
vertical axis at $5$, so its equation is $y = 3x + 5$, and at $x = 7$ that gives
$3(7) + 5 = 21 + 5$.
\[ \boxed{m = 3 \qquad y = 26 \text{ at } x = 7} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Savings plot: find the mean of the plotted values, then draw a line of fit through the
middle of the data.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Add the six values and divide by six:
\[ \frac{5 + 8 + 12 + 15 + 19 + 25}{6} = \frac{84}{6} \]
\ans{14}

\textbf{(b) Line of fit (instructor-judged).} A good line rises from left to right and passes
close to the middle of the data --- around the middle month, at a height near the mean of $14$ ---
with a similar number of points on each side. A line drawn only through the first and last points
tilts too steeply and does not earn full credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(2, 30)$ and $(8, 12)$. Find its slope.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $m = \dfrac{12 - 30}{8 - 2} = \dfrac{-18}{6}$. The negative sign matches
the picture: the line falls as you move right. \ans{m = -3}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(1, 8)$ and $(6, 28)$ and crosses the vertical axis at
$4$. Find the slope and predict at $x = 10$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $m = \dfrac{28 - 8}{6 - 1} = \dfrac{20}{5} = 4$, so the equation is
$y = 4x + 4$. At $x = 10$: $4(10) + 4 = 40 + 4$.
\[ \boxed{m = 4 \qquad y = 44 \text{ at } x = 10} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(0, 7)$ and $(5, 22)$. Find the slope, write the equation,
and evaluate at $x = 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $m = \dfrac{22 - 7}{5 - 0} = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$. The first point is on the
vertical axis, so $b = 7$ can be read straight off. At $x = 8$: $3(8) + 7 = 24 + 7$.
\[ \boxed{m = 3 \qquad y = 31 \text{ at } x = 8} \]

\textbf{(b) Equation (instructor-judged).} The equation is $y = 3x + 7$. Accept an equivalent
rearrangement. An equation with the $3$ and the $7$ swapped, or with the intercept missing, is
wrong even if the slope was found correctly.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$y = 6x + 48$. Predict the score after $7$ hours of revision.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Substitute the hours for $x$: $6(7) + 48 = 42 + 48$. \ans{90}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$y = 6x + 48$. Predict at $4$ hours, and say what the slope means.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad $6(4) + 48 = 24 + 48$. \ans{72}

\textbf{(b) Interpretation (instructor-judged).} The slope $6$ is a rate: according to the line,
each extra hour of revision goes with about $6$ more marks on the test. Accept any wording that
attaches the $6$ to one extra hour and to marks. Calling it only ``the steepness'' misses the
context and earns partial credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A line of fit passes through $(2, 26)$ and $(6, 14)$. Find the slope, the equation, and
the value at $x = 9$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $m = \dfrac{14 - 26}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{-12}{4} = -3$. Substituting the
point $(2, 26)$ gives $26 = -3(2) + b$, so $b = 32$. At $x = 9$: $-3(9) + 32 = -27 + 32$.
\[ \boxed{m = -3 \qquad y = 5 \text{ at } x = 9} \]

\textbf{(b) Equation (instructor-judged).} The equation is $y = -3x + 32$; accept an equivalent
rearrangement. A positive slope, or an intercept that does not fit both given points, is wrong.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Bean plant, days $1$ to $10$, line of fit $y = 2.5x + 12$. Predict day $6$, then judge a
prediction for day $40$.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad $2.5(6) + 12 = 15 + 12$. \ans{27}

\textbf{(b) Caution (instructor-judged).} The measurements only cover days $1$ to $10$, so day
$40$ is far outside the range the line was fitted to. Nothing in the data says the plant keeps
growing at the same steady rate for another month --- real plants level off. Accept any wording
that contrasts day $40$ with the range of the collected data.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Exercise against resting pulse, line of fit $y = -0.4x + 78$. Predict at $25$ minutes,
give the change per $10$ minutes, and judge causation.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad At $25$ minutes: $-0.4(25) + 78 = -10 + 78 = 68$. The slope is the change
per single minute, so over $10$ extra minutes the change is $-0.4 \times 10$.
\[ \boxed{\text{pulse} = 68 \qquad \text{change} = -4} \]

\textbf{(c) Judgement (instructor-judged).} A correct response says the line shows only an
association between exercise time and resting pulse in this class, and that an association is not
proof of cause. Students who are already fitter may both exercise more and have lower pulses, and
no experiment was carried out. Accept any wording that separates association from cause.
