\problem{Multiply both sides of $6 > -2$ by $-4$.

\textbf{Solution.} A negative times a negative is positive, so the right side is
$-4 \times (-2) = \ans{8}$
\noindent The left side is $-4 \times 6 = -24$. The new pair is $-24$ and $8$,
and $-24 < 8$, so the true statement is $-24 < 8$: the original ``$>$'' became
``$<$''. Multiplying both sides by a negative reversed the symbol.
\emph{Common wrong answer:} $-8$, from keeping the sign of the first factor.}

\problem{Solve $-4x > 12$.

\textbf{Solution.} Divide both sides by $-4$. Because $-4$ is negative, the
symbol reverses on that step:
\[ \frac{-4x}{-4} \;\overset{\text{flip}}{<}\; \frac{12}{-4} \]
$\ans{x < -3}$
\noindent \emph{Check:} $x = -10$ gives $-4(-10) = 40 > 12$ $\checkmark$, and
$x = 0$ gives $0 > 12$, which is false --- exactly what a solution set of
$x < -3$ predicts.}

\problem{Find and fix Priya's work on $-7x \le 21$.

\textbf{(a) Test her answer.} Priya claims $x \le -3$, so $x = -10$ should work.
Substituting into the original: $-7(-10) = 70$, and $70 \le 21$ is
\emph{false}. So her solution set contains numbers that are not solutions.

\textbf{(b) The error.} She divided both sides by $-7$ but kept the ``$\le$''
pointing the same way; dividing by a negative reverses the symbol.

\textbf{(c) Correct solution.} $\dfrac{-7x}{-7} \overset{\text{flip}}{\ge}
\dfrac{21}{-7}$, so $\ans{x \ge -3}$
\noindent \emph{Check:} $x = 0$ gives $-7(0) = 0 \le 21$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{Solve $-\dfrac{x}{3} < 2$.

\textbf{Solution.} The coefficient of $x$ is $-\tfrac{1}{3}$. Multiply both
sides by $-3$; that is a negative multiplier, so the symbol reverses:
\[ (-3)\left(-\frac{x}{3}\right) \;\overset{\text{flip}}{>}\; (-3)(2) \]
$\ans{x > -6}$
\noindent \emph{Grading note:} a student who multiplies by $3$ first (getting
$-x < 6$) and then by $-1$ (getting $x > -6$) is also correct --- the flip
happens on the $-1$ step. Only one of the two moves is a negative multiplier.}

\problem{Check the boundary of $-5x - 2 \le 18$ at $x = -4$.

\textbf{Solution.} Substitute and evaluate, minding the double negative:
$-5(-4) - 2 = 20 - 2 = \ans{18}$
\noindent The expression equals exactly $18$, so $x = -4$ is the boundary and
the solution set is $x \ge -4$ (dividing $-5x \le 20$ by $-5$ flips the symbol).
\emph{Common wrong answer:} $-22$, from writing $-5(-4)$ as $-20$.}

\problem{Solve $4 - 3x > 19$.

\textbf{Solution.} Subtract $4$ from both sides --- subtraction never changes
the symbol:
\[ -3x > 15 \]
Now divide by $-3$, which does:
\[ \frac{-3x}{-3} \;\overset{\text{flip}}{<}\; \frac{15}{-3} \]
$\ans{x < -5}$
\noindent \emph{Check:} $x = -10$ gives $4 - 3(-10) = 34 > 19$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{Find and fix Dev's work on $8 - 2x \ge 14$.

\textbf{(a) Which step is wrong.} Step 1 ($8 - 2x \ge 14 \to -2x \ge 6$) is
fine: subtracting $8$ from both sides is a shift, and shifting both numbers by
the same amount cannot change which one is larger. Step 2 is the error --- he
divided by $-2$ without reversing the symbol.

\textbf{(b) The counterexample.} $x = 0$ satisfies Dev's answer $x \ge -3$. In
the original, $8 - 2(0) = 8$, and $8 \ge 14$ is false. So $x=0$ is in his
solution set but is not a solution.

\textbf{(c) Correct solution.} $\dfrac{-2x}{-2} \overset{\text{flip}}{\le}
\dfrac{6}{-2}$, so $\ans{x \le -3}$
\noindent \emph{Check:} $x = -10$ gives $8 - 2(-10) = 28 \ge 14$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Manual review --- expected reasoning.} Adding the same number
to both sides slides both numbers the same distance in the same direction, so
their order is unchanged: if $2 < 5$ then $2 + (-5) = -3$ and $5 + (-5) = 0$,
and $-3 < 0$ --- still ``less than''.

Multiplying by a negative does something different: it reflects the number line
through $0$. Points that were on the left land on the right. If $2 < 5$, then
$2 \times (-5) = -10$ and $5 \times (-5) = -25$, and now $-10 > -25$. The
number that was smaller ends up larger, which is exactly what ``the symbol
reverses'' records.

Accept any answer that makes the ordering point: a shift preserves order, a
reflection across zero reverses it (or, equivalently, ``the farther a positive
number is from zero, the farther its negative is from zero on the other side'').
Do not accept ``because the rule says so'' or an answer that only restates the
procedure without reference to order or position.}
