# Anchor Facts: Twos, Fives, and Tens (Grades 2–3)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_anchorfacts_curr076.pdf`) — 8 guided concept problems on using twos, fives, and tens as anchor facts. Each problem pairs a model with notation: a fives skip-count strip feeding 5 × 6, a 2-by-8 dot array showing that a twos fact *is* a double, tens facts, then the three anchor moves (double it, add one more group, take one group away) written out as equations with blanks. Problem 7 has your child choose which anchor family fits 9 × 8, and the challenge asks them to pick and explain their own anchor for 7 × 6.
- **Answer key** (`ak_anchorfacts_curr076.pdf`) — worked reasoning showing each anchor step (e.g. 10 × 6 = 60, then 60 − 6 = 54), acceptable alternate anchors for the choice problems, a labeled quick-answers bank, and a Curriculum section (3.OA.C.7, difficulty 1–4).
- **Study guide** (`ss_anchorfacts_curr076.pdf`) — 2 pages: twos and doubling, fives and skip counting, tens plus-or-minus a group, each with rule box, worked example, and try-it, plus a watch-out on subtracting the *group* (one 6, not one 9) in the nines move.

**What is verified:** 15 of the 17 graded responses are machine-checked with SymPy — every numeric blank on all 8 problems, with the anchor-step arithmetic checked in the exact form the sheet teaches it (e.g. 6 × 8 verified as 5 × 8 + 8). The 2 open responses — which anchor was circled and used in problem 7, and the written anchor fact plus one-sentence explanation in the challenge — are instructor-judged, marked `---` in the quick-answers bank, with rubrics saying any anchor genuinely used earns credit.
