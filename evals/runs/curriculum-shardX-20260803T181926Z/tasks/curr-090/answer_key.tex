\problem{Swim lesson 45 minutes, then a 10 minute wait, then a 20 minute ride.
How many minutes from the start of the lesson until Maya is home?}

\textbf{Solution:} All three pieces of time happen one after another, so add them.
\emph{Step 1 --- the lesson and the wait:} $45 + 10 = 55$ minutes.
\emph{Step 2 --- add the ride home:} $55 + 20 = 75$ minutes.
\ans{75\ \text{minutes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A 95 minute movie; Ben watches 40 minutes, then 25 minutes. How many
minutes are left?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- how much has he watched?} $40 + 25 = 65$ minutes.
\emph{Step 2 --- take that away from the whole movie:} $95 - 65 = 30$ minutes.
Check by adding back: $65 + 30 = 95$ $\checkmark$
\ans{30\ \text{minutes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ella has \$20 and buys a \$8.75 book and a \$3.50 marker. How much is left,
in dollars?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- add what she spent:}
$\$8.75 + \$3.50 = \$12.25$.
\emph{Step 2 --- take that away from what she had:} $\$20.00 - \$12.25 = \$7.75$.
Line up the decimal points when you add and subtract money.
\ans{7.75}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{3 quarters and 4 dimes, then 55 cents are spent. How many cents are left?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- count the money he starts with.} A quarter is
25 cents and a dime is 10 cents, so
$3 \times 25 = 75$ cents and $4 \times 10 = 40$ cents, giving $75 + 40 = 115$ cents.
\emph{Step 2 --- take away what he spends:} $115 - 55 = 60$ cents.
\ans{60\ \text{cents}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Apples 250 g, pears 180 g, basket 400 g. What is the total mass?}

\textbf{Solution:} Every mass is already in grams, so nothing has to be changed first.
\emph{Step 1 --- the two bags of fruit:} $250 + 180 = 430$ g.
\emph{Step 2 --- add the basket:} $430 + 400 = 830$ g.
\ans{830\ \text{g}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A 2 L jug; 2 glasses of 350 mL are poured out. How many millilitres are left?}

\textbf{Solution:} The jug is measured in litres and the glasses in millilitres, so
change the litres first: $2$ L $= 2 \times 1000 = 2000$ mL.
\emph{Step 1 --- how much was poured out?} $2 \times 350 = 700$ mL.
\emph{Step 2 --- take it away from the jug:} $2000 - 700 = 1300$ mL.
\ans{1300\ \text{mL}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{25 minutes on each of 5 school days, plus 40 minutes on Saturday. How many
minutes in the week?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- the school days:} $5 \times 25 = 125$ minutes.
\emph{Step 2 --- add Saturday:} $125 + 40 = 165$ minutes.
\ans{165\ \text{minutes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{4 tickets at \$6 each, paid with a \$50 bill. How much change, in dollars?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- the cost of the tickets:}
$4 \times \$6 = \$24$.
\emph{Step 2 --- subtract from the money handed over:} $\$50 - \$24 = \$26$.
\ans{26}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A 3 kg box; 2 books of 450 g each are removed. What is the mass now, in grams?}

\textbf{Solution:} The box is in kilograms and the books are in grams, so change the
kilograms first: $3$ kg $= 3 \times 1000 = 3000$ g.
\emph{Step 1 --- the mass of the books removed:} $2 \times 450 = 900$ g.
\emph{Step 2 --- take it away from the box:} $3000 - 900 = 2100$ g.
\ans{2100\ \text{g}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A 5 L cooler; 1200 mL are used in the morning and 1500 mL in the afternoon.
How many millilitres are left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Change the litres to millilitres first:
$5$ L $= 5 \times 1000 = 5000$ mL.
\emph{Step 1 --- the water used all day:} $1200 + 1500 = 2700$ mL.
\emph{Step 2 --- take it away from the full cooler:} $5000 - 2700 = 2300$ mL.
\ans{2300\ \text{mL}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{2 bus rides a day on each of 5 school days, 35 minutes a ride. How many
minutes in the week?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- how many rides altogether?}
$2 \times 5 = 10$ rides.
\emph{Step 2 --- how long are 10 rides?} $10 \times 35 = 350$ minutes.
(Doing it the other way round gives the same answer: $2 \times 35 = 70$ minutes a day,
and $5 \times 70 = 350$ minutes.)
\ans{350\ \text{minutes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{6 groups, each needing a \$3 pack of paper and 2 glue sticks at \$2 each;
Ms Park starts with \$50. How much is left, in dollars?}

\textbf{Solution:} \emph{Step 1 --- what does one group cost?} The glue is
$2 \times \$2 = \$4$, and with the paper that is $\$3 + \$4 = \$7$ for one group.
\emph{Step 2 --- what do all six groups cost?} $6 \times \$7 = \$42$.
\emph{Step 3 --- how much is left?} $\$50 - \$42 = \$8$.
\ans{8}
