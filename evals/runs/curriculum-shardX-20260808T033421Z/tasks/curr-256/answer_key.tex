\problem{Balance: left pan $x + 4$ grams, right pan $11$ grams. Complete the model and give $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Removing $4$ grams from both pans leaves $x$ on the left and $7$ on the
right, and the balance is still level --- that is exactly what the subtraction property of equality
says. The completed model row reads $x$ and $7$. \ans{x = 7}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$3x - 7 = 14 \to 3x = 21 \to x = 7$: (a) name the first move; (b) name the second; (c) solve $5x + 2 = 27$.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} $7$ was added to both sides: the addition property of
equality. On the left $-7 + 7$ leaves $3x$; on the right $14 + 7 = 21$. Full credit states the
operation \emph{and} that both sides received it; naming only the number $7$ does not.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Both sides were divided by $3$ (equivalently,
multiplied by $\frac{1}{3}$): the division property of equality. Full credit again names the
operation and both sides.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c):} \quad Subtract $2$ from both sides: $5x = 25$. Divide both sides by
$5$. \ans{x = 5}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $4(x - 3) = 20$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Route one, divide first: dividing both sides by $4$ gives $x - 3 = 5$, then
adding $3$ to both sides gives $x = 8$. Route two, distribute first: $4x - 12 = 20$, add $12$ to
both sides for $4x = 32$, divide both sides by $4$. Both routes are legal because each move either
does the same thing to both sides or rewrites one side into an equal expression. \ans{x = 8}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $-3x < 12$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Divide both sides by $-3$. Dividing by a negative reverses the order of the
two sides, so the $<$ becomes $>$: $x > -4$. Checking with a value from the answer set, $x = 0$
gives $0 < 12$, which is true; and $x = -5$ gives $15 < 12$, which is false --- so the direction is
right. \ans{x > -4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Student work $2x + 5 = 3x - 1 \to 5 = x - 1 \to 6 = x$: (a) name the moves; (b) solve it.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Step one: $2x$ was subtracted from both sides
(subtraction property of equality), leaving $5 = x - 1$. Step two: $1$ was added to both sides
(addition property), leaving $6 = x$. Both moves are legal and the student's work is correct. Full
credit names both moves and says each was applied to both sides.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Subtracting $2x$ and then adding $1$ to both sides gives
$6 = x$, which is the same statement as $x = 6$. \ans{x = 6}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{x}{5} - 3 = 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Add $3$ to both sides: $\dfrac{x}{5} = 7$. Then multiply both sides by $5$:
$x = 35$. Check: $35 \div 5 - 3 = 7 - 3 = 4$. \ans{x = 35}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$5 - 2x \ge 11$: (a) solve; (b) does the sign change, and where?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Subtract $5$ from both sides: $-2x \ge 6$. Divide both sides by $-2$,
which reverses the sign: $x \le -3$. \ans{x \le -3}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Yes, it changes, and at exactly one step:
dividing both sides by $-2$. Subtracting $5$ from both sides does not affect the direction, because
shifting both sides by the same amount preserves which one is larger; multiplying or dividing by a
negative swaps which one is larger, so the symbol must be reversed to keep the statement true. Full
credit names that step as the reason; a bare yes or no earns half credit.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$3(x+2) = 18 \to 3x + 2 = 18 \to \ldots$: (a) which step is illegal; (b) solve correctly.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The very first step. $3(x + 2)$ was rewritten as
$3x + 2$: the factor $3$ reached the $x$ but not the $2$. The distributive property requires it to
multiply every term inside, giving $3x + 6$. Note that this is not a property-of-equality error at
all --- nothing was done to both sides --- it is a bad rewrite of one side, which is just as fatal.
Full credit points at that first line.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad $3(x + 2) = 18$ becomes $3x + 6 = 18$; subtract $6$ from both
sides for $3x = 12$; divide both sides by $3$. \ans{x = 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $-\dfrac{x}{4} + 1 > 3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Subtract $1$ from both sides: $-\dfrac{x}{4} > 2$. Now multiply both sides
by $-4$; the multiplier is negative, so the sign reverses: $x < -8$. Check with $x = -12$:
$3 + 1 = 4 > 3$, true. \ans{x < -8}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\dfrac{2x - 1}{3} + 4 = x$: (a) solve; (b) justify each move.}

\textbf{Solution (a):}
\begin{align*}
  \frac{2x - 1}{3} + 4 &= x\\
  2x - 1 + 12 &= 3x &&\text{multiply both sides by } 3\\
  2x + 11 &= 3x &&\text{combine like terms on the left}\\
  11 &= x &&\text{subtract } 2x \text{ from both sides}
\end{align*}
Check: $\dfrac{2(11) - 1}{3} + 4 = \dfrac{21}{3} + 4 = 7 + 4 = 11$. \ans{x = 11}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Multiplying both sides by $3$ is the
multiplication property of equality --- a both-sides move. Combining $-1 + 12$ into $11$ is a
rewrite of the left side only, justified by arithmetic, not by any property of equality. Subtracting
$2x$ from both sides is the subtraction property --- another both-sides move. Full credit
distinguishes the both-sides moves from the one-side rewrite.
