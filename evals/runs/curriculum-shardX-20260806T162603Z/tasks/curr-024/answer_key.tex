\problem{Warm-up. Count on the right way: for $6+2$, say ``6'', then hop two
times. What do you land on?}

\textbf{Solution:} Say 6 (no hop yet!), then hop: 7, 8. Two hops for the 2
being added, landing on 8. So $6+2=8$. \ans{8}

\problem{Jen added $5+3$. She counted: ``5, 6, 7'' --- and wrote $5+3=7$.
Write the correct sum, and tell why Jen's count went wrong.}

\textbf{Solution:} Jen said the start number 5 as her first count, so she
only hopped 2 times for the 3 being added. Count on correctly: say 5, then
hop 6, 7, 8 --- three hops. So $5+3=8$. \ans{8}

\emph{Tell why (instructor judges):} a good answer says Jen counted the
start number 5 as a hop, so she landed one short; the hops should be
6, 7, 8.

\problem{Kim added $6+3$. She counted: ``6, 7, 8'' --- and wrote $6+3=8$.
Find Kim's mistake. Write the correct sum, and show the count she should
have said.}

\textbf{Solution:} Kim's first count was the start number 6, so only two of
her counts were real hops. Say 6 first, then hop 7, 8, 9. So $6+3=9$.
\ans{9}

\emph{Find the mistake (instructor judges):} the student points at the
``6'' in Kim's count as the error and writes the correct count 7, 8, 9.

\problem{Raj added $8+2$. He counted: ``8, 9'' --- and wrote $8+2=9$. Cross
out the wrong sum, write the correct one, and tell how Raj should count.}

\textbf{Solution:} Raj used 8 as his first hop. Say 8 without counting it,
then hop 9, 10 --- two hops for the 2 being added. So $8+2=10$. \ans{10}

\emph{Tell how (instructor judges):} a good answer says Raj should say 8
first, then count exactly two hops: 9, 10.

\problem{Ava says $4+5=8$. Check her sum by counting on from 5. Is Ava
right? Write yes or no, then write the correct sum.}

\textbf{Solution:} Check by counting on from the bigger number 5: say 5,
then hop 6, 7, 8, 9 --- four hops for the 4. Ava is \emph{not} right: she
landed one short at 8. The true sum is $4+5=9$. \ans{9}

\emph{Is she right (instructor judges):} the student writes ``no'', shows
the count 6, 7, 8, 9, and fixes the sum to 9.

\problem{Challenge! Leo says: ``I never make the count-on mistake, because I
say the start number first, and then I count my hops on my fingers.'' Add
$3+6$ Leo's way, starting from the bigger number. Then tell how saying the
start number first stops the mistake.}

\textbf{Solution:} Start big: say 6 first. Then count three hops on three
fingers: 7, 8, 9. So $3+6=9$. \ans{9}

\emph{Tell how (instructor judges):} a good answer says that saying the
start number before any hops keeps it from being counted as a hop, so the
number of hops matches the number being added --- three fingers for the 3.
