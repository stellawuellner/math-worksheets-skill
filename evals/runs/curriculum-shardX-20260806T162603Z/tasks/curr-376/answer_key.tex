\problem{Rewrite $2^5 = 32$ in logarithmic form and give $\log_2 32$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The base $2$ stays the base; the exponent $5$ becomes the
value of the logarithm and $32$ becomes its input:
$2^5 = 32 \iff \log_2 32 = 5$. \ans{\log_2 32 = 5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_5 x = 3$: rewrite in exponential form and give $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A logarithm is an exponent, so $\log_5 x = 3$ says
``$5$ to the power $3$ gives $x$'': $5^3 = x$, and $5^3 = 125$.
\ans{x = 125}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\log_4 64$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The question is ``which power of $4$ gives $64$?''
$4^1 = 4$, $4^2 = 16$, $4^3 = 64$. \ans{\log_4 64 = 3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{(a) $7^{-2}$ as a fraction. (b) $\log_7(1/49)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the positive
power: $7^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{7^2} = \dfrac{1}{49}$. \ans{\tfrac{1}{49}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Part (a) is the exponential form of exactly the statement
being asked about, so switching it round gives
$\log_7\!\left(\tfrac{1}{49}\right) = -2$. A logarithm may be negative --- what
may not be negative is its \emph{input}. \ans{-2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_3 x = -2$: exponential form and $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $\log_3 x = -2$ becomes $3^{-2} = x$, and
$3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{3^2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$. \ans{x = \tfrac{1}{9}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Solve $2^x = \dfrac{1}{32}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Write the right side as a power of the same base:
$\dfrac{1}{32} = \dfrac{1}{2^5} = 2^{-5}$. Equal bases force equal exponents.
(In logarithmic form the same statement reads $\log_2 \tfrac{1}{32} = x$.)
\ans{x = -5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Evaluate $\log_9 3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Which power of $9$ gives $3$? Since $\sqrt{9} = 3$ and a
square root is the one-half power, $9^{1/2} = 3$. \ans{\log_9 3 = \tfrac{1}{2}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Doubling culture $N = 2^{\,t}$, $N = 4096$. Find $t$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The count statement $2^{\,t} = 4096$ becomes
$t = \log_2 4096$ in logarithmic form. Counting doublings:
$2^{10} = 1024$, $2^{11} = 2048$, $2^{12} = 4096$.
\ans{t = 12 \text{ hours}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_b 216 = 3$: find the base $b$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Switching form gives $b^3 = 216$. Here the unknown is the
\emph{base}, so undo the cube rather than a logarithm: $b = \sqrt[3]{216}$, and
$6^3 = 216$. A base must be positive, so $b = 6$. \ans{b = 6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Challenge: $\log_2(3x-1) = 4$. (a) Exact $x$. (b) Decimal.
(c) Why $x \le \tfrac{1}{3}$ is impossible.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Switch to exponential form, then solve the linear
equation:
\begin{align*}
  2^4 &= 3x - 1 \\
  16 &= 3x - 1 \\
  3x &= 17 .
\end{align*}
\ans{x = \tfrac{17}{3}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $\tfrac{17}{3} = 5.666\ldots$ \ans{5.67}

\textbf{Solution (c):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The exponential form says
$3x - 1 = 2^4$, and every power of $2$ is positive, so $3x - 1$ must be positive
--- that is, $x > \tfrac{1}{3}$. Equivalently, a logarithm is only defined for a
positive input, so $\log_2(3x-1)$ does not exist at all once $3x - 1 \le 0$.
Full credit needs the positivity of the input, tied to either form.
