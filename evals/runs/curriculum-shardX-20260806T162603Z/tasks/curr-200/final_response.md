# Comparing Distributions and Compound Events — Grades 6–7

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_distprob_curr200.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) comparing centre and spread — and why equal means
  do not mean equal distributions, (2) reading a data display question by question,
  (3) compound-event probability, with the outcome table that explains why the totals
  multiply.
- **`ws_distprob_curr200.pdf` — Worksheet (6 pages, 12 problems).** A review sheet: two
  warm-up mean calculations, then the three subskills interleaved so your child has to
  choose the method each time rather than repeat one. A single bar chart ("Display A") is
  printed once at the top and captioned with the three problems that use it — 3, 7 and 10 —
  so no problem is left guessing which figure is its own. The sheet finishes with a
  synthesis challenge where two classes have **identical means** and only spread can
  separate them.
- **`ak_distprob_curr200.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with the
  reasoning — the outcome counts for each probability, the additions behind each mean —
  plus a Quick Answers strip and a Curriculum block (standards 6.SP.B.5 and 7.SP.C.8).

## What is verified, honestly

- **20 machine-checked responses across the sheet.** Every mean, median and range was
  recomputed from the raw data by SymPy; every chart reading was recomputed from the same
  data array that draws the bars, so the picture and the answer cannot disagree; and every
  probability was checked as an exact fraction. Problem 9 is checked twice — once from the
  marble counts (three eighths times three eighths) and once as the reduced fraction — so
  the multiplication step itself is verified, not just the final answer.
- **Three problems (5, 8 and 12) also carry an instructor-judged part**, and these are the
  parts that matter most on a statistics sheet: which team was more spread out and what that
  says about consistency; whether the mean or the median better represents a typical day;
  and which class was steadier when the means tie. Written interpretation cannot be
  machine-checked, so each prints as `---` in the Quick Answers strip and the answer key
  carries a grading rubric saying exactly what a full-credit response must contain. That is
  why the key's own note reads "9 of 12 problems fully machine-checked" — the other three
  are part checked and part yours to read.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same way
  (10 checks, all machine-checked).

Probabilities are asked for as exact fractions in lowest terms throughout, so no calculator
is needed.
