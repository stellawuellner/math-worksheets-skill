\problem{Base: region bounded by $y = x$, the $x$-axis and $x = 4$ (metres). Cross sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are squares. Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution:} At position $x$ the side of the square runs from the $x$-axis up to the line, so $s = x$ and $A(x) = x^2$ square metres. Slicing perpendicular to the $x$-axis,
\[ V = \int_0^4 x^2\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^4 = \frac{64}{3}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{64}{3} \approx 21.33\ \text{m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Base: region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, the $x$-axis and $x = 9$ (centimetres). Cross sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are semicircles on the base. Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution:} The diameter is $d = \sqrt{x}$, so the radius is $\tfrac{\sqrt{x}}{2}$ and
\[ A(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{x}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi x}{8}. \]
Then $\displaystyle V = \int_0^9 \frac{\pi x}{8}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{8}\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^9 = \frac{81\pi}{16}$. \ans{V = \frac{81\pi}{16} \approx 15.90\ \text{cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Region bounded by $y = x^2$, the $x$-axis and $x = 3$ (feet), revolved about the $y$-axis. Use shells.}

\textbf{Solution:} A shell at position $x$ has radius $x$ and height $y = x^2$, so
\[ V = \int_0^3 2\pi x \cdot x^2\,dx = \int_0^3 2\pi x^3\,dx = 2\pi\left[\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^3 = \frac{81\pi}{2}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{81\pi}{2} \approx 127.23\ \text{ft}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Base: triangle bounded by $y = 4 - x$ and the axes (centimetres). Cross sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are equilateral triangles. Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution:} The side at position $x$ is $s = 4 - x$, so $A(x) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(4-x)^2$ and the region runs from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$:
\[ V = \int_0^4 \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(4-x)^2\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left[-\frac{(4-x)^3}{3}\right]_0^4 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot\frac{64}{3} = \frac{16\sqrt{3}}{3}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{16\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 9.24\ \text{cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Region bounded by $y = 4 - x^2$ and the $x$-axis, $x \ge 0$ (metres), revolved about the $y$-axis. Use shells.}

\textbf{Solution:} The region runs from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ (where $4 - x^2 = 0$). A shell at $x$ has radius $x$ and height $4 - x^2$:
\[ V = \int_0^2 2\pi x\,(4 - x^2)\,dx = 2\pi\left[2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 2\pi(8 - 4) = 8\pi. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = 8\pi \approx 25.13\ \text{m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, the $x$-axis and $x = 9$ (centimetres), revolved about the $y$-axis. (a) Volume by shells. (b) Why does the shell setup use the curve as written?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Shell radius $x$, shell height $\sqrt{x}$:
\[ V = \int_0^9 2\pi x\sqrt{x}\,dx = \int_0^9 2\pi x^{3/2}\,dx = 2\pi\left[\frac{2}{5}x^{5/2}\right]_0^9 = \frac{4\pi}{5}(243) = \frac{972\pi}{5}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{972\pi}{5} \approx 610.73\ \text{cm}^3}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Shells about the $y$-axis integrate in $x$, so $y = \sqrt{x}$ can be substituted for the shell height exactly as given. Washers about the $y$-axis integrate in $y$, which forces the curve to be rewritten as $x = y^2$ and splits the solid into an outer cylinder of radius $9$ minus the region left of $x = y^2$. Full credit names the variable of integration as the reason.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Base: region enclosed by $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ (metres); square cross sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis. (a) Where do the curves meet? (b) Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Set $x = x^2$, i.e. $x - x^2 = x(1 - x) = 0$. \ans{x = 0,\; x = 1}

\textbf{Solution (b):} On $[0,1]$ the line is above the parabola, so the side of the square is $s = x - x^2$ and
\[ V = \int_0^1 (x - x^2)^2\,dx = \int_0^1 \left(x^2 - 2x^3 + x^4\right)dx = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{30}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{1}{30} \approx 0.03\ \text{m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Region bounded by $y = x^2$, the $x$-axis and $x = 2$ (centimetres), revolved about the line $x = 4$. (a) Volume by shells. (b) Why is the radius $4 - x$?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The shell at position $x$ sits a distance $4 - x$ from the axis and has height $x^2$:
\[ V = \int_0^2 2\pi(4 - x)x^2\,dx = 2\pi\left[\frac{4x^3}{3} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2 = 2\pi\left(\frac{32}{3} - 4\right) = \frac{40\pi}{3}. \]
So the volume is \ans{V = \frac{40\pi}{3} \approx 41.89\ \text{cm}^3}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The shell radius is the distance from the axis of revolution to the shell, and the axis $x = 4$ lies to the right of the whole region, so that distance is $4 - x$. Checking the ends: at $x = 0$ the shell is $4$ units away, at $x = 2$ it is $2$ units away --- the radius shrinks as the shell approaches the axis, which is what the picture shows. Using $x$ would measure from the $y$-axis instead, which is not the axis of revolution here.
