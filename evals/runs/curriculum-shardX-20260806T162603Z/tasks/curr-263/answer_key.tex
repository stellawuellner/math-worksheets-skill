\problem{Solve by elimination: $3x + 2y = 16$ and $5x - 4y = 12$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $y$-terms are $+2y$ and $-4y$. Multiply the first equation by $2$ so they become exact opposites.
\[ 6x + 4y = 32 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 5x - 4y = 12 \]
Add the two equations: $11x = 44$, so $x = 4$. Substitute into $3x + 2y = 16$: $12 + 2y = 16$, so $y = 2$. Check in the second equation: $5(4) - 4(2) = 20 - 8 = 12$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 4,\ y = 2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve by elimination: $4x + 3y = 18$ and $2x - y = 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply the second equation by $3$ so its $y$-term becomes $-3y$, the opposite of $+3y$.
\[ 4x + 3y = 18 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 6x - 3y = 12 \]
Add: $10x = 30$, so $x = 3$. Substitute into $2x - y = 4$: $6 - y = 4$, so $y = 2$. Check in the first equation: $4(3) + 3(2) = 18$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 3,\ y = 2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve by elimination: $3x + 4y = 25$ and $2x + 3y = 18$.}

\textbf{Solution:} No coefficient of one equation divides the matching coefficient of the other, so scale both. The least common multiple of $3$ and $2$ is $6$: multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second by $3$.
\[ 6x + 8y = 50 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 6x + 9y = 54 \]
Subtract the first scaled equation from the second: $y = 4$. Substitute into $3x + 4y = 25$: $3x + 16 = 25$, so $x = 3$. Check in the second original equation: $2(3) + 3(4) = 18$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 3,\ y = 4}

\problem{Drama club tickets. Three adult and two student tickets cost \$37; five adult and four student tickets cost \$65. (a) Find both prices in dollars. (b) Explain why one multiplication is enough to eliminate $s$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With $a$ the adult price and $s$ the student price, both in dollars,
\[ 3a + 2s = 37 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 5a + 4s = 65. \]
Multiply the first equation by $2$: $6a + 4s = 74$. Subtract the second equation: $a = 9$. Substitute into $3a + 2s = 37$: $27 + 2s = 37$, so $s = 5$. Check: $5(9) + 4(5) = 45 + 20 = 65$ $\checkmark$ \ans{a = 9,\ s = 5}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response says that doubling the first equation turns $2s$ into $4s$, which already matches the $4s$ in the second equation, so subtracting removes $s$ and leaves a single equation in $a$. Full credit for naming the matched $s$-coefficients as the reason; partial credit for saying only that the numbers were made the same without naming which variable is removed.

\problem{Solve by elimination: $5x + 3y = 21$ and $2x + 5y = 16$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Match the $x$-terms. The least common multiple of $5$ and $2$ is $10$: multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second by $5$.
\[ 10x + 6y = 42 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 10x + 25y = 80 \]
Subtract: $19y = 38$, so $y = 2$. Substitute into $5x + 3y = 21$: $5x + 6 = 21$, so $x = 3$. Check in the second original equation: $2(3) + 5(2) = 16$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 3,\ y = 2}

\problem{Office order. Four notebooks and three folders cost \$27; six notebooks and five folders cost \$43. Find the price of one notebook and one folder, in dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} With $n$ the notebook price and $f$ the folder price, both in dollars,
\[ 4n + 3f = 27 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 6n + 5f = 43. \]
Match the $n$-terms: the least common multiple of $4$ and $6$ is $12$, so multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$.
\[ 12n + 9f = 81 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 12n + 10f = 86 \]
Subtract: $f = 5$. Substitute into $4n + 3f = 27$: $4n + 15 = 27$, so $n = 3$. Check: $6(3) + 5(5) = 18 + 25 = 43$ $\checkmark$ \ans{n = 3,\ f = 5}

\problem{Solve by elimination: $7x - 2y = 1$ and $3x + 4y = 15$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $y$-terms are $-2y$ and $+4y$. Multiply the first equation by $2$ so they become opposites.
\[ 14x - 4y = 2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 3x + 4y = 15 \]
Add: $17x = 17$, so $x = 1$. Substitute into $3x + 4y = 15$: $3 + 4y = 15$, so $y = 3$. Check in the first original equation: $7(1) - 2(3) = 1$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 1,\ y = 3}

\problem{Pencil and pen boxes. Five boxes of pencils and two boxes of pens cost \$46; three boxes of pencils and four boxes of pens cost \$50. (a) Find both prices in dollars. (b) Explain why scaling an equation leaves the solution set unchanged.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With $p$ the price of a box of pencils and $q$ the price of a box of pens, both in dollars,
\[ 5p + 2q = 46 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 3p + 4q = 50. \]
Multiply the first equation by $2$: $10p + 4q = 92$. Subtract the second equation: $7p = 42$, so $p = 6$. Substitute into $5p + 2q = 46$: $30 + 2q = 46$, so $q = 8$. Check: $3(6) + 4(8) = 18 + 32 = 50$ $\checkmark$ \ans{p = 6,\ q = 8}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response says that multiplying \emph{both sides} of an equation by the same nonzero number produces an equivalent equation: every pair $(p,q)$ that made the original true still makes the scaled one true, and the step can be undone by dividing. Full credit for tying the equivalence to multiplying both sides; no credit for answering only that the numbers get bigger.

\problem{Solve by elimination: $6x + 5y = 3$ and $8x - 3y = 33$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Match the $y$-terms. The least common multiple of $5$ and $3$ is $15$: multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $5$.
\[ 18x + 15y = 9 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 40x - 15y = 165 \]
Add: $58x = 174$, so $x = 3$. Substitute into $6x + 5y = 3$: $18 + 5y = 3$, so $5y = -15$ and $y = -3$. Check in the second original equation: $8(3) - 3(-3) = 24 + 9 = 33$ $\checkmark$ \ans{x = 3,\ y = -3}

\problem{Cashews and almonds. Three kilograms of cashews and two kilograms of almonds cost \$57; five kilograms of cashews and four kilograms of almonds cost \$101. (a) Find both prices per kilogram in dollars. (b) Compare the two scaling routes.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} With $c$ the cashew price and $d$ the almond price, both dollars per kilogram,
\[ 3c + 2d = 57 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 5c + 4d = 101. \]
Multiply the first equation by $2$: $6c + 4d = 114$. Subtract the second equation: $c = 13$. Substitute into $3c + 2d = 57$: $39 + 2d = 57$, so $d = 9$. Check: $5(13) + 4(9) = 65 + 36 = 101$ $\checkmark$ \ans{c = 13,\ d = 9}

\textbf{(b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response describes both routes and picks one. Matching the almond terms needs only the first equation doubled, because $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ already matches the second equation --- one multiplication. Matching the cashew terms needs the first equation times $5$ and the second times $3$ (both become $15c$) --- two multiplications and larger numbers. Full credit for naming the one-multiplication route and saying why it is fewer steps; partial credit for a correct route with no comparison.
