# Product and Quotient Laws for Integer Exponents — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_expolaws_curr206.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) the expanded-form model — an exponent is a count
  of factors, and that count is *why* both laws work, (2) the product law, (3) the quotient
  law, including what to do when the bottom exponent is larger. A watch-out box flags the
  single most common error, multiplying exponents instead of adding them.
- **`ws_expolaws_curr206.pdf` — Worksheet (4 pages, 10 problems).** The sheet keeps
  returning to the model rather than drilling the rule: problems 1 and 4 have your child
  write the factors out and count or cancel them before naming the law, and problem 9 asks
  for a comparison that can be settled by the laws without computing either value. Numbers
  in front of the powers appear in problems 7 and 8, and the sheet ends with a
  find-the-error challenge.
- **`ak_expolaws_curr206.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with the
  counting argument written out, plus a Quick Answers strip and a Curriculum block
  (standard 8.EE.A.1, difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **9 of the 10 problems are fully machine-checked.** Every simplification was checked as
  an *algebraic identity* — SymPy confirms the printed answer is the same expression as the
  original, for all values of the variable, not merely that a number came out right. The two
  expanded-form problems have both their exponent and their value checked separately, and
  the comparison in problem 9 is checked as a relation.
- **Problem 10 is split.** Part (a), the corrected simplification, is machine-checked. Part
  (b) — explaining why the exponents add — is **instructor-judged**. It prints as `---` in
  the Quick Answers strip, and the key gives you the rubric: the expanded-form reason or a
  numerical counterexample is required, not just a restatement of the rule.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same
  way (8 checks, all machine-checked).

One note for marking problem 6: $t^4/t^{10}$ is asked for with a *positive* exponent, so the
expected answer is $\tfrac{1}{t^6}$ rather than $t^{-6}$. Both are the same number; only the
requested form differs, and the answer key says so.
