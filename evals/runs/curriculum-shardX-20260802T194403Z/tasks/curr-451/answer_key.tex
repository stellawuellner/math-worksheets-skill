\problem{Direct substitution gives $\tfrac{0}{0}$. Factor the numerator as a
difference of squares: $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}=\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3$ for
$x\neq3$. Substituting $x=3$ gives $\ans{6}$, which matches the table's
approach from both sides.}

\problem{Factor: $x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$, so the quotient is $x-3$ for $x\neq2$.
Substituting $x=2$ gives $\ans{-1}$.}

\problem{Difference of cubes: $x^3-8=(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)$, so the quotient is
$x^2+2x+4$. At $x=2$ this is $4+4+4=\ans{12}$.}

\problem{Multiply by the conjugate $\dfrac{\sqrt{x+4}+2}{\sqrt{x+4}+2}$. The
numerator becomes $(x+4)-4=x$, giving $\dfrac{x}{x(\sqrt{x+4}+2)}=
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2}$. At $x=0$ this is $\dfrac{1}{2+2}=\ans{1/4}$.}

\problem{Multiply by $\dfrac{\sqrt{x}+3}{\sqrt{x}+3}$. The numerator becomes
$x-9$, so the quotient is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}+3}$. At $x=9$ this is
$\dfrac{1}{3+3}=\ans{1/6}$.}

\problem{Multiply by $\dfrac{2+\sqrt{x+3}}{2+\sqrt{x+3}}$. The numerator becomes
$4-(x+3)=1-x=-(x-1)$, so the quotient is $\dfrac{-1}{2+\sqrt{x+3}}$. At $x=1$
this is $\dfrac{-1}{2+2}=\ans{-1/4}$. The negative sign comes from the radical
being subtracted, not from an algebra slip.}

\problem{For $x\neq7$, $\dfrac{x^2-5x-14}{x-7}=\dfrac{(x-7)(x+2)}{x-7}=x+2$, so
$\lim_{x\to7}f(x)=9$. Continuity at $x=7$ requires $f(7)$ to equal that limit,
so $c=\ans{9}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Manual review --- expected reasoning.} A limit describes the
behaviour of $f$ as $x$ approaches 3, and the definition of the limit never
evaluates $f$ at 3 itself. For every $x\neq3$ the two expressions agree, so they
have the same limit. At $x=3$ they differ: $x+3$ is defined and equals 6, while
$\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ is undefined --- a removable discontinuity. Accept any answer
making both points: agreement away from the point, and the limit not depending on
the value at the point. Do not accept ``the 3s cancel.''}
