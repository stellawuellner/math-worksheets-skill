\problem{$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$, $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}$; prove $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$. Fill in the reasons.}

\textbf{Reasons:} 1. Given. \quad 2. Given. \quad 3. \textbf{Reflexive property of congruence} --- the diagonal $\overline{AC}$ belongs to both triangles, so it is congruent to itself. \quad 4. \textbf{SSS.}

\textbf{Grading:} line 3 must name the reflexive property; ``it is the same side'' is the observation, not the reason. Line 4 must name the criterion, not restate the conclusion.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\overline{PX} \cong \overline{SX}$, $\overline{QX} \cong \overline{RX}$; prove $\triangle PQX \cong \triangle SRX$. Fill in the reasons.}

\textbf{Reasons:} 1. Given. \quad 2. \textbf{Vertical angles are congruent} --- $\angle PXQ$ and $\angle SXR$ are the opposite pair formed by the two intersecting segments. \quad 3. Given. \quad 4. \textbf{SAS.}

\textbf{Why SAS and not SSA:} the angle sits \emph{between} the two marked sides in each triangle, which is exactly the SAS pattern. Read the order down the table --- side, angle, side --- and the criterion reads itself off.

\problem{SAS given; $BC = 3x + 2$, $EF = 5x - 8$ cm. (a) Criterion and reason. (b) $x$. (c) $BC$.}

\textbf{(a)} The three givens are a side, the angle \emph{between} the two sides, and a second side, so the criterion is \textbf{SAS}. Once the triangles are congruent, $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{EF}$ follows by \textbf{CPCTC} --- corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent. CPCTC is used after the criterion, never to establish it.

\textbf{(b)} CPCTC makes the two lengths equal:
\begin{align*}
  3x + 2 &= 5x - 8 \\
  10 &= 2x
\end{align*}
So $x = 5$. \ans{x = 5}

\textbf{(c)} $BC = 3(5) + 2 = 15 + 2$. Check: $EF = 5(5) - 8 = 17$ too. \ans{BC = 17 \, \text{cm}}

\problem{ASA; reasons printed, statements blank.}

\textbf{Statements:}

1. $\angle G \cong \angle J$ \quad 2. $\overline{GH} \cong \overline{JK}$ \quad 3. $\angle H \cong \angle K$ \quad 4. $\triangle GHI \cong \triangle JKL$

\textbf{Why this order:} the fourth reason is ASA, so the first three lines must be angle, included side, angle. $\overline{GH}$ is the side between $\angle G$ and $\angle H$, which is what makes the criterion ASA rather than AAS.

\textbf{Grading:} the three givens in any order earn most of the credit; naming the included side in the middle position earns all of it.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{AAS given; $m\angle C = 4x - 9$, $m\angle F = 2x + 15$. (a) Criterion, and why not ASA. (b) $x$. (c) $m\angle C$.}

\textbf{(a)} \textbf{AAS.} The marked side $\overline{BC}$ is not the side between $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ --- it lies opposite $\angle A$ --- and ASA requires the \emph{included} side. AAS still proves congruence, because the third angle is forced by the angle sum, which turns the AAS pattern into an ASA one.

\textbf{(b)} By CPCTC, $\angle C \cong \angle F$:
\begin{align*}
  4x - 9 &= 2x + 15 \\
  2x &= 24
\end{align*}
So $x = 12$. \ans{x = 12}

\textbf{(c)} $m\angle C = 4(12) - 9 = 48 - 9$. Check: $m\angle F = 2(12) + 15 = 39$ too. \ans{39^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{HL; right angles at $C$ and $Z$. Fill in the reasons.}

\textbf{Reasons:} 1. Given. \quad 2. \textbf{Definition of a right triangle} --- a triangle with a right angle. \quad 3. Given. \quad 4. Given. \quad 5. \textbf{HL.}

\textbf{Why line 2 is not optional:} the hypotenuse-leg criterion may only be cited once both triangles have been named right triangles. Without that line the proof is citing a rule whose hypothesis has not been established --- and the same three parts in a non-right triangle would be side-side-angle, which proves nothing.

\problem{$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$, $M$ the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$; reasons printed, statements blank.}

\textbf{Statements:}

1. $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ \quad 2. $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$ \quad 3. $\overline{BM} \cong \overline{MC}$ \quad 4. $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$ \quad 5. $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$

\textbf{Reading the reasons backwards:} line 3's reason is the definition of midpoint, so line 3 has to be the congruence that definition produces; line 4's reason is the reflexive property, so line 4 has to be the shared segment $\overline{AM}$. Three pairs of sides then give SSS.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{SSS given; $m\angle B = 5x + 4$, $m\angle E = 3x + 26$. (a) Criterion and reason. (b) $x$. (c) $m\angle B$.}

\textbf{(a)} Three pairs of congruent sides give \textbf{SSS}. The congruence of the angle pair then follows by \textbf{CPCTC}.

\textbf{(b)}
\begin{align*}
  5x + 4 &= 3x + 26 \\
  2x &= 22
\end{align*}
So $x = 11$. \ans{x = 11}

\textbf{(c)} $m\angle B = 5(11) + 4 = 55 + 4$. Check: $m\angle E = 3(11) + 26 = 59$ too. \ans{59^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\overline{PQ} \parallel \overline{SR}$ and $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{SR}$; prove $\triangle PQX \cong \triangle SRX$. Fill in the reasons.}

\textbf{Reasons:} 1. Given. \quad 2. \textbf{Alternate interior angles} --- $\overline{PQ} \parallel \overline{SR}$ cut by the transversal $\overline{PR}$ makes $\angle QPX$ and $\angle RSX$ alternate interior angles, hence congruent. \quad 3. \textbf{Vertical angles are congruent.} \quad 4. Given. \quad 5. \textbf{AAS.}

\textbf{Why AAS:} the congruent side $\overline{PQ}$ lies opposite $\angle PXQ$ rather than between the two angle pairs, so the pattern is angle-angle-side. The parallel mark is doing real work here --- it is the only thing that produces the first angle pair.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$, $\angle BAC \cong \angle DCA$; $BC = 2x + 7$, $DA = 4x - 13$ cm. (a) The whole proof. (b) $x$. (c) $BC$.}

\textbf{(a) Model proof:}

1. $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$ --- \emph{Given.}

2. $\angle BAC \cong \angle DCA$ --- \emph{Given.}

3. $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{CA}$ --- \emph{Reflexive property of congruence.}

4. $\triangle BAC \cong \triangle DCA$ --- \emph{SAS.}

The criterion is SAS because in each triangle the marked angle sits between the marked side and the shared diagonal: $\angle BAC$ is between $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$, and $\angle DCA$ is between $\overline{CD}$ and $\overline{CA}$.

\textbf{(b)} By CPCTC, $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}$:
\begin{align*}
  2x + 7 &= 4x - 13 \\
  20 &= 2x
\end{align*}
So $x = 10$. \ans{x = 10}

\textbf{(c)} $BC = 2(10) + 7 = 20 + 7$. Check: $DA = 4(10) - 13 = 27$ too. \ans{BC = 27 \, \text{cm}}

\textbf{Grading (a):} four justified lines, with the reflexive property named for the diagonal and the criterion named on the last line. Any order that keeps every line justified is acceptable.
