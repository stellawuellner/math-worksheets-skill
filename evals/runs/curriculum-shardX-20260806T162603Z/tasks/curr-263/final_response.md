# Solving Linear Systems by Elimination — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_elimination_curr263.pdf`) — 10 problems, all on elimination
  *with scaling*: multiplying one or both equations before adding. Three bare-symbolic
  warm-ups, then four realistic pricing situations (tickets, an office order, boxes of
  pencils and pens, nuts sold by the kilogram) with the unknowns and their units named
  in the stem. Difficulty ramps 1 → 5, and the three methods are interleaved after the
  warm-up so the student has to *choose* the scaling rather than repeat one.
- **Answer key** (`ak_elimination_curr263.pdf`) — every problem worked in full: which
  variable was targeted, the scaled equations actually added or subtracted, the
  back-substitution, and a check in the *other* original equation. A Quick Answers bank
  and a Curriculum block (standard HSA-REI.C.6, difficulty range) sit at the top for
  fast grading.
- **Study guide** (`ss_elimination_curr263.pdf`) — 2 pages, three sections that match
  the three worksheet skills: scaling one equation, scaling both to an LCM, and building
  a system from a word problem. Each has a rule box, a worked example whose first step
  says *why* that method was chosen, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down
  inside the box.

## What is verified, honestly

- **10 of 10** worksheet answers (every solve-the-system answer, including all four word
  problems) were recomputed by SymPy from the system itself and matched the key.
- **3 items are instructor-judged**, not machine-checked: the "(b) explain" halves of
  problems 4, 8 and 10. Those ask for written reasoning — why one multiplication suffices,
  why scaling does not change the solution set, and which of two scaling routes is fewer
  steps — and no computer algebra system can grade a sentence. The answer key prints a
  grading rubric for each, and the key's own "What is verified" note names problems 4, 8
  and 10 as the ones marked `---`.
- All six study-guide examples and try-its were verified the same way.

Nothing on the sheet is marked `[unchecked]`: every printed answer blank is either
machine-checked or explicitly flagged for you to read.
