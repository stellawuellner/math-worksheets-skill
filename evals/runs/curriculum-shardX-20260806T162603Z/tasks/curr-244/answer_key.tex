\problem{Right triangle with legs $9$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Both given sides are legs, so the hypotenuse is the side we are adding
towards: $c = \sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225}$.
\ans{15 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Right triangle with hypotenuse $25$ cm and one leg $7$ cm. Find the other leg.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Here the longest side is given, so the missing side is a leg and the
squares are subtracted: $b = \sqrt{25^2 - 7^2} = \sqrt{625 - 49} = \sqrt{576}$.
\ans{24 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Jamal wrote $\sqrt{13^2 + 5^2} = 13.93$ cm for the missing side of a right triangle with
hypotenuse $13$ cm and leg $5$ cm.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The $13$ is the hypotenuse, so it sits alone on the large side of
$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$: $\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144}$.
\ans{12 \text{ cm}}

\textbf{(b) Diagnosis (instructor-judged).} A correct response says that Jamal treated the
hypotenuse as a leg: he added the two squares when finding a leg calls for subtraction. Accept
any wording that names $13$ as the hypotenuse and the operation as subtraction. Note the size
check: his answer $13.93$ is longer than the hypotenuse itself, which is impossible. The
corrected value alone is not a diagnosis.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the distance between $(-2, 1)$ and $(4, 9)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The horizontal gap is $4 - (-2) = 6$ and the vertical gap is
$9 - 1 = 8$. Those two gaps are the legs of a right triangle and the distance is its hypotenuse,
so the squares are added: $d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100}$. \ans{d = 10}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Priya wrote $\sqrt{17^2 + 8^2} = 18.79$ ft for the height a $17$ ft ladder reaches with
its foot $8$ ft from the wall.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The ladder is the slanted side, so it is the hypotenuse; the wall
height is a leg: $\sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{289 - 64} = \sqrt{225}$.
\ans{15 \text{ ft}}

\textbf{(b) Diagnosis (instructor-judged).} A correct response says Priya added the squares as
though the ladder were a leg. The ladder is the longest side of the triangle, so it is the
hypotenuse, and finding a leg means subtracting. Her answer of $18.79$ ft would put the top of
the wall further away than the ladder is long. Accept any wording that identifies the ladder as
the hypotenuse and the operation as subtraction.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Solve $x^2 + 81 = 225$ and say which solution can be the leg.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Subtract to isolate the square, then take both square roots:
\[ x^2 = 225 - 81 = 144, \qquad x = \pm 12 \]
\[ \boxed{x = -12 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 12} \]

\textbf{Reason (instructor-judged).} A correct response chooses $12$ and explains that squaring
destroys the sign, so the equation has two solutions, but a side of a triangle is a length and
cannot be negative. Accept any wording that rejects $-12$ because lengths are positive; a circled
answer with no reason earns no credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A garden $24$ m by $10$ m: how long is the corner-to-corner path?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The path is the diagonal of the rectangle, and the two sides meet at a
right angle, so the sides are the legs and the path is the hypotenuse:
$\sqrt{24^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{576 + 100} = \sqrt{676}$. \ans{26 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Leg $20$ cm, hypotenuse $29$ cm. Marisa wrote $35.23$ cm; Ben wrote $21$ cm. Who is
right, and how could you tell?}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The missing side is a leg, so subtract:
$\sqrt{29^2 - 20^2} = \sqrt{841 - 400} = \sqrt{441}$. Ben is right.
\ans{21 \text{ cm}}

\textbf{(b) Reason (instructor-judged).} A correct response gives the size argument: the
hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle, so every leg must be shorter than $29$ cm.
Marisa's $35.23$ cm is longer than the hypotenuse, so it can be rejected before any arithmetic is
rechecked. Accept any wording that compares the answer with the hypotenuse; naming Ben without a
reason earns no credit.
