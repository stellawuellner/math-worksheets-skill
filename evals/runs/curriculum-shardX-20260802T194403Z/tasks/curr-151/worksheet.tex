\wstitleblock{Ratios and Equivalent Ratio Tables}{Grade 6--7 Mathematics}{}

\begin{center}
Reading ratios off models, part-to-part and part-to-whole forms, equivalent
ratio tables, and the value of a ratio
\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{Directions.} Every problem hands you a model --- a
sticker strip, a tape diagram, or a ratio table --- and asks you to turn it
into notation. Remember the three written forms of the same comparison:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item words: \textbf{$a$ to $b$} \qquad colon: \textbf{$a:b$} \qquad
        fraction: \textbf{$\dfrac{a}{b}$}
  \item the quantity named \emph{first} in the question is written first
\end{itemize}
Show the model work you used, then write the answer on the line.\normalsize

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Sticker strip.} The strip below is one sheet of stickers.
Each S is a star sticker and each M is a moon sticker.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
S & S & S & S & S & S & M & M & M & M & M & M & M & M \\[0.55cm]\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Write the ratio of \textbf{star stickers to moon stickers} in all three forms,
then give that ratio in simplest form as a fraction.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Same sticker strip.} This time compare a part with the
whole sheet. Write the ratio of \textbf{star stickers to all the stickers} as a
fraction in simplest form, and say in words what that fraction tells you about
the sheet.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Tape diagram.} A bracelet uses red beads and blue beads in
the ratio 4 to 3. The diagram shows one box for each part of the ratio, and
every box holds the same number of beads.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\hspace{4pt}}c@{}}
Red: & \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline ~~~ & ~~~ & ~~~ & ~~~ \\[0.45cm]\hline\end{tabular}\\[6pt]
Blue: & \begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}\hline ~~~ & ~~~ & ~~~ \\[0.45cm]\hline\end{tabular}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each box holds 6 beads. How many beads are on the whole bracelet?}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Ratio table.} A bead kit is packed so that there are
4 red beads for every 6 white beads.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}\hline
White beads & 6 & 3 & 15 \\[0.55cm]\hline
Red beads & 4 & & \\[0.55cm]\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Fill in both empty cells. Say which way you scaled to reach each one.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Value of a ratio.} A bottling machine fills 84 bottles in
4 minutes, at a steady rate. Write the ratio of bottles to minutes, then find
its \textbf{value}: the number of bottles filled in one single minute.}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Part to whole.} A shelf holds only nonfiction books and
fiction books, in the ratio $5:3$. What \textbf{fraction of all the books on the
shelf} is fiction? Explain where the denominator of your fraction comes from,
since it is not one of the two numbers in the ratio.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Ratio table.} Concert tickets are sold at one steady
price: 15 tickets cost 12 dollars.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}\hline
Tickets & 15 & 5 & 25 \\[0.55cm]\hline
Cost (dollars) & 12 & & \\[0.55cm]\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Complete the table. What does a block of 25 tickets cost, in dollars?}

\problem[5cm]{\textbf{Comparing by value.} Two drink mixes are made from scoops
of powder and liters of water.\\[3pt]
Mix A: 3 scoops in 5 liters. \qquad Mix B: 5 scoops in 8 liters.\\[3pt]
Write each mix as a ratio in fraction form, find the value of each ratio, then
write $<$ or $>$ between them in this order: value of Mix A
\underline{\hspace{1.2cm}} value of Mix B.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Ratio table, harder step.} A lemonade recipe uses
12 lemons for every 8 cups of water.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}\hline
Cups of water & 8 & 2 & 30 \\[0.55cm]\hline
Lemons & 12 & & \\[0.55cm]\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Fill in the middle column first --- dividing is the easy step --- then use that
column to reach 30 cups. How many lemons does the 30-cup batch need?}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Synthesis.} A mural paint is mixed from blue paint and
yellow paint in the ratio 5 to 4. Ravi needs 63 liters of mixed paint in
total.\\[3pt]
Draw a tape diagram with one box per share, work out how many liters one box is
worth, then find how many liters of \textbf{blue} paint Ravi needs. Write the
ratio of blue paint to total paint as a fraction as part of your reasoning.
\answerline{L}}
