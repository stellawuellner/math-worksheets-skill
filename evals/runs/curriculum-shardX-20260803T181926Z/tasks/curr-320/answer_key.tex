\problem{\textbf{$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, $AB = 6$ cm, $DE = 15$ cm
--- scale factor.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The scale factor from $\triangle ABC$ to $\triangle DEF$ is
the ratio of a side of $DEF$ to its corresponding side of $ABC$, in that
order.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{DE}{AB} = \dfrac{15}{6} = \dfrac{5}{2}$.

\ans{k = 2.5}

\textit{Listen for:} $\frac{6}{15} = 0.4$, which is the factor the other way
round. The direction is part of the answer.}

\problem{\textbf{$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR$: $AB = 8$, $BC = 12$,
$PQ = 20$ --- find $QR$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Corresponding sides come from the similarity statement:
$AB \leftrightarrow PQ$ and $BC \leftrightarrow QR$, so
$\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{8}{20} = \dfrac{12}{QR}$, so $8 \cdot QR = 12 \cdot 20
= 240$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $QR = \dfrac{240}{8} = 30$.

\ans{QR = 30 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Check:} $k = \frac{20}{8} = 2.5$ and $12 \times 2.5 = 30$
$\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Garden beds: sides $9$ cm and $12$ cm, smaller perimeter
$24$ cm.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Perimeter is a sum of sides, so it scales by the same factor
the sides do --- no squaring here.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{12}{9} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $P_{\text{large}} = 24 \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = 32$.

\ans{32 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Teaching note:} a student can also check by scaling each side and
adding --- slower, but it shows why the shortcut works.}

\problem{\textbf{Tiles: sides $10$ cm and $15$ cm, smaller area $40$
cm$^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Area is measured in two dimensions, so it scales by $k^2$,
not by $k$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{15}{10} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so
$k^2 = \dfrac{9}{4} = 2.25$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $A_{\text{large}} = 40 \times 2.25 = 90$.

\ans{90 \text{ cm}^2}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $60$ --- the area was multiplied by $\frac{3}{2}$
instead of by $\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2$.}

\problem{\textbf{$\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$, $AD = 6$, $AB = 15$,
$BC = 20$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} (a) Because $\overline{DE} \parallel \overline{BC}$, the
transversal $\overline{AB}$ makes $\angle ADE \cong \angle ABC$ (corresponding
angles), and $\angle A$ is shared. Two pairs of congruent angles is the
\textbf{AA} criterion, so $\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC$.

\textbf{Step 2.} (b) Corresponding sides then satisfy
$\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{DE}{BC}$, i.e. $\dfrac{6}{15} = \dfrac{DE}{20}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $15 \cdot DE = 6 \cdot 20 = 120$, so $DE = 8$.

\ans{DE = 8 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Manual review:} part (a) is the written half. Accept any correct AA
argument; the shared angle at $A$ must be named, not assumed.}

\problem{\textbf{Flagpole: student $6$ ft with a $4$ ft shadow, pole shadow
$26$ ft.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The sun's rays strike both objects at the same angle, and both
stand vertically, so the two triangles are similar by AA --- height to shadow
is the pair to compare.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{shadow}}$ is the same for both:
$\dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{h}{26}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $4h = 6 \cdot 26 = 156$, so $h = 39$.

\ans{h = 39 \text{ ft}}

\textit{Sanity check:} the pole's shadow is $6.5$ times the student's, and
$6 \times 6.5 = 39$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Perimeters $45$ cm and $30$ cm, $AB = 12$ cm --- find $XY$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The perimeter ratio IS the side ratio, so the two perimeters
give $k$ without knowing a single side of $\triangle XYZ$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{30}{45} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ (from $ABC$ to $XYZ$).

\textbf{Step 3.} $XY = 12 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = 8$.

\ans{XY = 8 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Listen for:} $18$ --- from scaling by $\frac{3}{2}$, the wrong
direction.}

\problem{\textbf{Areas $27$ cm$^2$ and $48$ cm$^2$, smaller perimeter $15$
cm.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The area ratio is $k^2$, and the perimeter needs $k$ --- so
take a square root before scaling anything.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k^2 = \dfrac{48}{27} = \dfrac{16}{9}$, so
$k = \sqrt{\dfrac{16}{9}} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $P_{\text{large}} = 15 \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = 20$.

\ans{20 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $26.67$ --- the perimeter was scaled by the AREA
ratio $\frac{16}{9}$ instead of by its square root.}

\problem{\textbf{Perimeters $30$ cm and $42$ cm, a $7$ cm side.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Perimeters scale like sides, so their ratio is the factor that
carries the $7$ cm side across.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{42}{30} = \dfrac{7}{5} = 1.4$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Corresponding side $= 7 \times 1.4 = 9.8$.

\ans{9.8 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Check:} $9.8$ is a little under one and a half times $7$, which matches
a perimeter that grew from $30$ to $42$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: perimeters $36$ cm and $54$ cm, area of
$\triangle ABC$ is $48$ cm$^2$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} (a) Perimeter ratio is the scale factor:
$k = \dfrac{54}{36} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

\ans{k = 1.5}

\textbf{Step 2.} (b) Area scales by $k^2 = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2
= \dfrac{9}{4} = 2.25$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $A_{DEF} = 48 \times 2.25 = 108$.

\ans{108 \text{ cm}^2}

\textbf{Step 4.} (c) \textit{Model answer:} a triangle's area is
$\frac{1}{2}bh$. Doubling every side doubles the base and doubles the height,
and the area depends on both, so it grows by $2 \times 2 = 4$. Scaling a length
stretches one direction; scaling an area stretches two, which is exactly why
the area ratio is $k^2$ while the perimeter ratio is $k$.

\textit{Manual review:} part (c) is the written half --- look for both
dimensions being named, not just the word ``squared''.}
