Here are three PDFs for take-from and comparison subtraction stories within 10.

**Worksheet (3 pages, 8 problems).** Every problem is a story with a stated unit
— crayons, stickers, cubes, grapes, frogs and ducks, buttons, shells, apples and
pears. Three problems come with a picture to cross out or match up, two ask the
child to draw the objects, and the numbers stay inside 10 throughout. The set
covers the two story shapes the focus names: *take from* (something leaves the
group) and *compare* (two groups side by side, nothing leaves), plus two
problems where the missing number sits in the middle of the story instead of at
the end. Problem 8 is a two-step challenge: three apples are eaten first, and
the comparison with the pears has to use what is left, not the starting count.

**Answer key (2 pages).** Each problem is worked in steps, not just answered:
which kind of story it is, why subtraction fits, then the arithmetic and the
boxed answer with its unit. Where a story invites a predictable mistake, the key
says so — the quick-answer bank at the top lists the common wrong answers
(adding the two groups instead of comparing, writing the number eaten instead of
the number left, counting every animal in the story) so you can tell at a glance
which misreading produced a wrong answer.

**Study guide (2 pages).** Three sections — take-from stories, comparison
stories, and stories with a missing part. Each has a plain-language rule, a
worked example, and a try-it problem with the answer printed upside down inside
the box so the child attempts it first. A closing watch-out box addresses the
single most common trap at this age: the word "more" in a comparison sentence
does not mean add.

**Verification.** All 8 worksheet problems and all 6 study-guide boxes were
machine-checked with SymPy: the six take-from and comparison answers were
recomputed from the givens, and the two missing-part problems were solved as
equations. Nothing is flagged for manual review — every answer on both documents
is machine-verified. The declared misconception traps were also checked to
confirm each wrong method really does land on a different number from the
correct one.

Standard tagged: K.OA.A / 1.OA.C.6 / 2.OA.B.2. Difficulty runs 1 to 4 across the
sheet.
