\problem{A shape with three straight sides, sitting flat. (a) Sides? (b) Name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Trace each straight side with a finger and count:
three sides. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} three straight sides makes it a
\textbf{triangle}.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{A tilted shape standing on a corner. (a) Sides? (b) Name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count the sides: four, all the same length, with
square corners. \ans{4}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} it is still a \textbf{square}. Tilting a shape
does not change what it is --- only how it sits on the page.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{A tipped-over shape. (a) Sides? (b) Corners? (c) Name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count the sides: \ans{4}

(b) Each pair of sides meets at a corner --- count them: \ans{4}

(c) \emph{Instructor judges:} opposite sides are equal and the corners are
square, so it is a \textbf{rectangle}, even lying at a slant.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{A shape pointing down. (a) Corners? (b) Name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count where the sides meet: three corners. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} it is a \textbf{triangle} --- pointing down
does not change the name.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Hexagon: 6 sides. Square: 4 sides. How many more sides has the
hexagon?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare the counts: $6 - 4 = 2$. The hexagon has
\ans{2} more sides.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ben says the tilted shape is a diamond, not a square. (a) Sides?
(b) Is Ben right?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count the sides: \ans{4}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} Ben is not right. The shape has 4 equal sides
and square corners, so it is a square --- a tilted square is still a square.
``Diamond'' only describes how it is sitting.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Kim's shape has 3 straight sides, drawn long, skinny, and tilted.
(a) Corners? (b) Name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) A shape with 3 straight sides always has 3 corners
--- every pair of sides meets once. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} it is a \textbf{triangle}. Long, skinny, or
tilted, the name comes from the 3 sides, not the look.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: square (4 corners) and triangle (3 corners). (a) Corners
all together? (b) Does turning change a name?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Add the two counts: $4 + 3 = 7$. \ans{7}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} no --- turning never changes a shape's name.
The name comes from the number of sides and corners, and turning changes
neither.
