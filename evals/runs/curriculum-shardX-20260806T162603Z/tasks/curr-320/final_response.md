Here are the three PDFs for the similarity synthesis set:

- **Worksheet** — `ws_similarscale_curr320.pdf` (4 pages)
- **Answer key** — `ak_similarscale_curr320.pdf` (3 pages)
- **Study guide** — `ss_similarscale_curr320.pdf` (2 pages)

**What the worksheet covers.** Ten problems that deliberately interleave the
four things similarity lets you do: find a missing side from the ratio, find the
scale factor itself, scale a perimeter, and scale an area. The point of the
sheet is choosing between them — after a short blocked warm-up the facets rotate
(side, side, factor, perimeter, area, side, area, perimeter, factor, area), so
the student cannot run the same method down the page.

It ramps from a one-step proportion to problems that run the relationships
backwards: recovering a side ratio from two areas (7), scaling a perimeter when
only the areas are known (8), and finding the side factor from an area
multiplier (9). Contexts are concrete — road signs, floor tiles, garden plots,
sails, a window enlargement.

**What is verified, honestly.** The verification file holds 13 entries, one per
response the sheet asks for.

- **11 entries are machine-checked** with SymPy: every length, ratio, perimeter
  and area (21 cm, 14 in, 2.5, 45 m, 75 cm², 17.5 cm, 1.5, 48 cm, 2.5, 210 cm,
  126 cm²). **8 of the 10 problems are fully machine-checked end to end.**
- **2 entries are instructor-judged**: problem 8(b) ("justify why the perimeter
  ratio is the square root of the area ratio") and problem 10(b) ("explain why
  scaling every side by a factor multiplies the area by its square"). These are
  written reasoning, so the key prints a grading rubric for each instead of an
  answer, and the "What is verified" note names problems 8 and 10 as the two
  carrying an instructor-judged part.

No answer is marked `[unchecked]`. Four problems (4, 5, 7, 8) carry declared
misconception traps, so the key's "Common wrong answers" block tells you what a
student who scaled a perimeter by k² (67.5 m), an area by k (45 cm²), or used an
area ratio where a side ratio belonged (2.25 and 76.8 cm) would have written.

**Study guide.** One shared rule box up front — lengths by k, perimeter by k,
area by k² — then four short sections, one per skill the worksheet mixes, each
with a worked example that opens with the reasoning and a try-it whose answer is
printed upside down. The closing watch-out box covers the reverse direction:
areas in the ratio 9:4 mean sides in the ratio 3:2.
