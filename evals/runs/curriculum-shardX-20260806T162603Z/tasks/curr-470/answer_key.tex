\problem{Oil slick, $\frac{dr}{dt} = 0.5$ m/s. Find $\frac{dA}{dt}$ when
$r = 20$ m.}

\textbf{Solution.} The relation is $A = \pi r^2$; differentiate with respect to
time, then substitute.
\[ \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\,\frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi(20)(0.5) = 20\pi \]
\[ \boxed{\frac{dA}{dt} \approx 62.83} \]

\problem{Garden, $48$ m of fence, $A(x) = x(24 - x)$. (a) $A'(x)$. (b) The
maximizing $x$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} Expand first: $A(x) = 24x - x^2$.
\[ \boxed{A'(x) = 24 - 2x} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $24 - 2x = 0$.
\[ \boxed{x = 12} \]
Then the other side is $24 - 12 = 12$ as well: the maximizing rectangle is a
square, and its area is $144$ square metres.

\problem{$f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 2$. (a) $f'(x)$. (b) Critical $x$-values.
(c) Inflection $x$-value.}

\textbf{Solution (a).}
\[ \boxed{f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3) = 0$.
\[ \boxed{x = 1 \text{ or } x = 3} \]

\textbf{Solution (c).} $f''(x) = 6x - 12 = 0$, and $f''$ changes sign there.
\[ \boxed{x = 2} \]

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Open-top box, square base $x$, volume $32000$ cm$^3$,
$S = x^2 + 4xh$. (a) $S(x)$. (b) The minimizing $x$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} The constraint $x^2h = 32000$ gives
$h = \dfrac{32000}{x^2}$, so $S = x^2 + 4x\cdot\dfrac{32000}{x^2}$.
\[ \boxed{S(x) = x^2 + \frac{128000}{x}} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $S'(x) = 2x - \dfrac{128000}{x^2} = 0$ gives
$2x^3 = 128000$, so $x^3 = 64000$.
\[ \boxed{x = 40} \]
Since $S''(x) = 2 + \dfrac{256000}{x^3} > 0$ for $x > 0$, this is a minimum.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2 + 5$. (a) $f''(x)$. (b) Inflection $x$-values.
(c) Classify $x = 0$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x$, so
\[ \boxed{f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $12x^2 - 12 = 0$, and the concavity changes at each root.
\[ \boxed{x = -1 \text{ or } x = 1} \]

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} $f'(0) = 0$, so the origin is a
critical point, and $f''(0) = -12 < 0$, so the curve is concave down there:
$x = 0$ gives a local maximum. A sign chart for $f'$ (positive to the left of
$0$, negative to the right) earns equal credit. A response that stops at
``$f'(0) = 0$'' has not classified anything.

\problem{Inverted cone, $\frac{dV}{dt} = 2$ m$^3$/min, $r = \frac{h}{2}$.
(a) $V$ in terms of $h$. (b) $\frac{dh}{dt}$ when $h = 4$ m.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} Substituting $r = \dfrac{h}{2}$ into
$V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ removes $r$ before any differentiation:
\[ \boxed{V = \frac{\pi h^3}{12}} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} Differentiating,
$\dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{\pi h^2}{4}\cdot\dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so
$\dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{4}{\pi h^2}\cdot\dfrac{dV}{dt}$. At $h = 4$ this is
$\dfrac{4(2)}{\pi(4)^2} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{dh}{dt} \approx 0.16} \]

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$g'(x) = (x - 1)^2(x - 4)$. (a) Critical $x$-values. (b) What happens
to $g$ at each.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} $g'$ is zero where either factor vanishes.
\[ \boxed{x = 1 \text{ or } x = 4} \]

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The factor $(x-1)^2$ is never
negative, so the sign of $g'$ is the sign of $(x - 4)$: negative for $x < 4$
(except at $x = 1$, where it is zero) and positive for $x > 4$. So $g'$ does
\emph{not} change sign at $x = 1$ --- the graph flattens and keeps falling, no
extremum --- while at $x = 4$ it changes from negative to positive, a local
minimum. Full credit needs both conclusions with the sign reasoning; a graph
sketch alone does not.

\problem{Two cars, north at $60$ km/h and east at $80$ km/h; after $2$ hours
they are $120$ km and $160$ km out, $200$ km apart. Find the rate the distance
changes.}

\textbf{Solution.} With $x$ east and $y$ north, $x^2 + y^2 = D^2$, so
$x\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt} = D\frac{dD}{dt}$ and
\[ \frac{dD}{dt} = \frac{(160)(80) + (120)(60)}{200} = \frac{12800 + 7200}{200}. \]
\[ \boxed{\frac{dD}{dt} = 100} \]
Kilometres per hour. Note that this is not $80 + 60$: the distance grows more
slowly than the sum of the speeds because the cars move at right angles.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Rectangle under $y = 12 - x^2$, $A(x) = 24x - 2x^3$. (a) $A'(x)$.
(b) Solutions of $A'(x) = 0$. (c) Maximum area.}

\textbf{Solution (a).}
\[ \boxed{A'(x) = 24 - 6x^2} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $24 - 6x^2 = 0$ gives $x^2 = 4$.
\[ \boxed{x = -2 \text{ or } x = 2} \]

\textbf{Solution (c).} Only $x = 2$ is in the region $x > 0$; there the
rectangle is $4$ wide and $8$ tall, so $A(2) = 48 - 16$.
\[ \boxed{A(2) = 32} \]

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Balloon, $\frac{dV}{dt} = 100$ cm$^3$/s. (a) $\frac{dr}{dt}$ at
$r = 10$. (b) $\frac{dS}{dt}$ there. (c) Compare with $r = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution (a).} $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\dfrac{dr}{dt}$, so
$\dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{100}{4\pi(10)^2} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{dr}{dt} \approx 0.08} \]

\textbf{Solution (b).} $\dfrac{dS}{dt} = 8\pi r\dfrac{dr}{dt}
= 8\pi(10)\left(\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\right)$.
\[ \boxed{\frac{dS}{dt} = 20} \]
Square centimetres per second --- and the $\pi$ cancels, which is a good sign
the setup is right.

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} Combining the two steps,
$\dfrac{dS}{dt} = 8\pi r\cdot\dfrac{1}{4\pi r^2}\dfrac{dV}{dt}
= \dfrac{2}{r}\dfrac{dV}{dt}$, which \emph{decreases} as $r$ grows. At $r = 5$
the rate is $40$, twice the value at $r = 10$, so the surface area grows faster
at the smaller radius. Full credit needs the comparison and a reason; naming
the larger number alone does not.
