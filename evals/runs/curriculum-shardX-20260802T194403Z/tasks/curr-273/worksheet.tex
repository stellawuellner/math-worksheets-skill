\wstitleblock{Arithmetic Sequences as Linear Functions}{Algebra 1}{}

\begin{center}
Writing recursive rules from listed sequences and from applications, using a
recursive rule to reach a later term, and rewriting a recursive rule as an
explicit linear function
\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{Directions.} A recursive formula needs \emph{two} lines:
the starting value $a_1$ and the step rule $a_n = a_{n-1} + d$ for $n \geq 2$,
where $d$ is the common difference. \textbf{Write both lines for every problem}
--- with units where the problem has them --- and then answer the question that
follows. The answer line is for the final numerical answer.\normalsize

\problem[5cm]{A growing tile pattern uses this many tiles in each stage:
\[ 4, \quad 11, \quad 18, \quad 25, \quad \ldots \]
Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the number of tiles in stage $n$, then
find the number of tiles in stage 6.}

\problem[5cm]{A drone descends in equal steps. Its height in meters at each
reading is
\[ 63, \quad 58, \quad 53, \quad \ldots \]
Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the height at reading $n$, then find the
height at reading 9. (Watch the sign of $d$.)}

\problem[5cm]{A tank already holds 240 liters of water at the end of hour 1, and
a pump adds 85 liters during each hour after that.\\[3pt]
Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the number of liters in the tank at the
end of hour $n$. Then find how much water the tank holds at the end of hour 5.
\answerline{L}}

\problem[5cm]{A sequence is defined by
\[ a_1 = 12, \qquad a_n = a_{n-1} + 9 \quad (n \geq 2). \]
Say in one sentence what this rule tells you to do, then find $a_{10}$.}

\problem[6cm]{A sequence is defined by
\[ a_1 = 7, \qquad a_n = a_{n-1} + 4 \quad (n \geq 2). \]
Because the same amount is added each step, this sequence is a linear function of
$n$. Rewrite it as an explicit rule $a_n = mn + b$, state what $m$ and $b$ mean
in terms of the recursive rule, and use the explicit rule to find $a_{20}$.}

\problem[5cm]{A sequence is defined by
\[ a_1 = 5, \qquad a_n = a_{n-1} + 6 \quad (n \geq 2). \]
One term of this sequence equals 89. Which term is it? Give the term number $n$.}

\problem[5cm]{One chair is 84 centimeters tall. Each additional chair stacked on
top of the pile adds 9 centimeters to the total height.\\[3pt]
Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the height in centimeters of a pile of $n$
chairs. Then find the height of a pile of 12 chairs.
\answerline{cm}}

\problem[6cm]{An arithmetic sequence is recorded in this table, but the first two
terms were lost.
\begin{center}\large
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
\rule{0pt}{0.7cm}$n$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\\hline
\rule{0pt}{0.7cm}$a_n$ & ? & ? & 20 & & & & 44 \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Find the common difference from the two known terms, write the recursive formula,
and give the missing value of $a_1$.}

\problem[5cm]{A student's savings account holds 45 dollars at the end of week 1,
and the student deposits 18 dollars at the end of every week after that.\\[3pt]
Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the balance in dollars at the end of week
$n$. In which week does the balance first reach 405 dollars? Give the week
number.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Synthesis.} A theater is built so that row 1 has 22 seats
and each row after it has 3 more seats than the row in front of it.\\[3pt]
(i) Write the recursive formula for $a_n$, the number of seats in row $n$.
(ii) Explain in one sentence why a recursive rule alone is awkward for this next
question. (iii) Find the total number of seats in the first 15 rows.}
