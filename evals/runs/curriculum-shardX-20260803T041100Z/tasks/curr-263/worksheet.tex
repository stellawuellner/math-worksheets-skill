\begin{center}\small\textit{Scaling one equation, scaling both equations, and
building a system from a real situation before eliminating}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The organizer.} Elimination needs one variable's coefficients to
be opposites (or equal). When they are not, \emph{scale}: multiply every term of
an equation --- both sides --- by a constant. Use this layout on every problem.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{2.6cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{5.0cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}m{5.6cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{What you write} & \textbf{Why} \\ \hline
Target & ``eliminate $y$'' & pick the variable that is cheaper to match \\ \hline
Scale & multiplier next to each equation & make the coefficients opposites \\ \hline
Combine & add the scaled equations & the target variable drops out \\ \hline
Back-substitute & put the value into either original & find the second variable \\ \hline
Check & test the pair in \emph{both} originals & a pair that fits only one is not a solution \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent\small\textit{The order of work for every problem on this sheet. No
values shown: use the numbers given in each problem.}\normalsize

\noindent\textbf{Directions.}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Write the multiplier you use beside each equation you scale.
  \item Solve for both variables and write the ordered pair on the answer line.
  \item Check your pair in both original equations before you write it down.
\end{itemize}

\problem[8cm]{A school store sells notebooks and binders. Two notebooks and one
binder cost \$$11$. One notebook and three binders cost \$$18$. Let $x$ be the
price of a notebook and $y$ the price of a binder, both in dollars.
\[ 2x + y = 11 \qquad\qquad x + 3y = 18 \]
Scale one equation, eliminate a variable, and find both prices.}

\problem[8cm]{Solve by elimination. Scaling one equation is enough here.
\[ 4x - 3y = 5 \qquad\qquad x + y = 3 \]}

\problem[8cm]{A theater sold $12$ tickets to one show for \$$88$ in all. Adult
tickets cost \$$9$ each and child tickets cost \$$5$ each. Let $a$ be the number
of adult tickets and $c$ the number of child tickets.
\[ a + c = 12 \qquad\qquad 9a + 5c = 88 \]
Solve the system by elimination and state how many of each ticket were sold.}

\problem[8cm]{Solve by elimination. Neither pair of coefficients matches, so
both equations must be scaled.
\[ 3x + 4y = 10 \qquad\qquad 5x + 6y = 16 \]}

\problem[8cm]{Solve by elimination.
\[ 5x + 2y = 4 \qquad\qquad 3x + y = 3 \]}

\problem[8cm]{A bakery sold $50$ items on Saturday and took in \$$172$. Muffins
sell for \$$3$ each and scones for \$$4$ each. Let $m$ be the number of muffins
and $s$ the number of scones.
\[ m + s = 50 \qquad\qquad 3m + 4s = 172 \]
Solve the system by elimination and state how many of each were sold.}

\problem[8cm]{Solve by elimination. Choose the variable that needs the smaller
multipliers, and say which one you chose.
\[ 4x + 5y = 7 \qquad\qquad 6x + 7y = 11 \]}

\problem[8cm]{A canoe travels $24$ kilometres downstream in $2$ hours and the
same $24$ kilometres back upstream in $3$ hours. Let $b$ be the canoe's speed in
still water and $c$ the speed of the current, both in kilometres per hour.
Downstream the speeds add, upstream they subtract:
\[ 2b + 2c = 24 \qquad\qquad 3b - 3c = 24 \]
Solve the system by elimination and state both speeds in kilometres per hour.}

\problem[9cm]{\textbf{Find and fix the mistake.} Maya is solving
\[ x + 4y = 14 \qquad\qquad 3x - 2y = 14 \]
She writes: ``Multiply the first equation by $3$ to match the $3x$: that gives
$3x + 12y = 14$.'' \par
(a) Explain in one or two sentences what is wrong with her scaled equation and
why multiplying \emph{every} term by $3$ leaves the solution unchanged.
\par (b) Scale correctly and solve the system.}

\problem[9cm]{A lab technician mixes a $30\%$ salt solution with a $50\%$ salt
solution to make $20$ litres of a $45\%$ salt solution. Let $x$ be the litres of
the $30\%$ solution and $y$ the litres of the $50\%$ solution. The volumes add,
and the salt adds:
\[ x + y = 20 \qquad\qquad 30x + 50y = 45 \cdot 20 \]
Solve the system by elimination and state how many litres of each solution are
used.}
