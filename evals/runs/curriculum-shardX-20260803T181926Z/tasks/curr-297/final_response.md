Here are three PDFs on transformations of absolute-value functions, built for an Algebra 1 student working toward procedural fluency.

**Worksheet (`ws_absvalue_curr297.pdf`, 6 pages, 12 problems)**
Everything on the sheet is the parent function `f(x) = |x|` pushed around: vertex form `g(x) = a|x - h| + k`. The set ramps from reading a vertex off an equation (problems 1–3), through sketching on printed coordinate grids (4, 6, 10) and solving from the shape of the V (5, 8), to writing the equation from a vertex plus one point (9, 11) and a closing modelling problem where `d(t) = |2t - 6| + 1` has to be rewritten as `2|t - 3| + 1` before it can be read (12). Difficulty runs 1 → 5 with no dips, and no two problems share a skeleton. Three problems get a full blank coordinate grid to draw on, plus real work space on every other problem.

**Answer key (`ak_absvalue_curr297.pdf`, 3 pages)**
Every problem is restated and worked step by step — not just answers. It opens with a "Quick Answers" strip for fast grading, a Curriculum block (level, standards codes HSF-IF.A–HSF-IF.C and HSA-CED.A.1–A.4 with the problems each covers, and the difficulty range), and a "Common wrong answers" list generated from the three misconception traps built into problems 3, 7 and 10 — so if your student writes 2 on problem 3, the key tells you they read `|x - 1|` as a shift left; if they write 2 on problem 10, they reflected the graph but forgot to stretch it.

**Study guide (`ss_absvalue_curr297.pdf`, 2 pages)**
Four skill sections, each with a rule box, a worked example that opens by saying *why* that method is the right one, and a try-it with the answer printed upside down inside the box: reading the vertex and transformations, sketching from vertex form, solving with the graph in mind, and writing the equation from the graph. Two watch-out boxes cover the two errors that cost the most marks (the sign inside the bars, and splitting the bars before dividing by `a`).

**What was verified.** 23 of the 26 checks in the worksheet's verification file were recomputed independently by SymPy and passed — every vertex coordinate, every intercept, every solution set, and both "find a and k" systems. Every study-guide example and try-it answer was verified the same way (11 checks, all passing). The three items flagged for manual review are exactly the three that cannot be machine-checked: the sketches themselves on problems 4, 6 and 10. The key shows a full model description of each graph for comparison. Problem 12(b), "explain what the vertex means for the drone", is likewise a written explanation — the key gives a model answer.
