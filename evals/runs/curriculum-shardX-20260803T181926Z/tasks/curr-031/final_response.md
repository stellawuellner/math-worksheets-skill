Here are the three PDFs on teen numbers.

**Worksheet — `ws_teennumbers_curr031.pdf` (5 pages, 8 problems).** Every
problem is about the same one idea: a teen number is one ten and some ones.
Nothing on the sheet is a bare number sentence — each problem gives a picture
to work from or to draw on. Problems 1 and 2 build a teen number from a model
(a full ten-frame plus extras; a ten-stick plus loose cubes) and ask for
$10 + \underline{\ \ } = \underline{\ \ }$. Problems 3 and 4 go the other way:
the numeral is given, the frames are empty, and your child fills the first
frame completely before counting the leftovers. Problems 5, 6 and 8 compare —
two block piles with matching ten-sticks so only the ones can decide, then
three numbers to place on a 10-to-20 number line, then a two-part story
question. Problem 7 is a number bond with the whole (17) and one part (the ten)
filled in and the other part missing, which is the same fact read backwards.
The models deliberately vary — ten-frames, base-ten blocks, a number line, a
number bond — so the idea is not tied to one picture. It is set in 14 pt with
generous writing space.

**Answer key — `ak_teennumbers_curr031.pdf` (2 pages).** Every problem worked
in words a parent can read aloud, not just the answer: "the first frame is
full, and a full frame always holds ten, so count only the second frame." Each
one also carries a short *Listen for* or *Note* line — what a child's mistake
would sound like, and what question to ask instead of correcting. The top of
the key has an at-a-glance answer bank plus a Curriculum section built from the
standards tags.

**Study guide — `ss_teennumbers_curr031.pdf` (2 pages).** A reference card, also
in 14 pt: the nine teen facts written as $10 + \text{ones}$, then three short
sections — building a teen number, breaking one apart, and comparing two of
them. Each has a plain-words rule, one worked example (the first with the
ten-frame picture drawn), and a separate try-it whose answer is printed upside
down inside the box so your child can check without being shown the work. It
ends with the mistake that matters most here: the ones are the leftovers, so 15
is one ten and 5 ones, never one ten and 15 ones.

**What was verified.** All nine machine checks passed and nothing on this sheet
is flagged for manual review — every answer, including both `<`/`>` comparisons
and the ordering of 12, 16, 19, was recomputed independently and matched. The
one declared misconception trap (writing the whole teen number in the ones box
on problem 3) was machine-checked to be visibly different from the correct
answer, and it is printed in the key as a common wrong answer so you know what
to look for.
