\problem{Write the 6th term of pattern A (start at 0, add 4).}

\textbf{Solution:} The 6th term is 5 steps past the start, not 6 --- the first term is the start itself. So $0 + 5 \times 4$. Listing confirms it: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Write the 6th term of pattern B (start at 0, add 8).}

\textbf{Solution:} Same count of steps, larger step: $0 + 5 \times 8$. Listing: 0, 8, 16, 24, 32, 40. \ans{40}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Each time pattern A adds its step, pattern B adds a step how many times as large?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare the steps themselves, not the terms: pattern B adds 8 where pattern A adds 4, so divide $8 \div 4$. \ans{2}

This single fact is what makes every later comparison on this sheet come out the way it does.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How much greater is the 10th term of pattern B than the 10th term of pattern A?}

\textbf{Solution:} Both 10th terms are 9 steps past a start of 0. Pattern B: $0 + 9 \times 8 = 72$. Pattern A: $0 + 9 \times 4 = 36$. Subtract: $72 - 36$. \ans{36}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Write the ordered pair (pattern A's 4th term, pattern B's 4th term).}

\textbf{Solution:} Both 4th terms are 3 steps past 0. Pattern A: $0 + 3 \times 4 = 12$, which is the across number. Pattern B: $0 + 3 \times 8 = 24$, the up number. \ans{(12, 24)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Maya has 12 dollars and adds 3 dollars a week; Liam has 4 dollars and adds 5 dollars a week. After how many weeks are they equal?}

\textbf{Solution:} Generate both patterns week by week.

Maya: 12, 15, 18, 21, 24. \quad Liam: 4, 9, 14, 19, 24.

They first agree in the fifth entry of each list, which is after 4 weeks of saving. Algebraically, $12 + 3w = 4 + 5w$ gives $8 = 2w$. \ans{4 \text{ weeks}}

Liam starts 8 dollars behind and gains 2 dollars a week on Maya, so he needs $8 \div 2$ weeks to catch up.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{How many dollars will each have at the moment their savings are equal?}

\textbf{Solution:} Put the number of weeks back into either rule --- both must give the same total, which is the check. Maya: $12 + 3 \times 4$. Liam: $4 + 5 \times 4 = 24$ as well. \ans{24}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{From the table of patterns P and Q, write the ordered pair (Q's 5th term, P's 5th term).}

\textbf{Solution:} Read down the Q row to term 5, which is the across number: 18. Then read the P row to term 5 for the up number: 19. The order named in the question decides which goes first. \ans{(18, 19)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{(a) Mean of pattern P's five terms. (b) Mean of pattern Q's five terms.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $7 + 10 + 13 + 16 + 19 = 65$, and $65 \div 5$. \ans{13}

\textbf{(b)} $2 + 6 + 10 + 14 + 18 = 50$, and $50 \div 5$. \ans{10}

Both means land exactly on the middle term of their own pattern. That is not luck: in a pattern that grows by a fixed step, the terms are balanced around the middle one.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Patterns A and B again. (a) 12th term of A. (b) 12th term of B. (c) Describe and justify the relationship between matching terms.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} 11 steps past 0: $0 + 11 \times 4$. \ans{44}

\textbf{(b)} 11 steps past 0 with the larger step: $0 + 11 \times 8$. \ans{88}

\textbf{(c) --- instructor-judged.} The relationship is that every term of pattern B is double the matching term of pattern A.

Look for a reason, not only the pattern spotted: both patterns start at the same place, 0, and pattern B adds twice as much on every single step, so after any equal number of steps B has added exactly twice as much as A. A response listing three matching pairs and stopping has noticed the rule but not shown why it cannot break.
