# Adding, Subtracting, and Missing Numbers to 20 — Kindergarten / Grade 1

Three PDFs are ready.

**Study guide (2 pages)** — sit with this one before the worksheet. It opens
with a "what is this one asking?" box (plus sign at the end means put together;
minus sign at the end means take away; a blank at the start or in the middle
means a number is missing), then three short sections, each with a rule, a
two-step worked example, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside
the box:

1. adding to 20 by making a ten — $8 + 6$
2. subtracting to 20 by stepping down to ten, or by thinking addition — $15 - 8$
3. finding the missing number by counting on — $6 + \underline{\ \ } = 13$

The watch-out box addresses the single biggest misreading at this age: the equals
sign means "the same as", not "the answer comes next".

**Worksheet (4 pages, 10 problems)** — the three kinds are deliberately mixed so
your child has to decide what each one is asking rather than repeat one
procedure ten times. Problems 1–2 are the warm-up, then addition, subtraction
and missing-number problems alternate. Two of them are deliberately awkward on
purpose: problem 6 puts the blank at the *start* ($\underline{\ \ } - 4 = 9$),
where subtracting the two printed numbers gives the wrong answer, and problem 9
asks "how many more does he need", which is a missing-addend question dressed as
a story. The last problem is a fact family: one addition fact ($9 + 4 = 13$) and
three more facts built from the same three numbers.

A number line from 0 to 20 is printed with the directions, and every problem has
4.5–6 cm of space to draw, count, or write in. Counters and fingers are
explicitly allowed.

**Answer key (2 pages)** — quick answers at the top for fast checking, then a
three-step explanation for every problem, written the way you would say it out
loud. Step 1 always names *which kind* of problem it is, because that decision is
what the sheet is teaching. Two problems carry a note about the specific wrong
answer to expect and what it tells you: $5$ on problem 6 means the child
subtracted instead of adding back, and $17$ on problem 9 means they added the two
numbers instead of finding the difference.

**Verification.** Every one of the 10 problems was machine-checked with SymPy —
12 checks in total, since the fact-family problem carries three. All 12 passed.

**Nothing is flagged for manual review** on this sheet: every answer is a single
number, so nothing needed to be left to human judgement. (The key does note that
the two addition facts in problem 10 may be written in either order.)

Standard: 1.OA.C.6 throughout. Difficulty ramps gently, 1 → 4.
