\problem{Complete the cycle table for $i^{1}$ through $i^{4}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each entry is the previous one multiplied by $i$. The
definition of the imaginary unit gives the second entry directly: \ans{i^2 = -1}

Multiplying again, $i^{2} \cdot i = -1 \cdot i$, so \ans{i^3 = -i}

Once more, $i^{3} \cdot i = -i \cdot i = -i^{2}$, so \ans{i^4 = 1}

After the fourth power the values repeat in the same order, which is the fact
every later problem on this sheet uses.

\problem{Simplify $i^{27}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Divide the exponent by $4$: $27 = 4 \cdot 6 + 3$, so
$i^{27} = (i^{4})^{6} \cdot i^{3} = 1 \cdot i^{3}$. From the table,
$i^{3} = -i$. \ans{-i}

\problem{$z = 2 + 5i$. (a) Write $-z$ and $2z$ in standard form. (b) Plot
$z$, $-z$ and $2z$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Negating a complex number negates both parts, so \ans{-z = -2 - 5i}

Multiplying by the real number $2$ scales both parts, so \ans{2z = 4 + 10i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} On the plane, $z$ is the point
$(2, 5)$, $-z$ is $(-2, -5)$ --- the reflection of $z$ through the origin ---
and $2z$ is $(4, 10)$, on the same ray from the origin but twice as far out.
All three points must be plotted and labelled. Grade the plot against the values
the student wrote in part (a).

\problem{Simplify $i^{40} + i^{22}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $40$ is a multiple of $4$, so $i^{40} = 1$. For $22$,
$22 = 4 \cdot 5 + 2$, so $i^{22} = i^{2} = -1$. Adding, $1 + (-1)$. \ans{0}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$z = 3 + i$. (a) Compute $iz$. (b) Plot $z$ and $iz$ and describe what
multiplying by $i$ did.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Distribute and replace $i^{2}$ with $-1$:
$i(3 + i) = 3i + i^{2} = -1 + 3i$. \ans{iz = -1 + 3i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} $z$ is the point $(3, 1)$ and $iz$ is
$(-1, 3)$. A correct description says that multiplying by $i$ turned the point a
quarter turn ($90^\circ$) counterclockwise about the origin, and that its
distance from the origin is unchanged --- both points are $\sqrt{10}$ from the
origin. Describing only the sign changes, without naming a turn about the
origin, does not earn the mark.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $3i^{14} - 5i^{7}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Reduce each exponent modulo $4$.
\begin{align*}
  i^{14} &= i^{12} \cdot i^{2} = i^{2} = -1 \\
  i^{7}  &= i^{4} \cdot i^{3} = i^{3} = -i
\end{align*}
So the expression is $3(-1) - 5(-i)$. \ans{-3 + 5i}

\problem{$w$ sits $4$ units left of the origin and $3$ units above it. (a) Write
$w$ in standard form and give its distance from the origin. (b) Plot $w$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Left is the negative real direction and up is the
positive imaginary direction, so the point is $(-4, 3)$ and \ans{w = -4 + 3i}

The distance from the origin is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs
$4$ and $3$, namely $\sqrt{4^{2} + 3^{2}} = \sqrt{25}$, so the distance is \ans{5}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The point must be marked four units
left and three units up, with the segment to the origin drawn. Credit the plot
against the number the student wrote in the first blank.

\problem{A point at $z = 6 - 2i$ is turned a quarter turn counterclockwise twice,
i.e. multiplied by $i^{2}$. Give the new position and explain why two quarter
turns negate both parts.}

\textbf{Solution:} $i^{2} = -1$, so the new position is $-(6 - 2i)$. \ans{-6 + 2i}

\textbf{Written part --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit explanation says that
one multiplication by $i$ is a quarter turn about the origin, so two of them
make a half turn of $180^\circ$; a half turn sends every point to the opposite
side of the origin, which is exactly what negating both parts does. Stating
$i^{2} = -1$ without connecting it to the turn does not earn the mark.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(2i)^{5} + i^{50}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Raise both factors of $2i$ to the fifth power first.
\begin{align*}
  (2i)^{5} &= 2^{5} \cdot i^{5} = 32 \cdot i^{4} \cdot i = 32i \\
  i^{50}   &= i^{48} \cdot i^{2} = -1
\end{align*}
Adding the two results gives the standard form. \ans{-1 + 32i}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A robot arm at $z = 5 + 2i$ is turned a quarter turn counterclockwise
three times. (a) Final position? (b) Plot start and finish and justify the use of
$i^{3}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Three quarter turns is multiplication by $i$ three times,
that is by $i^{3} = -i$. Distributing,
$-i(5 + 2i) = -5i - 2i^{2}$, and $-2i^{2} = +2$. \ans{2 - 5i}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} The start is $(5, 2)$ and the finish is
$(2, -5)$; both must be plotted. A full-credit justification says that each
quarter turn is one multiplication by $i$, so three turns in a row multiply by
$i$ three times, which is $i^{3}$. A student who adds that three quarter turns
counterclockwise land in the same place as one quarter turn clockwise has seen
the extra structure. Arithmetic with no statement linking repeated turning to
the repeated factor does not earn the justification mark.
