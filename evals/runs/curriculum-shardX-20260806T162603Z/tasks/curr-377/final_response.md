# Product, Quotient, and Power Rules for Logarithms — Algebra 2

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_logprops_curr377.pdf`) — 12 problems, all applying the
  product, quotient and power properties of logarithms.
- **Answer key** (`ak_logprops_curr377.pdf`) — quick-answer bank, then a worked
  solution per problem naming which rule is used at each step.
- **Study guide** (`ss_logprops_curr377.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections (product,
  quotient, power, and all three together), each with a rule, a worked example
  that names its strategy before computing, and a try-it.

## The ramp

The worksheet opens with a three-row table stating each rule beside a fully
worked numerical pattern. Problems 1–3 follow those patterns one rule at a time
with numbers that come out exact. Problems 4–6 switch to the symbolic direction
(expanding with a general base $b$). Problems 7–8 combine two rules. Problems
9–11 add a twist each: substituting given logarithm values, condensing a
fractional coefficient into a radical, and mixing an exact value with an
approximate one. Problem 12 is the challenge, needing all three rules in the
right order plus a written justification.

No two problems share a skeleton: each is a different combination of direction
(expand or condense), rule, and whether the answer is numeric or symbolic.

## Verification — the honest split

**15 verified responses across the 12 problems**:

- **14 are machine-checked** with SymPy. The symbolic expansions and
  condensations are checked as *identities* — SymPy confirms the two expressions
  are the same function, so an answer that is right in a different but equivalent
  form still passes, and a mis-applied rule cannot. The numeric ones are
  recomputed from the givens.
- **1 is instructor-judged**, marked `---` in the bank: **12(b)**, where the
  student must say why a coefficient of $\tfrac12$ may move inside as a square
  root and name the rule. The key prints what a correct response must contain.

The key's "What is verified" note reports the same 14 of 15, naming problem 12.
No `[unchecked]` marks.
