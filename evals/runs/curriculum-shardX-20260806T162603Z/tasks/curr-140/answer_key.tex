\problem{Estimate: $6.1 \times 4.8$}

\textbf{Solution:} Round each factor to the nearest whole number:
$6.1 \to 6$ and $4.8 \to 5$. Then $6 \times 5 = 30$. \ans{30}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Estimate: $19.6 \div 4.1$}

\textbf{Solution:} Round each number: $19.6 \to 20$ and $4.1 \to 4$. Then
$20 \div 4 = 5$. \ans{5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Add: $3.75 + 2.4$}

\textbf{Solution:} Line up the \emph{decimal points}, not the last digits:
$2.4$ is $2.40$. Then $3.75 + 2.40 = 6.15$. \ans{6.15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Subtract: $8 - 2.35$}

\textbf{Solution:} Write $8$ as $8.00$, then subtract with regrouping:
$8.00 - 2.35 = 5.65$. \ans{5.65}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Multiply: $0.7 \times 0.3$}

\textbf{Solution:} $7 \times 3 = 21$; the factors carry two decimal places
between them, so the product is $21$ hundredths. \ans{0.21}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Divide: $9.6 \div 0.8$}

\textbf{Solution:} Shift \emph{both} numbers one place so the divisor is
whole: $96 \div 8 = 12$. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Markers: $4$ packs at $3.15$ dollars}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) $3.15$ rounds to $3$, and $3 \times 4$ gives the estimate. \ans{12}

(b) Exact: $3.15 \times 4 = 12 + 0.60$, so the total is \ans{12.60} dollars
--- close to the estimate, so it is reasonable.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Sandwich $2.35$, juice $3.4$, paid with $10$ dollars}

\textbf{Solution:} First the total: $2.35 + 3.40 = 5.75$. Then the change:
$10.00 - 5.75 = 4.25$. \ans{4.25}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Plank: $5.4$ m cut into $0.9$-m shelves}

\textbf{Solution:} ``How many $0.9$s fit in $5.4$?'' Shift both one place:
$54 \div 9 = 6$. So the plank makes \ans{6} shelves.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Strawberries: $2.4$ dollars per kg, $1.5$ kg}

\textbf{Solution:} $2.4 \times 1.5$: as whole numbers $24 \times 15 = 360$;
two decimal places between the factors gives $3.60$. \ans{3.6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ella's estimate: $3.2 \times 4.1$ vs.\ $12$}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) The exact product is greater: \ans{3.2 \times 4.1 > 12}

(b) Ella rounded \emph{both} factors down ($3.2$ down to $3$, $4.1$ down to
$4$), so her estimate multiplied two smaller numbers. The true product must
therefore be \emph{greater} than $12$. A correct explanation names the
downward rounding of both factors; no exact computation is needed.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: apples $2.1$ dollars/kg, $3.9$ kg, $10$-dollar bill}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) Round: $3.9 \to 4$ and $2.1 \to 2$, so the cost is about $4 \times 2$
dollars. \ans{8}

(b) Exact: $3.9 \times 2.1$: as whole numbers $39 \times 21 = 819$; two
decimal places gives $8.19$ dollars. \ans{8.19}

(c) Change: $10.00 - 8.19 = 1.81$ dollars --- and $10 - 8 = 2$ from the
estimate confirms it is reasonable. \ans{1.81}
