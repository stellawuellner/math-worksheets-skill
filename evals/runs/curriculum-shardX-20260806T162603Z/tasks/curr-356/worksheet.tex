\begin{center}
{\small\textit{Adding, subtracting, and multiplying higher-degree polynomials}}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\textbf{The model to work from}

\noindent A product of two polynomials is the total area of a rectangle whose
sides are split into the terms. Every cell of the box is one partial product,
and adding the cells --- collecting like terms as you go --- gives the answer.
Here is $(x + 2)(x^{2} + 3x)$ laid out that way:

\vspace{0.1cm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
$\times$   & $x^{2}$   & $3x$   \\ \hline
$x$        & $x^{3}$   & $3x^{2}$ \\ \hline
$2$        & $2x^{2}$  & $6x$   \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\qquad
$\longrightarrow\quad x^{3} + 5x^{2} + 6x$
\end{center}

\vspace{0.1cm}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item Write every answer in standard form: highest power first, no like terms left.
  \item A subtraction sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of \emph{every}
    term inside it.
  \item Where a box is drawn for you, fill it in and then collect the cells.
\end{itemize}
