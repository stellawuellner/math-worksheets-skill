\problem{Simplify $(3x^{3} - 5x^{2} + 2) + (x^{3} + 4x^{2} - 7x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Adding means collecting like terms; nothing changes sign.
Cubes: $3x^{3} + x^{3} = 4x^{3}$. Squares: $-5x^{2} + 4x^{2} = -x^{2}$. The
$-7x$ and the $+2$ have no partner. \ans{4x^3 - x^2 - 7x + 2}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(6x^{4} + 2x^{2} - 9) - (2x^{4} - 5x^{2} + 3)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the subtraction over all three terms first:
$6x^{4} + 2x^{2} - 9 - 2x^{4} + 5x^{2} - 3$. Now collect:
$(6 - 2)x^{4}$, $(2 + 5)x^{2}$, and $-9 - 3$. \ans{4x^4 + 7x^2 - 12}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply $(x + 4)(x^{2} - 3x + 5)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The six cells of the box are
\begin{align*}
  &x^{3},\quad -3x^{2},\quad 5x \qquad \text{(top row, from } x) \\
  &4x^{2},\quad -12x,\quad 20 \qquad \text{(bottom row, from } 4)
\end{align*}
Collecting: $-3x^{2} + 4x^{2} = x^{2}$ and $5x - 12x = -7x$. \ans{x^3 + x^2 - 7x + 20}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $2(x^{3} - 4x) - 3(2x^{3} + x - 6)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute both multipliers, remembering that the $-3$ hits
every term of the second bracket: $2x^{3} - 8x - 6x^{3} - 3x + 18$. Collecting,
$(2 - 6)x^{3}$ and $(-8 - 3)x$. \ans{-4x^3 - 11x + 18}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply $(2x^{2} - x + 3)(x^{2} + 5)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The box has two rows because the second factor has two terms.
\begin{align*}
  &2x^{4},\quad -x^{3},\quad 3x^{2} \qquad \text{(from } x^{2}) \\
  &10x^{2},\quad -5x,\quad 15 \qquad \text{(from } 5)
\end{align*}
Only the $x^{2}$ cells combine: $3x^{2} + 10x^{2} = 13x^{2}$. \ans{2x^4 - x^3 + 13x^2 - 5x + 15}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$(x - 2)(x^{2} + 2x + 4)$. (a) Expand. (b) Substitute $x = 3$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The cells are $x^{3}, 2x^{2}, 4x$ from $x$ and
$-2x^{2}, -4x, -8$ from $-2$. Every middle pair cancels:
$2x^{2} - 2x^{2} = 0$ and $4x - 4x = 0$. This is the difference-of-cubes
pattern. \ans{x^3 - 8}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $3^{3} - 8 = 27 - 8$. As a check, the original factors
give $(3 - 2)(9 + 6 + 4) = 1 \cdot 19$, the same value --- which is what
substituting a number is for. \ans{19}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(x^{4} - 3x^{3} + x) - (4x^{3} - 2x^{2} + x - 6)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the minus over all four terms:
$x^{4} - 3x^{3} + x - 4x^{3} + 2x^{2} - x + 6$. Collecting,
$-3x^{3} - 4x^{3} = -7x^{3}$, and the two $x$ terms cancel completely, so the
answer has no linear term at all. \ans{x^4 - 7x^3 + 2x^2 + 6}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Multiply $(x^{2} + 3x - 1)(x^{2} - 3x + 1)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The nine cells collect as follows. The $x^{3}$ terms
($3x^{3}$ and $-3x^{3}$) cancel; the $x^{2}$ terms are
$-9x^{2} - x^{2} + x^{2} \cdot 1 = -9x^{2}$; the $x$ terms are $3x + 3x = 6x$;
the constant is $-1$.
\begin{align*}
  (x^{2} + 3x - 1)(x^{2} - 3x + 1) &= x^{4} - (3x - 1)^{2} \\
                                   &= x^{4} - (9x^{2} - 6x + 1)
\end{align*}
Seeing it as $A^{2} - B^{2}$ with $A = x^{2}$ and $B = 3x - 1$ is the quick
route. \ans{x^4 - 9x^2 + 6x - 1}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Garden bed $(2x + 5)$ m by $(x^{2} - x + 3)$ m. Area?}

\textbf{Solution:} Area is length times width.
\begin{align*}
  (2x + 5)(x^{2} - x + 3) &= 2x^{3} - 2x^{2} + 6x + 5x^{2} - 5x + 15
\end{align*}
Collecting, $-2x^{2} + 5x^{2} = 3x^{2}$ and $6x - 5x = x$. The unit is square
metres because two lengths in metres were multiplied. \ans{2x^3 + 3x^2 + x + 15 \text{ m}^2}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$(x^{2} - 2x + 1)(x + 3) - (x^{3} + x^{2} - 5)$. (a) Simplify.
(b) Explain the drop in degree.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Expand the product first:
$(x^{2} - 2x + 1)(x + 3) = x^{3} + x^{2} - 5x + 3$. Now subtract, distributing
the minus over all three terms: $x^{3} + x^{2} - 5x + 3 - x^{3} - x^{2} + 5$.
The cubes cancel and so do the squares. \ans{-5x + 8}

\textbf{Part (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit explanation says that the
degree of a product is the \emph{sum} of the degrees of its factors, so a
degree-2 times a degree-1 gives degree 3, and that subtracting another degree-3
polynomial can cancel the leading terms and leave something of lower degree ---
here both the $x^{3}$ and the $x^{2}$ terms cancelled, dropping the result to
degree 1. Restating the simplified expression with no comment on degree earns
nothing.
