\wstitleblock{Area and Perimeter --- Finding and Fixing Area/Perimeter Mix-Ups}{Grades 2--3}{}

\noindent\small Two questions sound almost the same but ask for different things.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Perimeter} is the distance all the way \emph{around} the outside.
        You \textbf{add} the sides. The answer is a length: cm or m.
  \item \textbf{Area} is how many unit squares \emph{cover} the inside. For a
        rectangle you \textbf{multiply} the long side by the short side. The
        answer is a number of squares: cm$^2$ or m$^2$.
  \item Before you compute, underline the words in the question that tell you
        which one it wants: \emph{around, fence, ribbon, edging} means perimeter;
        \emph{cover, fill, tiles, space inside} means area.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{\textbf{Perimeter.} A name card is $8$ cm long and $3$ cm wide. Ali glues
ribbon all the way around the outside edge of the card. How many cm of ribbon does
she use?
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{cm}}

\problem{\textbf{Area.} The same name card is $8$ cm long and $3$ cm wide. Ali
covers the whole card with one-centimetre squares. How many squares does it take?
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{cm$^2$}}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix.} Milo needs the \emph{perimeter} of a photo that is
$6$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. He wrote $6 \times 4 = 24$ and said the perimeter is
$24$ cm.
\par\smallskip
Which measurement did Milo actually find? Cross out his work, then find the
perimeter the right way.
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{cm}}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix.} Sara needs the \emph{area} of a rug that is $9$ m
long and $2$ m wide. She wrote $9 + 2 + 9 + 2 = 22$ and said the area is $22$
square m.
\par\smallskip
Which measurement did Sara actually find? Cross out her work, then find the area
the right way.
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{m$^2$}}

\problem{\textbf{Fence around a square.} A square garden has four sides that are
all the same length. Each side is $7$ m long. A fence goes all the way around the
garden. How many m of fence are needed?
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{m}}

\problem{\textbf{Which one does the question want?} Mr.~Ruiz has a rectangular
play mat that is $10$ m long and $6$ m wide. He wants soft edging along every
outside edge of the mat.
\par\smallskip
First circle the word that names what the question wants:
\quad \textbf{perimeter} \quad / \quad \textbf{area}.
\quad Then find how many m of edging he needs.
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{m}}

\problem{\textbf{Same fence, different space.} Ana's garden is a square with sides
of $5$ m. Ben's garden is a rectangle $8$ m long and $2$ m wide. Both gardens need
exactly the same length of fence around them --- check that for yourself first.
\par\smallskip
How many more square m of growing space does Ana's garden have than Ben's?
\par\vspace*{5.0cm}\answerline{m$^2$}}

\par
\textbf{(c)} Write one sentence a classmate could use to decide whether a question
