\problem{\textbf{Bacteria culture.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A table is exponential when consecutive outputs have a constant
\emph{ratio}. Divide: $150 \div 50 = 3$, $450 \div 150 = 3$, $1350 \div 450 = 3$.
The factor is the same every hour, so an exponential model fits.
\ans{b = 3}

\textbf{Step 2.} The value at $t = 0$ is the initial amount, so $a = 50$
thousand cells, and the model is $y = 50\cdot 3^{\,t}$ with $t$ in hours and $y$
in thousands of cells.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): substitute $t = 5$. \ $50\cdot 3^5 = 50\cdot 243$.
\ans{12150}

\textit{Reading the answer:} 12{,}150 thousand cells, i.e.\ about 12.15 million
cells. Keep the unit attached --- the model outputs whatever the table measured.}

\problem{\textbf{Medicine in the bloodstream.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ratios again: $40 \div 80 = \frac{1}{2}$, and the same halving
holds for the next two rows.
\ans{b = \tfrac{1}{2}}

\textbf{Step 2.} Because $0 < b < 1$, this is decay --- each hour keeps half of
what was there. With $a = 80$, the model is
$y = 80\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t}$, $y$ in mg and $t$ in hours.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $80\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{6} = \frac{80}{64}$.
\ans{1.25 \text{ mg}}

\textit{Half-life language:} a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ per hour means the
half-life is exactly one hour, which is why 6 hours leaves $80/2^6$.}

\problem{\textbf{Town population.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A 3 percent yearly increase keeps the original 100 percent and
adds 3 percent, so the factor is $b = 1 + 0.03 = 1.03$. Checking one year:
$8000(1.03) = 8240$ residents.
\ans{8240}

\textbf{Step 2.} With $a = 8000$, the model is $P = 8000(1.03)^{t}$, $t$ in years
from now and $P$ in residents.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $8000(1.03)^{10} = 8000(1.343916\ldots) =
10751.33\ldots$, and residents are whole people.
\ans{10751}

\textit{Why not add 3 percent ten times:} that would give $8000 + 10(240) =
10400$, because it charges 3 percent of the \emph{original} 8000 every year. The
model charges 3 percent of the current population, which is larger every year.}

\problem{\textbf{Laptop depreciation.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Losing 15 percent leaves 85 percent, so $b = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85$.
One year of the model: $1200(0.85) = 1020$ dollars.
\ans{1020}

\textbf{Step 2.} With $a = 1200$, the model is $V = 1200(0.85)^{t}$, $V$ in
dollars and $t$ in years.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $1200(0.85)^4 = 1200(0.52200625) = 626.4075$, rounded
to the cent.
\ans{626.41}

\textit{Decay never reaches zero:} the value keeps 85 percent of a shrinking
number, so the model approaches 0 without arriving --- a fair description of
resale value, and a reason not to extend it too many years.}

\problem{\textbf{Bouncing ball.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The rebound factor is one bounce height divided by the height
before it: $84 \div 120 = 0.7$. Confirm with the next row:
$58.8 \div 84 = 0.7$.
\ans{b = 0.7}

\textbf{Step 2.} The drop height is the value at $n = 0$, so $a = 120$ and the
model is $h = 120(0.7)^{n}$, $h$ in cm and $n$ the bounce number.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $120(0.7)^6 = 120(0.117649) = 14.11788$, to two
decimal places.
\ans{14.12 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Common slip:} using $41.16 \div 58.8$ is fine --- it is also 0.7 --- but
dividing the \emph{earlier} height by the later one gives $1.428\ldots$, which
would say the ball bounces higher each time. Always divide later by earlier.}

\problem{\textbf{Savings account, two readings.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two years of growth multiply by $b$ twice, so the readings give
$2000\,b^{2} = 2205$, hence $b^{2} = \frac{2205}{2000} = 1.1025$ and
$b = \sqrt{1.1025}$.
\ans{b = 1.05}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): $b = 1.05$ means $1 + r = 1.05$, so $r = 0.05$ --- a
5 percent annual rate. The model is $B = 2000(1.05)^{t}$, $B$ in dollars and $t$
in years.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $2000(1.05)^5 = 2000(1.2762815625) = 2552.563125$,
rounded to the cent.
\ans{2552.56}

\textit{Only the positive root is used:} $b = -1.05$ also satisfies the
equation, but a negative factor would flip the balance's sign every year, which
no account does.}

\problem{\textbf{Algae treatment.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Removing 22 percent leaves 78 percent, so $b = 1 - 0.22 = 0.78$.
After one week: $400(0.78) = 312$ square metres, which matches the 22 percent
removal ($400 - 88 = 312$).
\ans{312}

\textbf{Step 2.} With $a = 400$, the model is $A = 400(0.78)^{w}$, $A$ in square
metres and $w$ in weeks.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $400(0.78)^5 = 400(0.2887174\ldots) = 115.48697\ldots$,
to two decimal places.
\ans{115.49 \text{ m}^2}

\textit{Interpretation worth asking for:} after 5 weeks a little over a quarter
of the original cover remains. The treatment is working, but 22 percent per week
never clears the pond completely.}

\problem{\textbf{Drug with two readings.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The readings are 2 hours apart, so the hourly factor is applied
twice: $250\,b^{2} = 160$, giving $b^{2} = \frac{160}{250} = 0.64$ and
$b = \sqrt{0.64}$.
\ans{b = 0.8}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): the dose at $t = 0$ is known, so $a = 250$ and the
model is $y = 250(0.8)^{t}$, $y$ in mg and $t$ in hours.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $250(0.8)^5 = 250(0.32768)$.
\ans{81.92 \text{ mg}}

\textit{Check against the data:} the model must reproduce the reading it was
built from --- $250(0.8)^2 = 160$ mg, exactly the 2-hour blood test. Substituting
the given point back is the fastest way to catch a wrong root.}

\problem{\textbf{Is the campaign exponential?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Differences: $1800 - 1500 = 300$, $2160 - 1800 = 360$,
$2592 - 2160 = 432$. Not constant, so no linear model fits. Ratios:
$1800 \div 1500 = 1.2$, $2160 \div 1800 = 1.2$, $2592 \div 2160 = 1.2$. Constant
ratio means exponential.
\ans{b = 1.2}

\textbf{Step 2.} With $a = 1500$, the model is $T = 1500(1.2)^{t}$, $T$ in
dollars and $t$ in months. A factor of 1.2 is a 20 percent monthly increase.

\textbf{Step 3.} Part (c): $1500(1.2)^6 = 1500(2.985984) = 4478.976$, rounded to
the cent.
\ans{4478.98}

\textit{The trap this problem sets:} the first difference is 300, so a student
who stops there predicts $1500 + 6(300) = 3300$ --- more than a thousand dollars
low. Constant differences and constant ratios are different patterns, and only
the ratio test survives all three rows here.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: mould colony, neither reading at day 0.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Day 5 minus day 2 is 3 days, so the factor is applied three
times: $12\,b^{3} = 96$, giving $b^{3} = 8$ and $b = \sqrt[3]{8}$.
\ans{b = 2}

\textbf{Step 2.} Part (b): $a$ is the area at day 0, and the day 2 reading is
$a\,b^{2} = 12$. Working backwards, $a = \frac{12}{2^{2}} = \frac{12}{4}$.
\ans{a = 3}

\textbf{Step 3.} The model is $A = 3\cdot 2^{\,t}$, $A$ in square centimetres and
$t$ in days. Check it against both photographs: $3\cdot 2^2 = 12$ and
$3\cdot 2^5 = 96$.

\textbf{Step 4.} Part (c): $A = 3\cdot 2^{8} = 3(256)$.
\ans{768 \text{ cm}^2}

\textit{Why this one is harder:} when neither reading is at $t = 0$, the ratio
still gives $b$ --- but $a$ has to be recovered by dividing one reading by
$b$ raised to that reading's own time. Students who set $a = 12$ get every
prediction four times too large.}
