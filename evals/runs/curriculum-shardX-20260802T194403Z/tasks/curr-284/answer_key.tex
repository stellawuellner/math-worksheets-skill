\noindent\small\textbf{Grading note.} On this sheet the sign reasoning is the skill, so a
correct number reached by the wrong substitution should not earn full credit. The
``common wrong answers'' list above tells you which misreading produced each wrong value.
\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{Minimum value of $f(x) = (x - 3)^2 + 5$.}\par
The vertex $x$ is whatever makes the squared factor zero: $x - 3 = 0$ gives $x = 3$, so the
vertex is at $x = 3$, \emph{not} $x = -3$. (The graph moved right, in the opposite
direction from the printed sign.)
\[ f(3) = (3 - 3)^2 + 5 = 0 + 5 \]
Because $(x-3)^2 \ge 0$ for every $x$, adding 5 can never produce anything smaller, so this
really is the minimum. $\ans{5}$\par
\textit{If they got 41:} they substituted $x = -3$, reading the printed sign literally.}

\problem{\textbf{$y = x^2$ shifted 6 left and 2 down; evaluate at $x = -6$.}\par
A shift left is written with a \emph{plus} inside the parentheses, because the graph moves
to where $x + 6 = 0$, i.e. $x = -6$:
\[ y = (x + 6)^2 - 2 \]
\[ y(-6) = (-6 + 6)^2 - 2 = 0 - 2 \]
The value at $x=-6$ is the vertex height, which is the whole point of the check.
$\ans{-2}$\par
\textit{If they got 142:} they wrote $(x - 6)^2 - 2$ for ``left 6'' and then evaluated it at
$x = -6$, so the two sign choices compounded.}

\problem{\textbf{$x$-intercepts of $p(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 9$.}\par
Set $p(x) = 0$ and undo the transformations in reverse order:
\begin{align*}
(x - 2)^2 - 9 &= 0\\
(x - 2)^2 &= 9\\
x - 2 &= \pm 3 \qquad \text{(both roots --- dropping $-3$ loses an intercept)}\\
x &= 2 + 3 = 5 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = 2 - 3 = -1
\end{align*}
Sign check: the axis of symmetry is $x = 2$, and the two intercepts sit 3 units on either
side of it $\checkmark$
\[ \boxed{x = -1 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 5} \]}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: maximum of $y = -(x - 4)^2 + 3$.}\par
\emph{The error:} Jamal read $(x - 4)$ as a vertex at $x = -4$. The vertex sits where the
squared factor is zero, so $x - 4 = 0$ gives $x = 4$. His arithmetic was fine; his $h$ was
not.\par
Correct substitution:
\[ y(4) = -(4 - 4)^2 + 3 = -0 + 3 \]
Since $a = -1 < 0$ the parabola opens downward, so this vertex value is a \emph{maximum}
--- 3 is the largest output the function ever reaches. $\ans{3}$\par
\textit{If they got $-61$:} they repeated Jamal's substitution $x = -4$ instead of fixing it.}

\problem{\textbf{Reflect, stretch by 2, left 1, up 8; evaluate at $x = 1$.}\par
Build the equation one transformation at a time. Reflection and vertical stretch both live
in $a$: $a = -2$. Left 1 puts $+1$ inside the parentheses; up 8 adds 8 outside.
\[ q(x) = -2(x + 1)^2 + 8 \]
\[ q(1) = -2(1 + 1)^2 + 8 = -2(4) + 8 = -8 + 8 \]
So $x = 1$ is one of the $x$-intercepts of the transformed parabola. $\ans{0}$\par
\textit{If they got 16:} they kept $a = +2$ and lost the reflection. Note the vertex is at
$x = -1$ with value 8, so a positive $a$ would make 8 a minimum instead of a maximum ---
the shape of the answer changes, not just the number.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: minimum of $g(x) = (x + 5)^2 - 4$.}\par
\emph{The error:} Mia called $(x + 5)$ a shift 5 units \emph{right}. It is a shift 5 units
\emph{left}, because $x + 5 = 0$ at $x = -5$. Her ``down 4'' was correct.\par
Because she put the vertex at $x = 5$, she evaluated at the wrong place. Correct
substitution:
\[ g(-5) = (-5 + 5)^2 - 4 = 0 - 4 \]
$\ans{-4}$\par
\textit{If they got 96:} that is $g(5)$ --- Mia's value. It is a real point on the parabola,
just not the lowest one, which is exactly why a wrong $h$ can go unnoticed.}

\problem{\textbf{Reflect, right 3, up 16; find the $x$-intercepts.}\par
Reflection makes $a = -1$; right 3 gives $(x - 3)$; up 16 adds 16:
\[ y = -(x - 3)^2 + 16 \]
Set it to zero:
\begin{align*}
-(x - 3)^2 + 16 &= 0\\
(x - 3)^2 &= 16\\
x - 3 &= \pm 4\\
x &= 7 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = -1
\end{align*}
\[ \boxed{x = -1 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 7} \]
\textit{Why the reflection does not move the axis:} reflecting over the $x$-axis replaces
each output $y$ with $-y$ and leaves every input untouched. The axis of symmetry is a
statement about inputs, so it stays at $x = 3$ --- and indeed $-1$ and $7$ are each 4 units
from 3.}

\problem{\textbf{Error analysis --- Ravi's study card.} \quad (open response --- read the
explanations)\par
\textbf{R1 is wrong.} The shift is not in the direction of the printed sign. The vertex
lands where the squared factor is zero, so $(x + 4)$ puts the vertex at $x = -4$: the graph
moves \emph{left} 4. \textit{Corrected:} ``In $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ the graph shifts to
$x = h$, the value that makes $x - h$ zero. So $(x + 4)$ means $h = -4$: left 4.''\par
\textbf{R2 is correct.} $k$ is added to every output after the squaring, so a positive $k$
raises the whole graph and a negative $k$ lowers it. Here the printed sign and the
direction agree, because nothing is subtracted \emph{from} $y$.\par
\textbf{R3 is wrong.} $a$ multiplies the outputs; it does nothing to the inputs. A negative
$a$ flips the parabola upside down about its own vertex, but the vertex --- and therefore
both shifts --- stays exactly where $h$ and $k$ put it. \textit{Corrected:} ``When $a < 0$
the parabola opens downward and its vertex becomes a maximum instead of a minimum; the
shifts are unchanged.''\par
\textit{Full credit} requires naming R1 and R3 as wrong, an explanation that appeals to
``what makes the parentheses zero'' (R1) and to ``$a$ scales outputs, not inputs'' (R3),
and a corrected wording for each. Naming the wrong rules with no reason is partial credit.
$\ans{\text{R1 wrong, R2 correct, R3 wrong}}$}
