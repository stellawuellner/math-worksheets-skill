\problem{Warm-up. The ten-frame shows 7 dots. How many empty boxes are left
to make 10? \ $7\;+\;\underline{\hspace{1cm}}\;=\;10$}

\textbf{Solution:} The frame holds 10 boxes. 7 are filled, so count the empty
boxes: 8, 9, 10 --- that is 3 boxes. Check: $7+3=10$. \ans{3}

\problem{Warm-up. The ten-frame shows 4 dots. How many more dots would fill
it? \ $4\;+\;\underline{\hspace{1cm}}\;=\;10$}

\textbf{Solution:} 4 dots fill most of the top row. Count up from 4 to 10:
5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 --- that is 6 dots. Check: $4+6=10$. \ans{6}

\problem{Warm-up. Take 6 out of 10. How many are left? \ $10-6$}

\textbf{Solution:} 6 and its partner make 10. Think of the partner pair
$6+4=10$: taking 6 away leaves the partner. $10-6=\;$\ans{4}

\problem{Make ten first: $9+4$ is the same as $9+1+3$. Find $9+4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Move 1 from the 4 over to the 9: now $9+1=10$, and 3 is
left over. $10+3=13$, so $9+4=\;$\ans{13}

\problem{Think of a ten-frame with 2 dots in it. How many boxes are empty?
\ $2\;+\;\underline{\hspace{1cm}}\;=\;10$}

\textbf{Solution:} 2 dots leave the rest of the frame empty: 3 empty in the
top row and all 5 in the bottom row, $3+5=8$ empty boxes. Check: $2+8=10$.
\ans{8}

\problem{Think: 7 and how many more make 10? Find $10-7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} 7 needs 3 more to make 10 ($7+3=10$), so taking 7 from 10
leaves 3. $10-7=\;$\ans{3}

\problem{Make ten: fill 8 up to 10, then add what is left over. Find $8+6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} 8 needs 2 to make 10, so split the 6 into $2+4$. Then
$8+2=10$ and $10+4=14$, so $8+6=\;$\ans{14}

\problem{Zoe has 10 berries. She puts 5 of them on her plate. The rest stay
in the bowl. How many berries are in the bowl?}

\textbf{Solution:} Ten decomposes into 5 and its partner: $5+5=10$. So after
5 go on the plate, $10-5=5$ berries stay in the bowl. \ans{5}

\problem{Pick your plan, then add: $7+5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Make ten: 7 needs 3 to fill up, so split the 5 into
$3+2$. Then $7+3=10$ and $10+2=12$, so $7+5=\;$\ans{12}

\problem{Challenge! Mo adds three numbers: $6+4+2$. First find the partner of
6, then add all three, and tell how making ten helped.}

\textbf{Solution:} The partner of 6 is the number that fills it to ten:
$6+4=10$, so the partner is \ans{4}

Now use it: group $6+4$ first to make 10, then $10+2=12$. So
$6+4+2=\;$\ans{12}

\emph{Tell how making ten helped (instructor judges):} a good answer says
that 6 and 4 make a ten, and adding the last 2 onto 10 is easy, so grouping
the partners first makes the whole sum quick.
