\problem{$A = 42^\circ$, $B = 63^\circ$, $c = 10$. Find $C$.}

\textbf{Solution:} the three angles total $180^\circ$, so no law of sines is needed for this one:
$C = 180^\circ - 42^\circ - 63^\circ = 75^\circ$. The given side $c = 10$ is not used here; it fixes the size of the triangle, not its angles. \ans{C = 75^\circ}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$A = 40^\circ$, $B = 75^\circ$, $a = 12$. Find $b$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $a$ is paired with $A$ and $b$ with $B$, and both angles are known, so the law of sines applies directly --- no third angle needed.
\[ \frac{b}{\sin 75^\circ} = \frac{12}{\sin 40^\circ} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = \frac{12\sin 75^\circ}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{11.5911\ldots}{0.6428\ldots} = 18.0305\ldots \]
\ans{b \approx 18.03}

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Common wrong answer:} $7.99$, from writing the ratio upside down --- $12\sin 40^\circ / \sin 75^\circ$. The side you want goes on top with \emph{its own} angle underneath.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$A = 35^\circ$, $B = 80^\circ$, $c = 14$. Find $a$.}

\textbf{Solution:} the known side is $c$, but $C$ is not given, so find it first:
$C = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 80^\circ = 65^\circ$. Now $c$ has a partner.
\[ a = \frac{14\sin 35^\circ}{\sin 65^\circ} = \frac{8.0301\ldots}{0.9063\ldots} = 8.8603\ldots \]
\ans{a \approx 8.86}

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Common wrong answer:} $8.15$, from pairing $c = 14$ with the $80^\circ$ angle it happens to sit next to. A side pairs with the angle \emph{opposite} it, never an adjacent one.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$B = 28^\circ$, $C = 47^\circ$, $b = 9$. Find $c$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $b$ pairs with $B$ and $c$ pairs with $C$, and both are known.
\[ c = \frac{9\sin 47^\circ}{\sin 28^\circ} = \frac{6.5822\ldots}{0.4695\ldots} = 14.0204\ldots \]
Since $C > B$, the answer must be longer than $9$, which it is. \ans{c \approx 14.02}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$A = 22^\circ$, $C = 118^\circ$, $a = 7$. (a) Find $B$. (b) Why is the triangle determined?}

\textbf{(a)} $B = 180^\circ - 22^\circ - 118^\circ = 40^\circ$. \ans{B = 40^\circ}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} A correct response says the angle sum fixes the third angle, so all three are known and the \emph{shape} is settled, and the one given side then fixes the \emph{scale} through the law of sines --- every other side follows from it. Two angles alone give a family of similar triangles; the side selects one member. Restating the angle sum without the scaling role of the side is partial credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$B = 52^\circ$, $C = 61^\circ$, $a = 25$. Find $b$.}

\textbf{Solution:} the known side is $a$, so $A$ is needed: $A = 180^\circ - 52^\circ - 61^\circ = 67^\circ$.
\[ b = \frac{25\sin 52^\circ}{\sin 67^\circ} = \frac{19.7003\ldots}{0.9205\ldots} = 21.4016\ldots \]
\ans{b \approx 21.40}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{$A = 31^\circ$, $C = 96^\circ$, $c = 40$. Find $a$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $c$ is paired with the known $C$, and $a$ with the known $A$, so go straight across.
\[ a = \frac{40\sin 31^\circ}{\sin 96^\circ} = \frac{20.6015\ldots}{0.9945\ldots} = 20.7150\ldots \]
\ans{a \approx 20.71}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{Surveyors: $c = 500$ m apart, $A = 38^\circ$, $B = 47^\circ$. Find $a = BC$.}

\textbf{Solution:} the baseline $c$ is the side between the two measured angles, so this is ASA and the third angle comes first:
$C = 180^\circ - 38^\circ - 47^\circ = 95^\circ$. The mast sits at $C$, and the distance from $B$ to the mast is the side opposite $A$.
\[ a = \frac{500\sin 38^\circ}{\sin 95^\circ} = \frac{307.8181\ldots}{0.9962\ldots} = 309.0066\ldots \]
\ans{a \approx 309.01\text{ m}}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{Path: $b = 180$ m from bench $A$ to fountain $C$, $A = 25^\circ$, $C = 110^\circ$. Find $c = AB$.}

\textbf{Solution:} the known side $b$ runs between the two measured angles, so find $B$ first:
$B = 180^\circ - 25^\circ - 110^\circ = 45^\circ$.
\[ c = \frac{180\sin 110^\circ}{\sin 45^\circ} = \frac{169.1447\ldots}{0.7071\ldots} = 239.2067\ldots \]
The answer is longer than the path itself, as it must be since $C$ is the largest angle. \ans{c \approx 239.20\text{ m}}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.12cm}

\problem{Fire towers: $c = 150$ m apart, $A = 33^\circ$, $B = 29^\circ$. (a) Find $a = BC$. (b) Why is the triangle unique?}

\textbf{(a)} $C = 180^\circ - 33^\circ - 29^\circ = 118^\circ$, and the distance from tower $B$ to the smoke is the side opposite $A$.
\[ a = \frac{150\sin 33^\circ}{\sin 118^\circ} = \frac{81.6959\ldots}{0.8829\ldots} = 92.5263\ldots \]
\ans{a \approx 92.53\text{ m}}

\textbf{(b)} \emph{Instructor-judged.} A correct response contrasts this with the two-sides-and-a-non-included-angle case: here the angle sum hands over the third angle at once, leaving no choice of shape, so exactly one triangle exists. With two sides and a non-included angle the unknown angle comes from an inverse sine, which offers an acute value \emph{and} its obtuse partner, and both can produce a valid triangle. Full credit requires naming the inverse sine and its two candidate angles; "this one is easier" is not enough.
