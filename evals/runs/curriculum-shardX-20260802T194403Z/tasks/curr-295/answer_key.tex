\problem{\textbf{$x^2 = 49$ --- square roots.}
The variable appears only as $x^2$, with no linear term, so take square roots
directly; factoring $x^2 - 49 = (x-7)(x+7)$ works too but is slower.
\[ x = \pm\sqrt{49} \]
\ans{x = 7 \text{ or } x = -7}
Both signs are required: dropping the negative root is the standard slip here.}

\problem{\textbf{$x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ --- factoring.}
Monic with small integers: look for two numbers multiplying to $6$ and adding to
$-5$, namely $-2$ and $-3$.
\[ (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0 \]
Zero-product property: $x - 2 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$. \ans{x = 2 \text{ or } x = 3}}

\problem{\textbf{$x^2 + 4x + 1 = 0$ --- quadratic formula.}
No integer pair multiplies to $1$ and adds to $4$, so factoring fails; with
$a = 1$, $b = 4$, $c = 1$:
\[ x = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{12}}{2}
     = \frac{-4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = -2 \pm \sqrt{3} \]
\ans{x = -2 + \sqrt{3} \text{ or } x = -2 - \sqrt{3}}
The discriminant $12$ is positive but not a perfect square --- that is exactly
the signature of a quadratic that will not factor over the integers.}

\problem{\textbf{$2(x-3)^2 = 32$ --- square roots.}
The whole variable part sits inside one square, so isolate it and take roots;
expanding first would create a trinomial you would then have to factor back.
\begin{align*}
(x-3)^2 &= 16 \\
x - 3 &= \pm 4 \\
x &= 3 + 4 = 7 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 3 - 4 = -1
\end{align*}
\ans{x = 7 \text{ or } x = -1}}

\problem{\textbf{Ball from ground level, $h = -16t^2 + 48t$.}
Both terms share $-16t$, so factoring is fastest:
\[ -16t^2 + 48t = -16t(t - 3) = 0 \]
\[ \boxed{t = 0 \text{ or } t = 3} \]
Now read the roots. $t = 0$ is the instant of the throw --- the ball is at
ground level because it has not left yet. $t = 3$ is the landing. The ball is
therefore in the air for \ans{3 \text{ seconds}}.
Neither root is thrown away here: one is the start of the story, the other is
the answer to the question asked.}

\problem{\textbf{$3x^2 = 12x$ --- factoring after moving everything to one side.}
\[ 3x^2 - 12x = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3x(x - 4) = 0 \]
\ans{x = 0 \text{ or } x = 4}
\textbf{Common wrong answer:} if they wrote only $x = 4$, they divided both sides
by $x$. Dividing by a variable that can be zero deletes the root $x = 0$ --- move
everything to one side and factor instead.}

\problem{\textbf{$2x^2 - 3x - 5 = 0$ --- quadratic formula (or factoring by grouping).}
With $a = 2$, $b = -3$, $c = -5$: \quad $\Delta = 9 + 40 = 49$, a perfect square.
\[ x = \frac{3 \pm 7}{4} \]
\ans{x = \frac{5}{2} \text{ or } x = -1}
Because the discriminant is a perfect square, this one does factor:
$(2x - 5)(x + 1) = 0$ gives the same roots. Either method is efficient; the
formula needs no searching.}

\problem{\textbf{Garden: $w^2 + 5w - 84 = 0$.}
Find factors of $-84$ that add to $5$: $12$ and $-7$.
\[ (w + 12)(w - 7) = 0 \]
\[ \boxed{w = 7 \text{ or } w = -12} \]
Interpret: $w$ is a width, and a width cannot be negative, so $w = -12$ is
rejected as extraneous \emph{in this context} even though it solves the
equation. The garden is \ans{7 \text{ m}} wide (and $7 + 5 = 12$~m long, giving
$7 \cdot 12 = 84$ square metres $\checkmark$).}

\problem{\textbf{$3(x+2)^2 = 27$ --- square roots.}
\begin{align*}
(x+2)^2 &= 9 \\
x + 2 &= \pm 3 \\
x &= -2 + 3 = 1 \quad\text{or}\quad x = -2 - 3 = -5
\end{align*}
\ans{x = 1 \text{ or } x = -5}}

\problem{\textbf{$6x^2 + 7x - 3 = 0$ --- factoring with $a \neq 1$ (grouping).}
$a \cdot c = 6(-3) = -18$; find factors of $-18$ adding to $7$: $9$ and $-2$.
\begin{align*}
6x^2 + 9x - 2x - 3 &= 0 \\
3x(2x + 3) - 1(2x + 3) &= 0 \\
(3x - 1)(2x + 3) &= 0
\end{align*}
\ans{x = \frac{1}{3} \text{ or } x = -\frac{3}{2}}
The quadratic formula gives the same pair; grouping is quicker once $a \cdot c$
splits cleanly.}

\problem{\textbf{Ball from $5$ feet up, $h = -16t^2 + 40t + 5$.}
It does not factor, so use the formula with $a = -16$, $b = 40$, $c = 5$:
\[ \Delta = 40^2 - 4(-16)(5) = 1600 + 320 = 1920 \]
\[ t = \frac{-40 \pm \sqrt{1920}}{-32} = \frac{40 \mp \sqrt{1920}}{32} \]
The two roots are $t = \dfrac{40 + \sqrt{1920}}{32} \approx 2.6193$ and
$t \approx -0.1193$. Time started when the ball was released, so a negative time
is before the throw and cannot be the landing: reject it.
The ball lands after \ans{2.62 \text{ seconds}} (nearest hundredth).
\textbf{Common wrong answer:} $t \approx -0.12$ comes from taking the other branch
of the $\pm$ --- check every root against the situation before writing it down.}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis --- break-even for $P = -n^2 + 30n - 189$.}
(a) Multiply through by $-1$ (or factor out $-1$) so the leading coefficient is
positive, then factor: $n^2 - 30n + 189 = 0$ needs factors of $189$ adding to
$30$, namely $9$ and $21$.
\[ -(n - 9)(n - 21) = 0 \]
\ans{n = 9 \text{ or } n = 21}
(b) The manager is wrong. Break-even happens at exactly $9$ and $21$
sweatshirts, and $20$ is between them. Because the parabola opens downward
($a = -1 < 0$), $P$ is \emph{positive} strictly between the roots: at $n = 20$,
$P = -400 + 600 - 189 = 11$ dollars, a small profit rather than break-even.
Outside the roots (fewer than $9$ or more than $21$) the club loses money.
\emph{Note for the grader: part (b) is a written interpretation, judged by you;
only the two break-even roots are machine-verified.}}
