\problem{Revision hours against test score. Read the score at $5$ hours, then describe the
direction.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Go to $5$ on the horizontal axis and read up to the plotted point.
It sits level with $74$ on the score axis. \ans{74}

\textbf{(b) Direction (instructor-judged).} The association is positive: as revision time
increases the scores increase too, so the cloud of points climbs from left to right. Accept any
wording that names the direction and refers to both variables.

\problem{Television hours against test score. Give the highest score plotted, then describe the
direction.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Scan the vertical positions of the eight points; the top one is the
student who watched no television. \ans{92}

\textbf{(b) Direction (instructor-judged).} The association is negative: the more hours of
television, the lower the score, so the points fall from left to right. Accept any wording that
names the direction and links it to both variables.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Temperature through the day. Give the warmest hour, then describe the form.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The highest point on the plot is the one at hour $14$, where the
temperature reaches $28$ degrees. \ans{14}

\textbf{(b) Form (instructor-judged).} The form is not linear. The temperature climbs to a peak
in the early afternoon and then falls again, so the points make an arch. A straight line could
follow the rising part or the falling part but not both. Accept any wording that rejects a
straight line and describes the rise-then-fall shape.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Hours worked against pay. Find the mean pay, then describe the strength.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Add the five amounts and divide by five:
\[ \frac{24 + 48 + 72 + 96 + 120}{5} = \frac{360}{5} \]
\ans{72}

\textbf{(b) Strength (instructor-judged).} The association is as strong as it can be: every
point lies exactly on one straight line, with no scatter around it, so the hours worked predict
the pay perfectly. Accept any wording that ties strength to how closely the points hug the line.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Shoe size against books read. Find the range, then describe the direction.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The largest number of books is $14$ and the smallest is $5$, so the
range is $14 - 5$. \ans{9}

\textbf{(b) Direction (instructor-judged).} There is no association. The points are scattered
with no upward or downward trend, so knowing a student's shoe size tells you nothing about how
many books they read. Accept any wording that says the direction is neither positive nor
negative.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Practice hours against goals. Give the greatest number of goals, then circle the
unusual point.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad The topmost point is the one at $7$ hours of practice. \ans{30}

\textbf{(b) Outlier (instructor-judged).} The point to circle is the one at $7$ hours with $30$
goals. The other six points climb steadily by $2$ goals per hour of practice; this one leaps far
above that pattern, so it is an outlier. Accept any wording that names the point and compares it
with the trend of the rest. Circling with no reason is only half the answer.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Adverts against orders. Find the median number of orders, then describe direction and
strength.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad In order the eight values are $5, 8, 9, 13, 14, 18, 19, 24$. With an
even count the median is the mean of the middle two: $(13 + 14) \div 2$. \ans{13.5}

\textbf{(b) Direction and strength (instructor-judged).} The association is positive but only
moderately strong. The points climb from left to right, but they zig-zag around the trend rather
than sitting on a single line. A response must cover both features to earn full credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Day against bacteria colonies. Find the difference between day $7$ and day $1$, then
describe the form.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Day $7$ shows $64$ colonies and day $1$ shows $2$, so the difference
is $64 - 2$. \ans{62}

\textbf{(b) Form (instructor-judged).} The form is not linear. The first four days rise gently by
$2$ each day and then the counts double, so the pattern bends sharply upward. Accept any wording
that contrasts the gentle start with the steep finish.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Car age against price. Find the mean price, then describe direction, form and strength.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Add the six prices and divide by six:
\[ \frac{18000 + 16200 + 14600 + 13000 + 11800 + 10400}{6} = \frac{84000}{6} \]
\ans{14000}

\textbf{(b) Full description (instructor-judged).} A complete answer names all three features:
the direction is negative (older cars cost less), the form is linear (the points fall in a nearly
straight line) and the strength is strong (the points sit very close to that line). Naming only
one or two features earns partial credit.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Weeks against pages read. Give the value at the odd point, then describe the rest and
its effect.}

\textbf{(a) Solution:} \quad Every other week sits between $20$ and $42$ pages and rises steadily;
week $6$ drops to the bottom of the plot. \ans{5}

\textbf{(b) Description (instructor-judged).} A correct response sets that point aside and
describes the other six: they show a strong positive linear association, with the pages read
climbing steadily from week to week. It then notes that the single low week makes the overall
pattern look weaker and less regular than it really is, and that one unusual point can hide an
otherwise clear trend. Accept any wording that separates the outlier from the pattern and
describes both.
