# From Riemann Sums to the Fundamental Theorem — three PDFs

**Course:** AP Calculus AB/BC · **Topic:** definite integrals and the
Fundamental Theorem, focused on connecting Riemann sums, accumulation, and FTC
conclusions · **10 problems**

## What you get

- **`ws_riemann_ftc_curr475.pdf` — the student worksheet.**
  Problems 1–3 are a short warm-up, one idea each: a Riemann sum, an
  accumulated total, a derivative of an accumulation function. From problem 4
  on the three are shuffled, so the student has to recognise which idea a
  problem is about before starting — that recognition is what an interleaved
  review is for. Problem 7 works from a table of measured speeds rather than a
  formula, and problem 10 finishes by asking what a total and a rate each tell
  you about the same tank.
- **`ak_riemann_ftc_curr475.pdf` — the full answer key.**
  Every problem restated and worked, with the setup shown before the
  arithmetic. It opens with a generated Quick Answers bank, a "What is
  verified" note, and a Curriculum block listing the AP codes against the
  problems that carry them.
- **`ss_riemann_ftc_curr475.pdf` — the study guide (2 pages).**
  Three sections: Riemann sums as estimates (including which way each kind
  leans), accumulated totals from a rate, and what the FTC lets you conclude
  about a function you never actually compute. Each has a rule box, a worked
  example that opens by naming the strategy, and a try-it with its answer
  printed upside down.

## Verification, stated honestly

The ten problems ask for **17 responses**.

- **13 of 17 are machine-checked** with SymPy: both Riemann sums are recomputed
  term by term as symbolic summations (so the sample points and the widths are
  checked, not just the total), the trapezoidal estimate is recomputed from the
  table's own values, every definite integral is recomputed exactly, and the
  chain-rule FTC derivatives are recomputed from the integrand.
- **4 of 17 are instructor-judged**: problems 5(b), 7(b), 9(b) and 10(c) — the
  places the sheet asks for reasoning rather than a number. They print as `---`
  in the Quick Answers bank, and each has a matching Solution paragraph stating
  what a correct answer must contain (for example, 5(b) needs the link from
  "decreasing" to "rectangles too tall", not just the word *overestimate*).

The study guide's six results are all machine-checked.

## Interleaving

The verifier reports the mix directly: three Riemann-sum problems, three
accumulation, four FTC-conclusion, with a **maximum same-idea run of 2** after
the warm-up.

## Suggested order

Study guide first, then the worksheet, then the answer key.
