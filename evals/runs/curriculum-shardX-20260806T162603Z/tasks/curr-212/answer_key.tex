\problem{Solve $3(x + 4) = 21$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Distribute across both terms: $3x + 12 = 21$. Subtract twelve
from both sides to get $3x = 9$, then divide by three. \ans{x = 3}

\problem{Solve $5(x - 2) = 35$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Distributing gives $5x - 10 = 35$. Add ten to both sides:
$5x = 45$. Divide by five. \ans{x = 9}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{x}{4} + 3 = 7$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Subtract three from both sides first, leaving one quarter of the
unknown equal to four; then multiply both sides by four. \ans{x = 16}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{2x}{3} = 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Multiply both sides by three to clear the denominator:
$2x = 24$. Divide by two. \ans{x = 12}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{x + 6}{2} = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The fraction bar groups the whole numerator, so multiplying both
sides by two gives $x + 6 = 10$ --- the right side is doubled too, which is the step most
often missed. Subtract six. \ans{x = 4}

\problem{Solve $2(3x - 1) + 4 = 26$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Distribute first: $6x - 2 + 4 = 26$, so $6x + 2 = 26$. Subtract
two from both sides: $6x = 24$. Divide by six. \ans{x = 4}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{x}{6} = 3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The least common denominator is six, so multiply every term by
six: $2x + x = 18$. Collect on the left to get $3x = 18$, then divide by three. \ans{x = 6}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{x + 1}{2} + \dfrac{x - 3}{4} = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Multiply every term by four, treating each numerator as a bracket.
\begin{align*}
  2(x + 1) + (x - 3) &= 20 \\
  2x + 2 + x - 3 &= 20 \\
  3x - 1 &= 20 \\
  3x &= 21
\end{align*}
Divide both sides by three. \ans{x = 7}

\problem{Solve $4(2x + 3) = 3(x + 14)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Expand both brackets before collecting anything:
$8x + 12 = 3x + 42$. Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $5x + 12 = 42$. Subtract twelve:
$5x = 30$. Divide by five. \ans{x = 6}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{3x}{5} - 2 = \dfrac{x}{5} + 4$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Multiply every term by five: $3x - 10 = x + 20$. Subtract $x$ from
both sides: $2x - 10 = 20$. Add ten: $2x = 30$. Halve. \ans{x = 15}

\problem{Solve $\dfrac{2(x - 6)}{3} = \dfrac{x + 2}{2}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The least common denominator is six, so multiply both sides by it,
keeping each numerator grouped.
\begin{align*}
  4(x - 6) &= 3(x + 2) \\
  4x - 24 &= 3x + 6 \\
  x - 24 &= 6
\end{align*}
Add twenty-four to both sides. \ans{x = 30}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge.} Nia's work on $3(x - 4) = 18$. (a) Solve correctly. (b) What
went wrong?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The outside factor multiplies both terms inside:
$3x - 12 = 18$. Add twelve to both sides: $3x = 30$. Divide by three. \ans{x = 10}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Nia distributed to the variable term
only and left the constant inside untouched, so her second line is wrong and everything
after it inherits the error. The distributive property applies to \emph{every} term inside
the bracket. Substituting her value into the original equation does not give the right-hand
side, while the correct value gives eighteen exactly. Full credit requires naming the
every-term rule.
