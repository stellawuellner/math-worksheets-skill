\problem{Slope of $\overline{AB}$ for $A(2, 1)$, $B(6, 9)$; then the slope of any parallel line.}

\textbf{Solution:} $m_{AB} = \dfrac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{8}{4}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m = 2}

Parallel lines have equal slopes, so every line parallel to $\overline{AB}$ also has slope two.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Slope of $\overline{CD}$ for $C(-3, 5)$, $D(1, -3)$; then the slope of any perpendicular line.}

\textbf{Solution:} $m_{CD} = \dfrac{-3 - 5}{1 - (-3)} = \dfrac{-8}{4}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m = -2}

A perpendicular slope is the opposite reciprocal: flip the fraction and change the sign, so $-\left(\dfrac{1}{-2}\right)$ gives the value below. Check: $(-2)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = -1$ $\checkmark$

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{1}{2}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Is $\overline{PQ}$ with $P(0, -2)$, $Q(4, 4)$ parallel to $\overline{RS}$ with $R(-1, 3)$, $S(3, 9)$?}

\textbf{Solution:} $m_{PQ} = \dfrac{4 - (-2)}{4 - 0} = \dfrac{6}{4}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{PQ} = \tfrac{3}{2}}

$m_{RS} = \dfrac{9 - 3}{3 - (-1)} = \dfrac{6}{4}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{RS} = \tfrac{3}{2}}

The slopes are equal and the segments are not the same line, so $\overline{PQ} \parallel \overline{RS}$.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Is $\overline{EF}$ with $E(1, 2)$, $F(5, 4)$ perpendicular to $\overline{GH}$ with $G(0, 6)$, $H(2, 2)$?}

\textbf{Solution:} $m_{EF} = \dfrac{4 - 2}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{2}{4}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{EF} = \tfrac{1}{2}}

$m_{GH} = \dfrac{2 - 6}{2 - 0} = \dfrac{-4}{2}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{GH} = -2}

The product of the slopes is $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)(-2) = -1$, so the segments are perpendicular.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Slope of $\overline{JK}$ for $J(4, -1)$, $K(4, 7)$, and the slope of a perpendicular line.}

\textbf{Solution:} $m_{JK} = \dfrac{7 - (-1)}{4 - 4} = \dfrac{8}{0}$, and division by zero is not defined, so the segment is vertical and has no slope.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\text{undefined}}

\textbf{Why the opposite-reciprocal rule needs care here (accept any equivalent explanation):} the rule $m_1 m_2 = -1$ only applies when both slopes exist as numbers. A vertical segment has no slope to take a reciprocal of. Instead, argue from the geometry: any line perpendicular to a vertical line is horizontal, and a horizontal line has slope zero. So the answer is $0$, reached by reasoning about direction rather than by the product rule.

\problem{$ABCD$ with $A(-2, 1)$, $B(2, 3)$, $C(4, 7)$, $D(0, 5)$: show both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.}

\textbf{Solution:} Take the sides in order.
\[ m_{AB} = \frac{3 - 1}{2 - (-2)} = \frac{2}{4}, \qquad
   m_{DC} = \frac{7 - 5}{4 - 0} = \frac{2}{4} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{AB} = \tfrac{1}{2}}

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{DC} = \tfrac{1}{2}}

\[ m_{BC} = \frac{7 - 3}{4 - 2} = \frac{4}{2}, \qquad
   m_{AD} = \frac{5 - 1}{0 - (-2)} = \frac{4}{2} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{BC} = 2}

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{AD} = 2}

$\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DC}$ and $\overline{BC} \parallel \overline{AD}$, so $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Triangle $PQR$ with $P(-1, -1)$, $Q(3, 1)$, $R(1, 5)$: show the angle at $Q$ is right, then find the area.}

\textbf{Solution:} The angle at $Q$ is formed by $\overline{QP}$ and $\overline{QR}$, so those are the two slopes to compare.
\[ m_{PQ} = \frac{1 - (-1)}{3 - (-1)} = \frac{2}{4} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{PQ} = \tfrac{1}{2}}

\[ m_{QR} = \frac{5 - 1}{1 - 3} = \frac{4}{-2} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{QR} = -2}

The product is $-1$, so the two sides meet at a right angle at $Q$.

\textbf{Area:} with the right angle at $Q$, the legs are $QP$ and $QR$, of lengths $\sqrt{20}$ and $\sqrt{20}$, so the area is $\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{20}\sqrt{20} = \tfrac{1}{2}(20)$ square units.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{10}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$WXYZ$ with $W(0, 0)$, $X(6, 2)$, $Y(8, 8)$, $Z(2, 6)$: rectangle? And the diagonal midpoints.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Adjacent sides decide squareness of the corners.
\[ m_{WX} = \frac{2 - 0}{6 - 0} = \frac{2}{6}, \qquad m_{XY} = \frac{8 - 2}{8 - 6} = \frac{6}{2} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{WX} = \tfrac{1}{3}}

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{XY} = 3}

Their product is $\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)(3) = 1$, not $-1$, so the corner at $X$ is not a right angle and $WXYZ$ is \textbf{not} a rectangle. (It is a rhombus: opposite sides are parallel and all four sides have length $\sqrt{40}$.)

\textbf{Solution (b):} Midpoint of diagonal $\overline{WY}$:

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{(4, 4)}

Midpoint of diagonal $\overline{XZ}$:

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{(4, 4)}

The diagonals share a midpoint, so they bisect each other --- which confirms the figure is a parallelogram even though it is not a rectangle.

\problem{$EFGH$ with $E(1, 2)$, $F(4, 5)$, $G(7, 2)$, $H(4, -1)$: slopes and lengths of $\overline{EF}$ and $\overline{FG}$.}

\textbf{Solution:}
\[ m_{EF} = \frac{5 - 2}{4 - 1} = \frac{3}{3} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{EF} = 1}

\[ m_{FG} = \frac{2 - 5}{7 - 4} = \frac{-3}{3} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{m_{FG} = -1}

The product is $-1$, so the corner at $F$ is a right angle. Now the lengths, by the distance formula:
\[ EF = \sqrt{3^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{18}, \qquad FG = \sqrt{3^2 + (-3)^2} = \sqrt{18} \]

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{EF \approx 4.24}

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{FG \approx 4.24}

\textbf{What this proves:} two adjacent sides are perpendicular and equal in length. Repeating the same two calculations on $\overline{GH}$ and $\overline{HE}$ gives slopes $1$ and $-1$ and the same length, so all four angles are right angles and all four sides are congruent --- $EFGH$ is a square.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Triangle $TUV$ with $T(0, 0)$, $U(6, 2)$, $V(2, 8)$: the midsegment $\overline{MN}$ and $\overline{TU}$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Midpoint of $\overline{TV}$, averaging the coordinates:

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{M(1, 4)}

Midpoint of $\overline{UV}$:

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{N(4, 5)}

\textbf{Solution (b):}
\[ m_{MN} = \frac{5 - 4}{4 - 1} = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad m_{TU} = \frac{2 - 0}{6 - 0} = \frac{2}{6} \]
Both reduce to the same value.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{1}{3}}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- model coordinate proof (manual review; accept any complete equivalent):}
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $\overline{TV}$ and $N$ the midpoint of $\overline{UV}$. By the midpoint formula, $M$ and $N$ have the coordinates found in part (a). Applying the slope formula to $M$ and $N$ gives the slope of $\overline{MN}$, and applying it to $T$ and $U$ gives the slope of $\overline{TU}$; both simplify to the same number. Two distinct lines with equal slopes are parallel, and $\overline{MN}$ and $\overline{TU}$ are distinct because $M$ does not lie on $\overline{TU}$. Therefore $\overline{MN} \parallel \overline{TU}$, which is the triangle midsegment theorem for this triangle.
