\problem{The picture shows 12 counters. Find $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of 12.}

\textbf{Solution:} ``$\tfrac{1}{4}$ of 12'' means split 12 counters into 4
equal groups and take one group. $12 \div 4 = 3$, so each group holds
\ans{3}

\problem{Find $\tfrac{1}{5}$ of 30.}

\textbf{Solution:} Split 30 into 5 equal groups: $30 \div 5 = 6$.
One group is \ans{6}

\problem{Use equal groups to find two thirds of 18: (a) $\tfrac{1}{3}$ of 18,
(b) $\tfrac{2}{3}$ of 18.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Split 18 into 3 equal groups: $18 \div 3 = 6$, so
$\tfrac{1}{3}$ of 18 is \ans{6}

(b) Two thirds is two of those groups: $2 \times 6 = 12$, so $\tfrac{2}{3}$
of 18 is \ans{12}

\problem{Kofi gives $\tfrac{3}{5}$ of his 20 stickers to his sister. How many
does he give away?}

\textbf{Solution:} First one fifth: $20 \div 5 = 4$ stickers. Then three
fifths is three groups of 4: $3 \times 4 = 12$. He gives away \ans{12}
stickers.

\problem{A book has 56 pages; Lena reads $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of it. How many
pages?}

\textbf{Solution:} One eighth of 56 is $56 \div 8 = 7$. She reads \ans{7}
pages.

\problem{24 students; $\tfrac{3}{8}$ of the class walks to school.
(a) students in $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of the class; (b) students who walk.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) One eighth of the class: $24 \div 8 = 3$ students
\ans{3}

(b) Three eighths is three groups of 3: $3 \times 3 = 9$ students walk
\ans{9}

\problem{One fourth of Maya's marbles is 6. How many marbles in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} Work backward from one group to the whole. If one of the
4 equal groups holds 6 marbles, the whole is $4 \times 6 = 24$. Maya has
\ans{24} marbles. \emph{Check:} $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of $24 = 6$. \checkmark

\problem{42 apples; $\tfrac{5}{6}$ are red. How many red apples?}

\textbf{Solution:} One sixth of 42: $42 \div 6 = 7$. Five sixths is five
groups of 7: $5 \times 7 = 35$. There are \ans{35} red apples.

\problem{A gift card holds 20 dollars; Nia spends $\tfrac{5}{8}$ of it. How
many dollars does she spend?}

\textbf{Solution:} One eighth of 20 dollars: $20 \div 8 = 2.50$ dollars.
Five eighths is $5 \times 2.50 = 12.50$. She spends
$\ans{12.50\ \text{dollars}}$

\problem{Challenge: $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of the seats are filled, and 21 seats are
filled. (a) How many seats? (b) Explain with a drawing of equal groups.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The 21 filled seats make 3 of the 4 equal groups, so
one group (one fourth) is $21 \div 3 = 7$ seats. The whole is
$4 \times 7 = 28$. The theater has \ans{28} seats.

\emph{Explanation (instructor judges):} a correct drawing or sentence shows
4 equal groups of 7, with 3 groups (21 seats) filled --- so one fourth is 7
and the whole is 28.
