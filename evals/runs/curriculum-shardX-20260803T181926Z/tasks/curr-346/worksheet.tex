\wstitleblock{Rigid Motions on the Coordinate Plane}{Geometry}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Translating, reflecting and rotating points and segments on the
coordinate plane, and checking that lengths do not change}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{How to use this sheet.}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every problem gives you a rule in coordinate notation and a grid. Do both: apply
        the rule in symbols, then plot the image and check that the picture agrees with
        the algebra. When they disagree, the algebra is usually right and the plotting
        finger is usually wrong --- but look at both before deciding.
  \item The rules used here, all of them about the origin or the axes:
        translation $(x, y) \rightarrow (x + h,\, y + k)$;
        reflection over the $x$-axis $(x, y) \rightarrow (x,\, -y)$;
        over the $y$-axis $(x, y) \rightarrow (-x,\, y)$;
        over the line $y = x$ $(x, y) \rightarrow (y,\, x)$;
        rotation $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about the origin
        $(x, y) \rightarrow (-y,\, x)$;
        rotation $180^\circ$ about the origin $(x, y) \rightarrow (-x,\, -y)$.
  \item These are all \emph{rigid} motions, which means every distance survives them. On
        the segment problems you are asked to prove that for one side --- compute the
        length before and after with the distance formula, not with a ruler.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
