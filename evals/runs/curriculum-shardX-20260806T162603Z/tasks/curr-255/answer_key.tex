\problem{Write $(x - 4)(x + 9)$ in standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Four products, then collect the middle pair:
$x^2 + 9x - 4x - 36 = x^2 + 5x - 36$. The constant term $-36$ is the product of the two
constants, and it is invisible until the brackets come off. \ans{x^2 + 5x - 36}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $x^2 + 10x + 21$ in factored form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Two numbers with product $21$ and sum $10$: since $21 = 3\times 7$ and
$3 + 7 = 10$, the factors are $(x + 3)$ and $(x + 7)$. \ans{(x+3)(x+7)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$h(t) = -5t(t-4)$ metres: find both times at ground level.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The height is already factored, and a product is zero exactly when one of
its factors is zero. So $-5t = 0$ gives $t = 0$, and $t - 4 = 0$ gives $t = 4$. No expanding and no
quadratic formula is needed --- that is the whole advantage of the factored form here.
The ball leaves the ground at $t = 0$ and lands at $t = 4$.
\ans{t = 0\ \text{s} \quad\text{or}\quad t = 4\ \text{s}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $3x(x - 5) + 2$ in standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Distribute first: $3x\cdot x = 3x^2$ and $3x\cdot(-5) = -15x$. The $+2$ is
outside the product and simply follows: $3x^2 - 15x + 2$. \ans{3x^2 - 15x + 2}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $2x^2 - 18$ in fully factored form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Common factor first: $2x^2 - 18 = 2(x^2 - 9)$. What is left is a
difference of two squares, $x^2 - 3^2$, which factors as $(x - 3)(x + 3)$.
\ans{2(x-3)(x+3)}

\noindent Skipping the common factor and writing $(2x - 6)(x + 3)$ is not fully factored; the first
bracket still has a $2$ in it.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$g(x) = x^2 - 6x + 8$: (a) rewrite to show the zeros; (b) give the zeros.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Two numbers with product $8$ and sum $-6$ are $-2$ and $-4$.
\ans{(x-2)(x-4)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad A product is zero exactly when a factor is zero, so
$x - 2 = 0$ or $x - 4 = 0$. \ans{x = 2 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $(2x - 3)^2$ in standard form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad A squared binomial is the bracket times itself, so it has three terms:
$(2x)^2 = 4x^2$, twice the cross product $2\cdot 2x\cdot(-3) = -12x$, and $(-3)^2 = 9$.
\ans{4x^2 - 12x + 9}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $x^3 - 4x$ in fully factored form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Every term has an $x$, so take it out first: $x^3 - 4x = x(x^2 - 4)$.
The bracket is a difference of two squares: $x^2 - 2^2 = (x - 2)(x + 2)$.
\ans{x(x-2)(x+2)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$P(x) = (x+5)(x-1)(x-3)$: (a) the constant term; (b) which form and why.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The constant term of a polynomial is its value at $x = 0$, because
every other term carries at least one factor of $x$. Substituting into the factored form:
$P(0) = (0+5)(0-1)(0-3) = (5)(-1)(-3)$. \ans{15}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The factored form did the work. Substituting
$x = 0$ into three short brackets is three easy products; reading the constant term out of standard
form would have meant expanding the whole cubic first. Full credit compares the effort the two
forms demand; repeating the value $15$ is not an explanation.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Write $6x^2 + x - 12$ in factored form.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad With a leading coefficient of $6$, split the middle term. Two numbers with
product $6\times(-12) = -72$ and sum $1$ are $9$ and $-8$:
\begin{align*}
  6x^2 + x - 12 &= 6x^2 + 9x - 8x - 12\\
                &= 3x(2x + 3) - 4(2x + 3) \;=\; (2x + 3)(3x - 4).
\end{align*}
\ans{(2x+3)(3x-4)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$(x + 3)(x - 3) + (x + 3)^2$: (a) expand and simplify; (b) factor the result.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $(x+3)(x-3) = x^2 - 9$ and $(x+3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$. Adding them, the
$-9$ and $+9$ cancel: $2x^2 + 6x$. \ans{2x^2 + 6x}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Both terms share $2x$: $2x^2 + 6x = 2x(x + 3)$. Notice the
shortcut the factored view offers --- $(x+3)$ was a factor of both starting pieces all along.
\ans{2x(x+3)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Garden of area $A(x) = x^2 + 7x + 10$ square metres: (a) factor; (b) area at $x = 3$; (c) which form gives the sides?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Two numbers with product $10$ and sum $7$ are $2$ and $5$.
\ans{(x+2)(x+5)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Substituting $x = 3$ into either form gives the same number.
Using standard form: $9 + 21 + 10 = 40$. Using the factored form as a check:
$(3+2)(3+5) = 5\times 8 = 40$. \ans{40\ \text{m}^2}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} The factored form. A rectangle's area is the
product of its two sides, and $(x+2)(x+5)$ is exactly that product written down, so the sides are
$(x+2)$ m and $(x+5)$ m --- at $x = 3$, $5$ m by $8$ m. Standard form gives no sides at all until
it is factored. Full credit ties the two factors to the two side lengths; giving the numbers
without that reason earns half credit.
