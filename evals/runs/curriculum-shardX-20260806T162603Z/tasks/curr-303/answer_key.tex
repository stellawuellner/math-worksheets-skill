\problem{Shelf support: $3x + 7 = 22$ (cm). (a) Find $x$. (b) Give the two missing reasons.}

\textbf{(a)} Undo the bracket, then undo the tripling:
\begin{align*}
  3x + 7 &= 22 \\
  3x &= 15
\end{align*}
So the spacer is $5$ cm long. \ans{x = 5 \, \text{cm}}

\textbf{(b)} Line 2: \textbf{subtraction property of equality} ($7$ subtracted from both sides). Line 3: \textbf{division property of equality} (both sides divided by $3$). Naming the operation without naming the property earns half credit --- a proof cites the property.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Trail markers $A$, $B$, $C$: $AB = 2x$, $BC = x + 4$, $AC = 25$ (m). (a) Find $x$. (b) Find $BC$.}

\textbf{(a)} $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$, so the Segment Addition Postulate gives $AB + BC = AC$:
\begin{align*}
  2x + (x + 4) &= 25 \\
  3x + 4 &= 25 \\
  3x &= 21
\end{align*}
So $x = 7$. \ans{x = 7}

\textbf{(b)} $BC = x + 4 = 7 + 4$. Check: $AB = 2(7) = 14$ m and $14 + 11 = 25$ m. \ans{BC = 11 \, \text{m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Handrail $\overline{PQ}$ with midpoint $M$: complete the reasons for $PM = \frac{1}{2}PQ$.}

\textbf{Reasons, in order:}

2. \textbf{Definition of midpoint} --- a midpoint divides the segment into two congruent segments.

3. \textbf{Definition of congruent segments} --- congruent segments have equal lengths, which is what turns the congruence mark into an equation.

4. \textbf{Segment Addition Postulate} --- $M$ lies between $P$ and $Q$, so the two pieces sum to the whole.

Line 5 is already given: substituting $PM$ for $MQ$ gives $2\,PM = PQ$, and dividing by $2$ gives the result.

\textbf{Grading:} full credit needs the definition of midpoint and the definition of congruent segments as \emph{separate} reasons --- the first gives congruence, the second gives equality of lengths.

\problem{Cable runs: $5(x - 3) = 2x + 9$ (cm). (a) Find $x$. (b) Reason for each line.}

\textbf{(a)}
\begin{align*}
  5(x - 3) &= 2x + 9 \\
  5x - 15 &= 2x + 9 \\
  3x - 15 &= 9 \\
  3x &= 24
\end{align*}
So $x = 8$ cm. Check: $5(8 - 3) = 25$ and $2(8) + 9 = 25$. \ans{x = 8 \, \text{cm}}

\textbf{(b)} Line 2: \textbf{distributive property}. Line 3: \textbf{subtraction property of equality} ($2x$ from both sides). Line 4: \textbf{addition property of equality} ($15$ added to both sides). Line 5: \textbf{division property of equality}.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Markers $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ in order with $AB = CD$. (a) Prove $AC = BD$. (b) With $AB = CD = 3.5$ m and $BC = 12$ m, find $AC$.}

\textbf{(a) Model proof:} $AB = CD$ is given. Adding the shared middle distance $BC$ to both sides gives $AB + BC = BC + CD$ by the addition property of equality. By the Segment Addition Postulate, $AB + BC = AC$ and $BC + CD = BD$. Substituting these two into the previous line gives $AC = BD$.

\textbf{Grading:} the argument must (i) add the shared $BC$ to both sides with a named property, and (ii) use the Segment Addition Postulate to rename the two sums. An answer that only asserts ``the extra bits are equal so the totals are equal'' with no reasons is partial credit.

\textbf{(b)} $AC = AB + BC = 3.5 + 12$. \ans{AC = 15.5 \, \text{m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Same four markers, now given $AC = BD$: complete the reasons for $AB = CD$.}

\textbf{Reasons, in order:}

1. \textbf{Given.}

2. \textbf{Segment Addition Postulate} --- $B$ is between $A$ and $C$.

3. \textbf{Segment Addition Postulate} --- $C$ is between $B$ and $D$.

4. \textbf{Substitution} --- both sums equal the same quantity by line 1, so they equal each other.

5. \textbf{Subtraction property of equality} --- $BC$ removed from both sides.

\textbf{Grading:} lines 2 and 3 are the same postulate applied to two different betweenness facts; both must be cited. Line 5 must name subtraction, not ``cancel''.

\problem{Garden bed: $P = 2l + 2w$ with $P = 46$ m, $w = 8$ m. (a) Find $l$. (b) Name the properties.}

\textbf{(a)}
\begin{align*}
  2l + 2(8) &= 46 \\
  2l + 16 &= 46 \\
  2l &= 30
\end{align*}
So the length is $15$ m. Check: $2(15) + 2(8) = 30 + 16 = 46$ m. \ans{l = 15 \, \text{m}}

\textbf{(b)} Replacing $P$ by $46$ and $w$ by $8$: \textbf{substitution}. Removing the $16$: \textbf{subtraction property of equality}. Dividing by $2$: \textbf{division property of equality}.

\problem{Footbridge $W$, $X$, $Y$, $Z$ with $WX = YZ$, $XY = 6.4$ m, $WZ = 21.0$ m. (a) Prove $WY = XZ$. (b) Find $WX$.}

\textbf{(a) Model proof:} $WX = YZ$ is given. Adding the shared middle span $XY$ to both sides gives $WX + XY = XY + YZ$ by the addition property of equality. By the Segment Addition Postulate, $WX + XY = WY$ and $XY + YZ = XZ$. Substituting gives $WY = XZ$.

\textbf{Grading:} same two requirements as problem 5 --- the shared span added with a named property, and the postulate used to rename each sum.

\textbf{(b)} Let $w$ be the length of one outer span. The three spans make the whole bridge, so
\begin{align*}
  w + 6.4 + w &= 21.0 \\
  2w + 6.4 &= 21.0 \\
  2w &= 14.6
\end{align*}
So each outer span is $7.3$ m. Check: $7.3 + 6.4 + 7.3 = 21.0$ m. \ans{WX = 7.3 \, \text{m}}
