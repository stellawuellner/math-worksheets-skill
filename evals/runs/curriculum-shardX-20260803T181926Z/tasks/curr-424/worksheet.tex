\wstitleblock{Trigonometric Identities and Equations --- Illegal Cancellation and Lost Solutions}{Precalculus}{}

\noindent\small Cancelling is the fastest way to lose a solution you were never
told about. Three rules cover almost every case.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Never divide both sides by an expression containing the
        variable.} Move everything to one side and \emph{factor} instead: the
        zero-product property then keeps every branch.
  \item \textbf{Only factors of a product cancel.} A term of a sum may never be
        cancelled against a denominator.
  \item \textbf{Squaring both sides can create solutions that are not there.}
        Every candidate must be checked in the \emph{original} equation.
  \item In each problem: name the error in words first, then show corrected work.
        Unless told otherwise, solve on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem[5.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Solve
$\;2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin\theta\;$ on $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
Rafi divides both sides by $\sin\theta$, gets $\cos\theta = \frac{1}{2}$, and
reports $\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Name what Rafi's division assumed without saying so.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Solve the equation correctly and list every solution.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Simplify
$\;\dfrac{\cos^2 x - 1}{\cos x - 1}$.
\par\smallskip
Bea cancels the $\cos^2 x$ against the $\cos x$ and the $1$ against the $1$, and
writes $\cos x$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Simplify the expression correctly by factoring the numerator.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Show Bea's form is wrong by evaluating both at a value where
$\cos x = 0.5$, and give the correct value there.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Solve $\;2\cos^2\theta = \cos\theta\;$ on
$0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
Nils divides both sides by $\cos\theta$ and reports
$\theta = 60^\circ, 300^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Write the equation in factored form with $0$ on one side.
\par
\textbf{(b)} List every solution, and say which ones Nils's division threw away.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} A student simplifies
$\;\dfrac{\sin^2 x + \cos x}{\cos x}\;$ to $\;\sin^2 x + 1$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why the $\cos x$ in the numerator cannot be cancelled
against the denominator.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Test both forms at a value where $\sin^2 x = 0.75$ and
$\cos x = 0.5$. Give the correct value there.}

\problem[6.5cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Solve $\;4\sin^2\theta = 1\;$ on
$0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
Tam takes the square root of both sides, writes $\sin\theta = \frac{1}{2}$, and
reports $\theta = 30^\circ, 150^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Say what taking a square root leaves out.
\par
\textbf{(b)} List every solution on the interval.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Solve $\;\sin\theta - \cos\theta = 1\;$ on
$0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
Ola squares both sides, simplifies to $\sin\theta\cos\theta = 0$, and reports all
four of $\theta = 0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Ola's algebra is correct but her answer is not. Say what squaring
did to the problem.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Test each candidate in the original equation and list the solutions
that actually work.}

\problem[7cm]{\textbf{Find and fix.} Solve $\;\cos 2\theta = \cos\theta\;$ on
$0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
\par\smallskip
Yuki ``cancels the cosine from both sides'' to get $2\theta = \theta$, and
reports $\theta = 0^\circ$ only.
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why equal cosine values do not force equal angles.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Use a double-angle identity to write the equation with $0$ on one
side, factor it, and list every solution.}

\problem[7.5cm]{\textbf{Explain.}
\par\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Explain why dividing both sides of an equation by a trigonometric
expression can lose solutions, and state what the division silently assumes.
\par
\textbf{(b)} Explain why a term of a sum may never be cancelled against a
denominator, even though a factor of a product may. Give a numerical example that
makes the difference visible.
\par
\textbf{(c)} Explain why squaring both sides forces you to check every candidate,
and contrast that with what dividing does. One operation adds solutions and the
other removes them --- say which is which and why.
