\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt{49}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Look for the number that multiplies by itself to give the
radicand: $7 \times 7 = 49$. \ans{7}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{27}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad A cube root asks for the factor used three times:
$3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$. \ans{3}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt{144}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $12 \times 12 = 144$, so the square root is the side of a
$12$-by-$12$ square. \ans{12}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{64}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Test small cubes: $3^3 = 27$ is too small, $4^3 = 4 \times 4
\times 4 = 64$. \ans{4}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt{81} + \sqrt[3]{8}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Evaluate each root first, then add.
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt{81} &= 9 \qquad \text{since } 9 \times 9 = 81 \\
  \sqrt[3]{8} &= 2 \qquad \text{since } 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 \\
  9 + 2 &= 11
\end{align*}
The roots are evaluated before the addition. \ans{11}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt{225}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $15 \times 15 = 225$; a useful landmark between
$\sqrt{196} = 14$ and $\sqrt{256} = 16$. \ans{15}

\problem{Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{125}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125$. \ans{5}

\problem{Evaluate $3\sqrt[3]{216} - \sqrt{100}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The coefficient $3$ multiplies the root, it does not go
inside it.
\begin{align*}
  \sqrt[3]{216} &= 6 \qquad \text{since } 6 \times 6 \times 6 = 216 \\
  3 \times 6 &= 18 \\
  \sqrt{100} &= 10 \\
  18 - 10 &= 8
\end{align*}
Multiply first, then subtract. \ans{8}

\problem{A square patio covers exactly $169$ square feet of ground. How long is one
side of the patio?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad For a square, area $=$ side $\times$ side, so the side is the
square root of the area: $13 \times 13 = 169$. \ans{13\ \text{ft}}

\problem{A solid cube-shaped crate holds exactly $512$ cubic inches. How long is one
edge of the crate?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad For a cube, volume $=$ edge $\times$ edge $\times$ edge, so the
edge is the cube root of the volume: $8 \times 8 \times 8 = 512$. \ans{8\ \text{in}}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ on the blank to make the statement true.
\[ \sqrt{64} \quad \underline{\hspace{1.2cm}} \quad \sqrt[3]{216} \]}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Evaluate both roots before comparing: $\sqrt{64} = 8$ because
$8 \times 8 = 64$, and $\sqrt[3]{216} = 6$ because $6 \times 6 \times 6 = 216$. Since
$8$ is to the right of $6$ on the number line, the left side is larger. \ans{>}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge.} Kara evaluates $\sqrt[3]{64}$ and writes $8$.
(a) Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{64}$ correctly. (b) Explain what Kara did wrong.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad A cube root needs the factor used three times:
$4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$. \ans{4}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Kara took the \emph{square} root of
the radicand instead of the cube root: $8 \times 8 = 64$ is a square-root check, not a
cube-root check. A complete answer names that swap and shows a multiplication test ---
$4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$ works, while $8 \times 8 \times 8$ is far larger than the
radicand, so $8$ cannot be the cube root. Accept any wording that (i) identifies the
square-root-for-cube-root confusion and (ii) backs it with a multiplication check.
