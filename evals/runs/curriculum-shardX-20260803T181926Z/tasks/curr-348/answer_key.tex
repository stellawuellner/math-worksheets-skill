\problem{\textbf{Projector:} dilation centred at the origin, $k = 3$, applied
to $A(2,1)$ (centimetres).

\textbf{Solution:} With the centre at the origin, a dilation multiplies each
coordinate by the scale factor, because $A'$ must lie on ray $OA$ at three
times the distance: $(x, y) \mapsto (3x, 3y)$. So the first coordinate is
$3 \cdot 2$ and the second is $3 \cdot 1$. \ans{A'(6,\, 3)}

Check: $OA = \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{5}$ and
$OA' = \sqrt{6^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$, three times as far, on the
same ray.}

\problem{\textbf{Sail:} pattern $A(0,0)$, $B(4,0)$, $C(4,3)$ in metres, dilated
about $A$ with $k = 2.5$.

\textbf{Solution:} The centre is the origin, so multiply every coordinate by
$2.5$: $B' = (10, 0)$ and $C' = (10, 7.5)$. Side $B'C'$ runs straight up from
$(10,0)$ to $(10,7.5)$, so its length is the difference of the $y$-coordinates,
$7.5 - 0$ metres. \ans{7.5}

Side $A'C'$ runs from the centre $(0,0)$ to $(10, 7.5)$:
$\sqrt{10^2 + 7.5^2} = \sqrt{100 + 56.25} = \sqrt{156.25}$ metres. \ans{12.5}

Check the similarity claim: in the pattern $BC = 3$ and $AC = 5$, and
$2.5 \times 3 = 7.5$, $2.5 \times 5 = 12.5$. Every length scaled by the same
factor, which is what makes the sail similar to its pattern.}

\problem{\textbf{Blueprint:} $6$ cm by $4$ cm enlarged so the long side becomes
$21$ cm.

\textbf{Solution:} The scale factor of a dilation is the image length divided
by the matching pre-image length --- always image over pre-image, never the
other way round: $k = \dfrac{21}{6}$, which reduces. \ans{\frac{7}{2}}

Every length is multiplied by that same factor, so the enlarged width is
$\dfrac{7}{2} \times 4 = 14$ centimetres. \ans{14}

Check: $6 \times 3.5 = 21$ $\checkmark$, and the enlarged rectangle is
$21 \times 14$, whose sides are still in the ratio $3 : 2$.}

\problem{\textbf{Map zoom:} centre $P(1,2)$, $k = 3$, applied to $Q(3,5)$
(kilometres).

\textbf{Solution:} The centre is not the origin, so measure from the centre,
scale, and step back out:
$Q' = \big(h + k(x - h),\; j + k(y - j)\big)$ with $(h, j) = (1, 2)$.

Horizontally, $Q$ sits $3 - 1 = 2$ km east of the pin; tripling gives $6$ km
east of the pin, so the image's first coordinate is $1 + 3(3 - 1)$. \ans{7}

Vertically, $Q$ sits $5 - 2 = 3$ km north of the pin; tripling gives $9$ km
north, so the second coordinate is $2 + 3(5 - 2)$. \ans{11}

So $Q' = (7, 11)$. Note the pin itself does not move --- the centre of a
dilation is its only fixed point.}

\problem{\textbf{Photo:} $10$ cm by $15$ cm enlarged with $k = 2.4$.

\textbf{(a)} Lengths are multiplied by the scale factor, so the two sides
become $2.4 \times 10$ and $2.4 \times 15$ centimetres.
\ans{24} \quad \ans{36}

\textbf{(b)} Area is two-dimensional: both the length and the width are
multiplied by $2.4$, so the area is multiplied by $2.4 \times 2.4 = 2.4^2$.
\ans{5.76}

Check with the actual areas: $10 \times 15 = 150$ and
$24 \times 36 = 864$, and $864 \div 150 = 5.76$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Triangle $DEF$:} $D(-2,1)$, $E(4,1)$, $F(4,5)$ in metres,
dilated about the origin with $k = 2$.

\textbf{(a)} Multiply every coordinate by $2$: $D'(-4, 2)$, $E'(8, 2)$,
$F'(8, 10)$. Plotted, $D'E'F'$ is the same shape as $DEF$, twice as far from
the origin in every direction.

\textbf{(b)} $D$ and $E$ share the $y$-coordinate $1$, so $DE$ is the
difference of the $x$-coordinates: $4 - (-2)$ metres. \ans{6}

$D'$ and $E'$ share the $y$-coordinate $2$, so $D'E' = 8 - (-4)$ metres.
\ans{12}

\textbf{(c)} Dividing image length by pre-image length gives
$\dfrac{12}{6}$, and that quotient is the scale factor itself. \ans{2}

That is the point of the problem: the ratio of any pair of corresponding
lengths comes out to $k$, which is what "similar with scale factor $k$" means.}

\problem{\textbf{Model car:} $k = \frac{1}{12}$; real length $4.8$ m,
windshield area $0.72$ square metres; Ben says $0.06$ square metres.

\textbf{Solution:} Ben scaled an \emph{area} by the scale factor, computing
$0.72 \div 12 = 0.06$. A dilation multiplies every length by $k$, so an area
--- a product of two lengths --- is multiplied by $k^2$. The model's windshield
area is $0.72 \div 12^2 = 0.72 \div 144$ square metres. \ans{0.005}

The model's length is a length, so it does scale by $k$ alone:
$4.8 \div 12$ metres. \ans{0.4}

Sanity check on Ben's number: $0.06$ square metres is a square about $24$
centimetres on a side, which cannot fit on a car $0.4$ metres long.}

\problem{\textbf{Triangle $JKL$:} $J(1,1)$, $K(3,1)$, $L(3,4)$, dilated about
the origin with $k = 2$.

\textbf{(a)} Doubling each coordinate gives $J'(2,2)$, $K'(6,2)$, $L'(6,8)$.

\textbf{(b)} $K'$ and $L'$ share the $x$-coordinate $6$, so $K'L'$ is the
difference of the $y$-coordinates, $8 - 2$. \ans{6}

Check: $KL = 4 - 1 = 3$ in the pre-image, and $2 \times 3 = 6$ $\checkmark$.

\textbf{(c)} (Open response --- judge by reading.) Model answer: the three
lines $JJ'$, $KK'$, $LL'$ all pass through the origin --- they meet at the
centre of dilation. That is forced by the definition: each image point lies on
the ray from the centre through its pre-image point.

Why the triangles are similar: every image length is exactly $2$ times its
pre-image length ($J'K' = 4 = 2 \cdot 2$, $K'L' = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$,
$J'L' = 2\sqrt{13} = 2 \cdot \sqrt{13}$), so all three pairs of corresponding
sides are in the same ratio $2 : 1$, giving SSS similarity. Equivalently, a
dilation is by definition a similarity transformation: it preserves angle
measure and multiplies every length by $k$, so $\triangle JKL \sim \triangle
J'K'L'$ with ratio $1 : 2$.

Look for: the student states that the three lines are concurrent at the centre
(the origin), and justifies similarity by a constant ratio of corresponding
sides or by naming the dilation as a similarity transformation --- not merely
"they look the same shape".}
