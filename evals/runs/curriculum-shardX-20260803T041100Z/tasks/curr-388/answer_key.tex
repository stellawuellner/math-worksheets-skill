\problem{\textbf{Find $A + B$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both matrices are $2 \times 2$, so the sum is defined and is
computed entry by corresponding entry.

\textbf{Step 2.} Row 1: $3 + (-1) = 2$ and $-2 + 6 = 4$.
Row 2: $5 + 2 = 7$ and $4 + (-7) = -3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Collect the four entries in their original positions.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 7 & -3 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find $3A$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A scalar multiplies \emph{every} entry --- there is no
row-or-column selection to make.

\textbf{Step 2.} $3 \cdot 4 = 12$, \ $3 \cdot (-3) = -9$, \ $3 \cdot 2 = 6$,
\ $3 \cdot 6 = 18$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The result keeps the same $2 \times 2$ shape.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 12 & -9 \\ 6 & 18 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find the product $AB$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $A$ is $2 \times 2$ and $B$ is $2 \times 2$: the inner
dimensions agree, so $AB$ exists and is $2 \times 2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Entry $(1,1)$ is row 1 of $A$ paired with column 1 of $B$:
$(2)(4) + (1)(-2) = 8 - 2 = 6$. Entry $(1,2)$: $(2)(3) + (1)(5) = 11$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Row 2 the same way: $(3)(4) + (-1)(-2) = 12 + 2 = 14$, and
$(3)(3) + (-1)(5) = 9 - 5 = 4$.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 6 & 11 \\ 14 & 4 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find $A - B$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both matrices are $2 \times 3$, so the difference is defined
and is taken entry by entry.

\textbf{Step 2.} Row 1: $5 - 2 = 3$, \ $-2 - 3 = -5$, \ $7 - (-1) = 8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Row 2: $1 - 6 = -5$, \ $4 - (-4) = 8$, \ $-3 - 5 = -8$. Two of
these involve subtracting a negative, which adds.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -5 & 8 \\ -5 & 8 & -8 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find $2A + 3B$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Scale each matrix first, then add: scalar multiplication
distributes over the entries, so each entry of the answer is
$2a_{ij} + 3b_{ij}$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Entry $(1,1)$: $2(1) + 3(2) = 8$. Entry $(1,2)$:
$2(-2) + 3(4) = -4 + 12 = 8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Entry $(2,1)$: $2(3) + 3(-1) = 6 - 3 = 3$. Entry $(2,2)$:
$2(5) + 3(2) = 16$.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 8 & 8 \\ 3 & 16 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Stock value: find $SP$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $S$ is $2 \times 3$ and $P$ is $3 \times 1$, so $SP$ is
$2 \times 1$ --- one number per store, which is exactly what a total value
should be.

\textbf{Step 2.} North store: $(12)(40) + (8)(25) + (5)(60) = 480 + 200 + 300$.

\textbf{Step 3.} South store: $(9)(40) + (14)(25) + (6)(60) = 360 + 350 + 360$.
Each entry is units times price per unit, summed over the three products, so
the north store holds $980$ dollars of stock and the south store $1070$
dollars.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} 980 \\ 1070 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Determinant of $M$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} For a $2 \times 2$ matrix the determinant is the main-diagonal
product minus the anti-diagonal product.

\textbf{Step 2.} Main diagonal: $(7)(5) = 35$. Anti-diagonal: $(3)(2) = 6$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $35 - 6 = 29$. The determinant is nonzero, so $M$ is
invertible. \ans{29}}

\problem{\textbf{Is $AB$ defined? What size? Compute it.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $A$ is $2 \times 3$ and $B$ is $3 \times 2$. The inner
dimensions ($3$ and $3$) match, so $AB$ is defined, and the outer dimensions
give its size: $2 \times 2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Each entry pairs a row of $A$ (three numbers) with a column of
$B$ (three numbers): $(1)(2) + (2)(-1) + (-1)(4) = 2 - 2 - 4 = -4$, and
$(1)(1) + (2)(3) + (-1)(2) = 1 + 6 - 2 = 5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Row 2: $(3)(2) + (2)(-1) + (4)(4) = 6 - 2 + 16 = 20$, and
$(3)(1) + (2)(3) + (4)(2) = 3 + 6 + 8 = 17$.

\textit{Manual review:} the written size argument is an open response. Accept
any wording that matches inner dimensions to conclude the product exists and
uses the outer dimensions for its size.
\ans{\begin{bmatrix} -4 & 5 \\ 20 & 17 \end{bmatrix}}}

\problem{\textbf{Find $x$ and $y$ making the matrices equal.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two matrices are equal exactly when every pair of
corresponding entries is equal, so this one matrix statement is really four
ordinary equations.

\textbf{Step 2.} Entry $(1,1)$: $x + 2 = 7$, so $x = 5$. Entry $(2,2)$:
$y - 1 = 9$, so $y = 10$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The other two entries are already identical ($9 = 9$ and
$2 = 2$), so nothing else is required. \ans{x = 5, \; y = 10}}

\problem{\textbf{Write the system as a matrix equation, then solve.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The coefficients form $A$, the variables form the column $X$,
and the constants form the column $B$:
\[
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} 12 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
\]
Multiplying the left side back out reproduces $2x + 3y$ and $x - y$, which is
the check that the matrix equation says the same thing as the system.

\textbf{Step 2.} From the second row, $x = y + 1$. Substituting into the first:
$2(y + 1) + 3y = 12$, so $5y = 10$ and $y = 2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Then $x = 2 + 1 = 3$. Check in the first equation:
$2(3) + 3(2) = 12$ $\checkmark$

\textit{Manual review:} the matrix equation itself is the student's own
representation --- check that the coefficient matrix rows match the equations in
order, including the $-1$ coefficient of $y$ in the second row.
\ans{x = 3, \; y = 2}}
