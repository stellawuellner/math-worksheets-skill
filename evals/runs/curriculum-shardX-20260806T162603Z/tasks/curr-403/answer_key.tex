\problem{$C(m) = 0.05m + 20$ dollars for $m$ minutes. A bill of $45$ dollars --- find $m$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Swap and solve: from $C = 0.05m + 20$ we get $m = \dfrac{C - 20}{0.05}$, so $C^{-1}(C) = \dfrac{C - 20}{0.05}$. At $C = 45$: $\dfrac{45 - 20}{0.05} = \dfrac{25}{0.05} = 500$. \ans{m = 500 \text{ min}}

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$f(x) = 4x + 9$, $g(x) = \dfrac{x - 9}{4}$. Show $(f \circ g)(x) = x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $f\!\left(\dfrac{x - 9}{4}\right) = 4 \cdot \dfrac{x - 9}{4} + 9 = (x - 9) + 9$. The $4$ and the division by $4$ cancel, then the $+9$ undoes the $-9$. \ans{(f \circ g)(x) = x}

\textbf{Grading the shown work} (\emph{instructor-judged}): the value alone is not the answer here. Look for the substituted line and the cancellation, ending at $x$. A bare ``$= x$'' with no intermediate step, or a check at a single numerical input, does not establish the identity.

\problem{$A(r) = \pi r^{2}$ for $r > 0$ (radius in cm, area in square cm). \textbf{(a)} Domain of $A^{-1}$. \textbf{(b)} Radius when $A = 64\pi$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The domain of the inverse is the range of $A$. As $r$ runs over $r > 0$, $\pi r^{2}$ takes every positive value and no others, so the domain of $A^{-1}$ is \ans{A > 0}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Solve $A = \pi r^{2}$ for $r$: $r^{2} = \dfrac{A}{\pi}$, and the restriction $r > 0$ picks the positive root, so $A^{-1}(A) = \sqrt{A/\pi}$. At $A = 64\pi$: $\sqrt{64\pi/\pi} = \sqrt{64} = 8$. \ans{r = 8 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$L(w) = 12 + 0.4w$ cm for a load of $w$ kg. Find $w$ when $L = 22$ cm.}

\textbf{Solution:} From $L = 12 + 0.4w$, $w = \dfrac{L - 12}{0.4}$. At $L = 22$: $\dfrac{22 - 12}{0.4} = \dfrac{10}{0.4} = 25$. \ans{w = 25 \text{ kg}}

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$f(x) = \dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$, $g(x) = \dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}$. Show $(f \circ g)(x) = x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Substitute and clear the compound fraction by multiplying numerator and denominator by $x - 2$:
\[ f\bigl(g(x)\bigr)
   = \frac{2\left(\frac{3x+1}{x-2}\right) + 1}{\left(\frac{3x+1}{x-2}\right) - 3}
   = \frac{(6x + 2) + (x - 2)}{(3x + 1) - 3(x - 2)}
   = \frac{7x}{7} \]
\ans{(f \circ g)(x) = x}

\textbf{Grading the shown work} (\emph{instructor-judged}): look for the compound fraction actually being cleared --- the substituted line, then the multiplication by $x - 2$ top and bottom, then $7x/7$. A bare ``$= x$'' or a single numerical check earns no credit.

\problem{$d(t) = 5t^{2}$ metres for $t \geq 0$ seconds. \textbf{(a)} Time to fall $80$ metres. \textbf{(b)} Why the restriction matters.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} From $d = 5t^{2}$, $t^{2} = \dfrac{d}{5}$, and $t \geq 0$ selects the positive root: $d^{-1}(d) = \sqrt{d/5}$. At $d = 80$: $\sqrt{16} = 4$. \ans{t = 4 \text{ s}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The rule squares the time, so on all real numbers $t$ and $-t$ give the same distance and the assignment distance-to-time would not be a function at all. Restricting to $t \geq 0$ leaves exactly one time for each distance --- the rule becomes one-to-one --- and that is what lets the square root be taken with a single sign. A student who only says ``negative time is impossible'' has given the physical reason, not the mathematical one; credit that answer fully only if the one-to-one point is also made.

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$F(c) = \tfrac{9}{5}c + 32$. Find $c$ when $F = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Undo the operations in reverse order --- subtract $32$, then multiply by $\tfrac{5}{9}$:
\[ c = \tfrac{5}{9}(F - 32) = \tfrac{5}{9}(5 - 32) = \tfrac{5}{9}(-27) \]
\ans{c = -15 \text{ degrees Celsius}}

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$f(x) = x^{2} - 6x + 5$ on $x \geq 3$. \textbf{(a)} Domain of $f^{-1}$. \textbf{(b)} $f^{-1}(21)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Completing the square gives $f(x) = (x - 3)^{2} - 4$, so the vertex sits at $x = 3$ --- exactly where the restriction starts. On $x \geq 3$ the outputs run from $-4$ upward, so the domain of $f^{-1}$ is \ans{x \geq -4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Solve $y = (x-3)^{2} - 4$ for $x$: $(x-3)^{2} = y + 4$, and $x \geq 3$ forces the plus sign, so $f^{-1}(x) = 3 + \sqrt{x+4}$. Then $f^{-1}(21) = 3 + \sqrt{25} = 3 + 5 = 8$. \ans{f^{-1}(21) = 8}

\vspace{0.1cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x-4}$, $g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x} + 4$. Show $(f \circ g)(x) = x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The $+4$ inside $g$ is exactly what the $-4$ inside $f$ removes:
\[ f\bigl(g(x)\bigr) = \frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{x} + 4\right) - 4} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{x}} \]
and the reciprocal of $\tfrac{1}{x}$ is $x$. \ans{(f \circ g)(x) = x}

\textbf{Grading the shown work} (\emph{instructor-judged}): look for the $+4$ and $-4$ cancelling inside the denominator, then the reciprocal step. A bare ``$= x$'' or a single numerical check earns no credit.

\problem{$h(t) = 45 - 5t^{2}$ metres for $0 \leq t \leq 3$ seconds. \textbf{(a)} Time at height $25$ metres. \textbf{(b)} Why the restriction is needed.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} From $h = 45 - 5t^{2}$: $5t^{2} = 45 - h$, so $t^{2} = \dfrac{45 - h}{5}$ and the restriction $t \geq 0$ gives $h^{-1}(h) = \sqrt{\dfrac{45-h}{5}}$. At $h = 25$: $\sqrt{\dfrac{20}{5}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$. \ans{t = 2 \text{ s}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} Because the rule uses $t^{2}$, every height below $45$ metres is produced by two times, $t$ and $-t$, so without a restriction no single inverse rule could decide which to report. Cutting the domain to $0 \leq t \leq 3$ leaves exactly one time per height, which is what makes the model one-to-one and invertible. The upper end matters too: at $t = 3$ the height reaches $0$, and past that the model describes nothing. An answer that objects only to negative time earns partial credit.
