\problem{\textbf{Pencils: $6$ equal packs, $72$ pencils in all.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Name the unknown: let $p$ be the number of pencils in one
pack. Equal packs means repeated addition, so the situation multiplies.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $6p = 72$. Undo the multiplication by dividing
both sides by $6$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $p = 72 \div 6$, so each pack holds \ans{12 \text{ pencils}}

\textit{Check:} $6 \times 12 = 72$ pencils, which is what the story says.}

\problem{\textbf{Club fund: spent $45$, left with $130$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The unknown is the amount before the purchase, so let $x$ be
that amount in dollars. Spending subtracts.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $x - 45 = 130$. Undo the subtraction by adding
$45$ to both sides.

\textbf{Step 3.} $x = 130 + 45$, so the fund held \ans{175 \text{ dollars}}

\textit{Listen for:} $85$, from subtracting instead of adding. Ask which
number is the larger amount --- the fund before spending has to be more than
what is left.}

\problem{\textbf{Taxi: flat $3$ plus $2$ per mile, ride of $7$ miles.}

\textbf{Step 1.} One amount happens once (the flat charge) and one happens per
mile, so the rate multiplies the number of miles and the flat charge is added
on.

\textbf{Step 2.} The expression is $3 + 2m$ dollars for $m$ miles. Substitute
$m = 7$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $3 + 2(7) = 3 + 14$, so the fare is \ans{17 \text{ dollars}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $35$ --- the flat charge was included in the
per-mile rate, giving $(3 + 2)(7)$. The flat charge is paid once, not once per
mile.}

\problem{\textbf{Gym: $20$ joining fee plus $15$ a month, $110$ paid.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Let $n$ be the number of months. The joining fee is paid once
and the monthly charge repeats, so the total is a one-time amount plus a rate
times $n$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $20 + 15n = 110$. Undo the addition first:
$15n = 90$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Divide by the rate: $n = 90 \div 15$, so Ren has been a member
for \ans{6 \text{ months}}

\textit{Check:} $20 + 15(6) = 110$ dollars.}

\problem{\textbf{Garden bed: perimeter $46$ cm, width $9$ cm.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Let $x$ be the length in cm. A rectangle has two lengths and
two widths, so the perimeter is $2x + 2(9)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $2x + 18 = 46$. Subtract the two widths:
$2x = 28$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Divide by $2$: the length is \ans{14 \text{ cm}}

\textit{Listen for:} $x = 23$, from halving the perimeter and forgetting the
width, or $x = 37$, from subtracting one width instead of two.}

\problem{\textbf{Oats: $\tfrac{3}{4}$ cup per batch, $6$ cups used.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Let $n$ be the number of batches. Each batch uses the same
amount, so the situation is a rate times a count.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $\tfrac{3}{4}n = 6$. Undo multiplication by a
fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal, $\tfrac{4}{3}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $n = 6 \cdot \tfrac{4}{3} = \tfrac{24}{3}$, so the baker made
\ans{8 \text{ batches}}

\textit{Teaching note:} the answer is larger than $6$, which surprises
students. Each batch uses less than a cup, so more than six batches fit into
six cups.}

\problem{\textbf{Pool: $300$ gallons, draining $12$ gallons per minute.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The pool starts full and loses a fixed amount every minute, so
the rate multiplies the time and is subtracted from the starting amount.

\textbf{Step 2.} The expression is $300 - 12t$ gallons after $t$ minutes.
Substitute $t = 18$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $12 \times 18 = 216$, and $300 - 216$ leaves
\ans{84 \text{ gallons}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $288$ --- the drain rate was subtracted once
instead of once per minute.}

\problem{\textbf{Bill: $6$ coupon, then split three ways, each paying $14$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Let $x$ be the bill in dollars before the coupon. The story
happens in order: the coupon comes off first, then the rest is divided by
three.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $\dfrac{x - 6}{3} = 14$. Undo the division
first by multiplying both sides by $3$: $x - 6 = 42$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Add the coupon back: the bill was \ans{48 \text{ dollars}}

\textit{Listen for:} $x = 36$, from taking the coupon off after splitting.
Order matters, and the equation records the order.}

\problem{\textbf{Error analysis: skates cost $5$ plus $3$ per hour, total
$23$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Ask what each number does. The $5$ is paid once, no matter how
long Ana skates; the $3$ is paid for each hour. Ben's equation $5h + 3 = 23$
multiplies the one-time charge by the hours and adds the hourly rate once ---
it has the two roles swapped.

\textbf{Step 2.} The correct model attaches the variable to the rate:
$5 + 3h = 23$. Subtract the flat charge: $3h = 18$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Divide by the rate: Ana skated for \ans{6 \text{ hours}}

\textit{What to say:} read the equation back as a sentence. Ben's says ``five
dollars for each hour, plus three dollars once'', which is not what the sign
at the rink says.}

\problem{\textbf{Coding club: two thirds signed up, $4$ dropped out, $18$
left.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Let $s$ be the number of members. Two thirds of them is
$\tfrac{2}{3}s$, and the story then removes $4$ from that group.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $\tfrac{2}{3}s - 4 = 18$. Undo the subtraction
first: $\tfrac{2}{3}s = 22$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Multiply by the reciprocal $\tfrac{3}{2}$: the club has
\ans{33 \text{ students}}

\textit{Check:} two thirds of $33$ is $22$ contestants, and $22 - 4 = 18$
after the dropouts.}
