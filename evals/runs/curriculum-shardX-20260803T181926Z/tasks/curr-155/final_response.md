Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 6–7 ratio review.

**Worksheet — `ws_ratiochoice_curr155.pdf` (6 pages, 12 problems).**
The point of this sheet is not arithmetic with ratios — it is *choosing* the
right representation. Four tools are named in the directions (ratio table, unit
rate, proportion, direct comparison), and the problems are deliberately shuffled
so the student has to decide which one the numbers call for. Problems 1 and 3
are ratio tables to complete, where the multiplier is friendly. Problems 4 and 8
give numbers that are not friendly multiples, so a proportion is the efficient
route. Problems 5, 9 and 11 are comparisons where the two offers or rates cannot
be compared until both are put in the same "per one" form. Problem 7 is the
classic error: a student who *added* to both parts of a ratio instead of
multiplying. Problem 12 is the challenge — one story that needs a comparison for
part (a) and a scale-up for parts (b) and (c).

Problem 11 is worth watching: Team B has won more games, so the intuitive answer
is Team B, and reducing both ratios shows they are exactly equal. A tie is a
legitimate answer, and this problem is where that gets learned.

**Answer key — `ak_ratiochoice_curr155.pdf` (3 pages).**
Every solution opens by naming *why* that representation was chosen, then works
through it. Several problems show the alternative route as well (the printer
table is solved by multiplier and by unit rate) so you can accept whichever way
your student went. A quick-answer strip and a Curriculum section (standard
covered, difficulty range) sit at the top, and problem 7 carries a printed
"common wrong answer" line: 7 cups, from adding instead of multiplying.

**Study guide — `ss_ratiochoice_curr155.pdf` (2 pages).**
Four sections, one per tool, and each formula box says *when to reach for it* —
tables for friendly multiples, unit rates for awkward ones and for comparing
different pack sizes, proportions when there is one unknown and one known ratio,
and a direct comparison only after both rates are in the same form. Each section
has a worked example and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the
box.

**What was verified.** All 18 checks behind the 12 problems were recomputed
independently, including every cell of both ratio tables and the direction of
every comparison symbol. The comparison problems are checked as relations, not
just as numbers, so a key that printed the wrong way round would have failed the
build. Nothing on this sheet is flagged for manual review — the written
"say which representation you chose" parts sit alongside answers that are all
machine-checked.

The misconception in problem 7 is machine-checked too: the sheet's numbers were
chosen so that the additive shortcut lands on 7 while the correct answer is 10,
which is far enough apart that the mistake cannot hide.
