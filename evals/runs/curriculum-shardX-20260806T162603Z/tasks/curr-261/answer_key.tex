\problem{$A$: $y = x + 1$, \ $B$: $y = -x + 5$. Solution from the graph.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The two lines cross at the lattice point where $x = 2$: line $A$ gives
$y = 2 + 1 = 3$ and line $B$ gives $y = -2 + 5 = 3$, so both agree there. That point, and only that
point, sits on both lines. \ans{(2,\,3)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A$: $y = 2x - 1$, \ $B$: $y = x + 2$. Solution from the graph.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Reading the crossing point off the grid gives $x = 3$, $y = 5$. Algebra
confirms it: $2x - 1 = x + 2$ gives $x = 3$, and then $y = 3 + 2 = 5$. \ans{(3,\,5)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A$: $y = 2x + 1$, \ $B$: $y = 2x - 3$. (a) How many solutions? (b) Why?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The lines never meet, so no ordered pair lies on both.
\ans{\text{no solution}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Both equations have slope $2$, so the lines
climb at exactly the same rate and stay a constant vertical distance of $4$ apart forever; their
$y$-intercepts differ ($1$ and $-3$), so they are not the same line either. Setting the right-hand
sides equal gives $1 = -3$, which is false --- the algebra says the same thing the picture does.
Full credit links equal slopes and different intercepts to the absence of a crossing point.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Graph $y = -x + 6$ and $2y = x$; give the solution.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The first line has $y$-intercept $6$ and slope $-1$. The second is
$y = \tfrac{1}{2}x$, through the origin with slope $\tfrac{1}{2}$. They cross where
$-x + 6 = \tfrac{1}{2}x$, that is $6 = \tfrac{3}{2}x$, so $x = 4$ and $y = 2$. On the grid the
crossing sits at a lattice point, which is how a graphical reading can be trusted here.
\ans{(4,\,2)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Gym plans $A$: $y = 10x + 20$, \ $B$: $y = 5x + 40$. Equal-cost point.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The lines cross where $10x + 20 = 5x + 40$, so $5x = 20$ and $x = 4$
months; substituting back, $y = 10(4) + 20 = 60$ dollars. On the graph this is the one place where
the steeper plan $A$ line meets plan $B$. \ans{(4,\,60)}

\noindent Reading it in context: before $4$ months plan $A$ is cheaper, after $4$ months plan $B$ is.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$y = -2x + 4$ and $4x + 2y = 8$: (a) solve the second for $y$; (b) how many solutions?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Subtract $4x$ from both sides: $2y = 8 - 4x$. Divide both sides by
$2$: $y = 4 - 2x$. \ans{y = 4 - 2x}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} That is the first equation written the other
way round, so the two equations describe one and the same line --- which is why only one line
appears on the graph. Every point on that line satisfies both equations, so the system has
infinitely many solutions. Full credit says the lines coincide and that every point on the line is
a solution.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Graph $2x + y = 8$ and $y = x - 1$; give the solution.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Rewrite the first as $y = -2x + 8$ (intercept $8$, slope $-2$); the second
has intercept $-1$ and slope $1$. Setting them equal: $-2x + 8 = x - 1$, so $9 = 3x$ and $x = 3$,
giving $y = 3 - 1 = 2$. Check in the untouched first equation: $2(3) + 2 = 8$. \ans{(3,\,2)}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A$: $2y = x + 4$, \ $B$: $y = -x + 8$. (a) the solution; (b) why $(6,5)$ is not one.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Line $A$ is $y = \tfrac{1}{2}x + 2$. Setting the two right-hand sides
equal, $\tfrac{1}{2}x + 2 = -x + 8$, so $\tfrac{3}{2}x = 6$ and $x = 4$; then $y = -4 + 8 = 4$.
\ans{(4,\,4)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} $(6, 5)$ does satisfy line $A$:
$2(5) = 6 + 4$. But a solution of a \emph{system} has to satisfy both equations at the same time,
and in line $B$ it gives $5 \ne -6 + 8 = 2$. On the graph the point sits on one line and well away
from the other. Full credit uses the both-equations-at-once idea.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A$: $y = 2x + 3$, \ $B$: $y = -x + 3$. (a) the solution; (b) predicting it.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad The lines cross on the $y$-axis. \ans{(0,\,3)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Both equations have the same constant term,
$3$, so both lines pass through $(0, 3)$ --- they already share that point before any graphing is
done. Their slopes, $2$ and $-1$, are different, so they cannot share any other point; two distinct
lines meet at most once. Full credit refers to the shared intercept and the different slopes.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Van plans $A$: $y = 4x + 30$, \ $B$: $y = 8x + 10$. (a) graph; (b) break-even; (c) which is cheaper after it?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Plan $A$ starts at $30$ dollars and rises $4$ dollars per day; plan $B$
starts at $10$ dollars and rises $8$ dollars per day. Plotting the intercepts and using the slopes
gives two lines that cross inside the grid.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad $4x + 30 = 8x + 10$ gives $20 = 4x$, so $x = 5$ days, and
$y = 4(5) + 30 = 50$ dollars. Both plans cost $50$ dollars for a five-day rental. \ans{(5,\,50)}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} To the right of the crossing point the plan
$B$ line, being steeper, sits above the plan $A$ line, and higher on this graph means more dollars.
So for rentals longer than $5$ days plan $A$ costs less; for shorter rentals plan $B$ does. Full
credit reads the comparison off the graph as which line is on top after the crossing.
