\wstitleblock{Solving Radical Equations and Checking Extraneous Roots}{Algebra 2}{}

\begin{center}\small\itshape
Isolating and squaring, checking every candidate for extraneous roots, moving
between radical and rational-exponent form, and solving applied radical models
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Why checking is part of solving.} Squaring both sides is not
a reversible step: $a = b$ forces $a^2 = b^2$, but $a^2 = b^2$ only says
$a = \pm b$. So the squared equation can have roots the original does not ---
\textbf{extraneous roots}. A radical equation is not finished until every
candidate has been substituted into the \emph{original} equation.

\smallskip
\noindent\small Show the substitution check for every problem that asks for
one, and state units in applied answers.\normalsize

\problem[5cm]{Solve for $x$, then check your solution in the original
equation.
\[ \sqrt{x - 3} = 5 \]}

\problem[5cm]{Rewrite $16^{3/4}$ as a radical power, then evaluate it without
a calculator. (Take the root first, then the power --- the numbers stay
small.)}

\problem[6.5cm]{The equation $\sqrt{x + 6} = x$ leads to the squared equation
$x^2 - x - 6 = 0$.

\smallskip
(a) Solve the squared equation to get two candidates. \quad
(b) Substitute each candidate into $\sqrt{x + 6} = x$ and record the value of
$\sqrt{x + 6} - x$. \quad
(c) State which candidate is extraneous and which is the solution.}

\problem[7cm]{Solve $\sqrt{3x + 1} = x - 1$.

\smallskip
(a) Square both sides and simplify to $x^2 - 5x = 0$; solve it. \quad
(b) Check both candidates in the original equation and give the value of
$\sqrt{3x + 1} - (x - 1)$ for the one that fails. \quad
(c) Write the solution set.}

\problem{\textbf{Free fall (given: no air resistance, dropped from
rest).} The time $t$ in seconds for an object to fall $h$ feet is
$t = \sqrt{h/16}$.

\smallskip
A stone dropped from a bridge takes $t = 2.5$ seconds to reach the water. How
high is the bridge, in feet? Solve the radical equation and check the answer
in the model.\par\vspace*{6cm}
\answerline{ft}}

\problem{\textbf{Skid marks (given: dry asphalt, straight skid).} A
car's speed $s$ in miles per hour is $s = \sqrt{24x}$, where $x$ is the length
of the skid mark in feet.

\smallskip
An investigator needs the skid length that corresponds to a speed of $60$
miles per hour. Solve the radical equation for $x$.\par\vspace*{6cm}
\answerline{ft}}

\problem[6.5cm]{Solve, or explain why no real solution exists:
\[ \sqrt{x - 2} = -3 \]
Squaring gives a candidate. Substitute it into the original equation, report
the value of $\sqrt{x - 2} + 3$ there, and use the range of the principal
square root to explain the result.}

\problem[6cm]{Solve $x^{3/2} = 8$ for $x$.

\smallskip
Write the equation in radical form first, then undo the power and the root.
State why this equation has no negative solution.}

\problem[7cm]{Solve $\sqrt{x + 7} - \sqrt{x} = 1$.

\smallskip
Isolate one radical before squaring, then isolate the remaining radical and
square again. Check the candidate in the original equation.}

\problem[7cm]{The solutions of $\sqrt{x + 5} = x - 1$ are the $x$-values where
the graphs of $y = \sqrt{x + 5}$ and $y = x - 1$ meet. The table shows both
sides at the two candidates that squaring produces.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$x$ & $\sqrt{x + 5}$ & $x - 1$ & equal? \\\hline
$-1$ & $\sqrt{-1 + 5} = 2$ & $-2$ & \\\hline
$4$ & $\sqrt{4 + 5} = 3$ & $3$ & \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(a) Complete the last column. \quad (b) Give the solution set. \quad
(c) Squaring turned the line $y = x - 1$ into a parabola that meets the curve
twice. Explain, using the table, why the extra intersection is not a solution
of the original equation.}
