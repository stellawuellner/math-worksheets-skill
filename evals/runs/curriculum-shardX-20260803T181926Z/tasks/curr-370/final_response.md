# Rational graphs: intercepts, holes, and asymptotes — three PDFs

Here is a 12-problem Algebra 2 set on reading a rational graph from its
formula, plus a full answer key and a two-page study guide.

**What is in each document**

- **Worksheet** (`ws_ratgraphs_curr370.pdf`, 6 pages). Twelve problems that
  rotate between four decisions: *factor and simplify* (where the holes are),
  *vertical asymptotes*, *intercepts*, and *end behaviour / horizontal
  asymptote*. Problems 1–2 are a blocked warm-up on factoring and simplifying;
  after that the four skills interleave, so the student has to decide which
  tool a problem calls for rather than repeat one procedure. Two problems are
  error-analysis ("Dana says the $y$-intercept is $-0.5$", "Jonah says $x=2$ is
  a vertical asymptote"), and problem 12 is a synthesis challenge: analyse
  $F(x) = \frac{2x^2-8}{x^2-x-2}$ completely and sketch it on the grid
  provided. Every problem has real writing room; the grade level is
  deliberately not printed anywhere the student sees.
- **Answer key** (`ak_ratgraphs_curr370.pdf`, 5 pages). A quick-answer bank at
  the top for fast grading, then a numbered step-by-step solution for every
  problem with the final answer boxed, plus grading notes ("listen for…") that
  say what a wrong answer usually means. It also carries the curriculum
  section: standards codes, difficulty range and the level.
- **Study guide** (`ss_ratgraphs_curr370.pdf`, 2 pages). One section per skill:
  a rule box, a worked example that opens by naming *why* that method applies,
  and a try-it problem whose answer is printed upside down inside the box so
  the student can self-check. A watch-out box closes it with the two errors the
  worksheet targets.

**What was verified**

All 21 machine-checkable answers were recomputed with SymPy before anything was
compiled: the factorisations, the simplified forms, every hole height and
horizontal asymptote as a limit, every intercept, and the crossing point in
problem 11. The printed answer key was then bound problem-by-problem to those
verified values, so a boxed answer cannot drift from the checked one. The
declared misconception trap on problem 6 was machine-checked to be
distinguishably wrong (the wrong method gives $-0.5$; the correct value is
$\frac{1}{2}$).

**Flagged for manual review (2 items)**

- Problem 10 asks for an *explanation* of why a cancelling factor gives a hole
  rather than an asymptote. The numerical part (the hole is at $(2,4)$) is
  machine-verified; the written reasoning is for you to read. A model answer is
  in the key.
- Problem 12 asks for a *sketch*. Its five numerical features are all verified;
  the drawing itself needs an eye. The key describes the model sketch.

Suggested order: study guide first, then the worksheet, then the answer key.
