% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 1
\problem{A rectangular sticker is 7 cm long and 4 cm wide. What is the area?}

\textbf{Solution:} Area is the space covered inside, so multiply length by
width: $7 \times 4 = 28$. The area is \ans{28\ \text{cm}^2}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 2
\problem{A garden is 8 m long and 5 m wide. How many meters of fence go all
the way around it (the perimeter)?}

\textbf{Solution:} Perimeter is the distance around, so add all four sides:
$8 + 5 + 8 + 5$, which is $2 \times (8 + 5) = 26$. The fence is
\ans{26\ \text{m}} long.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 3
\problem{A rug is 6 ft long and 3 ft wide. (a) Circle the word that tells how
much floor the rug covers. (b) Find that amount.}

\textbf{Model answer (a), instructor judges:} \textbf{AREA} must be circled ---
``how much floor the rug covers'' is the space inside the rug's edges, not the
distance around them.

\textbf{Solution (b):} Area is length times width: $6 \times 3 = 18$. The rug
covers \ans{18\ \text{ft}^2}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 4
\problem{Find and fix. Mateo's poster is 9 cm long and 5 cm wide. He writes:
``The area is 28 square centimeters, because $9+5+9+5=28$.'' (a) What is the
real area? (b) Name Mateo's error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Area multiplies the side lengths: $9 \times 5 = 45$.
The real area is \ans{45\ \text{cm}^2}

\textbf{Model answer (b), instructor judges:} Mateo added the four sides, which
gives the \emph{perimeter} (the distance around the poster), not the area.
Area counts the square centimeters inside, so it comes from
length $\times$ width.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 5
\problem{Nia glues a ribbon around the edge of a card 12 cm long and 6 cm wide.
How many centimeters of ribbon does she need?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Around the edge'' means perimeter, so add the sides:
$2 \times (12 + 6) = 36$. Nia needs \ans{36\ \text{cm}} of ribbon.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 6
\problem{On the grid, each little square is one square unit. The rectangle is
5 units long and 3 units wide. What is its area?}

\textbf{Solution:} Count the grid squares or multiply: 3 rows of 5 squares is
$5 \times 3 = 15$. The area is \ans{15\ \text{square units}}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 7
\problem{Find and fix. Jaya's square garden has 6 m sides. She works out
$6 \times 6 = 36$ and buys 36 m of fence. (a) How many meters of fence does
the garden actually need? (b) Explain what Jaya mixed up.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Fencing goes \emph{around} the square, so add the four
equal sides: $4 \times 6 = 24$. The garden needs \ans{24\ \text{m}} of fence.

\textbf{Model answer (b), instructor judges:} $6 \times 6 = 36$ is the
\emph{area} of the garden --- the square meters of ground inside. Fence length
is the perimeter, the distance around the edge, so Jaya should have added the
four 6 m sides instead of multiplying them.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- Problem 8
\problem{Challenge. A play area is 10 m long and 3 m wide. (a) How many square
meters of grass cover it? (b) How many meters of fence go around it?
(c) Compare the two numbers with $>$, $<$, or $=$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Area: $10 \times 3 = 30$, so the grass covers
\ans{30\ \text{m}^2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Perimeter: $2 \times (10 + 3) = 26$, so the fence is
\ans{26\ \text{m}}

\textbf{Solution (c):} Compare the two numbers: 30 is more than 26, so
\ans{30 > 26} --- for this rectangle the area number is bigger than the
perimeter number. (Careful: for other rectangles the perimeter number can be
the bigger one!)
