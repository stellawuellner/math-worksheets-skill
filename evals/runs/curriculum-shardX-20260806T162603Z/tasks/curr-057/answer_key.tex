\problem{Add $347 + 128$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ones: $7 + 8 = 15$, so write $5$ and carry one ten. Tens: $4 + 2$ plus the carried ten is $7$. Hundreds: $3 + 1 = 4$. One regroup, out of the ones column only. \ans{475}

\problem{Add $256 + 137$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ones: $6 + 7 = 13$, write $3$ and carry one ten. Tens: $5 + 3$ plus the carry is $9$. Hundreds: $2 + 1 = 3$. \ans{393}

\problem{Add $486 + 275$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ones: $6 + 5 = 11$, write $1$ and carry one ten. Tens: $8 + 7$ plus the carry is $16$, so write $6$ and carry one hundred. Hundreds: $4 + 2$ plus the carry is $7$. Two regroups, one after the other --- the second only appears once the first carry has been added in. \ans{761}

\problem{(a) Estimate $412 + 389$ by rounding each to the nearest hundred. (b) Find the exact sum.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $412$ is nearer to $400$ than to $500$; $389$ is nearer to $400$ than to $300$. Adding the rounded numbers gives $400 + 400$. \ans{800}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Ones: $2 + 9 = 11$, write $1$ and carry. Tens: $1 + 8$ plus the carry is $10$, write $0$ and carry. Hundreds: $4 + 3$ plus the carry is $8$. The exact sum sits one above the estimate, which is what a good estimate should look like. \ans{801}

\problem{Add $594 + 268$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ones: $4 + 8 = 12$, write $2$ and carry one ten. Tens: $9 + 6$ plus the carry is $16$, write $6$ and carry one hundred. Hundreds: $5 + 2$ plus the carry is $8$. \ans{862}

\problem{A class read $354$ pages in March and $427$ pages in April. How many pages in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} The question asks for the two amounts combined, so add. Ones: $4 + 7 = 11$, write $1$ and carry one ten. Tens: $5 + 2$ plus the carry is $8$. Hundreds: $3 + 4 = 7$. \ans{781 \text{ pages}}

\problem{(a) Estimate $638 + 275$ by rounding each to the nearest hundred. (b) Find the exact sum.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $638$ rounds to $600$ (the tens digit $3$ is below $5$) and $275$ rounds to $300$. Adding the rounded numbers gives $600 + 300$. \ans{900}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Ones: $8 + 5 = 13$, write $3$ and carry. Tens: $3 + 7$ plus the carry is $11$, write $1$ and carry. Hundreds: $6 + 2$ plus the carry is $9$. The estimate was low by thirteen, so it was a good check. \ans{913}

\problem{Dev added $476$ and $258$ and wrote $624$. (a) Add correctly. (b) Explain what Dev did wrong.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Ones: $6 + 8 = 14$, write $4$ and carry one ten. Tens: $7 + 5$ plus the carry is $13$, write $3$ and carry one hundred. Hundreds: $4 + 2$ plus the carry is $7$. \ans{734}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response names the dropped carries. Full credit for the idea that each column sum over nine had its tens part thrown away instead of carried on: the ten inside the fourteen ones and the hundred inside the twelve tens both went missing, which is why the written answer sits more than a hundred below the true sum. Accept a shorter statement that names one column correctly and shows where its carry should have gone. Saying only that the answer is too small, or that the columns were added wrongly without naming the carry, earns half credit. Restating the correct sum earns no credit, since part (a) already asks for it.

\problem{A library has $268$, $145$ and $87$ books on three shelves. How many books altogether?}

\textbf{Solution:} Three addends, same method, one column at a time. Ones: $8 + 5 + 7 = 20$, write $0$ and carry two tens. Tens: $6 + 4 + 8$ is $18$, plus the two carried tens is $20$; write $0$ and carry two hundreds. Hundreds: $2 + 1$ plus the two carried hundreds is $5$. A carry can be more than one when there are three addends. \ans{500 \text{ books}}

\problem{(a) Estimate $487 + 356$ to the nearest hundred. (b) Find the exact sum. (c) Explain how close the estimate came.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $487$ rounds up to $500$ and $356$ rounds up to $400$, so the estimate is $500 + 400$. \ans{900}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Ones: $7 + 6 = 13$, write $3$ and carry. Tens: $8 + 5$ plus the carry is $14$, write $4$ and carry. Hundreds: $4 + 3$ plus the carry is $8$. \ans{843}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct response compares the two and explains the direction. Full credit for noticing that both numbers were rounded \emph{up}, so the estimate is bound to land above the exact sum --- and it does, by fewer than a hundred, which is close enough to catch a badly wrong answer but not close enough to replace the exact one. Accept the difference worked out and used as the reason. Saying only that the estimate is close, with no reason, earns half credit. Claiming the estimate should equal the exact sum earns no credit.
