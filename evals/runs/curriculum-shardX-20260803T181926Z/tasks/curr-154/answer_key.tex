\problem{\textbf{Flour to sugar.} The question asks for flour first, so flour goes on
top: $3$ cups of flour to $2$ cups of sugar is $\ans{\dfrac{3}{2}}$.
It is already in simplest form, since 3 and 2 share no common factor.
Sugar to flour would be $\dfrac{2}{3}$ --- a different comparison, not a different way
of writing the same one.}

\problem{\textbf{Are $4 : 6$ and $6 : 9$ equivalent?}
Simplify each by dividing both parts by their greatest common factor:
$\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ (divide by 2) and $\dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
(divide by 3).
Both reduce to the same ratio, so $\ans{=}$: they are equivalent.
Cross products confirm it: $4 \times 9 = 36$ and $6 \times 6 = 36$.}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake (Leo).}
(a) A ratio names its quantities in the order asked. Writing $\dfrac{12}{18}$ puts boys
on top, so it answers ``boys to girls'', a different comparison from the one requested.
[Manual review: any sentence saying the first quantity named goes on top.]

(b) Girls to boys means girls on top: $\dfrac{18}{12}$. Divide both parts by 6:
$\ans{\dfrac{3}{2}}$.
Sense check: there are more girls than boys, so a correct girls-to-boys ratio must be
greater than 1. Leo's $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is less than 1, which is the quickest way to catch
a reversed ratio.}

\problem{\textbf{Pencil table.} The two columns must be equivalent ratios, so
\[ \frac{3 \text{ pencils}}{2 \text{ dollars}} = \frac{12 \text{ pencils}}{x \text{ dollars}} \]
Cross multiply: $3x = 2 \times 12 = 24$, so $\ans{x = 8}$ dollars.
Scale-factor check: 3 pencils became 12 pencils, a factor of 4, so the dollars must also
be multiplied by 4: $2 \times 4 = 8$. Both parts scale by the SAME factor, which is what
makes the columns equivalent.}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake (Mia).}
(a) Multiplying scales both parts by the same factor and keeps their comparison the
same; adding a fixed amount changes the two parts by different \emph{fractions} of
themselves, so the comparison shifts. Here $4 : 6$ simplifies to $2 : 3$, but Mia's
$6 : 8$ simplifies to $3 : 4$ --- a different ratio.
[Manual review: credit any explanation contrasting scaling with adding.]

(b) The water went from 4 cups to 6 cups, a factor of $\dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$.
Multiply the powder by the same factor: $6 \times 1.5 = \ans{9}$ scoops.
Check: $6 : 9$ simplifies to $2 : 3$, matching $4 : 6$. Mia's 8 scoops would give
$6 : 8 = 3 : 4$, which is not equivalent.}

\problem{\textbf{Comparing two cars.}
First car: $150 \div 5 = 30$ miles per gallon.
Second car: $210 \div 7 = 30$ miles per gallon.
The unit rates are the same, so the two ratios are equivalent: $\ans{=}$.
Unit rate is the fastest equivalence test --- both ratios collapse to the same
``per one'' value, here 30 miles for each single gallon.}

\problem{\textbf{Find the mistake (Sam).}
(a) The unit named last in ``pages per minute'' is the one you divide BY, so pages go on
top and minutes on the bottom.
[Manual review: credit ``the quantity after \emph{per} is the divisor''.]

(b) Correct rate: $\dfrac{24 \text{ pages}}{3 \text{ minutes}} = \ans{8}$ pages per
minute.
Sam computed $\dfrac{3}{24} = 0.125$, which is the reversed rate --- it answers
``minutes per page''. A quick sanity check settles it: the printer clearly prints more
than one page each minute, so a rate below 1 must be upside down.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: the row that does not belong.}
(a) Compare the last column with the first: $\dfrac{8}{22} = \dfrac{4}{11} \approx 0.36$
while $\dfrac{2}{5} = 0.4$. So $\ans{<}$, and the column is not equivalent.
The other columns all reduce to $\dfrac{2}{5}$, so the entry 22 is the wrong one.

(b) Blue paint went from 2 to 8, a factor of 4, so white paint must also be multiplied
by 4: $5 \times 4 = 20$. Solving $\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{8}{x}$ gives $2x = 40$ and
$\ans{x = 20}$.
The corrected column is $8 : 20$, which reduces to $2 : 5$ like every other column.}
