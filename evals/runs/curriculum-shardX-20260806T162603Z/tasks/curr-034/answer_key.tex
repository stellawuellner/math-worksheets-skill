\problem{1 ten and 4 ones make the number \_\_.}

\textbf{Solution:} 1 ten is worth 10. Start at 10 and count on the 4 ones:
10, 11, 12, 13, 14. So 1 ten and 4 ones make \ans{14}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{The number 17 has \_\_ ten and \_\_ ones.}

\textbf{Solution:} Read 17 place by place. The left digit counts tens and the
right digit counts ones. 17 is 10 and 7 more, so it has \ans{1} ten
and it has \ans{7} ones.

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Ben used 3 tens and 1 one to show the number 13. Fix it, then tell how you know.}

\textbf{Solution:} Ben swapped the places. In 13 the left digit is the tens
digit and the right digit is the ones digit. So 13 has \ans{1} ten
and it has \ans{3} ones.

\emph{Tell how you know (instructor judges):} accept any wording that names
the swap --- for example, ``Ben switched the tens and the ones. The 1 in 13
means 1 ten, and the 3 means 3 ones. What Ben built shows a much bigger
number.''

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Write the number that has 9 ones and 1 ten.}

\textbf{Solution:} Careful --- the ones were said first! The tens digit still
goes first when we write the number: 1 ten and 9 ones is 10 and 9 more. The
number is \ans{19}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Write $>$, $<$, or $=$: \ 91 \_\_ 19.}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare the tens first. 91 has 9 tens; 19 has only 1 ten.
9 tens is more than 1 ten, so \ans{91 > 19}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Mia has 1 ten and 6 ones. She writes 61. Find her mistake and fix it,
then tell how you know.}

\textbf{Solution:} Mia put the 6 ones in the tens place. 1 ten and 6 ones is
10 and 6 more, so the number is really \ans{16}

\emph{Tell how you know (instructor judges):} accept any wording that names
the swapped places --- for example, ``61 would mean 6 tens and 1 one. Mia only
has 1 ten, so the 1 must come first.''
