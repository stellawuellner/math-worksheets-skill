\wstitleblock{Distributions, Regression, and Inference --- Comparing Models and Qualifying What They Support}{Precalculus / Advanced Statistics}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Summarising a distribution, comparing two candidate models
through their residuals, and saying exactly how far the resulting inference
reaches}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{Directions:}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Data Set A} and the two candidate models are in the box below, shared by
        most of the sheet. Read what you need from there; take everything else from the
        problem you are on.
  \item A \textbf{residual} is always \emph{observed} $-$ \emph{predicted}. The sign
        matters: positive means the model under-predicted that point.
  \item Decide what each problem is asking before computing --- some ask you to summarise
        the data, some to compare the two models, and some to say how far a conclusion
        can honestly be pushed.
  \item Round decimal answers to the nearest hundredth unless a problem says otherwise,
        and give probabilities as exact fractions.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\textwidth}
\begin{center}
\textbf{Data Set A --- bacterial colony count (hundreds) after $x$ hours}\\[5pt]
\begin{tabular}{l|cccccc}
\hline
Hour $x$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
Observed count $y$ & 6 & 9 & 14 & 21 & 31 & 47 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\medskip
\textbf{Two candidate models fitted to this data}\\[3pt]
\textbf{Model L (linear):} \ $y = 8x - 6$ \qquad\qquad
\textbf{Model E (exponential):} \ $y = 4(1.5)^{x}$
\end{center}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\bigskip

\problem[6cm]{Use \textbf{Data Set A}. Find the median of the six observed counts.}

\problem[7cm]{Use \textbf{Data Set A}. Find the mean of the six observed counts, to the
nearest hundredth.

\smallskip
Then say which of the mean and the median is larger, and what that ordering tells you
about the shape of this distribution.}

\problem[7cm]{Model L predicts $y = 8x - 6$. At $x = 3$ the observed count is 14.

\smallskip
Find Model L's residual at that hour, and say in words what its sign means.}

\problem[7cm]{Model E predicts $y = 4(1.5)^{x}$. At $x = 3$ the observed count is 14.

\smallskip
Find Model E's residual at that hour.}

\problem[7cm]{Compare your two residuals from the previous two problems.

\smallskip
(a) Which model fits better at that hour, and how do you know from the residuals alone?

\smallskip
(b) Explain why a single residual is not enough evidence to choose between two models,
and name what you would examine instead.}

\problem[7cm]{Use \textbf{Data Set A}. Find the interquartile range of the six observed
counts.

\smallskip
Then say why the interquartile range describes this data differently from the range.}

\problem[7cm]{Use Model L, $y = 8x - 6$, at $x = 6$, where the observed count is 47.

\smallskip
Find the residual there, and compare its size with the residual you found for Model L at
the earlier hour. What does the change tell you about where Model L goes wrong?}

\problem[7cm]{Four of Model E's six residuals are positive, and the other two are exactly
zero.

\smallskip
One of the six hours is selected at random. Find the probability that it is an hour with a
positive residual. Give an exact fraction.}

\problem[6cm]{Use \textbf{Data Set A}. Find the total of the six observed counts.}

\problem[8cm]{Model E's residuals, hour by hour, are:

\par\smallskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|cccccc}
\hline
Hour & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
Residual & 0 & 0 & 0.5 & 0.75 & 0.625 & 1.4375 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\smallskip
At which hour is Model E's residual largest? Then describe the pattern in the signs and
sizes of these residuals, and say what that pattern reveals about Model E that a single
``it fits well'' verdict would hide.}

\problem[8cm]{The experiment ran for six hours only.

\smallskip
A lab partner wants to use Model E to state the colony count at hour twelve. Explain what
Model E can and cannot support about that claim. Your answer should address the observed
range, the behaviour of exponential growth in a real culture, and the fact that this is
one run of one experiment.}

\problem[9cm]{\textbf{Challenge.} Write the conclusion paragraph of the lab report.

\smallskip
It must: name which model fits Data Set A better and cite residual evidence for that
choice; state the one systematic weakness the residual pattern exposes even in the better
model; give a prediction together with the range over which you are willing to defend it;
and say what a second, independent run of the experiment would add that more arithmetic on
this run cannot.}
