\problem{\textbf{Write $6382$ in expanded form.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Expanded form breaks a number into what each digit is worth,
so name the place of each digit first: $6$ thousands, $3$ hundreds, $8$ tens,
$2$ ones.

\textbf{Step 2.} Turn each into its value: $6 \times 1000 = 6000$,
$3 \times 100 = 300$, $8 \times 10 = 80$, $2 \times 1 = 2$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Write the values as a sum:
\ans{6382 = 6000 + 300 + 80 + 2}

\textit{Check:} adding the four values returns the original number. That is
the test for any expanded form.}

\problem{\textbf{Value of the digit $7$ in $47308$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Value'' asks what the digit is worth where it sits, not
what digit it is. Count places from the right: ones, tens, hundreds,
thousands --- the $7$ is in the thousands place.

\textbf{Step 2.} $7 \times 1000$. \ans{7000}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $7$ --- that is the digit, not its value.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $6382$ to the nearest hundred.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Rounding to the hundreds means choosing between the hundred
below ($6300$) and the hundred above ($6400$), so look at the digit one place
to the right --- the tens digit.

\textbf{Step 2.} The tens digit is $8$, which is five or more, so round up.

\textbf{Step 3.} The hundreds digit goes from $3$ to $4$ and everything to its
right becomes zero. \ans{6400}

\textit{Circle:} the $8$ in the tens place is the digit that decided it.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare $47308$ and $47380$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Start at the largest place and move right only while the
digits match: ten-thousands $4 = 4$, thousands $7 = 7$, hundreds $3 = 3$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The tens place is the first difference: $0$ against $8$.
That decides the whole comparison, whatever comes after it.

\textbf{Step 3.} \ans{47308 < 47380}

\textit{The deciding place:} the tens.}

\problem{\textbf{Standard form of $40000 + 3000 + 60 + 5$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Each addend names one place. Sort them by size:
$4$ ten-thousands, $3$ thousands, $6$ tens, $5$ ones --- and notice there is
no hundreds addend at all.

\textbf{Step 2.} A missing place does not disappear; it needs a zero to hold
the other digits in position.

\textbf{Step 3.} Writing the digits in order --- ten-thousands, thousands,
hundreds, tens, ones --- gives \ans{43065}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $4365$ --- the placeholder zero was left out, and
every digit to its left slid one place down in value.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $47308$ to the nearest thousand.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Rounding to thousands is a choice between $47000$ and
$48000$, so read the digit one place to the right: the hundreds digit.

\textbf{Step 2.} The hundreds digit is $3$, which is four or less, so the
thousands digit stays where it is.

\textbf{Step 3.} Everything to the right of the thousands place becomes zero.
\ans{47000}

\textit{The digit that decided it:} the $3$ in the hundreds place.}

\problem{\textbf{Kai wrote $8090 > 8900$. Correct him.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Comparing is decided by the largest place where the digits
differ --- not by which number contains the biggest digit anywhere.

\textbf{Step 2.} Thousands: $8 = 8$, so move right. Hundreds: $0$ against $9$.
That is the first difference, and $0$ is less than $9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} \ans{8090 < 8900}

Kai should have compared the \emph{hundreds} place first, once the thousands
matched. A $9$ in the tens place is worth far less than a $9$ in the hundreds
place --- position beats size of digit.}

\problem{\textbf{In $55505$: how many times greater is the ten-thousands
$5$ than the hundreds $5$?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both digits are $5$, so the question is entirely about their
places. Compare the two values directly by dividing.

\textbf{Step 2.} $50000 \div 500$. Both end in two zeros, so this is
$500 \div 5 = 100$. \ans{100}

\textit{Why it comes out as a power of ten:} the two places are two steps
apart, and each step to the left multiplies by ten --- so
$10 \times 10 = 100$.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $25649$ to the nearest thousand.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The choice is between $25000$ and $26000$, so read the
hundreds digit.

\textbf{Step 2.} The hundreds digit is $6$, which is five or more, so the
thousands digit rounds up from $5$ to $6$. \ans{26000}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $25000$ --- from reading the $4$ in the tens
place instead of the $6$ in the hundreds. Only the digit immediately to the
right of the rounding place matters.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $72405$, \ $72045$, \ $70425$ from least to
greatest.}

\textbf{Step 1.} All three use the same digits, so nothing but place value
separates them. Compare from the left.

\textbf{Step 2.} Thousands place: $72405$ and $72045$ both have $2$
thousands; $70425$ has $0$. So $70425$ is the smallest.

\textbf{Step 3.} Between the other two, the hundreds place decides: $4$
against $0$, so $72045$ comes before $72405$.

\textbf{Step 4.} Least to greatest:
\ans{70425,\ 72045,\ 72405}

\textit{Why this is a challenge problem:} a student comparing digit by digit
without tracking places will read all three as ``the same numbers''.}

\problem{\textbf{Round $8950$ to the nearest hundred.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The choice is between $8900$ and $9000$, so read the
tens digit.

\textbf{Step 2.} The tens digit is $5$, so round up: the hundreds digit $9$
must become $10$ hundreds.

\textbf{Step 3.} Ten hundreds is one thousand, so the carry moves left: the
$8$ thousands become $9$ thousands and the hundreds place becomes $0$.
\ans{9000}

The thousands digit changed because rounding the hundreds up past $9$ has
nowhere to go inside the hundreds place --- ten hundreds \emph{is} a thousand,
so the extra one carries into the next place, exactly like a carry in
addition.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: Friday $38472$, Saturday $38247$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Name each digit's place in Friday's number --- $3$
ten-thousands, $8$ thousands, $4$ hundreds, $7$ tens, $2$ ones --- then write
the values as a sum.
\ans{38472 = 30000 + 8000 + 400 + 70 + 2}

\textbf{Step 2 (b).} Compare from the left: ten-thousands match, thousands
match, and the hundreds place is the first difference, $4$ against $2$.
\ans{38472 > 38247}

Friday had more people.

\textbf{Step 3 (c).} Round each to the nearest thousand by reading the
hundreds digit. Friday: $4$, so the thousands stay. Saturday: $2$, so the
thousands stay. Both land on the same value. \ans{38000}

\textbf{Step 4 (d).} \textit{Sample answer (accept any equivalent
explanation):} ``Both days round to $38000$ because rounding throws away
everything below the thousands place, and the two days only differ in the
hundreds and tens. To settle which day had more people I would compare the
exact numbers, or write them in expanded form and compare the hundreds.''

\textit{Grading note for part (d):} look for the idea that rounding removes
the very places where the two numbers differ. This is the one item on the
sheet that is not machine-checked.}
