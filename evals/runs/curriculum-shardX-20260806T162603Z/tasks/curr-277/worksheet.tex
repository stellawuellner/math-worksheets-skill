\begin{center}
\small Evaluating exponential rules, building tables and graphs, and solving equations with matching powers
\end{center}
\vspace{0.15cm}

\noindent\textbf{Directions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item In $a \cdot b^{\,x}$ the exponent belongs to $b$ alone. Work out the power first, then multiply by $a$.
  \item $b^0 = 1$ for every base, and $b^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{b^{\,n}}$ --- a negative exponent gives a small positive value, never a negative one.
  \item To solve $a \cdot b^{\,x} = c$, divide by $a$, then write the right-hand side as a power of $b$ and match exponents.
  \item Plot each table point carefully and join the points with a smooth curve, not straight segments.
\end{itemize}
