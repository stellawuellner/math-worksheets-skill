# Accumulation Functions and the Fundamental Theorem — three PDFs

**Course:** AP Calculus AB/BC · **Topic:** definite integrals and the
Fundamental Theorem, focused on differentiating accumulation functions ·
**8 problems**

## What you get

- **`ws_ftc_accum_curr473.pdf` — the student worksheet.**
  Every problem is an accumulation function built from a measured rate — a
  pump, a bacterial culture, a cooling tower, a holding pond, a charge sensor,
  a detector — and every stem states its units and its interval. Six of the
  eight ask the student to *differentiate* the accumulation function (the
  requested focus); the other two ask for an accumulated amount, which is the
  contrast that makes the difference visible. The chain-rule cases build from a
  single variable upper limit, to a variable lower limit, to problem 8 where
  both limits move.
- **`ak_ftc_accum_curr473.pdf` — the full answer key.**
  Every problem restated and worked. It opens with a generated Quick Answers
  bank, a "What is verified" note, and a Curriculum block listing the AP codes
  against the problems that carry them.
- **`ss_ftc_accum_curr473.pdf` — the study guide (2 pages).**
  Three sections: the plain FTC derivative rule, the chain-rule form for a
  variable limit, and evaluating an accumulated amount (the case that *does*
  need an antiderivative). Each has a rule box, a worked example whose first
  step says why that rule applies, and a try-it with the answer upside down.

## Verification, stated honestly

The eight problems ask for **16 responses**.

- **12 of 16 are machine-checked** with SymPy: every derivative value is
  recomputed from the integrand and the chain-rule factor, both definite
  integrals are recomputed exactly, and the two exact-value answers
  ($4\sqrt{65}$ and $2e^{-1} - 2e^{-4}$) are checked symbolically before their
  decimals are checked separately.
- **4 of 16 are instructor-judged** — parts 3(b), 5(b), 6(c) and 8(c), the
  four places the sheet asks for a written explanation rather than a value.
  They print as `---` in the Quick Answers bank, and each has a matching
  Solution paragraph stating what a correct answer must contain and what falls
  short (for example, "$V'(6) = 0$" alone locates a candidate, not a maximum).

The study guide's six results are all machine-checked.

## Units

Problems 2 and 7 declare their answer units in the verification data (litres,
cubic metres), and the worksheet's answer lines and the key's boxed answers are
both bound to those declarations, so the sheet cannot ask for litres and be
answered in something else.

## Suggested order

Study guide first, then the worksheet, then the answer key.
