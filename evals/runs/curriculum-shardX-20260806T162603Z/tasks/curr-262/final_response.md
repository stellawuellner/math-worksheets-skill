# Solving systems by substitution — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_substitution_curr262.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: substituting when one
  equation is already solved for a variable (with the two habits that prevent most errors — bracket
  the substituted expression, and put it into the *other* equation), isolating a variable first and
  choosing the one with coefficient 1 to keep fractions out, and the two endings where the variable
  cancels entirely. Each section has a rule box, a worked example opening with the strategy
  sentence, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_substitution_curr262.pdf`, 5 pages) — 12 problems on a clean ramp from
  difficulty 1 to 5, no two on the same skeleton: $y$ ready, $x$ ready, a substitution where the
  minus sign must reach both terms, two where a variable has to be isolated first, both equations
  solved for $y$, a ticket-sales model the student must set up as well as solve, a system with a
  fractional coefficient, and the two special cases — one with no solution and one with infinitely
  many.
- **Answer key** (`ak_substitution_curr262.pdf`, 4 pages) — every problem worked step by step with
  a substitution check back into both original equations, and the standard slips called out where
  they happen (dropping the brackets in problem 3, isolating the variable that creates fractions).
  Includes a Quick Answers bank and a Curriculum block (HSA-REI.C.6, difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **9 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked**, and 12 checks in total are verified: SymPy
  substituted every ordered pair back into *both* equations of its system, and independently
  confirmed that problem 7's system really is inconsistent — the "no solution" is a checked result,
  not an assertion.
- **3 problems (7, 9, 12) carry an instructor-judged part** alongside their verified one: what a
  false leftover statement means, why an always-true one means infinitely many solutions, and how
  to spot before starting that substitution beats elimination. Those are prose. The answer key
  prints a rubric for each, and the Quick Answers bank marks them `—` instead of claiming they were
  machine-checked.
- No blanks are marked as working space; every printed blank is an answer.

The grade level prints on the answer key only.
