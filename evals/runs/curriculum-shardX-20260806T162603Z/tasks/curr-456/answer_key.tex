\problem{Cart with $s(t) = t^2 + 3t$ metres. Average velocity from $t = 1$ to
$t = 3$ seconds.}

\textbf{Solution:} An average rate is the change in output over the change in
input --- the slope of the line joining the two table points.
\[
  \frac{s(3) - s(1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{18 - 4}{2} = \frac{14}{2}
\]
so the average velocity is \ans{7\ \text{m/s}}

Dividing $s(3)$ by $3$ instead would give $6$; that is a rate measured from
$t = 0$, not from $t = 1$, and it answers a different question.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x) = x^2$. Find $f'(3)$ by the limit definition.}

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
  f'(3) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(3+h)^2 - 3^2}{h}
        = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{9 + 6h + h^2 - 9}{h} \\[2pt]
        &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h(6 + h)}{h}
        = \lim_{h \to 0} (6 + h)
\end{align*}
The $h$ in the denominator cancels --- that is the whole purpose of expanding
first --- and letting $h \to 0$ leaves \ans{6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Same cart, $s(t) = t^2 + 3t$. Instantaneous velocity at $t = 3$ seconds.}

\textbf{Solution:} $s(3) = 9 + 9 = 18$, so
\begin{align*}
  \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{s(3+h) - 18}{h}
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(3+h)^2 + 3(3+h) - 18}{h} \\[2pt]
    &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{9 + 6h + h^2 + 9 + 3h - 18}{h}
     = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h(9 + h)}{h}
     = \lim_{h \to 0} (9 + h)
\end{align*}
so the instantaneous velocity is \ans{9\ \text{m/s}}

The contrast with problem 1 is the lesson: $7$ m/s is the slope of the line
joining $(1,4)$ to $(3,18)$; $9$ m/s is the slope of the curve at the single
instant $t = 3$. A student who answers $7$ here has computed an interval rate
where an instant was asked for.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Find $f'(2)$ by the limit definition.}

\textbf{Solution:} Combine the numerator over a common denominator first:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\frac{1}{2+h} - \frac{1}{2}}{h}
    &= \frac{\frac{2 - (2+h)}{2(2+h)}}{h}
     = \frac{-h}{2(2+h)} \cdot \frac{1}{h}
     = \frac{-1}{2(2+h)} \qquad (h \neq 0).
\end{align*}
Letting $h \to 0$ gives $\dfrac{-1}{2(2)} = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. \ans{-\tfrac{1}{4}}

The negative sign is the graph telling the truth: $y = 1/x$ falls as $x$
increases, so its slope at $x = 2$ must be negative.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Draining tank, $V(t) = 60 - 2t^2$ litres. (a) $V'(5)$ by the limit
definition. (b) What does it mean?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $V(5) = 60 - 2(25) = 10$, so
\begin{align*}
  V'(5) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\left[60 - 2(5+h)^2\right] - 10}{h}
         = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{60 - 2\left(25 + 10h + h^2\right) - 10}{h} \\[2pt]
        &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-20h - 2h^2}{h}
         = \lim_{h \to 0} (-20 - 2h)
\end{align*}
which gives \ans{-20}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} The sentence must
read the sign and the size separately. The sign is negative, so the tank is
\emph{losing} water at that moment. The size is $20$, and the units are litres
per minute because the output is litres and the input is minutes. So: at
$t = 5$ minutes the volume is falling at $20$ litres per minute, at that instant.
Full credit needs the direction, the number with its rate units, and the idea of
an instant rather than a total. Do not accept ``20 litres'' with no rate unit.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$f(x) = \sqrt{x}$. Find $f'(9)$ by the limit definition.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply by the conjugate of the numerator:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\sqrt{9+h} - 3}{h} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{9+h} + 3}{\sqrt{9+h} + 3}
    &= \frac{(9+h) - 9}{h\left(\sqrt{9+h} + 3\right)}
     = \frac{1}{\sqrt{9+h} + 3} \qquad (h \neq 0).
\end{align*}
As $h \to 0$ this tends to $\dfrac{1}{3 + 3}$. \ans{\tfrac{1}{6}}

A tangent slope of about $0.17$ is what the graph of $\sqrt{x}$ should look
like at $x = 9$ --- rising, but slowly.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$g(x) = x^3 - 2x$. (a) $g'(x)$. (b) The slope at $x = 2$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Differentiate term by term with the power rule ---
$\frac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}$, which is what the limit definition produces once
and for all:
\[
  g'(x) = 3x^2 - 2 . \qquad \ans{3x^2 - 2}
\]

\textbf{Solution (b):} The derivative function is a machine for slopes; feed it
the input. At $x = 2$: $\ 3(2)^2 - 2 = 12 - 2$. \ans{10}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Challenge. $h(t) = 40t - 5t^2$ metres. (a) $h'(t)$. (b) Velocity at
$t = 2$. (c) Meaning of the velocity at $t = 5$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Differentiating term by term,
\[
  h'(t) = 40 - 10t . \qquad \ans{40 - 10t}
\]

\textbf{Solution (b):} At $t = 2$: $\ 40 - 10(2) = 40 - 20$, so the ball is
rising at \ans{20}

metres per second.

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged, no machine check.} Substituting
gives $h'(5) = 40 - 50 = -10$. The sentence must say that at $t = 5$ seconds the
ball is moving \emph{downward} at $10$ metres per second --- the negative sign is
direction, the $10$ is speed, and the statement is about that instant, not about
distance travelled. Accept ``falling at 10 m/s at $t = 5$''. Do not accept ``the
ball has gone down 10 metres'', which confuses a rate with a displacement.
