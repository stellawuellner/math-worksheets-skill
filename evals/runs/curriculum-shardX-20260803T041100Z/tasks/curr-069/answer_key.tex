\problem{\textbf{Array of dots: 3 rows, 5 in each row. How many in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} Name the two numbers before multiplying. The number of
\emph{rows} is 3 (count down the left side). The number \emph{in each row} is 5
(count across one row). The question asks for the total, so multiply
rows $\times$ in each row: $3 \times 5$. Skip-count by fives three times ---
5, 10, 15 --- or add $5 + 5 + 5$. Total dots: $\ans{15}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Array of squares: 4 rows, 6 in each row. Write the sentence and
find the total.}

\textbf{Solution:} The multiplication sentence records the picture in order:
rows first, then how many are in each row, so it is $4 \times 6 = \square$.
Skip-count by sixes four times: 6, 12, 18, 24. Total squares: $\ans{24}$.

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} a child who answers 4 has reported the number of
rows, which is the count of \emph{groups}, not the count of \emph{squares}.}

\problem{\textbf{Garden: 6 rows of carrots, 3 carrots in each row.}

\textbf{Solution:} The words ``in the garden altogether'' name the total, so the
answer must count carrots, not rows. Rows $= 6$, in each row $= 3$, so the total
is $6 \times 3$. Skip-count by threes six times: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18.
Carrots in all: $\ans{18}$.

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} the answer 9 comes from $6 + 3$. Adding joins two
groups; here there are six equal groups, which is multiplication.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Jonah's 5 rows of 4 stickers.}

\textbf{Solution:} Jonah answered the question ``how many rows?'' when the
question asked ``how many stickers in all?'' The 5 he counted is the number of
groups; each of those groups still holds 4 stickers. Multiply the two numbers he
already found: $5 \times 4$. Skip-count by fours five times: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20.
Correct total: $\ans{20}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Mia's 2 rows of 8 shells.}

\textbf{Solution:} Mia used addition, which would be right only if she had one
group of 2 and one group of 8. She actually has 2 equal groups of 8, and equal
groups call for multiplication: $2 \times 8$. Doubling 8 gives 16, and the
picture agrees --- $8 + 8 = 16$. Correct total: $\ans{16}$.

\noindent\textit{Watch for:} her answer 10 is $2 + 8$; it is smaller than one
full row of shells, which is a quick signal that the operation was wrong.}

\problem{\textbf{28 muffins in 4 equal rows --- how many in each row?}

\textbf{Solution:} Here the \emph{total} is known (28) and the number of rows is
known (4); the missing number is the size of one group. The multiplication
sentence is $4 \times n = 28$. Ask ``4 times what is 28?'' --- skip-count by
fours: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, which took 7 counts. Check: $4 \times 7 = 28$
$\checkmark$. Muffins in each row: $\ans{n = 7}$.}

\problem{\textbf{30 beads in rows of 5 --- how many rows?}

\textbf{Solution:} This time the size of each group is known (5 beads) and the
number of rows is missing: $5 \times n = 30$. Skip-count by fives until reaching
30: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 --- that is 6 counts, so 6 rows. Check:
$5 \times 6 = 30$ $\checkmark$. Number of rows: $\ans{n = 6}$.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge --- 36 counters in rows of 9.}

\textbf{Solution:} Sam read the total (36 counters) as if it were the number of
rows. Those are different quantities: 36 counts every counter on the board,
while a row is a whole group of 9 counters. The sentence for the board is
$9 \times n = 36$, where $n$ is the number of rows. Skip-count by nines:
9, 18, 27, 36 --- four counts. Check: $9 \times 4 = 36$ $\checkmark$.
Number of rows: $\ans{n = 4}$.}
