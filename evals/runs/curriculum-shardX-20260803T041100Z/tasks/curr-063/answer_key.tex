\problem{Library books: estimate $412 - 187$ to the nearest hundred. \\
$412$ rounds to $400$ (the tens digit is $1$, less than $5$, so round down).
$187$ rounds to $200$ (the tens digit is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so round up).
Subtract the rounded numbers: $400 - 200 = 200$. About $200$ books are still on
the shelf. \ans{200}
\par\noindent\emph{Grader's note:} the exact difference is $225$ books, so an
estimate of $200$ books is a good one. Accept the estimate only when \emph{both}
numbers were rounded --- rounding just one is the most common error here.}

\problem{Ferry seats: estimate $265 - 138$ to the nearest ten. \\
$265$ rounds to $270$ (the ones digit is $5$, so round up). $138$ rounds to
$140$ (the ones digit is $8$). Subtract the rounded numbers:
$270 - 140 = 130$. The ferry could have carried about $130$ more passengers.
\ans{130}
\par\noindent\emph{Grader's note:} the exact difference is $127$ passengers.
Rounding to the nearest ten lands within $3$ of the exact answer, much closer
than rounding to the nearest hundred would.}

\problem{Apples: check $175$ apples left by adding back the $165$ apples sold. \\
Addition undoes subtraction, so the answer plus the number taken away must give
back the starting number. Add $175 + 165$: ones, $5 + 5 = 10$, write $0$ and
carry $1$; tens, $7 + 6 + 1 = 14$, write $4$ and carry $1$; hundreds,
$1 + 1 + 1 = 3$. \ans{340}
\par\noindent Expected sentence: ``The crate started with $340$ apples.''}

\problem{Stadium seats: estimate $738 - 264$ to the nearest hundred. \\
$738$ rounds to $700$ (tens digit $3$). $264$ rounds to $300$ (tens digit $6$).
Subtract the rounded numbers: $700 - 300 = 400$. About $400$ seats are filled.
\ans{400}
\par\noindent\emph{Grader's note:} the exact difference is $474$ seats. The
estimate is about $74$ low because both numbers rounded in the same direction
that widens the gap --- worth saying aloud, but $400$ is still the correct
estimate.}

\problem{Recycling cans: estimate, then compute exactly. \\
\textbf{(a) Estimate.} $906$ rounds to $900$ and $497$ rounds to $500$, so
$900 - 500 = 400$. The club needs about $400$ more cans. \ans{400}
\par\noindent\textbf{(b) Exact.} $906 - 497$: the ones need a group but the tens
digit is $0$, so trade a hundred first --- $906$ is $8$ hundreds, $10$ tens,
$6$ ones, then $8$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $16$ ones. Now $16 - 7 = 9$;
$9 - 9 = 0$; $8 - 4 = 4$. \ans{409}
\par\noindent\textbf{(c) Reasonable?} Yes --- $409$ cans is only $9$ away from the
estimate of $400$ cans, so the exact answer is reasonable.}

\problem{Book pages: Ben's check. \\
\textbf{(a) Check by adding.} $552 + 175$: ones, $2 + 5 = 7$; tens,
$5 + 7 = 12$, write $2$ and carry $1$; hundreds, $5 + 1 + 1 = 7$. \ans{727}
\par\noindent The check gives $727$ pages, \emph{not} the $623$ pages the book
has, so Ben's answer is wrong.
\par\noindent\textbf{(b) Exact.} $623 - 175$: the ones need a group, so trade a
ten --- $6$ hundreds, $1$ ten, $13$ ones. The tens now need a group too, so
trade a hundred --- $5$ hundreds, $11$ tens, $13$ ones. Then $13 - 5 = 8$;
$11 - 7 = 4$; $5 - 1 = 4$. \ans{448}
\par\noindent Check the corrected answer: $448 + 175 = 623$ pages $\checkmark$}

\problem{Farm eggs: estimate $851 - 349$ to the nearest hundred. \\
$851$ rounds to $900$ (tens digit $5$, so round up). $349$ rounds to $300$ ---
look at the \emph{tens} digit, which is $4$, so round down. Subtract the rounded
numbers: $900 - 300 = 600$. About $600$ eggs are still at the farm. \ans{600}
\par\noindent\emph{Grader's note:} the exact difference is $502$ eggs. A student
who wrote $500$ rounded $349$ up to $400$ by looking at the ones digit $9$ ---
send them back to the tens digit rule.}

\problem{Theater tickets: estimate, then compute exactly. \\
\textbf{(a) Estimate.} $604$ rounds to $600$ (tens digit $0$) and $288$ rounds
to $300$ (tens digit $8$), so $600 - 300 = 300$. About $300$ adult tickets.
\ans{300}
\par\noindent\textbf{(b) Exact.} $604 - 288$: the ones need a group and the tens
digit is $0$, so trade a hundred --- $5$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $4$ ones --- then
trade a ten --- $5$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $14$ ones. Now $14 - 8 = 6$;
$9 - 8 = 1$; $5 - 2 = 3$. \ans{316}
\par\noindent $316$ adult tickets sits close to the estimate of $300$, so the
answer is reasonable. Check: $316 + 288 = 604$ tickets $\checkmark$}

\problem{Rico's stickers: why $655$ cannot be right, then the exact answer. \\
\textbf{Why it is unreasonable.} Round to the nearest hundred: $703 \approx 700$
and $158 \approx 200$, so the answer should be near $700 - 200 = 500$ stickers.
Rico's $655$ stickers is more than $150$ away from that, so it is far too big.
\par\noindent\textbf{What went wrong.} In each column Rico took the smaller digit
from the larger one instead of regrouping.
\par\noindent\textbf{Correct work.} $703 - 158$: the ones need a group and the
tens digit is $0$, so trade a hundred --- $6$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $3$ ones ---
then trade a ten --- $6$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $13$ ones. Now $13 - 8 = 5$;
$9 - 5 = 4$; $6 - 1 = 5$. \ans{545}
\par\noindent The exact answer $545$ stickers is close to the estimate of $500$
stickers $\checkmark$}

\problem{Food drive: estimate $900 - 348 - 275$ to the nearest hundred. \\
Round every number first: $900$ is already a hundred; $348$ rounds to $300$
(tens digit $4$); $275$ rounds to $300$ (tens digit $7$). Now subtract in order:
$900 - 300 = 600$, then $600 - 300 = 300$. About $300$ more cans are needed.
\ans{300}
\par\noindent\emph{Grader's note:} the exact answer is $277$ cans, so the
estimate of $300$ cans is close. Accept either subtracting one at a time or
adding the two weeks first ($300 + 300 = 600$ rounded) --- both give $300$.}
