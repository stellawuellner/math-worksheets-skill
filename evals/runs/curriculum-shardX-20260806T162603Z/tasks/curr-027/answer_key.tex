\problem{Here is the pattern. Start at 7. Hop back 2: you land on 6, then 5.
So $7-2=5$. Now trace it yourself and write the answer.}

\textbf{Solution:} Finger on 7, hop back two boxes: 6, then 5. Two hops for
the 2 being subtracted. So $7-2=5$. \ans{5}

\problem{Start at 4. Hop back 1.}

\textbf{Solution:} Finger on 4, one hop back lands on 3. So $4-1=3$. \ans{3}

\problem{Draw your hops on the path: start at 8 and hop back 3.}

\textbf{Solution:} Finger on 8, hop back three boxes: 7, 6, 5. So $8-3=5$.
\ans{5}

\emph{Hops (instructor judges):} three arcs drawn from 8 landing on 7, 6,
and 5.

\problem{Use the path to help you. \ $9-2$}

\textbf{Solution:} Start at 9, hop back twice: 8, 7. So $9-2=7$. \ans{7}

\problem{A new way! To find $6-4$, start at 4 on the path and count the hops
\emph{up} to 6.}

\textbf{Solution:} Finger on 4, hop up: 5, 6 --- that is 2 hops to reach 6.
The number of hops is the difference, so $6-4=2$. \ans{2}

\problem{Start at 7. Count the hops up to 10. \ $10-7$}

\textbf{Solution:} From 7, hop up: 8, 9, 10 --- three hops. So $10-7=3$.
\ans{3}

\problem{Pick your way: hop back, or count up. \ $10-4$}

\textbf{Solution:} Hopping back 4 from 10 lands on 9, 8, 7, 6. (Counting up
from 4 to 10 takes six hops --- same answer, more hops!) So $10-4=6$.
\ans{6}

\problem{The hop number is hiding! Start at 8. You land on 3. How many hops
back did you make? \ $8-\square=3$}

\textbf{Solution:} Count the boxes from 8 down to 3: 7, 6, 5, 4, 3 --- that
is 5 hops. Check: $8-5=3$. The hidden hop number is \ans{5}

\problem{Pick your way: hop back 6 times, or count up from 6. Draw your hops
on the path. \ $9-6$}

\textbf{Solution:} Counting up is quicker here: from 6, hop 7, 8, 9 --- only
3 hops instead of six back. So $9-6=3$. \ans{3}

\emph{Hops (instructor judges):} either six arcs back from 9 landing on 3,
or 3 arcs up from 6 to 9; the hop count must match the answer written.

\problem{Challenge! Mo starts at 10 and hops back. He lands on 6. How many
hops did he make? Tell how the path shows it. \ $10-\square=6$}

\textbf{Solution:} Count the boxes from 10 down to 6: 9, 8, 7, 6 --- that is
4 hops. Check: $10-4=6$. The missing hop number is \ans{4}

\emph{Tell how (instructor judges):} a good answer says the hops between 10
and 6 can be counted on the path --- 4 hops either direction --- so the
missing number is 4.
