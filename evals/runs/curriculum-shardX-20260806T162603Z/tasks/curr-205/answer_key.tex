\problem{Write $\dfrac{5}{8}$ as a decimal.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Divide the numerator by the denominator. The division terminates
after three places, which is what makes the number rational. \ans{0.625}

\problem{Give $\sqrt{11}$ to the nearest hundredth.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $9 < 11 < 16$ brackets the root between $3$ and $4$. Squeezing:
$3.31^2 = 10.9561$ and $3.32^2 = 11.0224$ trap the radicand, and the upper one is nearer.
\ans{3.32}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between $\dfrac{7}{9}$ and $0.78$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The fraction repeats: $7 \div 9 = 0.7777\ldots$. The two decimals
agree in the tenths place and first differ in the hundredths, where seven is less than
eight. \ans{<}

\problem{Write $\dfrac{17}{4}$ as a decimal.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Four goes into seventeen four times with one left over, and the
remainder makes a quarter. \ans{4.25}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between $\sqrt{17}$ and $4.2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Square the decimal instead of squeezing the root, which is faster
here: $4.2^2 = 17.64$, which is larger than the radicand, so the decimal is larger than the
root. \ans{<}

\problem{Order $\sqrt{5}$, $2.3$, $\dfrac{9}{4}$ from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Convert all three to two decimal places: $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$
(since $2.23^2 = 4.9729$ and $2.24^2 = 5.0176$), $\dfrac{9}{4} = 2.25$ exactly, and $2.3$
is already a decimal. Ordering the decimals and rewriting in the original forms:
\[ \boxed{\sqrt{5} \;<\; \tfrac{9}{4} \;<\; 2.3} \]

\problem{Give $\pi$ to the nearest hundredth.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The decimal expansion of the circle constant begins
$3.14159\ldots$, and the digit after the hundredths place is below five, so it rounds down.
\ans{3.14}

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between $\pi$ and $\sqrt{10}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad To two places both read $3.14$ and $3.16$; to three,
$\pi = 3.142$ while $\sqrt{10} = 3.162$ (check: $3.16^2 = 9.9856$, just under the
radicand). The root is the larger. \ans{<}

\problem{Order $\sqrt{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{2}$, $1.4$ from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ (since $1.41^2 = 1.9881$ and
$1.42^2 = 2.0164$), $\dfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$ exactly, and $1.4$ is already a decimal. The plain
decimal is smallest, and the fraction is largest.
\[ \boxed{1.4 \;<\; \sqrt{2} \;<\; \tfrac{3}{2}} \]

\problem{Write $<$, $>$, or $=$ between $-\sqrt{9}$ and $-3.1$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The radicand is a perfect square, so the first quantity is exactly
$-3$. On the number line $-3.1$ sits further left, and further left means smaller, so the
first quantity is the larger. \ans{>}

\problem{Order $\sqrt{30}$, $5.6$, $\dfrac{27}{5}$, $\sqrt{36}$ from least to greatest.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $\sqrt{36} = 6$ exactly, $\dfrac{27}{5} = 5.4$ exactly,
$\sqrt{30} \approx 5.48$ (since $5.47^2 = 29.9209$ and $5.48^2 = 30.0304$), and $5.6$ is
already a decimal. Note that the largest of the four is a root, not the plain decimal.
\[ \boxed{\tfrac{27}{5} \;<\; \sqrt{30} \;<\; 5.6 \;<\; \sqrt{36}} \]

\problem{\textbf{Challenge.} Nadia orders $\sqrt{16}$, $3.9$, $\dfrac{15}{4}$ by reading
the printed numerals. (a) Correct order? (b) Why does that not work?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad Convert first: $\sqrt{16} = 4$ exactly, $\dfrac{15}{4} = 3.75$
exactly, and $3.9$ is already a decimal.
\[ \boxed{\tfrac{15}{4} \;<\; 3.9 \;<\; \sqrt{16}} \]

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad Nadia compared the numerals as printed
rather than the quantities they stand for. A radical and a fraction give no reliable size
information until they are evaluated: the root here is not even irrational, and it turns out
to be the \emph{largest} of the three despite showing the biggest numeral inside it. A
full-credit answer names the conversion step as the thing that was skipped.
