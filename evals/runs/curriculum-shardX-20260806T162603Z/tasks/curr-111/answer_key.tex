\problem{$23$ counters in groups of $5$. Circle the groups. (a) Full groups? (b) Left over?}

\textbf{The circled model --- judged by eye.} Look for four rings, each around exactly five counters,
with three counters left outside every ring. Rings of different sizes mean the groups were not equal;
a fifth ring drawn round the three leftovers is the error this problem is built to expose --- a
remainder is not a group.

\textbf{Solution (a):} Circling fives across the row gives four complete circles before the counters
run out: $5, 10, 15, 20$. \ans{4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Four groups use $4 \times 5 = 20$ counters, and $23 - 20 = 3$ are left. The
remainder is smaller than the group size, which is the check that no further group could be made.
\ans{3}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$34$ tiles in rows of $6$. (a) Complete rows? (b) Tiles left over?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Count by sixes: $6, 12, 18, 24, 30$ --- five complete rows, and $36$ would be
too many. \ans{5}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $34 - 5 \times 6 = 34 - 30 = 4$, which matches the short row at the bottom of
the model. \ans{4}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$38$ students, vans seat $8$. (a) Vans needed? (b) Why do the leftover students change the answer?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $38 \div 8 = 4$ with remainder $6$. Four vans seat only $32$ students, so a
fifth van is needed for the remaining six. \ans{5\ \text{vans}}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer says the remainder is six students,
that those students still have to travel, and that a fifth van is therefore needed even though it
will not be full. Model answer: ``Dividing gives $4$ remainder $6$. Those $6$ students cannot be left
behind, so one more van is added, making $5$.'' Repeating the number five with no mention of the
leftover students is not full credit.

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$45$ cookies, boxes of $6$, only full boxes sold. How many full boxes?}

\textbf{Solution:} $45 \div 6 = 7$ with remainder $3$. Here the remainder is dropped, not rounded up:
the three extra cookies do not make a full box, and only full boxes are sold. Compare with the van
problem, where the same leftover forced an extra container --- the arithmetic is identical, the
decision is not. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$356$ bulbs into $8$ beds. (a) Round. (b) Estimate. (c) Exact.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The tens digit of $356$ is $5$, so it rounds up to the next hundred. \ans{400}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $400 \div 8 = 50$, so expect about fifty bulbs per bed. \ans{50}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $8 \times 40 = 320$, leaving $36$; $8 \times 4 = 32$, leaving $4$. So the
quotient is $40 + 4 = 44$ and the remainder is $4$. The exact answer sits just under the estimate,
which is what rounding up predicts. \ans{44} \quad remainder \ans{4}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A bar of $96$ counters split into $4$ equal parts. How many in each part?}

\textbf{Solution:} The bar model shows one whole of $96$ cut into four equal pieces, so this is
$96 \div 4$. Split it by place value: $80 \div 4 = 20$ and $16 \div 4 = 4$, so each part holds
$20 + 4 = 24$. There is nothing left over --- the parts really are equal. \ans{24}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$60$ counters in $7$ equal groups with $4$ left over. How many in each group?}

\textbf{Solution:} Read the model as an equation: $7 \times (\text{group size}) + 4 = 60$. Take the
remainder off first: $60 - 4 = 56$ counters are actually in groups. Then $56 \div 7 = 8$. \ans{8}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$52$ marbles into bags of $9$. How many are left over?}

\textbf{Solution:} $9 \times 5 = 45$ and $9 \times 6 = 54$ is too many, so five bags fill and
$52 - 45 = 7$ marbles remain. The question asks for the leftovers, not the bags, so the answer is
the remainder. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$1{,}235$ leaflets into $6$ cartons. (a) Round. (b) Estimate. (c) Exact.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The tens digit of $1235$ is $3$, below $5$, so it rounds down to the nearest
hundred. \ans{1200}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $1200 \div 6 = 200$, so expect about two hundred per carton. \ans{200}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $6 \times 200 = 1200$, leaving $35$; $6 \times 5 = 30$, leaving $5$. The
quotient is $200 + 5 = 205$ and the remainder is $5$. The estimate of $200$ was close, as expected
after rounding down by only $35$. \ans{205} \quad remainder \ans{5}

\vspace{0.12cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$83$ pencils, $6$ friends / boxes of $6$. (a) Each friend gets? (b) Left over? (c) Boxes needed? (d) Why do (a) and (c) differ?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $6 \times 13 = 78$ and $6 \times 14 = 84$ is too many, so each friend gets
$13$. \ans{13}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $83 - 78 = 5$. \ans{5}

\textbf{Solution (c):} Thirteen boxes hold $78$ pencils and five are still loose, so a fourteenth box
is needed. \ans{14}

\textbf{Solution (d) --- instructor-judged.} A full-credit answer says the division was the same
($83 \div 6 = 13$ remainder $5$) and only what happens to the remainder changed. Model answer: ``Both
times I got $13$ remainder $5$. When sharing pencils the $5$ left over just are not handed out, so the
answer is $13$. When packing boxes those $5$ still need somewhere to go, so I add a box and get $14$.''
Giving both numbers without saying what the remainder does in each case is not full credit.
