# Choosing expanded or factored form — Algebra 1

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_forms_curr255.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: writing an expression in
  standard form (and what that form shows), writing it in factored form (and what *that* shows),
  and letting the question pick the form. The third section states the zero-product property
  explicitly and gives the two-line decision rule: asked where it is zero, factor; asked for the
  constant term or the leading coefficient, expand. Each section has a rule box, a worked example
  opening with the strategy sentence, and a try-it with the answer inverted inside the box.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_forms_curr255.pdf`, 5 pages) — 12 problems, difficulty 1 → 5. The first four
  are a blocked warm-up (two in each direction); after that the two directions are interleaved so
  the student has to read the question and decide. Problem 3 is a thrown-ball model where the
  height is *already* factored and the landing times fall out with no algebra; problem 12 is the
  synthesis — factor a garden's area, evaluate it, then say which form gives the side lengths.
- **Answer key** (`ak_forms_curr255.pdf`, 4 pages) — every problem worked with the reasoning named
  (including why a partly-factored answer such as $(2x-6)(x+3)$ is not finished), a Quick Answers
  bank, and a Curriculum block (HSA-APR.A.1, HSA-APR.B.3, HSA-SSE.B.3; difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **10 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy: every expansion, factorisation,
  zero set and substitution was recomputed independently of the answer key before compiling. The
  units on problems 3 (seconds) and 12 (square metres) are checked in both directions too.
- **2 problems (9 and 12) are part machine-checked, part instructor-judged.** Their computed parts
  are verified; their "explain which form and why" parts are prose that no program can grade. Those
  are flagged for you, and the key prints the rubric — what a full-credit explanation must name and
  what earns half credit. The Quick Answers bank shows them as `—`.
- No blanks are marked as working space; every printed blank is an answer.

The grade level prints on the answer key only.
