\problem{$\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{5}$. Multiply straight across:
numerator $2 \times 3 = 6$, denominator $3 \times 5 = 15$, giving $\dfrac{6}{15}$.
Both parts share the factor 3, so divide top and bottom by 3.
$\ans{\,\dfrac{2}{5}}$}

\problem{$\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{2}$. Across the top: $3 \times 1 = 3$.
Across the bottom: $4 \times 2 = 8$. The numerator 3 and the denominator 8 share no
factor, so it is already simplest. $\ans{\,\dfrac{3}{8}}$}

\problem{$\dfrac{5}{6} \times 4$. Write the whole number as $\dfrac{4}{1}$ so both
factors are fractions: $\dfrac{5}{6} \times \dfrac{4}{1} = \dfrac{20}{6}$. Divide top
and bottom by 2. $\ans{\,\dfrac{10}{3}}$ \ (the same as $3\tfrac{1}{3}$).}

\problem{$\dfrac{3}{8} \times \dfrac{2}{9}$. Cancel first: the 3 on top and the 9
below share 3, leaving $\dfrac{1}{8} \times \dfrac{2}{3}$; the 2 on top and the 8
below share 2, leaving $\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{3}$. Now multiply across.
$\ans{\,\dfrac{1}{12}}$}

\problem{$2\frac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{5}$. A mixed number must become an improper
fraction first: $2\frac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2 \times 2 + 1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
Then $\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{5}$ --- cancel the 5s --- $= \dfrac{3}{2}$.
$\ans{\,\dfrac{3}{2}}$}

\problem{$\dfrac{3}{4} \times x = \dfrac{9}{20}$. The missing factor is found by
dividing: $x = \dfrac{9}{20} \div \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{20} \times \dfrac{4}{3}$.
Cancel 3 into 9 and 4 into 20: $\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{1}{1}$.
Check: $\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{9}{20}$ $\checkmark$
$\ans{x = \dfrac{3}{5}}$}

\problem{$1\frac{1}{3} \times 2\frac{1}{4}$. Convert both:
$1\frac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ and $2\frac{1}{4} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.
Multiply: $\dfrac{4}{3} \times \dfrac{9}{4}$. The 4s cancel and 3 divides into 9,
leaving $\dfrac{1}{1} \times \dfrac{3}{1}$. $\ans{3}$}

\problem{12 batches, each using $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of a cup. ``Each'' with a fraction
means multiply: $12 \times \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{12}{1} \times \dfrac{3}{8}
= \dfrac{36}{8}$. Divide top and bottom by 4. $\ans{\,\dfrac{9}{2}}$ cups
(that is $4\tfrac{1}{2}$ cups).}

\problem{$\dfrac{7}{10} \times \dfrac{5}{14}$. Cancel before multiplying: 7 divides
into 14, and 5 divides into 10, leaving $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2}$.
Multiplying across gives the answer. $\ans{\,\dfrac{1}{4}}$}

\problem{Area of a rectangle is length times width, so the area is
$2\frac{3}{4} \times 1\frac{1}{2}$. Convert both to improper fractions:
$\dfrac{11}{4} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{33}{8}$. As a decimal,
$33 \div 8 = 4.125$.
$\ans{4.125 \text{ ft}^2}$

\par Watch for the common error: multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts
separately gives 2.375, which is far too small --- the rectangle is wider than 2 ft
and taller than 1 ft, so its area must exceed 2 square feet by more than that.}

\problem{$x \times 2\frac{1}{2} = 4$. Rewrite the mixed number: $2\frac{1}{2}
= \dfrac{5}{2}$, so $x \times \dfrac{5}{2} = 4$. Divide by $\dfrac{5}{2}$, which
means multiplying by its reciprocal: $x = 4 \times \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{8}{5}$.
Check: $\dfrac{8}{5} \times \dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac{40}{10} = 4$ $\checkmark$
$\ans{x = \dfrac{8}{5}}$}

\problem{Saturday: Maya walks $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the trail, so the part left over is
$1 - \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ of the trail. The trail is
$4\frac{1}{2} = \dfrac{9}{2}$ kilometres, so the leftover part is
$\dfrac{9}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ km.
Sunday she walks half of that leftover piece:
$\dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.
$\ans{\,\dfrac{3}{4} \text{ km}}$}
