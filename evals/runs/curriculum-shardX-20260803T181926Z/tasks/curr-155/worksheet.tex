\wstitleblock{Choosing a Ratio Representation}{Grade 6--7 \quad Ratios and Equivalent Ratio Tables}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Extending an equivalent-ratio table, finding a unit rate, scaling a
ratio up with a proportion, and comparing two rates to decide which is better}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item You have four tools: a ratio \textbf{table}, a \textbf{unit rate}, a
\textbf{proportion}, and a direct \textbf{comparison}. Pick one, and write
down which one you picked before you compute.
\item Equivalent ratios come from \emph{multiplying} both parts by the same
number. Adding the same number to both parts changes the ratio.
\item When two rates are being compared, put them in the same form first ---
usually ``per one of something''.
\item Round money answers to the cent.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem[6cm]{A trail-mix recipe mixes oats and raisins in the ratio
3 cups of oats to 2 cups of raisins. Complete the ratio table.

\par\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|K|V|V|V|}
\hline
\textbf{Oats (cups)}\tstrut & 3 & 6 & 9 \\ \hline
\textbf{Raisins (cups)}\tstrut & 2 & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Then use the table to say how many cups of raisins go with 12 cups of oats,
and write down what you multiplied by to get there.}

\problem[5cm]{A five-pack of notebooks costs 12.50 dollars. Find the cost of
one notebook, in dollars. Say why a unit rate is the useful representation
here.}

\problem[7cm]{A printer prints 14 pages every 4 minutes. Complete the ratio
table.

\par\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|K|V|V|V|V|}
\hline
\textbf{Minutes}\tstrut & 4 & 8 & 12 & 20 \\ \hline
\textbf{Pages}\tstrut & 14 & & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Which column did you find easiest, and why?}

\problem[5cm]{On a hiking map, 4 centimetres represents 5 kilometres. Write and
solve a proportion to find how many centimetres represent 15 kilometres.

\[ \frac{x}{15} = \frac{4}{5} \]}

\problem[6cm]{The corner store sells notebooks 5 for 12.50 dollars. The
supermarket sells the same notebooks 6 for 17.40 dollars.

Which is the better buy? Show the two rates you compared, and write the
comparison as a statement using $<$ or $>$.}

\problem[5cm]{A car travels 174 miles on 6 gallons of fuel. Find the fuel
economy in miles per gallon.}

\problem[7cm]{Sam is extending the ratio 2 cups of concentrate to 5 cups of
water. For 4 cups of concentrate he wrote 7 cups of water, ``because I added 2
to each side''.

\smallskip
(a) Explain what is wrong with adding to both parts.

\smallskip
(b) Give the correct number of cups of water for 4 cups of concentrate.}

\problem[6cm]{A soup recipe for 8 servings uses 20 ounces of tomatoes. Write
and solve a proportion for the ounces of tomatoes needed for 14 servings.

\[ \frac{x}{14} = \frac{20}{8} \]}

\problem[7cm]{Runner A covers 9 kilometres in 40 minutes. Runner B covers 7
kilometres in 32 minutes.

Who is running faster? Choose a representation, show both rates in that same
form, and write the comparison using $<$ or $>$.}

\problem[7cm]{Three gallons of paint cover 1050 square feet. A wall needs 1750
square feet of coverage.

Find the unit rate first, then use it to find how many gallons are needed.}

\problem[6cm]{Team A has won 6 of its 9 games. Team B has won 10 of its 15
games.

Which team has the better winning ratio? Be careful --- write both ratios in
the same form before you decide, and state the comparison using $<$, $>$ or
$=$.}

\problem[9cm]{\textbf{Challenge.} Two lemonade recipes: Recipe R mixes 2 cups
of concentrate with 5 cups of water. Recipe S mixes 3 cups of concentrate with
8 cups of water.

\smallskip
(a) Which recipe tastes stronger? Compare concentrate per cup of water, and
state the comparison using $<$, $>$ or $=$. \ansline

\smallskip
(b) How many cups of concentrate does Recipe R need for 35 cups of
water? \ansblank

\smallskip
(c) How many cups of concentrate does Recipe S need for 24 cups of
water? \ansblank}
