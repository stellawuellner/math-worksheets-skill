\problem{\textbf{$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, $AB = 6$, $BC = 8$,
$DE = 9$. Find $EF$.}\\
Step 1: read the correspondence off the statement: $A \leftrightarrow D$,
$B \leftrightarrow E$, $C \leftrightarrow F$, so $\overline{AB}$ pairs with
$\overline{DE}$ and $\overline{BC}$ pairs with $\overline{EF}$.
Step 2: decide the direction. $AB = 6$ grows to $DE = 9$, so $DEF$ is the larger
triangle and $EF$ must come out larger than $BC = 8$.
Step 3: set up one proportion, both ratios running small-to-large:
$\dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{8}{EF}$.
Step 4: cross-multiply: $6 \cdot EF = 72$, so $\ans{EF = 12}$.
Step 5: check against step 2 --- $12 > 8$ $\checkmark$, and
$\dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Kai's $TU$ for $\triangle PQR \sim \triangle STU$,
$PQ = 10$, $QR = 14$, $ST = 15$.}\\
Step 1: check each of Kai's ratios for direction. $\dfrac{ST}{PQ}$ runs
small-to-large is $\dfrac{15}{10}$: large over small. His other ratio
$\dfrac{QR}{TU}$ runs from triangle $PQR$ to triangle $STU$: small over large.
Step 2: name the error --- the two ratios point in opposite directions, so his
equation says the large triangle is both bigger and smaller than the small one.
Written consistently it must be $\dfrac{ST}{PQ} = \dfrac{TU}{QR}$.
Step 3: since $ST = 15$ is longer than $PQ = 10$, triangle $STU$ is the larger
one and $TU$ must exceed $QR = 14$ --- Kai's $9.33$ is already impossible.
Step 4: solve the corrected proportion:
$TU = 14 \cdot \dfrac{15}{10} = \ans{21}$.
Step 5: check: $\dfrac{15}{10} = \dfrac{21}{14} = 1.5$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle FED$, $AB = 12$,
$BC = 9$, $FE = 8$. Find $ED$.}\\
Step 1: read the correspondence in the order written: $A \leftrightarrow F$,
$B \leftrightarrow E$, $C \leftrightarrow D$. So $\overline{AB}$ pairs with
$\overline{FE}$ and $\overline{BC}$ pairs with $\overline{ED}$ --- Rho's pairing
is right.
Step 2: find the direction. $AB = 12$ corresponds to $FE = 8$, so the second
triangle is \emph{smaller}: the scale factor from $ABC$ to $FED$ is
$\dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, a number less than $1$.
Step 3: name the error --- Rho used $\dfrac{12}{8}$, the factor for going the
other way. Multiplying by a factor greater than $1$ makes the side of the smaller
triangle longer than the side it came from, which cannot happen.
Step 4: $ED = 9 \cdot \dfrac{8}{12} = \ans{6}$.
Step 5: check: $\dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{6}{9}$ $\checkmark$, and $6 < 9$
$\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Sides in ratio $5 : 8$, smaller area $50$ square centimetres.
Find the larger area.}\\
Step 1: name what Rae scaled incorrectly --- she applied the \emph{side} factor
$\frac{8}{5}$ to an \emph{area}. Lengths scale by $k$; areas scale by $k^2$,
because an area is a product of two lengths and each one is multiplied by $k$.
Step 2: the linear factor from smaller to larger is $k = \dfrac{8}{5} = 1.6$, so
the area factor is $k^2 = \dfrac{64}{25} = 2.56$.
Step 3: multiply: $50 \times 2.56 = \ans{128}$ square centimetres.
Step 4: check the size: $128$ is more than twice $50$, which is right for sides
that grew by $60\%$; Rae's $80$ is only $1.6$ times the original area.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: person $5$ ft with a $4$ ft shadow, flagpole with a
$22$ ft shadow. Find the flagpole's height $h$.}\\
Step 1: state the correspondence in words. In each right triangle, the height is
one leg and the shadow is the other, so heights correspond to heights and shadows
correspond to shadows.
Step 2: locate Ines's error. In her $\dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{22}{h}$, the left side
is height over shadow, but on the right she put the flagpole's \emph{shadow} on
top and its height underneath --- shadow over height. She paired the flagpole's
shadow ($22$) with the person's height ($5$).
Step 3: write it with both ratios in the same order, height over shadow:
$\dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{h}{22}$.
Step 4: solve: $h = 22 \cdot \dfrac{5}{4} = \ans{27.5}$ ft.
Step 5: check the scale: the flagpole's shadow is $5.5$ times the person's, so its
height must be $5.5$ times $5$ ft $\checkmark$. Ines's $17.6$ ft is barely three
times the person's height while the shadow is over five times as long.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: $\triangle JKL \sim \triangle MNP$, $JK = 15$,
$MN = 9$, $KL = 20$. Diagnose Tomas's $NP$ and find the correct value.}\\
\emph{Parts (a) and (b) are open explanations --- flagged for manual review.
Model answers below; part (c) is machine-verified.}\\
\textbf{(a)} $JK = 15$ corresponds to $MN = 9$, so $\triangle MNP$ is the
\emph{smaller} triangle. Every side of $MNP$ must therefore be shorter than the
side of $JKL$ it matches. $NP$ corresponds to $KL = 20$, so $NP$ has to be less
than $20$ --- Tomas's $33.33$ is longer, so it is impossible no matter what the
arithmetic says.\\
\textbf{(b)} A usable rule: put the side of the triangle you are going \emph{to}
on top, and its matching side in the triangle you are coming \emph{from}
underneath:
\[ k = \frac{\text{going to}}{\text{coming from}} \]
Going to the smaller triangle that fraction is less than $1$; going to the larger
it is greater than $1$. Decide which before multiplying, then check the answer
against that decision.\\
\textbf{(c)} Step 1: the factor from $JKL$ to $MNP$ is
$\dfrac{MN}{JK} = \dfrac{9}{15} = 0.6$, which is less than $1$ as part (a)
requires.
Step 2: $NP = KL \cdot \dfrac{9}{15} = 20 \cdot 0.6 = \ans{12}$.
Step 3: check: $\dfrac{9}{15} = \dfrac{12}{20}$ $\checkmark$, and $12 < 20$
$\checkmark$. Tomas's $33.33$ is exactly $20 \div 0.6$ --- the signature of a
scale factor used upside down.\\
\emph{Full credit on (a) and (b) needs:} $MNP$ named as the smaller triangle,
with the pair $15$ and $9$ given as the reason; the conclusion that $NP$ must be
under $20$; and a rule that fixes the \emph{direction} of the factor, not a
restatement of the arithmetic.}
