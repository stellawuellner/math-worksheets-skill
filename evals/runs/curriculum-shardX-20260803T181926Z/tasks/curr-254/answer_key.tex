\problem{\textbf{Combine like terms:} $7x^2 + 3x - 5x^2 + 8x - 2$.

\textbf{Solution:} Group terms with the identical variable part, keeping each
coefficient's sign attached to the term in front of it:
\[ (7x^2 - 5x^2) + (3x + 8x) - 2 \]
The $x^2$ terms give $2x^2$, the $x$ terms give $11x$, and the constant is
unchanged. \ans{2x^2 + 11x - 2}

\noindent The $x^2$ group and the $x$ group are never merged: $2x^2 + 11x$ is
already as simple as it gets.}

\problem{\textbf{Subtract:} $(5x^2 - 3x + 4) - (2x^2 + 7x - 6)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Distribute the subtraction sign across all three terms of the
second polynomial first --- this is the line worth writing every time:
\[ 5x^2 - 3x + 4 - 2x^2 - 7x + 6 \]
Now combine: $5x^2 - 2x^2 = 3x^2$; $-3x - 7x = -10x$; $4 + 6 = 10$.
\ans{3x^2 - 10x + 10}

\noindent Note the constant grew from $4$ to $10$. Subtracting $-6$ adds $6$,
which is exactly the term students most often leave alone.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply:} $-3x(2x^2 - 4x + 5)$.

\textbf{Solution:} Multiply each term of the trinomial by $-3x$, tracking both
the sign and the exponent:
\[ (-3x)(2x^2) = -6x^3, \qquad (-3x)(-4x) = +12x^2, \qquad (-3x)(5) = -15x \]
\ans{-6x^3 + 12x^2 - 15x}

\noindent The middle term turns positive because a negative times a negative is
positive. A sign pattern that alternates like this is normal, not a warning
sign.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} $(4x^2 - 6x + 1) - (x^2 - 2x - 5)$.

\textbf{The mistake:} the subtraction sign was applied to $x^2$ only. The
student's second and third terms, $-2x$ and $-5$, were copied down unchanged,
so they wrote $-6x - 2x = -8x$ and $1 - 5 = -4$.

\textbf{Correct work:} multiply every term of the second polynomial by $-1$:
\[ 4x^2 - 6x + 1 - x^2 + 2x + 5 \]
Then $4x^2 - x^2 = 3x^2$; $-6x + 2x = -4x$; $1 + 5 = 6$.
\ans{3x^2 - 4x + 6}

\textbf{Check at} $x = 2$: $3(4) - 4(2) + 6 = 12 - 8 + 6$. \ans{10}

\noindent The student's expression at $x = 2$ gives $-8$, so the two are not the
same polynomial and the substitution settles it in one line.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} $-2x(3x - 7) + 5(x - 4)$.

\textbf{The mistake:} the middle term. $(-2x)(-7) = +14x$, not $-14x$; the
student carried the $7$'s minus sign through instead of letting the two
negatives cancel. That turned $14x + 5x = 19x$ into $-14x + 5x = -9x$.

\textbf{Correct work:}
\[ (-2x)(3x) = -6x^2, \quad (-2x)(-7) = +14x, \quad 5(x) = 5x, \quad 5(-4) = -20 \]
so the expansion is $-6x^2 + 14x + 5x - 20$. \ans{-6x^2 + 19x - 20}

\textbf{Check at} $x = 1$: $-6 + 19 - 20$. \ans{-7}

\noindent The student's version gives $-35$ at $x = 1$ --- a gap of $28$, which
is exactly twice the $14x$ term that was mis-signed.}

\problem{\textbf{Factor} $x^2 - 5x - 24$ \textbf{and check.}

\textbf{Solution:} We need two numbers whose product is $-24$ and whose sum is
$-5$. A negative product means the signs differ, and the sum is negative, so the
number with the larger absolute value carries the minus: $-8$ and $+3$, since
$(-8)(3) = -24$ and $-8 + 3 = -5$. \ans{(x - 8)(x + 3)}

\textbf{Check at} $x = 1$: the factored form gives $(1 - 8)(1 + 3) = (-7)(4)$,
and the original gives $1 - 5 - 24$. Both equal $-28$. \ans{-28}

\noindent A single substitution catches a swapped pair immediately: the wrong
choice $(x + 8)(x - 3)$ gives $-18$ at $x = 1$, not $-28$.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix:} is $(2x - 3)^2$ the same as $4x^2 + 9$?

\textbf{Decision:} no. Squaring a binomial is a product of two identical
factors, so the cross terms are part of the answer:
\[ (2x - 3)^2 = (2x - 3)(2x - 3) = 4x^2 - 6x - 6x + 9 \]
\ans{4x^2 - 12x + 9}

\textbf{Check at} $x = 1$: the correct expansion gives $4 - 12 + 9$. \ans{1}

\noindent The student's $4x^2 + 9$ gives $13$ at $x = 1$. The whole difference
is the missing $-12x$, and it never vanishes: only $x = 0$ makes the two
expressions agree, which is why testing at $x = 0$ is a poor check.}

\problem{\textbf{Explain: two claims to correct.}
\textit{(Open response --- flagged for manual review; a model answer follows.)}

\textbf{Model answer:} The first claim is a half-remembered version of the real
rule. Subtracting a polynomial means multiplying the entire polynomial by $-1$
before adding, so every term inside the parentheses changes sign, not just the
leading one. Writing the "add the opposite" line explicitly --- turning
$-(x^2 - 2x - 5)$ into $-x^2 + 2x + 5$ --- makes the rule visible instead of
remembered.

\noindent The second claim confuses adding coefficients with adding terms.
$3x^2$ and $5x$ have different variable parts, so they are not like terms and
cannot be merged at all. Substituting $x = 2$ proves it: $3(4) + 5(2) = 22$,
while $8x^2$ gives $8(4) = 32$. One counterexample is enough to end the
argument, and $x = 1$ would not have worked, since both sides give $8$ there.

\noindent Two terms may be combined only when their variable parts are
identical --- the same variables raised to the same powers. Coefficients are
added; exponents are never touched.

\noindent\textit{What a reviewer should look for:} the subtraction rule stated
as multiplying every term by $-1$, a substitution that actually separates the
two expressions, and a definition of like terms that mentions the variable part
rather than the coefficients.}
