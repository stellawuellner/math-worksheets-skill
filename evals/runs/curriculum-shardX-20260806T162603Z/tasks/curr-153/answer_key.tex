\problem{Tape diagram: 2 juice boxes and 3 water boxes, each box worth 4 liters.
How many liters of juice?}

\textbf{Solution:} The juice tape has 2 boxes and every box stands for 4 liters,
so juice $= 2 \times 4 = 8$ liters. \ans{8\ \text{L}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Hose fills 10 liters every 2 minutes. Complete the double number line
at 4 and 6 minutes.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each 2-minute step adds 10 liters, so the marks go up by 10:

(a) at 4 minutes: $10 \times 2 = 20$ liters. \ans{20}

(b) at 6 minutes: $10 \times 3 = 30$ liters. \ans{30}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Maria and Josh share 35 dollars in the ratio $4{:}3$. How much does
Maria receive?}

\textbf{Solution:} The tape has $4 + 3 = 7$ equal boxes for 35 dollars, so one
box is worth $35 \div 7 = 5$ dollars. Maria's tape has 4 boxes:
$4 \times 5 = 20$ dollars. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Sugar to flour is 2 to 5. Complete the sugar row for 10, 15, and 20
cups of flour.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each flour step of 5 pairs with a sugar step of 2. For 10
cups of flour the multiplier is 2, so sugar $= 2 \times 2 = 4$. \ans{4}

For 15 cups the multiplier is 3: sugar $= 2 \times 3 = 6$. \ans{6}

For 20 cups the multiplier is 4: sugar $= 2 \times 4 = 8$. \ans{8}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Ana and Ben have stickers in the ratio $5{:}2$, and Ana has 12 more.
How many does Ana have?}

\textbf{Solution:} Ana's tape shows $5 - 2 = 3$ more boxes than Ben's, and those
3 extra boxes hold the 12 extra stickers, so one box $= 12 \div 3 = 4$ stickers.
Ana has 5 boxes: $5 \times 4 = 20$ stickers. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Apples cost 6 dollars for 4 pounds. Find the money marks for 1 pound
and 10 pounds.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Divide both quantities by 4 to reach one pound:
$6 \div 4 = 1.5$ dollars per pound. \ans{1.50}

(b) Multiply the one-pound mark by 10: $1.5 \times 10 = 15$ dollars. \ans{15}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Fiction to nonfiction is $7{:}5$, and the 5 nonfiction boxes hold 45
books. How many fiction books?}

\textbf{Solution:} One box is worth $45 \div 5 = 9$ books. The fiction tape has
7 boxes: $7 \times 9 = 63$ books. \ans{63}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{A 91-cm rope is cut in the ratio $9{:}4$. How much longer is the long
piece?}

\textbf{Solution:} The full tape has $9 + 4 = 13$ boxes for 91 cm, so one box is
$91 \div 13 = 7$ cm. The long piece has $9 - 4 = 5$ more boxes than the short
piece, so the difference is $5 \times 7 = 35$ cm. \ans{35\ \text{cm}}

(A common slip is reporting the long piece itself, $9 \times 7 = 63$ cm, instead
of the difference.)

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{27 km in 90 minutes at a steady pace. Find the distance for 10 minutes
and for 50 minutes.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Divide both quantities by 9 to get a 10-minute step:
$27 \div 9 = 3$ km every 10 minutes. \ans{3}

(b) 50 minutes is five 10-minute steps: $3 \times 5 = 15$ km. \ans{15}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Berries to yogurt is $3{:}2$; the batch uses 12 cups of berries. How
many cups of yogurt? Explain how the tape helped.}

\textbf{Solution:} The 3 berry boxes hold 12 cups, so one box is worth
$12 \div 3 = 4$ cups. The yogurt tape has 2 boxes: $2 \times 4 = 8$ cups.
\ans{8}

\textbf{Explanation (instructor judges):} Full credit for one sentence that
finds the value of one box from the berries ($12 \div 3 = 4$ cups per box) and
then counts the 2 yogurt boxes ($2 \times 4 = 8$ cups) — the diagram turns the
ratio into equal boxes you can count.
