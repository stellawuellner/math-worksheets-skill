# Find and Fix: Legs, Hypotenuses, and the Pythagorean Theorem — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_pythag_curr244.pdf`, 4 pages) — 8 problems built around one
  confusion: which given side is the hypotenuse, and therefore whether the squares get
  added or subtracted. Three are full find-and-fix items (problems 3, 5 and 8) where a
  named student's wrong work is printed, and problem 6 pushes on the same idea from the
  algebra side by asking which root of `x² + 81 = 225` can be a length. A value-free
  reference triangle sits with the directions to fix the `a`/`b`/`c` labelling; no
  problem carries a figure of its own, so no diagram can be applied to the wrong
  question.
- **Answer key** (`ak_pythag_curr244.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a **Common wrong answers** block, a curriculum block, and a worked
  solution per problem that names which side is the hypotenuse before doing any
  arithmetic.
- **Study guide** (`ss_pythag_curr244.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: finding the
  hypotenuse (add), finding a leg (subtract), and checking someone else's substitution,
  each with a rule box, a worked example and a try-it.

## Machine-checked misconception traps

Every wrong number printed on the sheet is declared in the verification data and
recomputed, then checked to be *distinguishably* wrong:

| Problem | Planted wrong answer | The error |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 21 | added the legs instead of their squares |
| 2 | 25.96 | added the squares when the hypotenuse was given |
| 3 | 13.93 | Jamal substituted the hypotenuse 13 into a leg slot |
| 4 | 5.29 | subtracted the coordinate gaps' squares instead of adding |
| 5 | 18.79 | Priya treated the ladder as a leg |
| 7 | 21.82 | subtracted, treating the diagonal as a side |
| 8 | 35.23 | Marisa put the hypotenuse 29 into a leg slot |

Notice that four of those wrong answers come out *longer than the hypotenuse* — that
size check is what the study guide's third section teaches, and it is the shortcut
problem 8 asks the student to articulate.

## What is verified

**4 of the 8 problems are fully machine-checked** (problems 1, 2, 4 and 7). The other
four — 3, 5, 6 and 8 — are **partly instructor-judged**: in each, the numeric answer is
machine-checked and the written diagnosis is not.

| Problem | Machine-checked | Instructor-judged |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | the corrected length, 12 cm | the description of Jamal's error |
| 5 | the corrected height, 15 ft | the description of Priya's error |
| 6 | both roots, −12 and 12 | which root can be a length, and why |
| 8 | the correct leg, 21 cm | how you can tell Marisa is wrong without arithmetic |

That is the same split printed in the key's own "What is verified" note, and every
instructor-judged half carries a rubric in the key.
