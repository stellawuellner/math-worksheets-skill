\wstitleblock{Compositions of Rigid Motions and Symmetry}{Geometry}{}

\begin{center}\small\textit{Carrying out a two-step composition of rigid motions, naming the
single transformation that does the same job, and describing the line and rotational symmetry
of a figure}\end{center}

\noindent\small\textbf{How to use this sheet.}
\begin{itemize}[label={$\bullet$}, leftmargin=1.1cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item A \emph{composition} is one transformation followed by another. Order matters: do the
        first one to the original figure, then do the second one to \emph{that} image.
  \item Translations, reflections and rotations are \emph{rigid motions}: every length, every
        angle and every area survives them untouched. That is what makes the checks in
        problems 2, 3 and 6 worth doing.
  \item A composition of two rigid motions is itself a single rigid motion, so it always has a
        one-step name. Two reflections in parallel mirrors give a translation; two reflections
        in intersecting mirrors give a rotation about the point where they cross.
  \item A figure has a \emph{symmetry} when some rigid motion maps it exactly onto itself ---
        a reflection in a line of symmetry, or a rotation about its centre.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
