\begin{center}
{\small\textit{Using the rational root theorem to find zeros}}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\textbf{Directions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item Every rational zero of a polynomial has the form $\pm \dfrac{p}{q}$,
    where $p$ divides the constant term and $q$ divides the leading coefficient.
  \item The theorem gives \emph{candidates}, not answers. Test them --- by
    substitution or synthetic division --- until one gives $0$.
  \item Once a zero is found, divide it out and finish with the smaller
    polynomial that is left. Keep going until every zero is accounted for.
  \item Where a problem says \emph{all} zeros, count them: a degree-$n$
    polynomial has $n$ zeros.
\end{itemize}
