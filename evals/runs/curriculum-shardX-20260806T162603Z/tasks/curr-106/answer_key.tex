\problem{$14 \times 23$ with the model split $10+4$ and $20+3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each cell multiplies one part of each factor:
$10 \times 20 = \ans{200}$, \ $10 \times 3 = \ans{30}$, \
$4 \times 20 = \ans{80}$, \ $4 \times 3 = \ans{12}$.

Add the cells: $200 + 30 + 80 + 12 = 322$, so $14 \times 23 = \ans{322}$

\problem{$26 \times 31$ with the model split $20+6$ and $30+1$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $20 \times 30 = \ans{600}$, \ $20 \times 1 = \ans{20}$, \
$6 \times 30 = \ans{180}$, \ $6 \times 1 = \ans{6}$.

Add: $600 + 20 + 180 + 6 = 806$, so $26 \times 31 = \ans{806}$

\problem{Fill in the splits for $47 \times 35$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A two-digit number splits into its tens and its ones:
$47 = 40 + 7$, so the first blank is \ans{7}. Likewise $35 = 30 + 5$: \ans{5}

\problem{Label the model for $24 \times 15$ and find the product.}

\textbf{Solution:} Split $24 = 20 + 4$ and $15 = 10 + 5$. The four cells are
$20 \times 10 = 200$, $20 \times 5 = 100$, $4 \times 10 = 40$,
$4 \times 5 = 20$. Sum: $200 + 100 + 40 + 20 = 360$, so
$24 \times 15 = \ans{360}$

A correctly filled model shows exactly those four cells.

\problem{$32 \times 28$: the four cells and their total.}

\textbf{Solution:} Split $32 = 30 + 2$ and $28 = 20 + 8$:
$30 \times 20 = \ans{600}$, \ $30 \times 8 = \ans{240}$, \
$2 \times 20 = \ans{40}$, \ $2 \times 8 = \ans{16}$.

Total: $600 + 240 + 40 + 16 = 896$, so $32 \times 28 = \ans{896}$

\problem{Estimate $38 \times 21$ by rounding, then compute exactly.}

\textbf{Solution:} Round each factor to the nearest ten: 38 rounds to 40 and
21 rounds to 20, so the estimate is $40 \times 20 = \ans{800}$

Exact, by the model ($30+8$ and $20+1$): $600 + 30 + 160 + 8 = 798$:
$38 \times 21 = \ans{798}$ --- close to the estimate, so it is reasonable.

\problem{Rows 10 and 3; cells 240 and 72; unknown shared side.}

\textbf{Solution:} The top cell says $10 \times \text{side} = 240$, so the side
is $240 \div 10 = 24$. \ans{24}

Check with the bottom cell: $72 \div 3 = \ans{24}$ --- both cells agree, so the
side is 24.

\problem{A patio is 34 tiles long and 26 tiles wide. How many tiles?}

\textbf{Solution:} Split $34 = 30 + 4$ and $26 = 20 + 6$. Cells:
$30 \times 20 = 600$, $30 \times 6 = 180$, $4 \times 20 = 80$,
$4 \times 6 = 24$. Sum: $600 + 180 + 80 + 24 = 884$ tiles.
$34 \times 26 = \ans{884}$

\problem{Elena computes $45 \times 22$ and writes 900.}

\textbf{Solution:} Split $22 = 20 + 2$. The model has two cells:
$45 \times 20 = 900$ and $45 \times 2 = 90$. Elena stopped after the first
cell. The full product is $900 + 90 = 990$: \ans{990}

Full-credit explanation: the model for $\times\,22$ has a tens cell
\emph{and} a ones cell; Elena dropped the $45 \times 2$ cell.

\problem{Challenge: $63 \times 47$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Split $63 = 60 + 3$ and $47 = 40 + 7$:
$60 \times 40 = \ans{2400}$, \ $60 \times 7 = \ans{420}$, \
$3 \times 40 = \ans{120}$, \ $3 \times 7 = \ans{21}$.

Total: $2400 + 420 + 120 + 21 = 2961$, so $63 \times 47 = \ans{2961}$
