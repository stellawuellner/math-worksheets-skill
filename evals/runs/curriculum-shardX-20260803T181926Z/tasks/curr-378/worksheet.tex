\wstitleblock{Solving Exponential and Logarithmic Equations}{Algebra 2 \quad Exponential and Logarithmic Functions}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Solving exponential equations with logarithms, solving logarithmic
equations by rewriting them in exponential form, and using the logarithm laws
to rewrite an equation before solving it}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Before you start.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item A logarithm is an exponent: $\log_b M = k$ and $b^k = M$ say the same
thing. Every problem here is one of those two sentences rewritten as the other.
\item When the unknown sits in the exponent and both sides can be written with
the same base, match the exponents. When they cannot, take a logarithm of both
sides and use $\log(M^k) = k\log M$.
\item Change of base: $\log_b M = \dfrac{\log M}{\log b}$, which is what makes
a calculator answer possible.
\item A logarithm is only defined for a positive argument, so every candidate
root of a logarithmic equation must be checked in the original equation.
\item Give a decimal answer only where the problem asks for one, and state the
unit it carries.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{A culture of yeast triples in size every hour, and it is measured as a
multiple of its starting size. The model is $3^x = M$, where $x$ is the number
of hours elapsed. After how many hours is the culture $81$ times its starting
size?
\par\vspace*{4cm}\answerline{hours}}

\problem{A bacterial population doubles once per day, so after $x$ days it is
$2^x$ times its starting size. Solve $2^x = 5$ to find when the population
reaches $5$ times its starting size. Give the answer to the nearest
thousandth of a day.
\par\vspace*{4cm}\answerline{days}}

\problem[4.5cm]{A doubling-time model records the elapsed time in hours as
$t = \log_2 n$, where $n$ is the current size as a multiple of the starting
size. Rewrite the equation in exponential form and find $n$ when $t = 5$.}

\problem[4.5cm]{Rewrite $\log_3(9x^4)$ as a sum of a number and a multiple of
$\log_3 x$, for $x > 0$. Show which logarithm law you use at each step.}

\problem{A radioactive sample has a mass of $200$ grams and a half-life of $6$
years, so its mass after $t$ years is $200\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6}$
grams. After how many years does $25$ grams remain?
\par\vspace*{4cm}\answerline{years}}

\problem{An account opens with $1500$, in dollars, and grows by four percent
each year, so the balance is multiplied by $1.04$ annually and after $t$ years
it is $1500(1.04)^t$ dollars. After how many years does the balance reach
$2000$ dollars? Give the answer to the nearest hundredth of a year.
\par\vspace*{4.5cm}\answerline{years}}

\problem[5cm]{Solve $\log x + \log(x - 3) = 1$, where $\log$ is the common
logarithm, base $10$. Condense the left side first, then rewrite in exponential
form. State every candidate root and say which one the equation rejects, and
why.}

\problem[4.5cm]{Condense $2\log_2 x - \log_2 3$ into a single logarithm with
base $2$, for $x > 0$. This is the step that turns an equation of that shape
into one you can rewrite in exponential form.}

\problem[5cm]{To solve $4^{x+1} = 32$, Dev divided $32$ by $4$ and set $x + 1$
equal to the quotient. Explain in one sentence why dividing the two numbers is
not a legal step here, then solve the equation correctly by writing both sides
as powers of $2$.}

\problem[5cm]{Solve $2\log x = \log(x + 6)$, where $\log$ is the common
logarithm. Condense the left side, rewrite in exponential form, and then state
which of the two candidate roots the equation rejects and why.}
