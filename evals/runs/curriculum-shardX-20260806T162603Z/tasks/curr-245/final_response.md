# Multistep Spatial and Coordinate Problems — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_spatial_curr245.pdf`, 7 pages) — 12 problems that interleave four
  methods after a three-problem warm-up: a missing side of a right triangle, a distance
  on a coordinate grid, a diagonal inside a box (done in two stages), and a multistep
  application where the Pythagorean step is only the first move — a saving, a perimeter,
  a total length of edging. A value-free labelled prism sits with the directions so the
  box problems share one convention for `d` (base diagonal) and `D` (box diagonal); no
  problem carries a diagram of its own, so no figure can be applied to the wrong
  question.
- **Answer key** (`ak_spatial_curr245.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem that states
  which side is the hypotenuse before any arithmetic.
- **Study guide** (`ss_spatial_curr245.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections matching the four
  methods, each with a rule box, a worked example and a try-it.

## What is verified

**8 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy — 27 individual checks,
because most problems ask for two, three or four values and every printed blank has its
own check. Four problems are **partly instructor-judged**:

| Problem | Machine-checked | Instructor-judged |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | both roots of `x² + 576 = 625` (−7 and 7) | which root is the leg, and why |
| 8 | the space diagonal, 26.57 cm | whether the 26 cm pencil fits, and the comparison that settles it |
| 10 | all three sides (5, 5, 7.07) | the isosceles conclusion drawn from them |
| 12 | support 13 m, perimeter 34 m, total 47 m | why the support is shorter than half the perimeter |

That is the same split the key's own "What is verified" note prints, and each
instructor-judged half carries a rubric in the key.

## Notes for you

- The sheet runs 7 pages because the work space is real: several problems have two or
  three parts and need room for a two-stage calculation. Nothing was compressed to save
  paper.
- Problem 8 is the one worth talking about: a 26 cm pencil in a box whose longest edge
  is 20 cm still fits, because the space diagonal is 26.57 cm. Students who compare the
  pencil with an edge rather than the diagonal get it wrong.
- Standards covered: 8.G.B.7 (problems 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11) and 8.G.B.8 (problems
  2, 5, 10, 12).
- Answers carry the unit named in the question — centimetres or metres — printed on the
  answer line. Pure coordinate problems answer in grid units.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
