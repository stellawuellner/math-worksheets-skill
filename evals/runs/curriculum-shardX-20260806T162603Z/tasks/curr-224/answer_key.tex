\problem{$C = 12x + 40$ dollars for $x$ months. (a) Slope. (b) $y$-intercept.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} One more month changes the cost from $12x + 40$ to
$12(x + 1) + 40$, a difference of 12 dollars. That is the number multiplying
$x$. \ans{12}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Substitute $x = 0$: the $12x$ term vanishes and 40 is
left. \ans{40}

\problem{Line through $(0, 4)$ and $(5, 19)$; Tomas wrote $y = 4x + 3$.
(a) Slope. (b) $y$ at $x = 2$ from the correct equation. (c) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{19 - 4}{5 - 0} = \dfrac{15}{5}$. \ans{m = 3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The point $(0, 4)$ is already on the $y$-axis, so the
$y$-intercept is 4 and the correct equation is $y = 3x + 4$. At $x = 2$:
$3(2) + 4$. \ans{y = 10}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} The two numbers are in each
other's places. Because 4 is the value of $y$ when $x$ is 0, it is the
$y$-intercept; 3 is the amount $y$ grows for each unit of $x$, so it is the
slope. His equation would give $y = 11$ at $x = 2$, one more than the true
value --- a small gap here, but the same swap is catastrophic in problems 4
and 8. A correct response names which number belongs where.

\problem{Nia says $y = 7x + 2$ crosses the $y$-axis at 7 and rises 2 per unit.
(a) Crossing value. (b) Rise per unit. (c) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Substitute $x = 0$: $7(0) + 2$. \ans{2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Going from $x$ to $x + 1$ adds 7 to $y$. \ans{7}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} She has the two roles reversed.
The number multiplying $x$ is the rise per unit across; the number added on is
the height where the graph meets the vertical axis. Substituting $x = 0$ is the
quickest way to show it. A pair of corrected numbers with no statement of which
role each plays does not earn full credit.

\problem{Phone plan: \$25 at 0 GB and \$45 at 5 GB; Reza wrote $C = 25g + 4$.
(a) Cost per gigabyte. (b) Cost at 8 GB from the correct equation.
(c) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{45 - 25}{5 - 0} = \dfrac{20}{5}$. \ans{m = 4}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The cost at 0 GB is 25, so the correct equation is
$C = 4g + 25$. At $g = 8$: $4(8) + 25$. \ans{57}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} 25 dollars is what the plan costs
before any data is used, so it is the constant; 4 dollars is the extra cost per
gigabyte, so it multiplies $g$. His equation predicts 204 dollars for 8
gigabytes, which is more than four times the true cost. A correct response
names which number belongs where; testing the equation at 0 GB is valid
evidence.

\problem{Taxi table: miles 0, 2, 4, 6 against charges \$3, \$11, \$19, \$27.
Kai says $y = 3x + 4$. (a) Charge at 0 miles. (b) Charge per mile.
(c) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Read the first column of the table. \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} From the first column to the last, the charge rises
$27 - 3 = 24$ dollars over $6 - 0 = 6$ miles, so $\dfrac{24}{6}$.
\ans{m = 4}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} The table's first row gives the
charge at zero miles, which belongs as the constant; the step from row to row
gives the charge per mile, which multiplies the variable. Kai has them the
wrong way round, and his equation fails at every row of the table --- at 2
miles it gives 10 dollars, not 11. A correct response names the roles; a
corrected equation on its own does not.

\problem{Candle: $h = 20 - 3t$ centimetres; Lena wrote $h = 3 - 20t$.
(a) Height after 4 hours. (b) The error.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $20 - 3(4) = 20 - 12$. \ans{8}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} 20 cm is the height before any
burning, so it is the constant; 3 cm per hour is the loss rate, so it
multiplies $t$. Her version gives $3 - 20(4) = -77$ cm after four hours, and a
candle cannot have a negative height --- valid supporting evidence, but the
roles of the two numbers must be named for full credit.

\problem{$y = 5x + 2$ and $y = 2x + 5$. (a) Compare slopes. (b) Compare
$y$-intercepts. (c) Why the lines differ.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The slopes are 5 and 2, first line then second.
\ans{5 > 2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The $y$-intercepts are 2 and 5, in the same order.
\ans{2 < 5}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} Position decides the role. The
number multiplying $x$ sets the steepness and the number added on sets where
the line starts, so exchanging them exchanges a steep line starting low for a
shallow line starting high. The two graphs cross at $(1, 7)$ and go different
ways from there. ``The numbers are in different places'' with no account of
what each place means is not enough.

\problem{Savings: \$60 at week 0 and \$132 at week 8; Marcus wrote
$y = 60x + 9$. (a) Amount saved per week. (b) Balance at week 10.
(c) Why his prediction is far too large.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\dfrac{132 - 60}{8 - 0} = \dfrac{72}{8}$. \ans{m = 9}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The balance at week 0 is 60, so the correct equation is
$y = 9x + 60$. At $x = 10$: $9(10) + 60$. \ans{150}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} An estimate settles it without
computing his value: the account gained 72 dollars in the first eight weeks,
so two more weeks add only about twenty more --- nothing near the hundreds.
His equation would give 609 dollars, which is roughly four times the true
balance. Accept any rough argument of this shape; a full recomputation
presented as the whole answer misses the point of the question.
