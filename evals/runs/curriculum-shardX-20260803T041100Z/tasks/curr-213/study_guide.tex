\sstitleblock{One Solution, No Solution, or Infinitely Many}

\noindent\small The method never changes: \textbf{distribute, combine like terms, then
collect the variable on one side}. What is left when the variable terms cancel (or fail
to cancel) tells you which of the three cases you are in.\normalsize

\skillheading{Skill 1 --- One solution: the variable survives}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Sign:} after simplifying, the two sides have \emph{different}
$x$-terms (for example $5x$ against $3x$).\\[3pt]
Subtracting the smaller $x$-term leaves a real equation to finish, and exactly one value
of $x$ makes it true. In a context, the two quantities are equal at exactly one moment.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad Classify and solve $5x + 2 = 3x + 10$.
\step{The $x$-terms differ ($5x$ against $3x$), so the variable cannot cancel --- expect exactly one solution.}
\step{Subtract $3x$: $2x + 2 = 10$. Subtract 2: $2x = 8$. Divide by 2: $\ans{x = 4}$ --- one solution.}
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad Classify and solve $7x - 4 = 2x + 11$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: one solution, $\ans{x = 3}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- No solution: a false statement is left}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Sign:} after simplifying, the $x$-terms are \emph{identical} but the
constants are \emph{different}.\\[3pt]
The variable cancels completely and leaves something false, such as $12 = 7$. No value of
$x$ can fix a false statement, so the equation has no solution. In a context, two
quantities that change at the same rate but start apart never meet.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad Classify $3(x + 4) = 3x + 7$.
\step{Distribute first --- only then can the $x$-terms on the two sides be compared.}
\step{$3x + 12 = 3x + 7$. Subtract $3x$: $12 = 7$, which is false, so $\ans{\text{no solution}}$.}
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad Classify $5x - 2 = 5x + 6$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{\text{no solution}}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Infinitely many: a true statement is left}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Sign:} after simplifying, the two sides are the \emph{same
expression} --- same $x$-term \emph{and} same constant.\\[3pt]
The variable cancels and leaves something true, such as $0 = 0$. Every value of $x$ works.
In a context, the two expressions were two ways of writing the same quantity.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad Classify $4(2x - 3) = 8x - 12$.
\step{The right side looks like the expanded left side, so expand and compare rather than solving.}
\step{$4(2x - 3) = 8x - 12$, so the equation reads $\ans{8x-12=8x-12}$ --- true for every $x$, infinitely many solutions.}
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad Classify $-3(x - 5) = 15 - 3x$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{15-3x=15-3x}$, infinitely many}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 4 --- Building an equation to order}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Working backwards:} to force a case, control the two sides.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{No solution:} make the $x$-terms match, the constants differ.
  \item \textbf{Infinitely many:} make the $x$-terms \emph{and} the constants match.
  \item \textbf{One solution:} make the $x$-terms differ (the constants do not matter).
\end{itemize}
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad Find $a$ so that $5(2x - 3) = ax + 1$ has no solution.
\step{No solution means the $x$-terms must match, so expand the left side and read off its $x$-term.}
\step{$5(2x - 3)$ expands to $\ans{10x-15,\ \text{so } a = 10}$; the constants $-15$ and 1 differ, so the leftover statement is false.}
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad Find $a$ so that $4(3x + 2) = ax + 5$ has no solution.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{12x+8,\ \text{so } a = 12}$}\end{tryitbox}
