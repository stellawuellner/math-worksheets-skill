\problem{Write 46 in expanded form.}

\textbf{Solution:} 46 has 4 tens and 6 ones. The 4 tens are worth $40$ and the
6 ones are worth $6$, so $46 = {}$ \ans{40 + 6}

\problem{Write the number in standard form: $300 + 20 + 9$}

\textbf{Solution:} Put each part in its place: 3 hundreds, 2 tens, 9 ones.
Reading the places together gives \ans{329}

\problem{Write 573 in expanded form.}

\textbf{Solution:} 573 has 5 hundreds ($500$), 7 tens ($70$), and 3 ones
($3$), so $573 = {}$ \ans{500 + 70 + 3}

\problem{Write 285 in word form.}

\textbf{Solution:} Say the hundreds, then the rest: 2 hundreds is ``two
hundred''; 85 is ``eighty-five''. Model answer: \emph{two hundred
eighty-five} (hyphen optional; no ``and'').

\problem{Write the number in standard form: six hundred forty-one}

\textbf{Solution:} ``six hundred'' is 6 hundreds, ``forty'' is 4 tens,
``one'' is 1 one: $600 + 40 + 1$. \ans{641}

\problem{Write 705 in expanded form.}

\textbf{Solution:} 705 has 7 hundreds, \emph{zero} tens, and 5 ones. The
empty tens place contributes nothing, so only two parts are written:
$705 = {}$ \ans{700 + 5}

\problem{Write 460 in word form.}

\textbf{Solution:} 4 hundreds is ``four hundred''; 60 is ``sixty''; there are
no ones to say. Model answer: \emph{four hundred sixty}.

\problem{Write the number in standard form: $9 + 800 + 30$}

\textbf{Solution:} Sort the parts by place first: $800$ is the hundreds part,
$30$ is the tens part, $9$ is the ones part. That is 8 hundreds, 3 tens,
9 ones --- not nine hundred anything! \ans{839}

\problem{Write the number in standard form: nine hundred seven}

\textbf{Solution:} ``nine hundred'' is 9 hundreds and ``seven'' is 7 ones.
No tens are named, so the tens place gets a zero placeholder: \ans{907}

\problem{Challenge: $800 + \underline{\;?\;} + 2 = 862$, and explain.}

\textbf{Solution:} The number 862 has 8 hundreds ($800$, already shown),
6 tens, and 2 ones ($2$, already shown). The missing part is the tens:
$6$ tens $= {}$ \ans{60}

\emph{What a good explanation contains:} the 800 and the 2 already cover the
hundreds and ones places, so the missing part must come from the tens place;
the tens digit of 862 is 6, which is worth 60.
