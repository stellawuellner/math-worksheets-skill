\problem{\textbf{2 flats, 4 rods, 3 units.}
Flats: $2 \times 100 = 200$. Rods: $4 \times 10 = 40$. Units: $3 \times 1 = 3$.
Read it as ``2 hundreds, 4 tens, 3 ones'', which is $200 + 40 + 3$. Each digit
sits in the place its blocks name: $\ans{243}$.}

\problem{\textbf{3 flats, 0 rods, 6 units.}
Flats: $3 \times 100 = 300$. There are no rods, so the tens place is empty --- but
it still needs a digit to hold the ones out of the tens column, and that digit is
$0$. Units: $6$. So $300 + 0 + 6 = \ans{306}$.
(Writing ``36'' is the classic slip: it would mean 3 tens and 6 ones.)}

\problem{\textbf{5 flats, 7 rods, 2 units.}
Expanded form writes each place's value on its own:
\[ 5 \times 100 = 500, \qquad 7 \times 10 = 70, \qquad 2 \times 1 = 2 \]
so the expanded form is $500 + 70 + 2$, and adding the parts gives $\ans{572}$.}

\problem{\textbf{1 flat, 8 rods, 0 units.}
$1 \times 100 = 100$, \ $8 \times 10 = 80$, \ $0 \times 1 = 0$, so the expanded form
is $100 + 80 + 0$. Adding gives $\ans{180}$. The $0$ in the ones place keeps the
$8$ in the tens place --- ``18'' would be a different number.}

\problem{\textbf{4 flats, 6 rods, 7 units --- value of the rods.}
There are $6$ rods, and one rod is worth ten. Six rods are therefore worth
$6 \times 10 = \ans{60}$. The whole model reads 467, and the $6$ in it stands for
sixty, not six: its place tells its value.}

\problem{\textbf{2 flats, 13 rods, 5 units --- trade first.}
Thirteen rods is more than nine, so trade ten of them for one flat:
\[ 13 \text{ rods} = 1 \text{ flat} + 3 \text{ rods} \]
Now count again: $2 + 1 = 3$ flats, $3$ rods, $5$ units, so
$300 + 30 + 5 = \ans{335}$.
Check without trading: $200 + 130 + 5 = 335$ --- same number, as it must be.}

\problem{\textbf{Compare 3 flats, 4 rods, 2 units with 3 flats, 2 rods, 8 units.}
Model A $= 300 + 40 + 2 = 342$. \ Model B $= 300 + 20 + 8 = 328$.
Compare from the biggest place down: the hundreds tie at $3$, so look at the tens.
$4$ tens beats $2$ tens, and that settles it --- the ones never get a vote.
So Model A $\ans{>}$ Model B.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: 4 flats, 2 rods, 6 units versus 3 flats, 12 rods, 9 units.}
Model A $= 400 + 20 + 6 = 426$.
Model B has twelve rods, so trade ten rods for one flat: $3 + 1 = 4$ flats,
$2$ rods, $9$ units, giving $400 + 20 + 9 = 429$.
Now both have $4$ hundreds and $2$ tens, so the ones decide: $6 < 9$.
Model A $\ans{<}$ Model B.
Rosa counted flats before trading; after the trade both models have the same
number of hundreds, so ``more flats on the table'' was never the right test.}
