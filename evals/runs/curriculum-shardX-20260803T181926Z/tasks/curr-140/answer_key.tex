\problem{Notebook \$3.89, pen \$2.15. Estimate the total to the nearest whole dollar.}

\textbf{Solution:} Round \emph{first}, then add. $3.89$ is between $3$ and $4$ and
its tenths digit is $8$, so it rounds up to $4$; $2.15$ has tenths digit $1$, so it
rounds down to $2$. Then $4 + 2 = 6$.
(The exact total is $\$6.04$, so the estimate is a good one.)
\ans{6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{48.6 km one week, 27.3 km the next. Estimate the total to the nearest ten
kilometres.}

\textbf{Solution:} Round each distance to the nearest ten before adding.
$48.6$ is nearer to $50$ than to $40$; $27.3$ is nearer to $30$ than to $20$.
Then $50 + 30 = 80$.
\ans{80\ \text{km}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{\$50 in the account; \$18.75 and \$6.40 spent. How much is left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Two steps. First add what was spent, lining up the decimal points:
$18.75 + 6.40 = 25.15$. Then subtract from the starting amount, writing the 50 as
$50.00$ so the columns line up: $50.00 - 25.15 = 24.85$.
Check by adding back: $25.15 + 24.85 = 50.00$ $\checkmark$
\ans{24.85}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{6 bags of rice at 2.4 kg each. Exact total mass?}

\textbf{Solution:} $6 \times 24 = 144$, and $2.4$ has one digit after the decimal
point, so the answer has one digit after the decimal point too: $14.4$.
Estimate as a check: $6 \times 2 = 12$, so an answer near 12 is sensible --- 1.44
would have been far too small.
\ans{14.4\ \text{kg}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Order 0.9, 0.85, 0.905 and 0.89 from shortest to longest.}

\textbf{Solution:} Give every number the same number of decimal places first, by
writing zeros on the end (which does not change a decimal's value):
$0.900$, $0.850$, $0.905$, $0.890$. Now compare them as though they were whole
numbers of thousandths: $850 < 890 < 900 < 905$.
\[ \boxed{0.85 \;<\; 0.89 \;<\; 0.9 \;<\; 0.905} \]

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Recipe with 1.25 L milk and 0.4 L cream. (a) How much more milk?
(b) How much altogether?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Write $0.4$ as $0.40$ so the columns line up, then subtract:
$1.25 - 0.40 = 0.85$.
\ans{0.85\ \text{L}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Add the same two amounts: $1.25 + 0.40 = 1.65$.
\ans{1.65\ \text{L}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{6 folders at \$4.85 each. Estimate the total to the nearest whole dollar.}

\textbf{Solution:} Round the price first: $4.85$ is nearer to $5$ than to $4$.
Then $6 \times 5 = 30$.
(The exact cost is $\$29.10$, so the estimate is about 90 cents high --- which is what
you would expect, since every folder was rounded up.)
\ans{30}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A 7.2 m ribbon cut into 4 equal pieces. How long is each?}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing equally means dividing: $7.2 \div 4$. Since
$72 \div 4 = 18$ and $7.2$ has one decimal place, each piece is $1.8$ m.
Check by multiplying back: $4 \times 1.8 = 7.2$ $\checkmark$ --- and note that each
piece must be \emph{shorter} than the whole ribbon, so an answer bigger than 7.2
cannot be right.
\ans{1.8\ \text{m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Sam wrote $4.6 \times 3 = 1.38$. (a) Find the exact product.
(b) Compare it with Sam's answer. (c) What went wrong?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $46 \times 3 = 138$, and $4.6$ has one decimal place, so the
product has one decimal place: $13.8$.
\ans{13.8}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $13.8$ is much bigger than $1.38$.
\ans{13.8 > 1.38}

\textbf{Solution (c):} Sam put his decimal point two places from the right instead of
one, giving hundredths where the answer should have tenths --- his answer is a tenth
of the true product. An estimate catches this immediately: $4.6$ is nearly $5$, and
$5 \times 3 = 15$, so the answer has to be near 15, not near 1.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A hike of 3.75 km, 2.8 km and 4.05 km. (a) Total distance.
(b) How far past a 10 km target?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Write all three with two decimal places and add:
$3.75 + 2.80 + 4.05 = 10.60$.
\ans{10.6\ \text{km}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Subtract the target from the total:
$10.60 - 10.00 = 0.60$.
\ans{0.6\ \text{km}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A tap delivers 0.35 L a second. (a) Water after 12 seconds. (b) How many
0.6 L jugs does that fill?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $35 \times 12 = 420$, and $0.35$ has two decimal places, so
the product has two: $4.20$ L, which is $4.2$ L.
\ans{4.2\ \text{L}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Dividing by a decimal is easier if both numbers are made ten
times bigger --- that does not change the answer:
$4.2 \div 0.6 = 42 \div 6 = 7$.
Check: $7 \times 0.6 = 4.2$ $\checkmark$
\ans{7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{4 packs of paper at \$6.75 and 3 markers at \$2.40, from \$40.
(a) Estimate. (b) Exact total. (c) Compare. (d) Money left.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Round each price first: $6.75$ rounds to $7$ and $2.40$
rounds to $2$. Then $4 \times 7 = 28$ and $3 \times 2 = 6$, giving $28 + 6 = 34$.
\ans{34}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Exactly: $4 \times 6.75 = 27.00$ and $3 \times 2.40 = 7.20$,
so the total is $27.00 + 7.20 = 34.20$.
\ans{34.2}

\textbf{Solution (c):} The estimate came out slightly below the exact total, because
the markers were rounded down by 40 cents each while the paper was only rounded up by
25 cents each.
\ans{34 < 34.2}

\textbf{Solution (d):} Subtract the exact total from what the class had:
$40.00 - 34.20 = 5.80$.
Check: $34.20 + 5.80 = 40.00$ $\checkmark$
\ans{5.8}
