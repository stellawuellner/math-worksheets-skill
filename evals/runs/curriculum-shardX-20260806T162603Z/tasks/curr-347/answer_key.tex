\problem{Translate by $(x + 3,\ y - 5)$, then by $(x - 8,\ y + 2)$. Find the single translation.}

\textbf{Solution:} Translations compose by adding their components, because each one shifts every point by a fixed amount and the shifts simply stack.
\[ \text{horizontal: } 3 + (-8), \qquad \text{vertical: } (-5) + 2 \]
So the single rule is $(x, y) \to (x - 5,\ y - 3)$: horizontal \ans{-5} and vertical \ans{-3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Smallest rotation mapping a regular decagon onto itself.}

\textbf{Solution:} A regular $n$-gon looks identical after a turn of $360^\circ / n$, because its $n$ vertices are equally spaced around a full turn. With $n = 10$: $360^\circ / 10$. \ans{36^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$P(4, -6)$ reflected in the $x$-axis then in the $y$-axis. (a) $P''$. (b) The single equivalent motion.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The first reflection negates $y$, giving $(4, 6)$; the second negates $x$.
\[ P'' = (-4,\ 6) \]
so the coordinates are \ans{-4} and \ans{6}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: both coordinates of $P$ have been negated, which is exactly the rule $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$ --- a half turn, that is a rotation of $180^\circ$, about the origin. Reflecting in two perpendicular lines always gives a rotation about their intersection through twice the angle between them, and $2 \times 90^\circ = 180^\circ$.

\emph{Full credit:} names a rotation of $180^\circ$ about the origin and cites the negation of both coordinates. \emph{Half credit:} says rotation with no centre or angle. \emph{No credit:} names a reflection or a translation.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Reflect in $x = 1$, then in $x = 5$. Find the translation distance.}

\textbf{Solution:} Two reflections in parallel lines give a translation perpendicular to them, through twice the gap between the lines --- not once.
\[ 2(5 - 1) = 2(4) \]
So every point moves \ans{8} units to the right.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$A(5, -1)$ translated by $(x - 2,\ y + 7)$, then given a half turn about the origin.}

\textbf{Solution:} Do the translation first, then negate both coordinates.
\begin{align*}
  A' &= (5 - 2,\ -1 + 7) = (3,\ 6) \\
  A'' &= (-3,\ -6)
\end{align*}
So the coordinates are \ans{-3} and \ans{-6}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Smallest rotation is $24^\circ$. Find the number of sides.}

\textbf{Solution:} The smallest rotation is $360^\circ / n$, so read that relation backwards.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{360}{n} &= 24 \\
  24n &= 360 \\
  n &= 15
\end{align*}
So the polygon has \ans{n = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Two reflections in lines through the origin meeting at $35^\circ$. Find the rotation angle.}

\textbf{Solution:} Reflecting twice in intersecting lines gives a rotation about the crossing point through \emph{twice} the angle between the lines.
\[ 2 \times 35^\circ \]
So the rotation is \ans{70^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$Q(-2, 5)$ turned a quarter turn counterclockwise about the origin, then reflected in $y = x$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The quarter turn sends $(x, y)$ to $(-y, x)$, so $Q' = (-5, -2)$. Reflecting in $y = x$ swaps the coordinates, giving $Q'' = (-2, -5)$. The $y$-coordinate is \ans{-5}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: $Q(-2, 5)$ has become $(-2, -5)$ --- the first coordinate is unchanged and the second has changed sign, which is the rule $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$. That is a reflection in the $x$-axis.

\emph{Full credit:} names a reflection in the $x$-axis and cites the unchanged $x$ with the negated $y$. \emph{Half credit:} says reflection with the wrong mirror line. \emph{No credit:} names a rotation or a translation.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Regular hexagon. (a) Smallest rotation. (b) Two of them in succession. (c) Why six return every vertex.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $360^\circ / 6$. \ans{60^\circ}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Rotations about the same centre add, so two turns of $60^\circ$ give $2 \times 60^\circ$. \ans{120^\circ}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: six turns of $60^\circ$ add to $6 \times 60^\circ = 360^\circ$, which is one complete revolution about the centre. A full revolution carries every point of the plane all the way round and back onto itself, so each vertex ends exactly where it began.

\emph{Full credit:} computes $360^\circ$ and identifies it as one full revolution returning every vertex. \emph{Half credit:} reaches $360^\circ$ without the revolution argument. \emph{No credit:} answers only that a hexagon has six vertices.

\problem{$R(1, 2)$, $S(6, 2)$ reflected in $y = x$, then translated by $(x + 3,\ y - 4)$; $S''(5, 2)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Reflecting $R(1, 2)$ in $y = x$ swaps its coordinates to $(2, 1)$; the translation then adds $3$ and subtracts $4$.
\[ R'' = (2 + 3,\ 1 - 4) \]
so the coordinates are \ans{5} and \ans{-3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $R$ and $S$ share a $y$-coordinate, so $RS = |6 - 1|$. \ans{RS = 5}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $R''(5, -3)$ and $S''(5, 2)$ share an $x$-coordinate, so the length is $|2 - (-3)|$. \ans{R''S'' = 5}

Notice the segment turned from horizontal to vertical --- the reflection changed its direction but not its size.

\textbf{Solution (d) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: a reflection preserves every distance and a translation preserves every distance, so performing one and then the other preserves distance as well. The image of a segment of length $5$ therefore has to have length $5$; the two answers agreeing is forced, not lucky.

\emph{Full credit:} identifies both steps as rigid motions and states the distance-preserving property. \emph{Half credit:} says lengths are preserved without identifying both steps. \emph{No credit:} offers the two equal answers as the reason.
