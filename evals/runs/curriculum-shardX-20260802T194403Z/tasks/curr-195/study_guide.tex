\sstitleblock{Multistep Surface Area and Scale Design}

\noindent\small\textbf{Method for every problem here:} (1) name the surface or region, (2) break
it into faces or rectangles you have a formula for, (3) decide which pieces are actually
included, (4) add, and keep the unit --- lengths in m or cm, areas in m$^2$ or cm$^2$.\normalsize

\skillheading{1. Surface area of a rectangular prism}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Formula:}\quad $SA = 2(\ell w + \ell h + wh)$\\[3pt]
The six faces come in three matching pairs, which is where the 2 comes from. If a face is
missing (an open box, an unpainted floor), do \emph{not} use this formula --- add the faces you
actually need, one pair at a time.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad A closed box is 10 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. Find its surface area.
\step{All six faces are wanted, so the paired-faces formula applies directly.}
\step{$SA = 2(10 \cdot 4 + 10 \cdot 3 + 4 \cdot 3) = 2(40 + 30 + 12) = 2(82)$}
$\Rightarrow\ \ans{164 \text{ cm}^2}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad A closed box is 9 cm by 5 cm by 2 cm. Find its surface area.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{146 \text{ cm}^2}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Surface area of a cylinder (surface-area-cylinder)}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Formulas:}\quad curved side $= 2\pi r h$ \quad(a rectangle
$2\pi r$ long and $h$ tall)\\[2pt]
one circular end $= \pi r^2$ \qquad closed can: $SA = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi r(r + h)$\\[3pt]
Count the ends the problem really has: a label has none, an open tub has one, a sealed can has two.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad A closed cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 10 cm. Find its surface area.
\step{Sealed at both ends, so the surface is the curved side plus two circles: $2\pi r(r+h)$.}
\step{$2\pi(5)(5 + 10) = 150\pi \approx 471.2388\ldots$}
$\Rightarrow\ \ans{471.24 \text{ cm}^2}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad A closed cylinder has radius 2 cm and height 7 cm. Find its surface area.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{113.10 \text{ cm}^2}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Composite plan areas (composite-area)}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{Two ways, same answer:}\\[3pt]
\textbf{Add:} cut the shape into rectangles, find each area, add them.\quad
\textbf{Subtract:} enclose it in one big rectangle and take away the missing corner.\par
Pick whichever needs fewer measurements, then check with the other.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad A patio is a 9 m by 5 m rectangle with a 3 m by 2 m corner cut out. Find its area.
\step{The shape is one rectangle with a bite taken out, so subtracting is quicker than cutting it into pieces.}
\step{$9 \cdot 5 - 3 \cdot 2 = 45 - 6$}
$\Rightarrow\ \ans{39 \text{ m}^2}$
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad A deck is a 12 m by 7 m rectangle with a 4 m by 3 m corner cut out. Find its area.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{72 \text{ m}^2}$}\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{4. Scale factors: length, area, volume}
\begin{formulabox}\textbf{The rule that catches everyone:} if every \emph{length} is multiplied
by $k$, then every \emph{area} is multiplied by $k^2$ and every \emph{volume} by $k^3$.\\[3pt]
Safest route: convert the lengths first, then use the area formula on the real lengths. If you
start from a drawing's area instead, remember to multiply by $k^2$, not $k$.
\end{formulabox}
\begin{examplebox}\textbf{Example:}\quad A drawing uses 1 cm $=$ 3 m. A room is 4 cm by 2.5 cm on the drawing. Find the real area.
\step{Area is asked for, so convert each length with the scale first and multiply afterwards.}
\step{Real lengths: $4 \cdot 3 = 12$ m and $2.5 \cdot 3 = 7.5$ m, so the area is $12 \cdot 7.5$.}
$\Rightarrow\ \ans{90 \text{ m}^2}$ \quad (Same as drawing area $10$ cm$^2$ times $3^2$.)
\end{examplebox}
\begin{tryitbox}\textbf{Try it:}\quad A drawing uses 1 cm $=$ 6 m. A room is 3 cm by 2 cm on the drawing. Find the real area.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{216 \text{ m}^2}$}\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}\textbf{(!) Watch out:} Three habits fix most lost marks here ---
count only the faces that exist (an unpainted floor is not part of the answer), never apply a
length scale factor straight to an area, and finish with the right unit: m$^2$ for area,
m for a length.
\end{watchoutbox}
