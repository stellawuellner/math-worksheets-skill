# Classifying Rational and Irrational Numbers — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_classify_curr201.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) proving a number is rational by producing the
  fraction, (2) deciding whether a square root is rational, (3) placing an irrational
  number on a number line by trapping it between perfect squares. A watch-out box tackles
  the two misconceptions this topic lives on — that a radical sign makes a number
  irrational, and that a decimal which "runs on" must be irrational.
- **`ws_classify_curr201.pdf` — Worksheet (4 pages, 10 problems).** The sheet uses a
  concrete rule as its spine: *if it is rational, prove it by writing the fraction; if it
  is irrational, place it.* Models used include place-value fraction conversion, the
  subtract-the-copy method for repeating decimals, and two blank number lines your child
  marks and labels. The last problem is a find-the-flawed-reasoning challenge.
- **`ak_classify_curr201.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with the
  reasoning written out, a Quick Answers strip, and a Curriculum block (standard 8.NS.A,
  difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

This sheet is deliberately split between computation and written reasoning, so the
verification split matters:

- **Every numeric answer on the sheet is machine-checked** — 13 machine-checked responses
  in all, recomputed independently with SymPy (the fractions in lowest terms, the two
  whole numbers bracketing $\sqrt{54}$, the hundredth-place approximations, the $\pi$ vs
  $\tfrac{22}{7}$ comparison, and the four-number ordering). A wrong value would have
  blocked the build.
- **Five problems (3, 4, 5, 6 and 10) also carry an instructor-judged part** — the marked
  number lines in 3 and 6, the shown subtract-the-copy derivation in 4, and the written
  explanations in 5 and 10. Those print as `---` in the Quick Answers strip, and the answer
  key gives you a grading rubric for each one: what a full-credit response must contain and
  what does not earn credit. That is why the key's own note reads "5 of 10 problems fully
  machine-checked" — the other five are *partly* machine-checked and partly yours to read.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same
  way (6 of 6 machine-checked).

A calculator is not needed. The two hundredth-place estimates (problems 3 and 6) are meant
to be squeezed by hand, and the answer key shows that squeeze.
