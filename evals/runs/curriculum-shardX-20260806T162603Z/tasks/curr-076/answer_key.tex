\problem{Count by fives: $5, 10, 15, \underline{\ \ }, \underline{\ \ }, \underline{\ \ }$}

\textbf{Solution:} Each step adds 5 to the number before it: $15 + 5 = 20$, then $20 + 5 = 25$, then $25 + 5 = 30$. The 6th number, 30, is $5 \times 6$.
\[ \boxed{20,\quad 25,\quad 30} \]

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Array with 2 rows of 8: $2 \times 8$ and $8 + 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The two rows hold 8 dots each, so $2 \times 8 = 16$ --- and adding the rows, $8 + 8 = 16$. A twos fact \emph{is} a double: same array, two names. \ans{16 \text{ and } 16}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$10 \times 4$ and $10 \times 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Counting by tens: four tens are 40, eight tens are 80. $10 \times 4 = 40$ and $10 \times 8 = 80$. \ans{40 \text{ and } 80}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$2 \times 6$, then double it for $4 \times 6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The anchor: $2 \times 6 = 12$. Four groups is twice two groups, so double it: $12 + 12 = 24$. So $4 \times 6 = 24$. \ans{12 \text{ and } 24}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$5 \times 8$, then one more 8 for $6 \times 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The anchor: $5 \times 8 = 40$. Six groups is one more group of 8 than five groups: $40 + 8 = 48$. So $6 \times 8 = 48$. \ans{40 \text{ and } 48}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$10 \times 6$, then one group of 6 away for $9 \times 6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The anchor: $10 \times 6 = 60$. Nine groups is one group of 6 fewer than ten groups: $60 - 6 = 54$. So $9 \times 6 = 54$. \ans{60 \text{ and } 54}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Choose an anchor family for $9 \times 8$, then use it.}

\textbf{Solution:} Tens is the most direct choice: $10 \times 8 = 80$, and nine groups is one 8 fewer, $80 - 8 = 72$. (Fives also works: $5 \times 8 = 40$ and $4 \times 8 = 32$, and $40 + 32 = 72$ --- credit any anchor that is actually used.) \ans{72}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Challenge: use any anchor fact for $7 \times 6$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The fives anchor is closest: $5 \times 6 = 30$, and the two more groups are $2 \times 6 = 12$, so $30 + 12 = 42$. A correct explanation names the anchor fact and says how it built the answer (e.g. ``I knew five 6s, then added two more 6s''). Other anchors count too: doubling $2 \times 6$ twice and adding one more 6, or $10 \times 6 = 60$ minus three 6s. \ans{42}
