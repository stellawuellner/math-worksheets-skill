\problem{$302 - 147$. \\
\textbf{Ones:} $2 - 7$ is not possible, and the tens digit is $0$, so the tens
cannot give a group. \textbf{Trade a hundred:} $302 = 2$ hundreds, $10$ tens,
$2$ ones. \textbf{Now trade a ten:} $2$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $12$ ones.
\textbf{Subtract each place:} $12 - 7 = 5$ ones; $9 - 4 = 5$ tens;
$2 - 1 = 1$ hundred. \ans{155}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $155 + 147 = 302$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{$605 - 238$. \\
The ones need a group but there are $0$ tens. Trade a hundred: $5$ hundreds,
$10$ tens, $5$ ones. Trade a ten: $5$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $15$ ones.
$15 - 8 = 7$; $9 - 3 = 6$; $5 - 2 = 3$. \ans{367}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $367 + 238 = 605$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{$704 - 526$. \\
Trade a hundred: $6$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $4$ ones. Trade a ten:
$6$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $14$ ones. $14 - 6 = 8$; $9 - 2 = 7$; $6 - 5 = 1$.
\ans{178}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $178 + 526 = 704$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{Estimate $608 - 293$ to the nearest hundred. \\
$608$ rounds to $600$ (the tens digit $0$ is less than $5$). $293$ rounds to
$300$ (the tens digit $9$ is $5$ or more). $600 - 300 = 300$. \ans{300}
\noindent\emph{Note for the grader:} the exact difference is $315$, so an estimate of
$300$ is sensible. A student who rounded $293$ down to $200$ has rounded, not
estimated well --- send them back to the hundreds digit rule.}

\problem{$500 - 236$. \\
Both the ones and the tens are $0$, so travel left to the hundreds.
Trade a hundred: $4$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $0$ ones. Trade a ten:
$4$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $10$ ones. $10 - 6 = 4$; $9 - 3 = 6$; $4 - 2 = 2$.
\ans{264}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $264 + 236 = 500$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{Kim wrote $502 - 138 = 364$. Check by adding. \\
Addition undoes subtraction, so a correct difference plus the number subtracted
must give back the starting number: $364 + 138$. Ones: $4 + 8 = 12$, write $2$
carry $1$. Tens: $6 + 3 + 1 = 10$, write $0$ carry $1$. Hundreds:
$3 + 1 + 1 = 5$. \ans{502}
\noindent Because the check returns $502$, which is exactly the number Kim started
from, her answer is correct. \emph{Expected sentence:} ``The check gives $502$,
so Kim is right.''}

\problem{$800 - 345$. \\
Trade a hundred: $7$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $0$ ones. Trade a ten: $7$ hundreds,
$9$ tens, $10$ ones. $10 - 5 = 5$; $9 - 4 = 5$; $7 - 3 = 4$. \ans{455}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $455 + 345 = 800$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{Estimate $702 - 268$ to the nearest ten. \\
$702$ rounds to $700$ (ones digit $2$). $268$ rounds to $270$ (ones digit $8$).
$700 - 270 = 430$. \ans{430}
\noindent\emph{Note for the grader:} the exact difference is $434$, so this estimate
lands within $4$ --- rounding to the nearest ten gives a much tighter estimate
than rounding to the nearest hundred would.}

\problem{Find and fix Rico's mistake in $903 - 458$. \\
\textbf{The mistake:} in each column Rico subtracted the smaller digit from the
larger one ($8-3$, $5-0$, $9-4$) instead of regrouping. Subtraction is not
``take the little digit from the big one'' --- the top number must give the
bottom number away, and when the top digit is too small it has to borrow.
\\ \textbf{Correct work:} the ones need a group and the tens digit is $0$, so
trade a hundred: $8$ hundreds, $10$ tens, $3$ ones. Trade a ten: $8$ hundreds,
$9$ tens, $13$ ones. $13 - 8 = 5$; $9 - 5 = 4$; $8 - 4 = 4$. \ans{445}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $445 + 458 = 903$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{$1000 - 487$. \\
Every digit to the left of the ones is $0$, so the trade starts at the
thousands. $1000 = 9$ hundreds, $9$ tens, $10$ ones (one thousand traded down
through all three places). $10 - 7 = 3$; $9 - 8 = 1$; $9 - 4 = 5$; nothing is
left in the thousands. \ans{513}
\noindent\emph{Check by adding:} $513 + 487 = 1000$ $\checkmark$}
