\sstitleblock{Perimeter and Area of Coordinate Polygons}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{Distance} (one side length):
$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$\\[3pt]
\textbf{Midpoint:} $M = \left(\tfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \tfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$
\qquad
\textbf{Slope:} $m = \tfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$\\[3pt]
\textbf{Shoelace area:} list the vertices in order around the polygon and take
half the absolute value of $\sum (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)$. It works for
any polygon, however slanted.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Which one, and why:} distance builds a perimeter, shoelace gives an
area directly, and slope or midpoint prove a claim about the shape rather than
measure it.
\end{formulabox}

\skillheading{1. Perimeter from coordinates}

\begin{formulabox}
A side parallel to an axis has length equal to the difference of the two
coordinates that change --- no formula needed. Use the distance formula only on
the slanted sides, then add every side once.\\[3pt]
Two slanted sides of equal length appear as equal radicals, so a parallelogram
or rhombus halves the work.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Find the perimeter of the triangle with vertices $(1,1)$,
$(7,1)$ and $(7,9)$.
\step{Two sides are axis-parallel, so only the slanted side needs the distance
formula --- that is the one measurement the coordinates do not hand you.}
\step{Horizontal side $7 - 1 = 6$; vertical side $9 - 1 = 8$; slanted side
$\sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = \ans{10}$.}

Perimeter $= 6 + 8 + 10 = \ans{24}$.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Find the slanted side and the perimeter of the triangle with
vertices $(0,0)$, $(9,0)$ and $(9,12)$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: slanted side $\ans{15}$, perimeter $\ans{36}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Area from coordinates}

\begin{formulabox}
Shoelace never asks for a side length, so it is the safe default when no side
is horizontal or vertical. Write the vertices in boundary order, multiply
down-right, subtract down-left, halve the absolute value.\\[3pt]
Sanity check: the polygon is always smaller than the smallest rectangle that
surrounds it, and a triangle is at most half of it.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Find the area of the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(6,0)$
and $(2,5)$.
\step{The apex sits over the middle of the base, so there is no clean
rectangle to subtract from --- take the vertices in order and use shoelace.}
\step{$\tfrac{1}{2}\left|\,0(0-5) + 6(5-0) + 2(0-0)\,\right|$.}

The area is $\ans{15}$ square units.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Find the area of the quadrilateral with vertices $(0,0)$,
$(6,0)$, $(7,4)$ and $(1,4)$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{24}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Justify the shape, then measure it}

\begin{formulabox}
Slopes whose product is $-1$ mean perpendicular sides, so a right angle is
established rather than assumed --- and a right angle turns two sides into a
base and a height.\\[3pt]
Diagonals with the same midpoint bisect each other. Neither fact is a
measurement; both are what makes a measuring shortcut legal.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Triangle $(0,0)$, $(4,2)$, $(2,6)$. Is the corner at $(4,2)$
a right angle, and what is the area?
\step{Two sides meet at that corner, so their slopes decide the angle before
any area work begins.}
\step{$\ans{\tfrac{1}{2}}$ and $\ans{-2}$ are the two slopes, and their product
is $-1$, so the sides are perpendicular; shoelace on the three vertices then
gives $\ans{10}$ square units.}
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Triangle $(0,0)$, $(6,3)$, $(3,9)$. Find the slope of the side
from $(0,0)$ to $(6,3)$, and the area of the triangle.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: slope $\ans{\tfrac{1}{2}}$, area $\ans{22.5}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} shoelace only works if the vertices are listed in order
around the boundary. Jumping across a diagonal folds the polygon and returns a
smaller number, so read your vertex list off the plot, not off the problem.
\end{watchoutbox}
