\problem{Write 507 in expanded form.}

\textbf{Solution:} 507 has 5 hundreds ($500$), zero tens (write nothing for
an empty place), and 7 ones ($7$). So $507 = {}$ \ans{500 + 7}

\problem{Write the number in standard form: $600 + 3$}

\textbf{Solution:} 6 hundreds and 3 ones, with \emph{no} tens part --- so a
zero must hold the tens place between them. Not 63! \ans{603}

\problem{Write the number in standard form: $200 + 9$}

\textbf{Solution:} 2 hundreds and 9 ones. The tens place is empty, so a zero
holds it: 2 hundreds, 0 tens, 9 ones. \ans{209}

\problem{Find and fix: Nia wrote $830 = 800 + 3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) In 830 the 8 counts hundreds and the 3 sits in the
\emph{tens} place, so it is worth 30, and the 0 shows there are no ones:
$830 = {}$ \ans{800 + 30}

(b) \emph{The error:} Nia treated the 3 as 3 ones. Her sum $800 + 3$ only
makes 803, not 830 --- the 3 of 830 is in the tens place and is worth 30.

\problem{Find and fix: Owen wrote $402 = 400 + 20$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) In 402 the 2 is in the \emph{ones} place, and the zero
in the tens place means no tens part is written: $402 = {}$ \ans{400 + 2}

(b) \emph{What went wrong:} Owen moved the 2 into the empty tens place.
His sum $400 + 20$ makes 420, a different number --- the zero of 402 already
holds the tens place, so the 2 stays in the ones.

\problem{Write 640 in expanded form.}

\textbf{Solution:} This time the \emph{ones} place holds the zero: 640 has
6 hundreds ($600$), 4 tens ($40$), and no ones. So $640 = {}$ \ans{600 + 40}

\problem{Kai says 506 and 56 must be equal. True comparison, and why the zero matters.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) 506 has 5 \emph{hundreds}; 56 has only 5 \emph{tens}.
A number with hundreds beats a two-digit number: \ans{506 > 56}

(b) \emph{Why the zero matters:} the zero in 506 holds the tens place open,
pushing the 5 into the hundreds place. In 56 the 5 only counts tens. Same
digits, different places --- very different sizes.

\problem{Challenge: 5 hundreds, no tens, 3 ones.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Five hundreds, a zero to hold the empty tens place,
then 3 ones: \ans{503}

(b) Only the places that hold something are written: $500$ and $3$, so the
expanded form is \ans{500 + 3}
