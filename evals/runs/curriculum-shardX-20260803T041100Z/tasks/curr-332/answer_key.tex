\problem{Interior Angle Sum Theorem: a convex $n$-gon splits into $n - 2$ triangles
from one vertex, and each triangle contributes $180^\circ$. A hexagon has $n = 6$,
so the sum is $(6 - 2)180^\circ = 4 \cdot 180^\circ$. $\ans{720^\circ}$}

\problem{Same theorem with $n = 15$: the polygon splits into $15 - 2 = 13$
triangles, so the sum is $(15 - 2)180^\circ = 13 \cdot 180^\circ$.
$\ans{2340^\circ}$}

\problem{An octagon has $n = 8$, so its interior angles total
$(8 - 2)180^\circ = 1080^\circ$. \emph{Regular} means all eight interior angles are
congruent, so divide the total equally: $1080^\circ \div 8$.
$\ans{135^\circ}$}

\problem{The exterior angles of any convex polygon total $360^\circ$, and a regular
12-gon has 12 congruent exterior angles. Divide: $360^\circ \div 12$.
$\ans{30^\circ}$

\par Note that the number of sides never enters through the interior-sum formula
here --- the exterior total is the same $360^\circ$ for every convex polygon.}

\problem{A pentagon has $n = 5$, so its interior angles total
$(5 - 2)180^\circ = 540^\circ$. Set the five angles equal to that total:
\[ 102 + 118 + 96 + 131 + x = 540 \]
The four known angles add to $447$, so $x = 540 - 447$.
$\ans{x = 93^\circ}$}

\problem{Every convex polygon's exterior angles total $360^\circ$. A regular polygon
with $n$ sides has $n$ congruent exterior angles, so
\[ 24n = 360 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n = 360 \div 24 \]
$\ans{n = 15}$ sides. (Check: a regular 15-gon's interior angle is
$180^\circ - 24^\circ = 156^\circ$.)}

\problem{Working from the interior angle sum: a regular $n$-gon has $n$ congruent
interior angles that together make $(n - 2)180^\circ$, so
\[ (n - 2)180 = 150n \]
Expand: $180n - 360 = 150n$, so $30n = 360$ and $n = 12$.
$\ans{n = 12}$ sides. (Faster check: the exterior angle is
$180^\circ - 150^\circ = 30^\circ$, and $360 \div 30$ gives the same 12.)}

\problem{A quadrilateral has $n = 4$, so its interior angles total
$(4 - 2)180^\circ = 360^\circ$. The ratio $1 : 2 : 3 : 4$ means the angles are
$x$, $2x$, $3x$, $4x$:
\[ x + 2x + 3x + 4x = 360 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 10x = 360 \]
$\ans{x = 36^\circ}$ \ (so the four angles are $36^\circ$, $72^\circ$, $108^\circ$,
and $144^\circ$, which do add to $360^\circ$).}

\problem{Set the interior angle sum formula equal to the given total and solve for
the side count:
\[ (n - 2)180 = 1980 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n - 2 = 11 \]
$\ans{n = 13}$ sides. A sum that is not a multiple of $180^\circ$ would mean no such
convex polygon exists, so dividing first is also the existence check.}

\problem{\textbf{Manual review --- open justification. A complete argument contains:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item At each of the $n$ vertices the interior angle and its exterior angle form a
        linear pair, so they add to $180^\circ$. Across all vertices, interior plus
        exterior totals $180n$ degrees.
  \item The interior angles alone total $(n - 2)180 = 180n - 360$ degrees.
  \item Subtracting, the exterior angles total
        $180n - (180n - 360) = 360$ degrees. The $180n$ terms cancel, so the result
        does not depend on $n$ --- it is $360^\circ$ for every convex polygon.
  \item Equivalent accepted argument: walking once around the polygon, the turn at
        each vertex is that vertex's exterior angle, and one full circuit turns
        through exactly one complete rotation.
\end{itemize}
Accept any argument that is general in $n$ and shows the $n$-dependence cancelling;
do not accept a single worked example (for instance only a pentagon).}
