\problem{$A(6, -4)$ dilated about the origin by factor $0.5$. Find $A'$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A dilation about the origin multiplies each coordinate by the scale factor.
\[ x' = 0.5 \times 6 = 3, \qquad y' = 0.5 \times (-4) = -2 \]
So the coordinates are \ans{3} and \ans{-2}

A factor between $0$ and $1$ shrinks the figure: $A'$ is half as far from the origin as $A$, in the same direction.

\problem{Photograph $12$ cm wide enlarged to $30$ cm wide. Find the scale factor.}

\textbf{Solution:} A scale factor is a ratio of matching lengths, image over original --- a division, never a difference.
\[ k = \frac{30}{12} \]
So \ans{k = 2.5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Blueprint at $1$ cm to $40$ cm; a wall measures $8.5$ cm on the plan. Find the real length.}

\textbf{Solution:} Each drawn centimetre stands for $40$ real centimetres, so multiply the drawn length by $40$.
\[ 8.5 \times 40 \]
So \ans{340\text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$P(2, 1)$, $Q(6, 4)$ dilated about the origin by factor $3$; $Q'(18, 12)$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $x' = 3 \times 2 = 6$ and $y' = 3 \times 1 = 3$, so the coordinates are \ans{6} and \ans{3}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $PQ = \sqrt{(6-2)^2 + (4-1)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25}$, so \ans{PQ = 5}

\textbf{Solution (c):} $P'Q' = \sqrt{(18-6)^2 + (12-3)^2} = \sqrt{144 + 81} = \sqrt{225}$, so \ans{P'Q' = 15}

The image length is exactly $3$ times the original --- which is what "scale factor $3$" means, and it is the one property that separates a dilation from a rigid motion.

\problem{Model car $18$ cm long; real car $450$ cm long. How many times longer is the real car?}

\textbf{Solution:} Both lengths must be in the same unit before dividing, and they are --- centimetres.
\[ \frac{450}{18} \]
So the real car is \ans{25} times longer.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Student $1.6$ m tall with a $2.4$ m shadow; flagpole shadow $13.5$ m. Find the flagpole's height.}

\textbf{Solution:} The two right triangles are similar, so matching sides are in the same ratio: height over shadow is the same for both.
\begin{align*}
  \frac{h}{13.5} &= \frac{1.6}{2.4} \\
  h &= \frac{1.6 \times 13.5}{2.4} = \frac{21.6}{2.4}
\end{align*}
So \ans{h = 9.00\text{ m}}

Sense check: the flagpole's shadow is about $5.6$ times the student's, and $9$ m is about $5.6$ times $1.6$ m $\checkmark$

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$D(5, 1)$ dilated about $C(2, 3)$ by factor $4$. Find $D'$.}

\textbf{Solution:} With a centre other than the origin, measure the offset from the centre, scale that offset, and add it back to the centre.
\begin{align*}
  x' &= 2 + 4(5 - 2) = 2 + 12 = 14 \\
  y' &= 3 + 4(1 - 3) = 3 - 8 = -5
\end{align*}
So the coordinates are \ans{14} and \ans{-5}

Multiplying the raw coordinates instead would give $(20, 4)$ --- the right size but the wrong place, because that dilates about the origin.

\problem{Courtyard $12$ m by $8$ m scaled by $2.5$. (a) Perimeter. (b) Area. (c) Why they differ.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The original perimeter is $2(12 + 8) = 40$ m, and a perimeter is a sum of lengths, so it is multiplied by $2.5$ once.
\[ 40 \times 2.5 \]
So \ans{100\text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The original area is $12 \times 8 = 96$ m$^2$. Both the length and the width are stretched by $2.5$, so the area picks up the factor twice, $2.5^2 = 6.25$.
\[ 96 \times 6.25 \]
So \ans{600\text{ m}^2}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: a perimeter is a sum of lengths, and every length is multiplied by $2.5$, so the sum is too. An area is a product of two lengths, and both of them are multiplied by $2.5$, so the area is multiplied by $2.5 \times 2.5 = 6.25$. One scale factor, two different multipliers, because of how many lengths each measurement is built from.

\emph{Full credit:} distinguishes a sum of lengths from a product of lengths and gives both multipliers. \emph{Half credit:} gives $2.5$ and $6.25$ with no reason. \emph{No credit:} says only that area grows faster.
