\sstitleblock{Completing Two-Column Triangle-Congruence Proofs}

\skillheading{Skill 1 --- Supplying the reason for a line}

\begin{formulabox}
Every reason is one of a short list: \textbf{Given}; a \textbf{definition} (midpoint, angle bisector, right triangle, congruent segments); the \textbf{reflexive property} for a shared side or angle; a \textbf{theorem} (vertical angles congruent, alternate interior angles congruent); or a \textbf{criterion} --- SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL --- on the final line.\par
\textbf{Two reasons no mark ever shows:} the reflexive property for a shared part, and vertical angles at a crossing. Look for them before deciding a proof is short of evidence.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad A proof reaches $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ by SSS using a shared diagonal, so by CPCTC two corresponding sides are equal: $2x + 9$ and $4x - 5$ cm. Find $x$.
\step{The shared diagonal enters by the reflexive property, which completes SSS; only then does CPCTC let me set the two corresponding lengths equal.}
\step{$2x + 9 = 4x - 5$, so $14 = 2x$.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{x = 7}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad After a congruence proof, CPCTC gives two corresponding sides $6x - 1$ and $3x + 20$ cm. Find $x$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{x = 7}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- Supplying the statement for a reason}

\begin{formulabox}
Read the reason column and work backwards. ``Definition of midpoint'' must be followed by the congruence of the two halves; ``reflexive property'' must be the shared part; the criterion on the last line tells you what the lines above it have to contain.\par
\textbf{The criterion names the pattern:} ASA needs angle, included side, angle; SAS needs side, included angle, side; AAS needs angle, angle, non-included side. If your statements do not match that pattern, one of them is in the wrong place.\par
\textbf{Free third angle:} two matching angles force the third, since the angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Two angles of a triangle measure $a = 63^\circ$ and $b = 49^\circ$. Find the third angle, the statement an ``angle sum'' reason would carry.
\step{The reason cited is the triangle angle sum, so the statement it justifies must be the value of the remaining angle.}
\step{Third angle $= 180 - a - b = 180 - 63 - 49$.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{68}$ degrees
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad Two angles of a triangle measure $55^\circ$ and $82^\circ$. Find the third.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{43}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Finishing with CPCTC}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{CPCTC} --- corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent --- is the step \emph{after} the criterion. It converts a proved triangle congruence into a congruence of any one pair of remaining sides or angles.\par
\textbf{Order matters:} you may never use CPCTC to establish the criterion. The proof runs givens $\rightarrow$ criterion $\rightarrow$ CPCTC $\rightarrow$ equation.\par
Then set the two corresponding expressions equal and solve, and substitute back into both to check.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} \quad Two triangles are proved congruent, and CPCTC gives corresponding angles of $(7x - 12)^\circ$ and $(4x + 15)^\circ$. Find $x$.
\step{CPCTC comes after the criterion, so the congruence is already established and the two corresponding measures may simply be set equal.}
\step{$7x - 12 = 4x + 15$, so $3x = 27$.}

$\Rightarrow\quad \ans{x = 9}$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} \quad CPCTC gives corresponding angles of $(5x + 6)^\circ$ and $(2x + 33)^\circ$. Find $x$.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{x = 9}$}
\end{tryitbox}
