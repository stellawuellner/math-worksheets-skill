# Using Zero and Negative Exponents — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_zeroneg_curr207.pdf` — Skills Summary (study guide, 2 pages).** Read this first.
  Three sections, each with a rule box, a worked example, and a "try it" whose answer is
  printed upside down inside the box: (1) the zero exponent and *why* it is 1 (dividing a
  power by itself), (2) turning a negative exponent into a fraction, (3) applying the
  product and quotient laws when the exponents go negative and then cleaning up. A watch-out
  box states the one thing this topic keeps tripping students on: a negative exponent never
  makes a value negative.
- **`ws_zeroneg_curr207.pdf` — Worksheet (4 pages, 12 problems).** A fluency ramp that never
  repeats the same skeleton twice in a row: numeric zero powers, exact-fraction negative
  powers, a bracketed zero power, symbolic rewrites, the quotient law running negative, a
  product with a coefficient, a comparison, and a find-the-error challenge. Problem 9
  ($10^{-4}$) is deliberately the scientific-notation case.
- **`ak_zeroneg_curr207.pdf` — Answer Key (3 pages).** Every problem re-printed with the
  reasoning, plus a Quick Answers strip, a Curriculum block (standards 8.EE.A.1 and
  8.EE.A.4), and a "Common wrong answers" note.

## What is verified, honestly

- **11 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked.** Numeric answers were recomputed
  exactly as fractions — not as decimals — and the symbolic simplifications were checked as
  *identities*, meaning SymPy confirmed the printed answer is the same expression as the
  original for every value of the variable. The comparison in problem 11 is checked as a
  relation.
- **Problem 12 is split.** Part (a), the corrected value of $3^{-2}$, is machine-checked.
  Part (b) — explaining what the negative exponent actually does — is
  **instructor-judged**, since it is written reasoning. It prints as `---` in the Quick
  Answers strip, and the key gives you the rubric: the answer must state the reciprocal
  meaning, not merely produce the right fraction.
- **One misconception trap was declared and proved distinguishable:** reading the
  exponent's minus sign as a minus sign on the answer. That is exactly where Dara's $-9$
  comes from in problem 12, and the build confirmed it can never be confused with the
  correct value.
- The study guide's three worked examples and three try-it answers are verified the same
  way (6 of 6 machine-checked).

Answers are asked for as exact fractions throughout, so a calculator is not needed and
decimals are not the wanted form.
