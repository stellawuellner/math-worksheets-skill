\problem{Exam with $\mu = 500$, $\sigma = 100$. Find the $z$-score of $620$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $z = \dfrac{620 - 500}{100} = \dfrac{120}{100}$. \ans{z = 1.2}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same exam. Find the $z$-score of $455$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $z = \dfrac{455 - 500}{100} = \dfrac{-45}{100}$. The negative sign says
the score is below the mean. \ans{z = -0.45}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same exam. What percentage of scores fall below $600$?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $600$ is exactly $1\sigma$ above the mean. The empirical rule puts
$68\%$ within $1\sigma$, so $34\%$ sits between the mean and $600$, and $50\%$ sits below the
mean: $50 + 34$. \ans{84}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same exam. A student's $z$-score is $1.5$. Find their score.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $x = \mu + z\sigma = 500 + 1.5(100) = 500 + 150$. \ans{x = 650}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{History: $88$ with $\mu = 80$, $\sigma = 5$. Biology: $76$ with $\mu = 70$,
$\sigma = 4$. \ (a) and (b) the $z$-scores. \ (c) which was stronger?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $z = \dfrac{88 - 80}{5} = \dfrac{8}{5}$. \ans{z = 1.6}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad $z = \dfrac{76 - 70}{4} = \dfrac{6}{4}$. \ans{z = 1.5}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} \quad The history result is $1.6$ standard
deviations above its class mean; the biology result is $1.5$ above its own. Relative to the
class, the history performance is very slightly stronger. The raw scores cannot settle it,
because $88$ and $76$ are measured against different means ($80$ versus $70$) and different
spreads ($5$ versus $4$) --- standardizing is what makes them comparable. Full credit requires
the comparison of $z$-scores and the reason the raw scores are not comparable.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Heights: $\mu = 170$ cm, $\sigma = 8$ cm. About $95\%$ lie between two values.
\ (a) lower. \ (b) upper.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $95\%$ corresponds to $2\sigma$ each side:
$170 - 2(8) = 170 - 16$. \ans{154 \text{ cm}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad $170 + 2(8) = 170 + 16$. \ans{186 \text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same heights. Find the $z$-score of $182$ cm.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $z = \dfrac{182 - 170}{8} = \dfrac{12}{8}$. \ans{z = 1.5}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same heights. What percentage is shorter than $162$ cm?}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $162$ is $1\sigma$ below the mean. Of the $68\%$ within $1\sigma$,
half ($34\%$) lies between $162$ and the mean, and $50\%$ lies below the mean, so the
percentage below $162$ is $50 - 34$. \ans{16}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Batteries: $\mu = 40$ hours, $\sigma = 3$ hours. Find the $97.5$th percentile.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $97.5\%$ below means $2.5\%$ above, which is one tail of the $95\%$
band, so $z = 2$: $x = 40 + 2(3) = 40 + 6$. \ans{46 \text{ hours}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Same batteries: $25$ in every $1000$ last longer than the $97.5$th percentile. Give
the probability as a fraction.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad $P = \dfrac{25}{1000}$, and dividing both parts by $25$ gives
$\dfrac{1}{40}$ --- the $2.5\%$ upper tail. \ans{P = \tfrac{1}{40}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{Test with $\mu = 72$, $\sigma = 6$; a student's $z$-score is $-1.5$. \ (a) raw score.
\ (b) what the sign means and the percentile.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $x = 72 + (-1.5)(6) = 72 - 9$. \ans{x = 63}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} \quad A negative $z$ means the score is below the
mean --- here $1.5$ standard deviations below. For the percentile: about $32\%$ of the
distribution lies more than $1\sigma$ from the mean and about $5\%$ more than $2\sigma$, so the
lower tail beyond $1.5\sigma$ holds roughly $6$--$7\%$ of the values. The student is near the
$7$th percentile. Full credit for "below the mean" plus any percentile estimate between about
$5$ and $10$ with a stated reason.

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.15cm}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis.} Line~1: $\mu = 40$, $\sigma = 3$, one battery at $46$ hours.
Line~2: $\mu = 45$, $\sigma = 5$, one battery at $52$ hours. \ (a), (b) the $z$-scores.
\ (c) which is more unusual?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} \quad $z = \dfrac{46 - 40}{3} = \dfrac{6}{3}$. \ans{z = 2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad $z = \dfrac{52 - 45}{5} = \dfrac{7}{5}$. \ans{z = 1.4}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} \quad The Line~1 battery is $2$ standard
deviations above its own mean; the Line~2 battery only $1.4$ above its own. The Line~1 result
is the more unusual, even though the Line~2 battery lasted $6$ hours longer in absolute terms.
Comparing $46$ with $52$ directly is meaningless here because the two lines have different
means and different spreads --- Line~2 is both better on average and more variable, so a long
life there is less surprising. Full credit requires the $z$-comparison and an explanation of
why the raw lifetimes mislead.
