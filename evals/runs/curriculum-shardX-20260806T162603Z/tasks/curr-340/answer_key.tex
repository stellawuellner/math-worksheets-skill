\problem{An inscribed angle intercepts an arc of $86^\circ$. Find the inscribed angle.}

\textbf{Solution:} The inscribed angle theorem: an inscribed angle is half the arc it intercepts. So the angle is $86^\circ / 2$. \ans{43^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Inscribed angle $\angle ABC = 37^\circ$ intercepts arc $AC$. Find arc $AC$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same theorem, read backwards: the arc is twice the inscribed angle, $2 \times 37^\circ$. \ans{74^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Radius $9$ cm, central angle $40^\circ$. Find the arc length.}

\textbf{Solution:} An arc is the fraction $\frac{40}{360}$ of the whole circumference $2\pi r$:
\[ s = \frac{40}{360}\cdot 2\pi(9) = \frac{1}{9}\cdot 18\pi = 2\pi = 6.2832\ldots \]
So \ans{s \approx 6.28\text{ cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Chords $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ meet at $E$ inside the circle; $AE = 6$, $EB = 10$, $CE = 4$. Find $ED$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The intersecting-chords theorem says the two products of the pieces are equal: $AE \cdot EB = CE \cdot ED$.
\begin{align*}
  6 \cdot 10 &= 4 \cdot ED \\
  60 &= 4 \cdot ED \\
  ED &= 15
\end{align*}
So \ans{ED = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Fountain $(x-5)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 49$ in metres; tangent at the top of the fountain. (a) Radius. (b) $y$ at the tangent point.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The right-hand side of centre-radius form is $r^2$, so $r = \sqrt{49}$. \ans{r = 7\text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} The centre is $(5, -2)$. The point directly above the centre sits one radius higher, so its $y$-coordinate is $-2 + 7$. \ans{5}

(A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, which is why the tangent path here runs horizontally.)

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$: $m\angle A = (2x+15)^\circ$, $m\angle C = (3x+40)^\circ$. (a) Find $x$. (b) Find $m\angle A$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ are opposite angles of a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle, so they are supplementary.
\begin{align*}
  (2x + 15) + (3x + 40) &= 180 \\
  5x + 55 &= 180 \\
  5x &= 125 \\
  x &= 25
\end{align*}
So \ans{x = 25}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $m\angle A = 2(25) + 15 = 50 + 15$. \ans{65^\circ}

Check: $m\angle C = 3(25) + 40 = 115^\circ$, and $65 + 115 = 180$ $\checkmark$

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Radius $12$ cm, central angle $120^\circ$. Find the sector area.}

\textbf{Solution:} A sector is the fraction $\frac{120}{360}$ of the whole area $\pi r^2$:
\[ A = \frac{120}{360}\cdot \pi (12)^2 = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 144\pi = 48\pi = 150.7964\ldots \]
So \ans{A \approx 150.80\text{ cm}^2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Tangent $PT = 12$, secant from $P$ with external part $PA = 8$. Find $PB$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The tangent-secant theorem: the tangent length \emph{squared} equals the product of the whole secant and its external part, $PT^2 = PA \cdot PB$.
\begin{align*}
  12^2 &= 8 \cdot PB \\
  144 &= 8 \cdot PB \\
  PB &= 18
\end{align*}
So \ans{PB = 18}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Radius $10$ cm, minor arc $AB = 72^\circ$. (a) Arc length. (b) Sector area.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $s = \frac{72}{360}\cdot 2\pi(10) = \frac{1}{5}\cdot 20\pi = 4\pi = 12.5664\ldots$, so \ans{s \approx 12.57\text{ cm}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $A = \frac{72}{360}\cdot \pi (10)^2 = \frac{1}{5}\cdot 100\pi = 20\pi = 62.8319\ldots$, so \ans{A \approx 62.83\text{ cm}^2}

Note the two formulas share the same fraction $\frac{72}{360}$; only the whole-circle quantity changes.

\problem{Circle $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 4y - 71 = 0$. (a) Centre-radius form and radius. (b) Distance to a $16$-unit chord. (c) Why no $24$-unit chord?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Complete both squares. Half of $-10$ is $-5$ and $(-5)^2 = 25$; half of $4$ is $2$ and $2^2 = 4$.
\begin{align*}
  (x^2 - 10x + 25) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) &= 71 + 25 + 4 \\
  (x - 5)^2 + (y + 2)^2 &= 100
\end{align*}
So \ans{(x-5)^2 + (y+2)^2 = 100}

The centre is $(5, -2)$ and $r = \sqrt{100}$, giving \ans{r = 10}

\textbf{Solution (b):} A radius drawn to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it, so the radius, half the chord, and the centre-to-chord distance form a right triangle with the radius as hypotenuse. Half the chord is $8$.
\[ d = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} \]
So \ans{6}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: the longest chord of any circle is a diameter, and this circle's diameter is $2 \times 10 = 20$. A chord of $24$ would have to be longer than the diameter, which is impossible. (Algebraically, the right triangle in part (b) would need $d = \sqrt{100 - 144}$, and no real distance has a negative square.)

\emph{Full credit:} computes the diameter as $20$ and states that no chord can exceed the diameter. \emph{Half credit:} gives the diameter but never names it as the maximum. \emph{No credit:} only observes that $24$ is bigger than $10$.
