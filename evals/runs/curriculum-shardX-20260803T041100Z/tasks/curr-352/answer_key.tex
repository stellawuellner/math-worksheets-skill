\problem{\textbf{Simplify $(3 + 5i) + (4 - 2i)$.}
\par
Real parts combine with real parts, imaginary parts with imaginary parts ---
$i$ behaves exactly like a variable here.
\par
Real: $3 + 4 = 7$. \quad Imaginary: $5i - 2i = 3i$.
\par
$\ans{7+3i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $(8 - i) - (3 + 6i)$.}
\par
Distribute the minus sign over both parts of the second number before
combining:
\[ 8 - i - 3 - 6i \]
Real: $8 - 3 = 5$. \quad Imaginary: $-i - 6i = -7i$.
\par
$\ans{5-7i}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $5 + 5i$ means only the real part of the second number was
negated.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $3i(4 - 7i)$.}
\par
Distribute, then use $i^2 = -1$:
\[ 3i \cdot 4 - 3i \cdot 7i = 12i - 21i^2 = 12i - 21(-1) \]
The $i^2$ term becomes a real number, so it moves to the front.
\par
$\ans{21+12i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(2 + 3i)(5 - i)$.}
\par
FOIL first, replace $i^2$ second:
\[ 10 - 2i + 15i - 3i^2 = 10 + 13i - 3(-1) = 10 + 13i + 3 \]
\par
$\ans{13+13i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $i^{18}$.}
\par
Powers of $i$ repeat every four steps, so divide the exponent by $4$ and keep
the remainder: $18 = 4 \cdot 4 + 2$, remainder $2$.
\par
So $i^{18} = (i^4)^4 \cdot i^2 = 1^4 \cdot i^2 = i^2$.
\par
$\ans{-1}$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $(-4 + 7i) + (9 - 7i) - (2 - 3i)$.}
\par
Distribute the minus over the third number, then collect:
\[ -4 + 7i + 9 - 7i - 2 + 3i \]
Real: $-4 + 9 - 2 = 3$. \quad Imaginary: $7i - 7i + 3i = 3i$.
\par
The imaginary parts of the first two numbers are additive inverses, so they
cancel completely; the whole imaginary part of the answer comes from the third
number.
\par
$\ans{3+3i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(5 + 2i)(5 - 2i)$.}
\par
These are conjugates, so the outer and inner products cancel:
\[ 25 - 10i + 10i - 4i^2 = 25 - 4i^2 = 25 - 4(-1) = 25 + 4 \]
The result is real because the $-4i^2$ term turns into $+4$: a complex number
times its conjugate is always the real number $a^2 + b^2$.
\par
$\ans{29}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $21$ is the diagnostic wrong answer --- it means $i^2$ was
treated as $+1$ (or the $-4i^2$ was simply read as $-4$). Every product problem
on this sheet turns on that one substitution.}

\problem{\textbf{Expand $(4 - 3i)^2$.}
\par
Squaring a binomial is multiplying it by itself, so there are four products,
not two:
\[ (4-3i)(4-3i) = 16 - 12i - 12i + 9i^2 = 16 - 24i + 9(-1) \]
\par
$\ans{7-24i}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $25$ means the student squared each term separately and
dropped the middle. $16 + 9$ is not $(4-3i)^2$; it is the square of the
modulus.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $i^{43} + i^{20}$.}
\par
Take each exponent mod $4$.
\par
$43 = 4 \cdot 10 + 3$, so $i^{43} = i^3 = -i$.
\par
$20 = 4 \cdot 5 + 0$, so $i^{20} = i^0 = 1$.
\par
Adding: $-i + 1$, which in standard form is written real part first.
\par
$\ans{1-i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Solve $x^2 - 6x + 13 = 0$ over the complex numbers.}
\par
The discriminant is negative, which is exactly when the solutions are a
conjugate pair:
\[ \Delta = (-6)^2 - 4(1)(13) = 36 - 52 = -16 \]
\[ x = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{-16}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm 4i}{2} \]
Divide both parts of the numerator by $2$.
\par
$\ans{x=3+2i \ \text{or} \ x=3-2i}$
\par\smallskip
Check by multiplying: $(3+2i)^2 - 6(3+2i) + 13 = 5 + 12i - 18 - 12i + 13 = 0$
$\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Multiply $(2 + i)(3 - 2i)(1 + i)$.}
\par
Do it in two stages, returning to standard form after each stage --- carrying
an unsimplified $i^2$ into the next product is where this problem goes wrong.
\par
Stage 1: $(2+i)(3-2i) = 6 - 4i + 3i - 2i^2 = 6 - i + 2 = 8 - i$.
\par
Stage 2: $(8-i)(1+i) = 8 + 8i - i - i^2 = 8 + 7i + 1$.
\par
$\ans{9+7i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: expand $\bigl(x-(5+i)\bigr)\bigl(x-(5-i)\bigr)$ and
state its zeros.}
\par
Group the real part with $x$ and treat the pair as a difference of squares:
\[ \bigl((x-5) - i\bigr)\bigl((x-5) + i\bigr) = (x-5)^2 - i^2 = (x-5)^2 + 1 \]
Expand the square: $x^2 - 10x + 25 + 1$.
\par
$\ans{x^2-10x+26}$
\par
The factors were built from $x$ minus each root, so the zeros are exactly the
two numbers that were subtracted.
\par
$\ans{x=5+i \ \text{or} \ x=5-i}$
\par\smallskip
The coefficients came out real because the two roots are conjugates: their sum
$(5+i)+(5-i) = 10$ and their product $(5+i)(5-i) = 26$ are both real, and those
are precisely the coefficients the expansion produces.}
