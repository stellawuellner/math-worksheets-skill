\problem{12 eggs make 4 equal pancake batches. Eggs per batch?}

\textbf{Solution:} Sharing 12 eggs equally among 4 groups is the division
$12 \div 4 = 3$ — each circle in the model ends up with the same count. The unit
rate is 3 eggs per batch. \ans{3}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{60 pages in 3 hours. Pages in one hour?}

\textbf{Solution:} To go from 3 hours to one hour, divide both rows of the table
by 3: $60 \div 3 = 20$ pages per hour. \ans{20}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{One ticket costs 8 dollars. Cost of 7 tickets?}

\textbf{Solution:} The unit rate is already given (8 dollars per ticket), so
multiply by the number of tickets: $8 \times 7 = 56$ dollars. \ans{56}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{6 comic books cost 18 dollars. Dollars per book?}

\textbf{Solution:} Unit price $=$ total cost $\div$ number of books:
$18 \div 6 = 3$ dollars per book. \ans{3}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{150 miles in 3 hours. Speed as a unit rate?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``Miles per hour'' puts miles on top: $150 \div 3 = 50$.
\ans{50\ \text{mph}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{24 posters every 2 hours. Complete the table for 1 hour and 5 hours.}

\textbf{Solution:} Unit rate first: $24 \div 2 = 12$ posters in 1 hour. \ans{12}

Then use the rate: $12 \times 5 = 60$ posters in 5 hours. \ans{60}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{A 28-oz jar fills 4 equal serving bowls. Ounces per serving?}

\textbf{Solution:} $28 \div 4 = 7$ ounces in one serving. \ans{7\ \text{oz}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Offer A: 3 pens for 12 dollars. Offer B: 5 pens for 15 dollars. Which
is the better deal?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Offer A: $12 \div 3 = 4$ dollars per pen. \ans{4}

(b) Offer B: $15 \div 5 = 3$ dollars per pen. \ans{3}

(c) $4 > 3$, so offer B costs less per pen — B is the better
deal. \ans{4 > 3\text{, offer B}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{Car A: 240 miles on 8 gallons. Car B: 210 miles on 6 gallons. Which
goes farther per gallon?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Car A: $240 \div 8 = 30$ miles per gallon. \ans{30}

(b) Car B: $210 \div 6 = 35$ miles per gallon. \ans{35}

(c) $30 < 35$, so car B goes farther on one gallon. \ans{30 < 35\text{, car B}}

\vspace{0.3cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.3cm}

\problem{6-pack for 12 dollars vs 10-pack for 20 dollars. Compare unit prices
and interpret.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) 6-pack: $12 \div 6 = 2$ dollars per can. \ans{2}

(b) 10-pack: $20 \div 10 = 2$ dollars per can. \ans{2}

(c) The unit prices are equal. \ans{2 = 2}

(d) \textbf{Interpretation (instructor judges):} Full credit says both packs
charge 2 dollars per can, so neither is a better deal per can — a shopper can
choose by how many cans they need, not by price per can.
