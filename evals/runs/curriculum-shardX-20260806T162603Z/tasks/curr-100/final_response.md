# Data Displays and Shapes — Grade 3 mixed review (12 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ws_mixedreview_curr100.pdf`** — the student worksheet (4 pages)
- **`ss_mixedreview_curr100.pdf`** — a 2-page skills summary
- **`ak_mixedreview_curr100.pdf`** — the full step-by-step answer key (3 pages)

## What the worksheet covers

Three references sit at the top and all twelve problems draw on them: **Bar Graph A** (the shapes in
the class shape box — 8 squares, 6 rectangles, 5 triangles, 3 trapezoids), **Line Plot B** (how many
sides each cut-out shape has), and a four-shape **Shape Set** labelled P, Q, R, T.

The sheet is built to force method choice rather than repetition:

- **1–3** are the warm-up, all on Bar Graph A: read one bar, add them all, compare two.
- From problem 4 on, the three strands **interleave** — a shape-attribute question, then a combined
  one, then a shape one, then a display one, and so on — so the child has to decide *what kind of
  question this is* before counting.
- The combined problems (**5, 8, 11**) are the point of the review: each one needs a shape fact
  before any arithmetic. Problem 5 counts quadrilaterals (triangles excluded), problem 8 counts
  shapes with four square corners (trapezoids excluded), and both use the same graph as problem 2,
  which asked for everything. Three questions, one display, three different answers.
- **9** pairs a count with a written explanation of why "has four sides" is not the attribute the
  group asks for.
- **12** is the closing synthesis: two new square cut-outs join Line Plot B, and the child works out
  the new four-sided count and explains why the three-sided column does not move.

Difficulty ramps 1 → 5.

## What is verified, and what is not

- **12 of the 14 checks are machine-verified** with SymPy, covering **10 of the 12 problems fully**.
  The bar-graph values, the line-plot columns and the shape attribute table all live in the
  verification data, so the printed displays and the answers come from one source.
- **Two responses are instructor-judged**, marked `---` in the key's Quick Answers list: the written
  explanations in 9(b) and 12(b). The key gives a model answer for each and says what falls short —
  for 12(b), giving the number 7 without explaining why the three-sided column is unchanged is not
  full credit.
- Three **common wrong answers** are declared and machine-checked as genuinely wrong, then printed in
  the key: 22 on problem 5 (counted every bar including the triangles), 2 on problem 6 (assumed every
  four-sided shape has two pairs of parallel sides), and 17 on problem 8 (left the trapezoids in the
  four-square-corners group). Those three are the whole misconception story of this review.

## The study guide

Three sections mirroring the three strands — reading a display, testing a shape's attributes, and
using a shape fact *inside* a data question, with the two-step order spelled out (decide the group
first, add second). Each has a rule box, a worked example that opens with the strategy, and a try-it
with the answer upside down inside the box.

The grade level and standards codes (3.MD, 3.G.A) appear on the answer key only.
