\problem{Second, third and fourth terms $11$, $18$, $25$. Find $d$, $a_1$, and $a_{10}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Consecutive terms differ by the step, so $18 - 11$ gives it directly. \ans{d = 7}

Term $2$ is one step past term $1$, so walk back: $11 - 7$. \ans{a_1 = 4}

The recursive rule is $a_1 = 4$, $a_n = a_{n-1} + 7$ for $n \ge 2$. Reaching term $10$ takes nine steps from term $1$: $4 + 9(7) = 4 + 63$. \ans{a_{10} = 67}

\problem{$a_3 = 20$ and $a_6 = 8$. Find $d$, $a_1$, and $a_9$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Terms $3$ and $6$ are three steps apart, so the step is $\frac{8 - 20}{3} = \frac{-12}{3}$. \ans{d = -4}

Walk back two steps from term $3$: $20 - 2(-4) = 20 + 8$. \ans{a_1 = 28}

The rule is $a_1 = 28$, $a_n = a_{n-1} - 4$. Term $9$ is eight steps on: $28 + 8(-4) = 28 - 32$. \ans{a_9 = -4}

\problem{Theatre rows: row $4$ holds $18$ seats, row $8$ holds $30$. Find $d$, row $1$, and row $12$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Rows $4$ and $8$ are four rows apart: $\frac{30 - 18}{4} = \frac{12}{4}$. \ans{d = 3}

Row $1$ is three rows back from row $4$: $18 - 3(3) = 18 - 9$. \ans{a_1 = 9}

With $a_1 = 9$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 3$, row $12$ is eleven steps on: $9 + 11(3) = 9 + 33$. \ans{a_{12} = 42}

\problem{$a_1 = 6$, $a_n = a_{n-1} + 5$. (a) Next three terms. (b) $a_{20}$. (c) Why the step alone is not enough.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Add $5$ each time. First step: $6 + 5$. \ans{a_2 = 11}

Second step: $11 + 5$. \ans{a_3 = 16}

Third step: $16 + 5$. \ans{a_4 = 21}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Term $20$ is nineteen steps past term $1$, so $6 + 19(5) = 6 + 95$. \ans{a_{20} = 101}

\textbf{(c) --- instructor-judged.} A correct answer says the step rule alone leaves the sequence undetermined: it says every term is five more than the one before, but without a stated starting value there is one such sequence through every possible first number. The recursive rule needs both an anchor and a step. Full credit for naming the missing starting value and saying that infinitely many sequences fit the step alone; partial credit for saying only that the first term is missing.

\problem{$a_5 = 3$ and $a_9 = 23$. Find $d$, $a_1$, and $a_{15}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Terms $5$ and $9$ are four steps apart: $\frac{23 - 3}{4} = \frac{20}{4}$. \ans{d = 5}

Walk back four steps from term $5$: $3 - 4(5) = 3 - 20$. \ans{a_1 = -17}

With $a_1 = -17$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 5$, term $15$ is fourteen steps on: $-17 + 14(5) = -17 + 70$. \ans{a_{15} = 53}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Weekly deposits: balance \$145 after week $3$, \$225 after week $7$. Find $d$, $a_1$, and the balance after week $12$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Weeks $3$ and $7$ are four deposits apart: $\frac{225 - 145}{4} = \frac{80}{4}$. \ans{d = 20}

Walk back two weeks from week $3$: $145 - 2(20) = 145 - 40$. \ans{a_1 = 105}

With $a_1 = 105$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 20$, week $12$ is eleven steps on: $105 + 11(20) = 105 + 220$. \ans{a_{12} = 325}

\problem{$a_1 = 5$, $a_n = a_{n-1} + 6$. (a) Find $a_{10}$. (b) Find the sum of the first ten terms.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Nine steps past the start: $5 + 9(6) = 5 + 54$. \ans{a_{10} = 59}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Pair the first and last terms: an arithmetic sum of $n$ terms is $\frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)$, here $\frac{10}{2}(5 + 59) = 5(64)$. \ans{\text{Sum} = 320}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$a_2 = -1$ and $a_6 = 15$. Find $d$, $a_1$, and $a_{11}$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Terms $2$ and $6$ are four steps apart: $\frac{15 - (-1)}{4} = \frac{16}{4}$. \ans{d = 4}

Walk back one step from term $2$: $-1 - 4$. \ans{a_1 = -5}

With $a_1 = -5$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 4$, term $11$ is \emph{ten} steps on, not eleven: $-5 + 10(4) = -5 + 40$. \ans{a_{11} = 35}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Ladder rungs: rung $2$ at $34$ cm, rung $5$ at $79$ cm. Find $d$, rung $1$, and rung $10$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Rungs $2$ and $5$ are three gaps apart: $\frac{79 - 34}{3} = \frac{45}{3}$. \ans{d = 15}

Rung $1$ is one gap below rung $2$: $34 - 15$. \ans{a_1 = 19}

With $a_1 = 19$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 15$, rung $10$ is nine gaps up: $19 + 9(15) = 19 + 135$. \ans{a_{10} = 154\ \text{cm}}

\problem{Sequence $A$: $a_1 = 3$, step $+5$. Sequence $B$: $b_1 = 57$, step $-4$. Where are they equal, and at what value?}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Write each term with its number of steps: $a_n = 3 + 5(n-1)$ and $b_n = 57 - 4(n-1)$. Setting them equal, $3 + 5(n-1) = 57 - 4(n-1)$, so $9(n-1) = 54$ and $n - 1 = 6$. \ans{n = 7}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Substitute back: $3 + 5(6) = 3 + 30$. Check with $B$: $57 - 4(6) = 57 - 24 = 33$ $\checkmark$ \ans{\text{value} = 33}
