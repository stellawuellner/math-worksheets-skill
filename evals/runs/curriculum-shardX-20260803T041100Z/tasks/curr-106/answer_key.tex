\noindent\textbf{How to grade this.} The four partial products are the work; the
sum is only the last line. If a student's total is wrong, look first at whether
all four regions were filled --- the classic error is keeping only
tens\,$\times$\,tens and ones\,$\times$\,ones.

\problem{$23 \times 14$, model split as $20 + 3$ across and $10 + 4$ down.

\textbf{Solution:} Fill the four regions, then add.
\[ 20 \times 10 = 200 \qquad 3 \times 10 = 30 \qquad 20 \times 4 = 80 \qquad 3 \times 4 = 12 \]
Adding the partial products: $200 + 30 + 80 + 12 = 322$, and the model is the
whole rectangle, so $23 \times 14 = \ans{322}$.}

\problem{$32 \times 15$, model split as $30 + 2$ across and $10 + 5$ down.

\textbf{Solution:}
\[ 30 \times 10 = 300 \qquad 2 \times 10 = 20 \qquad 30 \times 5 = 150 \qquad 2 \times 5 = 10 \]
$300 + 20 + 150 + 10 = 480$, so $32 \times 15 = \ans{480}$.}

\problem{$26 \times 34$, model split as $20 + 6$ across and $30 + 4$ down.

\textbf{Solution:} Both cross regions are large here, which is exactly why they
cannot be skipped.
\[ 20 \times 30 = 600 \qquad 6 \times 30 = 180 \qquad 20 \times 4 = 80 \qquad 6 \times 4 = 24 \]
$600 + 180 + 80 + 24 = 884$, so $26 \times 34 = \ans{884}$.}

\problem{Estimate $38 \times 42$ by rounding each factor to the nearest ten.

\textbf{Solution:} $38$ is nearer to $40$ than to $30$; $42$ is nearer to $40$
than to $50$. Rounding \emph{both} factors gives $40 \times 40$, and
$4 \times 4 = 16$ tells you the estimate is $16$ hundreds:
$\ans{1600}$. (The exact product is $1596$, so the estimate is a good size
check.)}

\problem{$47 \times 23$, model split as $40 + 7$ across and $20 + 3$ down.

\textbf{Solution:}
\[ 40 \times 20 = 800 \qquad 7 \times 20 = 140 \qquad 40 \times 3 = 120 \qquad 7 \times 3 = 21 \]
$800 + 140 + 120 + 21 = 1081$, so $47 \times 23 = \ans{1081}$.}

\problem{$54 \times 26$ with three regions printed ($1000$, $80$, $300$) and one
region empty.

\textbf{Solution:} The empty region is the row labelled $6$ meeting the column
labelled $4$, so it holds $4 \times 6 = \ans{24}$.

Now add all four regions: $1000 + 300 + 80 + 24 = 1404$, so
$54 \times 26 = \ans{1404}$.}

\problem{$68 \times 15$, model split as $60 + 8$ across and $10 + 5$ down.

\textbf{Solution:}
\[ 60 \times 10 = 600 \qquad 8 \times 10 = 80 \qquad 60 \times 5 = 300 \qquad 8 \times 5 = 40 \]
$600 + 80 + 300 + 40 = 1020$, so $68 \times 15 = \ans{1020}$.}

\problem{Estimate $63 \times 29$ by rounding each factor to the nearest ten.

\textbf{Solution:} $63$ rounds down to $60$. The second factor is the trap:
$29$ is one away from $30$ and nine away from $20$, so it rounds \emph{up}.
The estimate is $60 \times 30 = \ans{1800}$. (Exact: $1827$.)}

\problem{$45 \times 37$, student supplies the split.

\textbf{Solution:} Split each factor by place value: $45 = 40 + 5$ and
$37 = 30 + 7$. That labels the model $40$ and $5$ across, $30$ and $7$ down.
\[ 40 \times 30 = 1200 \qquad 5 \times 30 = 150 \qquad 40 \times 7 = 280 \qquad 5 \times 7 = 35 \]
$1200 + 280 + 150 + 35 = 1665$, so $45 \times 37 = \ans{1665}$.}

\problem{Maya's model for $24 \times 36$: she wrote only tens\,$\times$\,tens and
ones\,$\times$\,ones and answered $624$.

\textbf{Solution:} Maya found $20 \times 30 = 600$ and $4 \times 6 = 24$, then
stopped. She left out the two cross regions, the ones\,$\times$\,tens and
tens\,$\times$\,ones rectangles:
\[ 4 \times 30 = 120 \qquad 20 \times 6 = 120 \]
All four regions together give $600 + 120 + 120 + 24 = 864$, so
$24 \times 36 = \ans{864}$. A one-sentence answer: \emph{she multiplied only the
matching parts and skipped the two rectangles where tens meet ones.} The
estimate $20 \times 40 = 800$ also shows $624$ is far too small.}
