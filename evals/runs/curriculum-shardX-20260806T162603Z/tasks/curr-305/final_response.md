# Building Multi-Step Arguments from Givens — high-school geometry (10 problems)

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_arguments_curr305.pdf`, 7 pages) — 10 problems on getting from a
  stated given to a claim without skipping a link. It opens with two short warm-ups where
  the statements of a valid argument are printed out of order and the student numbers them
  and writes the reason for each. After that the three subskills interleave, so the student
  has to decide which chain the problem needs: order-and-justify, a segment argument
  (midpoint, segment addition, overlapping segments), or an angle argument (linear pair,
  vertical angles, bisector). The last problem is the synthesis: two givens, a bisector and
  a linear pair, chained into one equation.
- **Answer key** (`ak_arguments_curr305.pdf`, 4 pages) — every argument written out as a
  numbered chain with the reason on each line, every equation solved step by step, and a
  substitution check on each numeric answer.
- **Study guide** (`ss_arguments_curr305.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections matching the three
  subskills, each with a rule box, a worked example whose first step names the strategy,
  and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.

## What is verified, and what is not

**10 of the 17 responses are machine-checked with SymPy** — every numeric answer:
$x = 4$ and $AB = 30$ cm, $x = 20$ and $m\angle CVD = 75^\circ$, $AC = 15.2$ cm,
$x = 15$, $x = 4$ and $RT = 28$ cm, $x = 12$, and $x = 15$. All six study-guide answers
are verified the same way. Three problems (3, 4 and 8) are checked end to end --- the key's
"What is verified" note says so; the other seven each carry a written part as well.

**7 responses are flagged for your judgement** — the three ordering problems (1, 2, 5) and
the written arguments in 6(a), 7(a), 9(a) and 10(a). Ordering a chain and naming its
reasons is exactly the skill this sheet is for, and it is prose plus structure, which no
computer algebra system grades. The answer key prints the correct order with each reason,
and a grading note for the harder ones saying which link students usually drop (the
substitution step that joins two separate sums, and the shared stretch added with a named
property). The Quick Answers bank marks these `---`; no `[unchecked]` marks appear.

## Notes

Standards: HSG-CO.A for the midpoint and bisector definitions, HSG-CO.C for the linear
pair, vertical angle and complementary-angle theorems. Difficulty ramps 1 to 5. Every
diagram carries labels only — all measurements are in the text — so no figure can be read
as belonging to a neighbouring problem.
