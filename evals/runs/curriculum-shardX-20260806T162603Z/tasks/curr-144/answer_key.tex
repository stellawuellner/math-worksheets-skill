\problem{Sticker: rectangle $7$ cm by $4$ cm --- area}

\textbf{Solution:} Area covers a flat surface with unit \emph{squares}:
$7 \times 4 = 28$ of them fit. The unit is square centimeters.
\ans{28 \text{ cm}^2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Box packed with centimeter cubes: $6$ cubes per layer, $4$ layers}

\textbf{Solution:} Volume counts unit \emph{cubes}: $6$ cubes in each layer,
$4$ layers, so $6 \times 4 = 24$ cubes fill the box.
\ans{24 \text{ cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Pick the best unit (cm, m$^2$, mL, or cm$^3$)}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) A classroom floor is a flat surface, so it takes square units:
\emph{square meters} (m$^2$).

(b) Juice fills space inside the glass, so it takes a capacity or cubic
unit: \emph{milliliters} (mL) --- cubic centimeters (cm$^3$) also correct.

(c) A pencil's length runs along one dimension: \emph{centimeters} (cm).

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Cereal box $5 \times 3 \times 2$ cm --- volume}

\textbf{Solution:} $V = \ell \times w \times h = 5 \times 3 \times 2$. The
base holds $5 \times 3 = 15$ cubes and there are $2$ layers, giving $30$
cubes. \ans{30 \text{ cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Tomas's jewelry box: ``$6 \times 2 \times 3 = 36$ cm$^2$''}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) The number $36$ is correct, but the \emph{unit} is wrong: volume counts
unit cubes filling the box, so it needs \emph{cubic} centimeters. Square
centimeters measure a flat surface. A correct response must say the unit
should be cubed, not squared.

(b) Corrected: $6 \times 2 \times 3 = 36$, in cubic units.
\ans{36 \text{ cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Aquarium $40 \times 20 \times 30$ cm}

\textbf{Solution:} $V = 40 \times 20 \times 30$. Step by step:
$40 \times 20 = 800$ for the base, times $30$ layers gives $24{,}000$.
\ans{24000 \text{ cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Lena's conversion: ``$2$ m$^2 = 2 \times 100 = 200$ cm$^2$''}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) Lena used the \emph{length} conversion once, but square units need it
\emph{twice}: a square meter is $100$ cm by $100$ cm. A correct response
must say the factor of $100$ has to be applied twice (or that
$1$ m$^2 = 10000$ cm$^2$).

(b) Corrected: $2 \times 100 \times 100 = 20{,}000$.
\ans{20000 \text{ cm}^2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: cubes with edges $2$ cm and $4$ cm}

\textbf{Solution:}
(a) $V = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$. \ans{8 \text{ cm}^3}

(b) $V = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$. \ans{64 \text{ cm}^3}

(c) $64 \div 8 = 8$, so the large volume is \ans{8} times the small one.

(d) Roy doubled the volume once, but the edge is a factor \emph{three}
times: length, width, and height all doubled, so the volume is multiplied by
$2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$, not by $2$. A correct response must say all three
dimensions doubled.
