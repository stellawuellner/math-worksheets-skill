\problem{A $6$ m ladder makes an angle of $62^\circ$ with level ground. How far
up the wall does it reach, in metres?}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The ladder is the hypotenuse and the wall height is
the side opposite the $62^\circ$ angle, so use sine:
\[ \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \sin 62^\circ
   \qquad \text{height} = 6 \sin 62^\circ = 5.2977\ldots \]
Rounded to two decimal places, \ans{5.30 \text{ m}}

\problem{A surveyor stands $40$ ft from a flagpole; the angle of elevation to
the top is $35^\circ$. Find the height above eye level, in feet.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The $40$ ft run is adjacent to the angle and the
height is opposite it, so use tangent:
\[ \text{height} = 40 \tan 35^\circ = 28.0083\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{28.01 \text{ ft}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{From the top of a $25$ m lighthouse the angle of depression to a boat
is $18^\circ$. Find the horizontal distance to the boat, in metres.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The angle of depression at the top equals the angle
of elevation at the boat, so in the right triangle the $25$ m height is opposite
$18^\circ$ and the horizontal distance is adjacent:
\[ \tan 18^\circ = \frac{25}{d} \qquad d = \frac{25}{\tan 18^\circ} = 76.9421\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{76.94 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A guy wire runs from an anchor $12$ m from the base of a mast to a
point $16$ m up the mast. Find the angle of elevation of the wire, to the
nearest tenth of a degree.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The height up the mast is opposite the angle and the
ground distance is adjacent, so the tangent is known and the angle comes from
the inverse tangent:
\[ \tan x = \frac{16}{12} \qquad x = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{16}{12}\right) = 53.1301\ldots^\circ \]
Therefore \ans{x = 53.1^\circ}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A drone hovers $120$ m above ground; the angle of depression to a
landing pad is $41^\circ$. Find the straight-line distance to the pad, in
metres.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The $120$ m altitude is opposite the $41^\circ$
elevation angle at the pad, and the straight-line distance is the hypotenuse:
\[ \sin 41^\circ = \frac{120}{c} \qquad c = \frac{120}{\sin 41^\circ} = 182.9104\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{182.91 \text{ m}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A hiker stands $85$ ft from a cliff base; the angle of elevation to the
top is $58^\circ$. Find the length of the line of sight, in feet.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The $85$ ft is adjacent to the $58^\circ$ angle and
the line of sight is the hypotenuse, so use cosine:
\[ \cos 58^\circ = \frac{85}{c} \qquad c = \frac{85}{\cos 58^\circ} = 160.4018\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{160.40 \text{ ft}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{From a window $15$ m above ground the angle of elevation to a tower top
is $32^\circ$; the tower is $48$ m away horizontally. Find the total height of
the tower above the ground, in metres.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution.} The right triangle sits above the window line: its
adjacent side is $48$ m and its opposite side is the part of the tower above the
window.
\[ \text{above window} = 48 \tan 32^\circ = 29.9937\ldots \]
The window itself is $15$ m up, so the total height is
$15 + 48\tan 32^\circ = 44.9937\ldots$, giving \ans{44.99 \text{ m}}

\problem{A zip line runs from a platform $22$ m above ground to a landing point;
the angle of depression along the line is $27^\circ$. (a) Find the length of the
zip line, in metres. (b) Find the horizontal distance from the base of the
platform to the landing point, in metres. (c) Explain why the angle of
depression at the platform equals the angle of elevation at the landing point.}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (a).} The $22$ m drop is opposite the $27^\circ$
angle at the landing point and the zip line is the hypotenuse:
\[ c = \frac{22}{\sin 27^\circ} = 48.4592\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{48.46 \text{ m}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b).} The horizontal distance is adjacent to the same
angle:
\[ d = \frac{22}{\tan 27^\circ} = 43.1774\ldots \]
Therefore \ans{43.18 \text{ m}}

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A complete response says
the horizontal line at the platform and the ground are parallel, and the zip
line is a transversal cutting both, so the angle of depression at the top and
the angle of elevation at the bottom are alternate interior angles and therefore
equal. Award full credit only when the parallel lines and the alternate-interior
angle relationship both appear; simply asserting that the two angles are the
same is not a justification.
