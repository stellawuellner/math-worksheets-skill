# A Fraction of a Whole Number or Set — Grades 4–5

Three PDFs are ready: the student worksheet, a full step-by-step answer key,
and a two-page study guide.

## What the worksheet covers

Ten problems, all on the same idea taken in three directions: *a fraction of a
set is equal groups*. The two moves — divide by the bottom number to make the
groups, multiply by the top number to take some of them — are stated in the
directions and then practised until they are automatic.

1. **One equal group** (problems 1, 2, 7) — $\frac{1}{4}$ of 24 crayons and
   $\frac{1}{3}$ of 21 stickers come with the counters actually drawn, so your
   child rings the groups before writing anything. Problem 7 applies the same
   move to $2.4$ litres, which shows the idea does not depend on counting
   objects.
2. **Several equal groups** (3, 4, 6, 8, 10) — bar models your child divides
   and shades. Problem 6 uses minutes; problem 8 has a second step (it asks how
   many students do *not* ride the bus, which is not the number the fraction
   gives); problem 10 is an error-analysis item where a classmate makes the
   groups correctly and then forgets the top number.
3. **Working backwards** (5, 9) — given the part, find the whole. Problem 5
   comes with the bar already partitioned; problem 9 gives three eighths at
   once, so the part has to be shared between three before the whole is
   rebuilt.

## What was verified

**Every one of the ten answers was recomputed independently before anything was
printed**, and the answer key was then bound to that verified data problem by
problem — nothing on this sheet is flagged for manual review. The gate chain
finished fully green.

Five problems also carry declared *misconception traps*, each one confirmed to
produce a visibly different number from the correct answer, so the problem can
genuinely tell the two apart:

- dividing by the top number and multiplying by the bottom (problem 3)
- stopping after making the groups and reporting just one (problems 4 and 10)
- multiplying by the denominator instead of dividing (problem 7)
- answering the opposite of what was asked (problem 8)

The answer key prints each of these as a "common wrong answer" note, so if your
child's answer is one of those numbers you know immediately which step went
wrong rather than just that it did.

## The study guide

Two pages, three sections — one equal group, several equal groups, and working
backwards — each with a rule box, a worked example whose first step explains
*why* that move applies, and a try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside
the box. Two of the examples carry their own bar model. The closing watch-out
box names the two errors this topic reliably produces: dividing by the top
number, and stopping after the division.

## Notes

The grade level does not print on the worksheet or the study guide by design —
it appears on the answer key, together with a generated curriculum section
listing the standard covered (5.NF.B) and the difficulty range.
