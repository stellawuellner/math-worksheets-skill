\problem{Cylindrical silo, radius $4$ m, height $7$ m. Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution:} $V = \pi r^2 h = \pi (4)^2 (7) = 112\pi = 351.8584\ldots$, so \ans{V \approx 351.86\text{ m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Block $1.2 \times 0.8 \times 0.5$ m with a cylindrical hole of radius $0.2$ m drilled through the $0.5$ m height.}

\textbf{Solution:} Volume of concrete $=$ block $-$ hole. The hole is a cylinder whose height is the block's $0.5$ m.
\begin{align*}
  V &= (1.2)(0.8)(0.5) - \pi (0.2)^2 (0.5) \\
    &= 0.48 - 0.0628\ldots
\end{align*}
So \ans{V \approx 0.42\text{ m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Open-topped cylinder, radius $2$ m, height $6$ m. Find the sheet metal for wall $+$ base.}

\textbf{Solution:} The wall is a rectangle when unrolled, of area $2\pi r h$; the base is one circle, of area $\pi r^2$. There is no top.
\[ S = 2\pi(2)(6) + \pi(2)^2 = 24\pi + 4\pi = 28\pi = 87.9645\ldots \]
So \ans{S \approx 87.96\text{ m}^2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Oak block, volume $2400$ cm$^3$, density $0.75$ g/cm$^3$. Find the mass.}

\textbf{Solution:} Density is mass per unit volume, so mass $=$ volume $\times$ density $= 2400 \times 0.75$. \ans{1800\text{ g}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Silo: cylinder radius $3$ m, height $8$ m, topped by a hemisphere of radius $3$ m.}

\textbf{Solution:} Add the two pieces. A hemisphere is half a sphere, so its volume is $\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3$.
\begin{align*}
  V &= \pi (3)^2 (8) + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi (3)^3 \\
    &= 72\pi + 18\pi = 90\pi = 282.7433\ldots
\end{align*}
So \ans{V \approx 282.74\text{ m}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Bronze statuette, mass $5400$ g, density $8.8$ g/cm$^3$. Find the volume.}

\textbf{Solution:} Rearranging density $=$ mass $\div$ volume gives volume $=$ mass $\div$ density.
\[ V = \frac{5400}{8.8} = 613.6363\ldots \]
So \ans{V \approx 613.64\text{ cm}^3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Cylindrical tank holding $15$ m$^3$ with radius fixed at $1.5$ m. Find the height.}

\textbf{Solution:} The capacity is the constraint, so solve $\pi r^2 h = 15$ for $h$.
\[ h = \frac{15}{\pi (1.5)^2} = \frac{15}{2.25\pi} = 2.1220\ldots \]
So \ans{h \approx 2.12\text{ m}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Hopper: cone radius $5$ cm, slant $13$ cm, on a closed-bottom cylinder radius $5$ cm, height $9$ cm.}

\textbf{Solution:} Three surfaces are exposed --- the cone's curved surface, the cylinder's curved wall, and the cylinder's base. The disc where the two solids meet is inside the solid and is not painted.
\begin{align*}
  S &= \pi r \ell + 2\pi r h + \pi r^2 \\
    &= \pi (5)(13) + 2\pi (5)(9) + \pi (5)^2 \\
    &= 65\pi + 90\pi + 25\pi = 180\pi = 565.4867\ldots
\end{align*}
So \ans{S \approx 565.49\text{ cm}^2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Glass cube of edge $6$ cm with a hemispherical hollow of radius $2$ cm; density $2.5$ g/cm$^3$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Find the glass volume first, then convert to mass with the density.
\begin{align*}
  V &= 6^3 - \tfrac{2}{3}\pi (2)^3 = 216 - 16.7551\ldots = 199.2448\ldots \\
  m &= 2.5 \times 199.2448\ldots = 498.1121\ldots
\end{align*}
So \ans{m \approx 498.11\text{ g}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Grain bin, radius $2.5$ m, capacity $60$ m$^3$, steel $18$ kg per m$^2$ on wall and base only.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} The capacity fixes the height:
\[ h = \frac{60}{\pi (2.5)^2} = \frac{60}{6.25\pi} = 3.0557\ldots \]
So \ans{h \approx 3.06\text{ m}}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Steel area $=$ curved wall $+$ base. Keeping $h$ exact avoids rounding error:
\begin{align*}
  \text{wall} &= 2\pi (2.5) h = 2\pi (2.5)\cdot \frac{60}{6.25\pi} = 48 \ \text{exactly} \\
  \text{base} &= \pi (2.5)^2 = 19.6349\ldots \\
  \text{mass} &= 18 (48 + 19.6349\ldots) = 1217.4292\ldots
\end{align*}
So \ans{m \approx 1217.43\text{ kg}}

\textbf{Solution (c) --- instructor-judged.} A model response: the capacity is fixed, so the height is not free --- it is $60/(\pi r^2)$, and doubling the radius divides the height by four. The curved wall is $2\pi r h$, and since $r$ doubles while $h$ is quartered the wall area actually \emph{halves}; but the base is $\pi r^2$, which is multiplied by four, from about $19.63$ to about $78.54$ m$^2$. Working it through, the wall drops from $48$ to $24$ m$^2$ while the base rises from about $19.63$ to about $78.54$, so the total area rises from about $67.63$ to about $102.54$ m$^2$ and the steel goes \emph{up}, not down.

\emph{Full credit:} says the height is forced by the fixed capacity and separates the wall's behaviour from the base's. \emph{Half credit:} says only that the base grows with $r^2$. \emph{No credit:} treats the height as unchanged.
