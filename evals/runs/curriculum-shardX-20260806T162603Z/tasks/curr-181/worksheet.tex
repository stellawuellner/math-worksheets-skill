\wstitleblock{Writing Inequalities from Verbal Constraints}{Grade 7}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Turning a limit in words into an inequality: at most and at least, more
than and fewer than, and limits with a starting amount}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Four phrases carry the whole sheet:\par
        \textbf{at most} / no more than $\rightarrow \le$ \qquad
        \textbf{at least} / minimum $\rightarrow \ge$ \qquad
        \textbf{fewer than} / under $\rightarrow <$ \qquad
        \textbf{more than} / over $\rightarrow >$
  \item \textbf{The test-value model.} Before you solve, try numbers either side
        of the limit and mark which ones the situation allows. The boundary is
        the number where the answer changes, and whether the boundary itself is
        allowed is exactly what tells $\le$ apart from $<$.
  \item For each problem: name the unknown with the letter given, write an
        inequality that says what the sentence says, then write the solution on
        the answer line (for example $x \le 6$).
\end{itemize}

into it weighs 25 kilograms. Let $b$ be the number of boxes.\par\vspace{4pt}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
number of boxes $b$ & 17 & 18 & 19 \\ \hline
mass of the boxes, $25b$ kg & & & \\[0.35cm] \hline
allowed by the 450 kg limit? & & & \\[0.35cm] \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}\par\vspace{4pt}
Write the inequality for $b$ and give its solution.}
