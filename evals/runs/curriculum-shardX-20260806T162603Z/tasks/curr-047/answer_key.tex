\problem{Shapes A (triangle), B (gray square), C (tilted triangle),
D (rectangle). (a) How many have exactly 3 sides? (b) Letters?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count sides shape by shape, ignoring color and tilt:
A has 3, B has 4, C has 3, D has 4. Two shapes have exactly 3 sides. \ans{2}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} the letters are \textbf{A and C}.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Five shapes: B square, A triangle, D rectangle, E tilted square,
F circle. (a) How many have exactly 4 corners? (b) Letters?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) B, D, and E each have 4 corners; A has 3; the circle
F has none. That is three shapes. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} the letters are \textbf{B, D, and E} --- the
tilted square still counts.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Big gray triangle, small white triangle. (a) Sides of each? (b) Is
Kim right?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Each shape has three straight sides. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} Kim is not right. Both are triangles because
both have 3 straight sides --- size and color are not defining attributes,
so the two shapes belong in the same group.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Six shapes sorted by sides. (a) 3-sided? (b) 4-sided? (c) Neither?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The two triangles: \ans{2}

(b) The square, the tilted square, and the rectangle: \ans{3}

(c) Only the circle has no straight sides, so it fits neither group. \ans{1}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Six shapes sorted by corners. (a) 3 corners? (b) 4 corners?
(c) $>$ or $<$?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The four triangles: \ans{4}

(b) The square and the rectangle: \ans{2}

(c) Four is more than two, so \ans{4 > 2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Raj's color sort: 3 gray shapes and 5 white shapes. (a) How many in
all? (b) What should he count instead?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Add the two groups: $3 + 5 = 8$. \ans{8}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} color is not a defining attribute --- Raj
should sort by something countable that never changes, like the number of
sides or corners.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{The set with squares, rectangles, and pentagons. (a) 4-sided?
(b) 5-sided? (c) How many more 4-sided?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Two squares, two rectangles, and the tilted square:
\ans{5}

(b) The two pentagons: \ans{2}

(c) Find the difference: $5 - 2 = 3$. \ans{3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{A square, B rectangle, C triangle, D tilted square. (a) Corners of
the odd one out? (b) Its letter?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) A, B, and D all have 4 corners; the odd one out has
3. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} the letter is \textbf{C} --- the triangle.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Group rule: exactly 4 equal sides. Lily says the tilted shape is in
the wrong group. (a) Equal sides? (b) Does it belong?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Count them: the tilted shape has 4 equal sides.
\ans{4}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} yes, it belongs. It matches the rule exactly
--- 4 equal sides. ``Looks like a diamond'' is about how it sits, and
position is not a defining attribute.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: seven shapes. (a) 3-sided? (b) 4-sided? (c) 6-sided?
(d) Self-check sum?}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The three triangles: \ans{3}

(b) The square and the rectangle: \ans{2}

(c) The two hexagons: \ans{2}

(d) Add the group counts: $3 + 2 + 2 = 7$ --- all seven shapes are sorted,
none missed, none counted twice. \ans{7}
