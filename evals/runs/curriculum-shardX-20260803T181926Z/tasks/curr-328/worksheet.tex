\wstitleblock{Perimeter and Area of Coordinate Polygons}{High School Geometry \quad Coordinate Geometry}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Finding a polygon perimeter from coordinates, finding a polygon area
from coordinates, and using slope or midpoint to justify a shape before
measuring it}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The three tools, and when each one earns its place.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Distance} gives one side length:
$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$. A perimeter is the sum of the side
lengths, so find the ones you cannot read off the grid and add.
\item \textbf{Shoelace} gives the area of any polygon straight from its
vertices in order:
$A = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\sum (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\right|$. Splitting the
polygon into right triangles and rectangles gives the same number.
\item \textbf{Slope and midpoint} do not measure anything. They justify a
claim about the shape --- perpendicular sides, bisected diagonals --- which is
often what makes a shortcut legal.
\item Plot the polygon before computing. Every problem gives you a grid for it,
and a sketch catches a mis-copied coordinate in seconds.
\item Leave exact radicals unless the problem names a unit to round to, and
label areas in square units.
\end{itemize}
