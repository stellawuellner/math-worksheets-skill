\problem{\textbf{Whispering gallery:} $\dfrac{x^2}{169} + \dfrac{y^2}{144} = 1$
(feet). How far from the centre is each focus?

\textbf{Solution:} The larger denominator sits under $x^2$, so the major axis
is horizontal with $a^2 = 169$ and $b^2 = 144$. For an ellipse the focal
relation is $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ (subtract, because the foci lie inside the
curve): $c^2 = 169 - 144 = 25$, so $c = \sqrt{25}$. Each focus is that far
from the centre, one on each side. \ans{c = 5\ \text{ft}}}

\problem{\textbf{Radio towers:} $\dfrac{x^2}{36} - \dfrac{y^2}{64} = 1$
(kilometres). Give the vertices and the foci.

\textbf{Solution:} The $x^2$ term is positive, so the transverse axis is
horizontal: $a^2 = 36$ and $b^2 = 64$. The vertices are $(\pm a, 0)$ with
$a = \sqrt{36}$. \ans{(\pm 6,\, 0)}

For a hyperbola the focal relation adds: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$, so
$c = \sqrt{100}$ and the foci are $(\pm c, 0)$. \ans{(\pm 10,\, 0)}

Sanity check: $c > a$, as it must be --- the foci always sit beyond the
vertices on a hyperbola.}

\problem{\textbf{Sprinkler patch:} $\dfrac{(x-3)^2}{25} + \dfrac{(y+2)^2}{9} =
1$ (metres). Give the endpoints of the major axis, then the foci.

\textbf{Solution:} The centre is $(h, k) = (3, -2)$, read by flipping the signs
inside the parentheses. The larger denominator, $25$, sits under the $x$ term,
so the major axis is horizontal with $a = \sqrt{25} = 5$ and $b = \sqrt{9} = 3$.
Step $5$ metres right and left of the centre: $3 + 5$ and $3 - 5$, each paired
with $y = -2$. \ans{(8,\, -2)} \quad \ans{(-2,\, -2)}

Focal distance: $c^2 = a^2 - b^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$, so $c = 4$. Step $4$ metres
right and left of the centre along the same axis: $3 + 4$ and $3 - 4$.
\ans{(7,\, -2)} \quad \ans{(-1,\, -2)}}

\problem{\textbf{Cooling tower:} $\dfrac{x^2}{144} - \dfrac{y^2}{81} = 1$
(metres). Find the asymptote slopes and equations.

\textbf{Solution:} Here $a = \sqrt{144} = 12$ and $b = \sqrt{81} = 9$. For a
hyperbola opening left and right, the asymptotes through the centre have slopes
$\pm \dfrac{b}{a} = \pm \dfrac{9}{12}$, which reduces. \ans{\frac{3}{4}}

The centre is the origin, so the two asymptote equations are
$y = \frac{3}{4}x$ and $y = -\frac{3}{4}x$; together
\ans{y = \pm \frac{3}{4}\,x}}

\problem{\textbf{Satellite orbit:} closest approach $4200$ km, farthest
$9800$ km, with the planet at one focus. Solve for $a$ and $c$.

\textbf{Solution:} The closest point is the vertex nearer the focus, a distance
$a - c$ away; the farthest is the opposite vertex, $a + c$ away. That is the
system
\begin{align*}
  a - c &= 4200\\
  a + c &= 9800
\end{align*}
Add the two equations to eliminate $c$: \ $2a = 14000$, so $a = 7000$ km.
\ans{a = 7000}

Substitute back into the second equation: $7000 + c = 9800$, so $c = 2800$ km.
\ans{c = 2800}

Check: $7000 - 2800 = 4200$ $\checkmark$ and $7000 + 2800 = 9800$ $\checkmark$.}

\problem{\textbf{Navigation stations:} foci $20$ km apart, distance difference
$12$ km. Find $a$, then $b^2$.

\textbf{Solution:} On a hyperbola the constant difference of distances to the
foci equals $2a$, so $2a = 12$ and $a = \dfrac{12}{2}$. \ans{a = 6}

The stations are the foci, so $2c = 20$ and $c = 10$. The hyperbola relation
$c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ rearranges to
$b^2 = c^2 - a^2 = \left(\frac{20}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{12}{2}\right)^2
= 100 - 36$. \ans{b^2 = 64}}

\problem{\textbf{Hyperbolic mirror:} vertices $(\pm 5, 0)$ cm, asymptotes
$y = \pm \frac{2}{5}x$. Find $b$ and $c$.

\textbf{Solution:} The vertices give $a = 5$. The asymptote slope of a
left-right hyperbola is $\dfrac{b}{a}$, so $\dfrac{b}{5} = \dfrac{2}{5}$, which
means $b = 5 \cdot \dfrac{2}{5}$. \ans{b = 2}

Then $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 5^2 + 2^2 = 29$, so $c = \sqrt{29} = 5.385\ldots$
centimetres, which rounds to two decimal places. \ans{c \approx 5.39}}

\problem{\textbf{Bridge arch:} upper half of an ellipse centred at the origin
through $(3, 4)$ and $(6, 2)$ (metres). Find $m = a^2$, $n = b^2$, and the foci.

\textbf{Solution:} Substituting each point into
$\dfrac{x^2}{m} + \dfrac{y^2}{n} = 1$ gives
\begin{align*}
  \frac{9}{m} + \frac{16}{n} &= 1\\
  \frac{36}{m} + \frac{4}{n} &= 1
\end{align*}
These are linear in $\frac{1}{m}$ and $\frac{1}{n}$. Multiply the second
equation by $4$ and subtract the first:
$\frac{144}{m} - \frac{9}{m} = 4 - 1$, so $\frac{135}{m} = 3$ and $m = 45$.
\ans{m = 45}

Substitute into the first equation: $\frac{9}{45} + \frac{16}{n} = 1$ gives
$\frac{16}{n} = \frac{4}{5}$, so $n = 20$. \ans{n = 20}

Since $m > n$, the major axis is horizontal and
$c^2 = m - n = 45 - 20 = 25$, so $c = 5$ metres. \ans{(\pm 5,\, 0)}}

\problem{\textbf{Tunnel, error analysis:} $\dfrac{y^2}{49} - \dfrac{x^2}{16} =
1$ (metres). Jae reports slopes $\pm 0.57$. Find the mistake and the correct
slopes.

\textbf{Solution:} This hyperbola has the $y^2$ term positive, so it opens up
and down and its transverse axis is vertical: $a^2 = 49$ sits under $y^2$ and
$b^2 = 16$ sits under $x^2$. For an up-down hyperbola the asymptotes are
$y = \pm \dfrac{a}{b}x$ --- the number from under $y^2$ goes on top. Jae used
$\dfrac{4}{7} = 0.57$, which is $\dfrac{b}{a}$: the slope was inverted by
copying the left-right formula. The correct slope is
$\dfrac{\sqrt{49}}{\sqrt{16}} = \dfrac{7}{4}$. \ans{y = \pm \frac{7}{4}\,x}

Reading check: the tunnel is taller than it is wide near the vertex, so its
walls must rise faster than $1$ metre up per $1$ metre across --- a slope
bigger than $1$, not smaller.}

\problem{\textbf{Skate ramp:} $\dfrac{x^2}{36} - \dfrac{y^2}{25} = 1$ (metres).
Asymptote slope, sketch, and explanation.

\textbf{(a)} $a = \sqrt{36} = 6$ and $b = \sqrt{25} = 5$, so the slopes are
$\pm \dfrac{b}{a}$. \ans{\frac{5}{6}}

\textbf{(b)} Model sketch: mark the vertices at $(\pm 6, 0)$; draw the central
rectangle with corners $(\pm 6, \pm 5)$; draw the two diagonals of that
rectangle all the way out --- those are $y = \pm \frac{5}{6}x$; then draw one
branch opening right from $(6,0)$ and one opening left from $(-6,0)$, each
hugging the diagonals as it goes.

\textbf{(c)} Model explanation (judge by reading): solving the equation for $y$
gives $y = \pm \frac{5}{6}\sqrt{x^2 - 36} = \pm \frac{5}{6}\,x\sqrt{1 -
\frac{36}{x^2}}$. As $|x|$ grows, $\frac{36}{x^2}$ shrinks toward $0$, so the
square-root factor creeps up toward $1$ and the branch creeps up toward
$\pm \frac{5}{6}x$. It never arrives: the factor is always strictly less than
$1$ because $\frac{36}{x^2}$ is never $0$ for a finite $x$, so the branch stays
strictly inside its asymptote. Equivalently, a point on the asymptote would
force $x^2/36 - y^2/25 = 0$, not $1$.}
