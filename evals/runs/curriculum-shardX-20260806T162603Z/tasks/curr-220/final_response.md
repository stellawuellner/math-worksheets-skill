Here are the three PDFs for a Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra interleaved review set on
**slope and rate of change, focused on comparing rates of change across tables,
graphs, and equations**.

**Skills Summary (study guide)** — `ss_raterepr_curr220.pdf`, 2 pages.
Four sections, one per representation plus one on the comparison itself: rate
from a table, rate from an equation, rate from a graph (with a small worked
grid), and comparing two rates given in different forms. Each has a rule box, a
two-step worked example whose first step says *why* that method applies, and a
try-it with the answer printed upside down inside the box.

**Worksheet** — `ws_raterepr_curr220.pdf`, 7 pages, 12 problems.
A shared **Graph Bank** at the top carries three labelled lines $P$, $Q$ and
$R$; only the problems that name a line by letter use it, and its caption says
so, so no figure can be misread as belonging to a neighbouring problem. Three
short warm-ups pull a rate out of each representation in turn; after that the
representations rotate so no method is used more than twice in a row and the
student has to decide which one applies. Five problems put two representations
side by side and ask for the comparison written with $>$, $<$ or $=$, and the
last two are synthesis items that also ask what a graph would show. Every part
has its own answer blank.

**Answer key** — `ak_raterepr_curr220.pdf`, 3 pages.
Quick Answers bank labelled by response ("(a) litres per minute", "(b)
comparison"), then a worked solution per problem that shows the subtraction or
the coefficient the rate comes from, not just the number. Curriculum block:
8.EE.B.5 and 8.EE.B.6, difficulty 1–4.

**What is verified, honestly.** Twenty-one checks were declared across the
twelve problems. **Nineteen are machine-checked with SymPy**: every rate of
change (from tables, from graphs and from equations), every table difference,
and every one of the six comparisons — a comparison is verified as a relation,
so the key cannot print `>` where `<` is correct.

**Two responses are instructor-judged, not machine-checked**: part (c) of
problems 11 and 12, both of which ask the student to explain what a graph would
show. That matches the key's own generated note — 10 of 12 problems fully
machine-checked, with problems 11 and 12 each carrying one instructor-judged
part, marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank. The worked solution for each
states what a correct explanation must contain and what does not earn credit.
