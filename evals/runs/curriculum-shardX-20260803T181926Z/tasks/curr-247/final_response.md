Here are three PDFs on scatter plots and informal lines of fit, for a Grade 8 / pre-algebra student.

**Worksheet (`ws_scatter_curr247.pdf`, 7 pages, 12 problems)**
Every problem carries its own printed scatter plot, so nothing has to be flipped back to. Four data sets carry the sheet: revision minutes against quiz score (positive association), screen time against sleep (negative), shoe size against quiz score (no association at all), and plant height over eight days with one clear outlier. The sequence walks the whole method once and then makes the student choose: read a point off the plot (1, 6), describe the association in three words — direction, strength, shape (2, 7), draw a line of fit and get its slope (3), turn two points on that line into $y = mx + b$ (4, 8), predict from it (5, 9), and then the two problems that decide whether the student really understands what a line of fit is — the shoe-size plot where no line should be drawn (10) and the outlier that would tilt one (11). It closes by asking for the score the line predicts after 200 minutes of revision (12): the arithmetic gives 195, and the student has to say why that number is worthless.

**Answer key (`ak_scatter_curr247.pdf`, 3 pages)**
Each problem restated and worked, with the reasoning written out rather than the answer alone — the slope computed from the named points, the substitution shown before every prediction, the residual named in problem 9, and the interpolation/extrapolation distinction spelled out in 5 and 12. It also carries the two cautions worth saying out loud: that the screen-time slope describes an association and not a cause, and that a line of fit is a summary of the range it was drawn through. The key opens with a Quick Answers strip, a Curriculum block (Grade 8 / pre-algebra, 8.SP.A.1, difficulty 1–5), and a Common wrong answers list built from the three declared traps — a student who answers 30 on problem 1 read the horizontal value; one who answers 42 on problem 5 or 140 on problem 12 dropped the y-intercept.

**Study guide (`ss_scatter_curr247.pdf`, 2 pages)**
Four sections with a rule box, a worked example that opens by naming the method and why, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box: reading a point off a plot, describing the association (direction, strength, shape, then outliers and clusters), drawing a line of fit and writing its equation from two points on the line, and predicting from it. The line-of-fit box states the two rules students most often break — the line does not have to touch a data point, and it is never the line joining the first and last dots.

**What was verified.** 14 of the 20 checks were recomputed independently: every value read off a plot (each plot is drawn from the same numbers the check uses, so the picture and the answer cannot disagree), both max/min readings, both slopes, both intercepts, all three predictions, the mean of the shoe-size scores, and both ranges in the outlier problem. All 12 study-guide values were verified the same way. Six items are correctly flagged for manual review, all of them written explanations: the two association descriptions (2, 7), the slope interpretation (8), the "no line of fit here" argument (10), the outlier's effect on the line (11), and the extrapolation warning (12). The key gives a model answer for each.
