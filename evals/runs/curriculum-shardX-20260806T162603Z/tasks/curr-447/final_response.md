Here are the three PDFs for a 12-problem precalculus/statistics set on **using normal-model
z-scores and percentiles**.

**Worksheet — `ws_zscores_curr447.pdf` (4 pages).** The ramp runs from a single
standardization to a two-distribution comparison, and no two problems share a skeleton.
Problems 1–4 work one exam distribution four different ways (standardize a high score, a low
score, turn $z=1$ into a percentage, and unstandardize). Problems 5–8 move to a second
context — heights — and problems 9–12 to a third, battery life, so the student meets the
same four moves on units they have not seen them on before. The closing synthesis item puts
two production lines side by side: one battery lasts longer in hours, the other is more
unusual for its own line, and the student has to say which is which and why.

No normal table is needed: percentages come from the empirical rule, which is stated in the
directions, and the two percentile items are built at exactly $1\sigma$ and $2\sigma$ so the
rule answers them cleanly.

**Answer key — `ak_zscores_curr447.pdf` (3 pages).** Every problem restated with the
substitution written out, plus a quick-answer bank, a curriculum block, and a "common wrong
answers" list from three machine-checked traps: dividing the deviation by the mean instead of
the standard deviation, subtracting in the wrong order and losing the sign, and reporting
$34\%$ (the band between the mean and $-1\sigma$) when the question asked for the percentage
*below* that point.

**Study guide — `ss_zscores_curr447.pdf` (2 pages).** Four sections — standardizing,
unstandardizing, percentiles from the empirical rule, and comparing across distributions —
each with a formula box, a worked example that opens by naming why that move is the right
one, and a try-it.

**What was verified, honestly.** The sheet asks for **18 separate responses**. **15 are
machine-checked** with SymPy: every $z$-score, every unstandardized value, both empirical-rule
percentages, and the tail probability, all recomputed from the same $\mu$ and $\sigma$
printed in the stems. **3 are instructor-judged** and marked `---` in the quick-answer bank:
the "which performance was stronger" argument in 5(c), the sign-and-percentile explanation in
11(b), and the synthesis comparison in 12(c). Those are the interpretation the sheet exists
to teach, and prose cannot be machine-graded; the key prints a model answer and an explicit
full-credit criterion for each — including, for 11(b), the range of percentile estimates that
should be accepted. The eight study-guide examples and try-its are machine-checked.

No response is marked `[unchecked]`.
