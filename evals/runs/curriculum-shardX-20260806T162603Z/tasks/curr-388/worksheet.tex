\wstitleblock{Matrix Operations at a Bike-Share Depot}{Algebra 2}{}

\begin{center}
\small\textit{Adding, subtracting and scaling matrices, multiplying them, and writing a system as a matrix equation}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Every matrix on this sheet is a table of real quantities. The row and column meanings are stated with the matrix; the units are stated in the question.
  \item Adding, subtracting or scaling works entry by entry. Multiplying does not: the entry in row $i$, column $j$ of a product pairs row $i$ of the left matrix with column $j$ of the right one.
  \item Write your result in the empty bracket provided, one number per position.
\end{itemize}

\problem{Two riders buy day passes at the same depot. Two standard passes and three electric passes cost $22$ dollars; four standard passes and one electric pass cost $24$ dollars. Let $x$ be the price of a standard pass and $y$ the price of an electric pass, both in dollars.

\problem{Matrix $H$ counts one day's rentals in bikes (rows North depot then South depot, columns standard, electric, cargo). Column $k$ lists the price per rental in dollars for the three bike types.

\problem{A depot holds $100$ bikes in three classes: standard, electric and cargo. It has $20$ more electric bikes than standard bikes. Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ be the number of standard, electric and cargo bikes.
\par\smallskip
(a)~Write the three conditions as a matrix equation and find $x$, $y$ and $z$, in bikes.
\par\vspace*{5.4cm}\ansline
\par\smallskip
(b)~Explain what the coefficient matrix, the variable column and the constant column each stand for in this situation.
