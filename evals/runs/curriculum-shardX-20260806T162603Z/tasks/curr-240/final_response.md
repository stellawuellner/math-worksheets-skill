# Transformation Evidence for Congruence and Similarity — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_congruence_curr240.pdf`, 6 pages) — 12 problems that deliberately
  switch between four kinds of evidence: equal lengths (congruence), a constant ratio
  of lengths (similarity), equal slopes (parallel matching sides, so equal angles), and
  naming the transformation from the coordinate rule. After a short warm-up the four
  are interleaved, never more than two of a kind in a row, so the student has to decide
  which evidence the question needs before calculating. Problems 5 and 9 include a
  blank coordinate grid to plot on.
- **Answer key** (`ak_congruence_curr240.pdf`, 4 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is
  verified" note, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per problem showing the
  distance or slope arithmetic.
- **Study guide** (`ss_congruence_curr240.pdf`, 2 pages) — four sections matching the
  four kinds of evidence, each with a rule box, a worked example and a try-it, plus a
  watch-out box on the congruent-vs-similar distinction.

## What is verified

**7 of the 12 problems are fully machine-checked** by SymPy — 33 individual checks,
since most problems ask for two or three values and every printed blank has its own
check. Five problems are **partly instructor-judged**:

| Problem | Machine-checked | Instructor-judged |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | both bases (4 and 4) | naming the reflection in the x-axis and the congruence argument |
| 6 | both bases (6 and 3) | the judgement that the triangles are similar, not congruent |
| 9 | both bases (3 and 3) | naming the reflection in the y-axis and the congruence argument |
| 11 | both slopes (−3/4 and −3/4) | what equal slopes say about the angles |
| 12 | AB = 4, DE = 6, ratio 3/2 | the classification and its justification |

That matches the key's own "What is verified" note. Every instructor-judged item has a
rubric in the key stating what a correct response must contain.

## Notes for you

- Problem 6 is the one to watch: a student who has just done two congruence problems
  will often answer "yes" out of habit. The two bases, 6 and 3, are what settles it,
  and the correct word is *similar*.
- Problem 12 is the synthesis: it needs a length argument to rule out congruence and a
  ratio argument to establish similarity, and the rubric asks for both.
- All lengths on the coordinate problems are in grid units, stated in the directions.
- Standard covered: 8.G.A across all twelve problems.
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
