\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Lin reads $y = 3x + 5$.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: name the slip. The slope is the number \emph{attached to}
$x$, so $m = 3$; the $y$-intercept is the number standing alone, so $b = 5$. Lin
matched them by position on the page rather than by role.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: substitute into the equation as written:
$y = 3(4) + 5 = 12 + 5$.\\
Step 3: $y = \ans{17}$.\\
Step 4: check the roles with the $x = 0$ test --- $y = 3(0) + 5 = 5$, so $5$ is
the value at $x = 0$, which is exactly what a $y$-intercept is $\checkmark$. And
each step of $1$ in $x$ raises $y$ by $3$, which is what a slope is.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $23$, from $5(4) + 3$. The swap changes the answer because $x$
is multiplied by the wrong number.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Rae reads $y = 12 - 2x$.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: the order the terms are written in carries no meaning ---
$12 - 2x$ and $-2x + 12$ are the same expression. What identifies the slope is
that it multiplies $x$, so $m = -2$, and the constant $12$ stands alone, so
$b = 12$.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: substitute: $y = 12 + (-2)(5) = 12 - 10$.\\
Step 3: $y = \ans{2}$.\\
Step 4: check by direction --- the slope is negative, so $y$ must \emph{fall} as
$x$ grows. From $x = 0$ (where $y = 12$) to $x = 5$, $y$ drops to $2$
$\checkmark$. Rae's answer of $58$ is far above the starting value, which is
impossible for a falling line.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $58$, from $-2 + 12(5)$. A wrong answer larger than the value
at $x = 0$ on a decreasing line is a role swap, not an arithmetic slip.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Dev's slope through $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: Dev put the change in $x$ on top. Slope answers ``how much
does $y$ change per $1$ of $x$'', so the change in $y$ goes on top:
$m = \dfrac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$.\\
Step 2: compute in that order:
$m = \dfrac{11 - 3}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{8}{4}$.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 3: the slope is $\ans{2}$.\\
Step 4: check by walking the line --- from $x = 2$ to $x = 6$ is $4$ steps, and
$4$ steps at a slope of $2$ raises $y$ by $8$, taking $3$ up to $11$
$\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $0.5$, the reciprocal. An inverted slope and a correct one are
always reciprocals, so if your answer times the right answer gives $1$, you
divided the wrong way round.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Sofia calls $13$ the rate of change.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: a rate of change measures how much $y$ moves for each step
of $1$ in $x$. It is a \emph{difference between rows}, not a number read out of
one row. The value $13$ is a single output, so it can never be a rate.\\
Step 2: subtract consecutive $y$ values: $17 - 13 = 4$, and check it repeats:
$21 - 17 = 4$ and $25 - 21 = 4$.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 3: $y$ increases by $\ans{4}$ for every step of $1$ in $x$.\\
Step 4: check that the $x$ steps really are $1$ apart --- $1, 2, 3, 4$
$\checkmark$. If they were not, each difference would have to be divided by its
own $x$ step.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $13$. Any ``rate'' taken from a single cell of a table is this
error.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Sofia calls $13$ the $y$-intercept.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: the $y$-intercept is the output at $x = 0$. This table
starts at $x = 1$, so the intercept is not in the table at all --- $13$ is the
value at $x = 1$, one step to the right of where the intercept lives.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: walk one step backwards. Going left by $1$ in $x$ lowers $y$
by the step size, so $13 - 4$.\\
Step 3: the $y$-intercept is $\ans{9}$.\\
Step 4: check by rebuilding the rule: $y = 4x + 9$ gives $4(1) + 9 = 13$ at
$x = 1$ and $4(4) + 9 = 25$ at $x = 4$, matching both ends of the table
$\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $13$. A table that does not contain $x = 0$ cannot show you the
intercept --- you always have to step back to it.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: Owen's line, slope $4$ through $(3, 20)$.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: test Owen's equation $y = 4x + 20$ at $x = 3$:
$4(3) + 20 = 32$, not $20$. So his line misses the point it was supposed to pass
through. The $y$-value of a point is only the intercept when that point sits on
the $y$-axis, i.e.\ when $x = 0$ --- and here $x = 3$.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 2: put the point into $y = 4x + b$ and solve for $b$:
$4(3) + b = 20$, so $12 + b = 20$.\\
Step 3: $b = \ans{8}$, so the correct line is $y = 4x + 8$.\\
Step 4: check the point again: $4(3) + 8 = 20$ $\checkmark$.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $20$. Whenever a written equation fails the very point it was
built from, suspect that a coordinate was used as an intercept.}

\problem{\textbf{Find and fix: the draining pool.}\\
\textbf{(a)} Step 1: both errors come from confusing a rate with a reading. The
rate of change is gallons \emph{per minute}, so gallons go on top:
$m = \dfrac{22 - 46}{8 - 2} = \dfrac{-24}{6}$.\\
Step 2: the rate of change is $\ans{-4}$ gallons per minute --- the pool loses
$4$ gallons each minute.\\
\textbf{(b)} Step 3: the starting amount is the value at $0$ minutes, and the
first reading was taken at $2$ minutes, after $8$ gallons had already drained.
Use $b = y - mx$ with the point $(2, 46)$: $b = 46 - (-4)(2) = 46 + 8$.\\
Step 4: the pool held $\ans{54}$ gallons at the start.\\
Step 5: check with the other reading: $-4(8) + 54 = -32 + 54 = 22$ $\checkmark$,
which is the $8$-minute measurement.\\
\emph{Watch for:} $-0.25$ (the inverted rate) and $46$ (a reading treated as a
starting value). Notice the two errors are the same mistake twice: a number that
belongs to one particular moment was used as though it described the whole
relationship.}

\problem{\textbf{Explain: two different questions.}\\
\emph{Open response --- flagged for manual review. Model answer below; accept any
equivalent reasoning.}\\
\textbf{(a)} The slope answers ``for each increase of $1$ in $x$, how much does
$y$ change?'' It compares two quantities, so it is a rate and carries units of
$y$ per unit of $x$. The $y$-intercept answers ``what is $y$ when $x$ is $0$?''
That is one output at one input, so it is a single value in the units of $y$
alone. A rate describes the whole line; the intercept describes one point on it.\\
\textbf{(b)} In $y = mx + b$ the two numbers sit in structurally different
places: $m$ is multiplied by $x$ and $b$ is not. Swapping them changes both the
steepness and the starting height. For example $y = 3x + 5$ passes through
$(0,5)$ climbing $3$ per step, while $y = 5x + 3$ passes through $(0,3)$ climbing
$5$ per step --- different height, different tilt, and they agree at only one
point. The same digits do not make the same line, because position in the
equation \emph{is} the meaning.\\
\textbf{(c)} \emph{Table:} the intercept is the $y$ value in the row where
$x = 0$; if that row is missing, find the step size first and walk backwards to
it. The slope is the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$ between any two
rows. \emph{Graph:} the intercept is where the line crosses the $y$-axis; the
slope is rise over run between two lattice points. \emph{Equation with the
constant first:} ignore the order and look at the roles --- the number multiplied
by $x$ is the slope, the number standing alone is the intercept.\\
\emph{What a full-credit answer must contain:} slope named as a rate per unit of
$x$ and the intercept as the output at $x = 0$ in (a); a concrete demonstration
that swapping produces a different line in (b); and in (c) a method for each of
the three representations, including what to do when a table omits $x = 0$.}
