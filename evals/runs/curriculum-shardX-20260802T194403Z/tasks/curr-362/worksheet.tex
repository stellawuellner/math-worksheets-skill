\wstitleblock{Finding Zeros with the Rational Root Theorem}{Algebra 2}{}

\noindent\small
Testing candidates, factoring completely, and finding every zero.
For a polynomial with integer coefficients, every rational zero has the form
$\pm\frac{p}{q}$, where $p$ divides the constant term and $q$ divides the
leading coefficient. The routine is always the same: \textbf{(1)} list the
candidates, \textbf{(2)} test them until one gives a remainder of $0$,
\textbf{(3)} divide it out, \textbf{(4)} finish the depressed quotient by
factoring or by the quadratic formula. Problems 1--2 walk that routine on a
single polynomial; after that you run it yourself. Show your candidate list and
your division work --- both are part of the answer.
\normalsize

\problem[5cm]{Let $f(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 6$. The rational root candidates are
$\pm 1$, $\pm 2$, $\pm 3$, $\pm 6$. Evaluate $f(2)$ to decide whether $x = 2$ is
a zero of $f$.}

\problem[6.5cm]{Using your result from problem 1, divide out the corresponding
factor and find \emph{all} zeros of $f(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + x + 6$.}

\problem[6.5cm]{Factor $g(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6$ completely over the
integers.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Let $h(x) = 2x^3 - x^2 - 8x + 4$. Because the leading
coefficient is $2$, the candidate list includes halves. Evaluate
$h\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$ to decide whether $x = \frac{1}{2}$ is a zero.}

\problem[7cm]{Find all zeros of $h(x) = 2x^3 - x^2 - 8x + 4$.}

\problem[7cm]{Find all zeros of $p(x) = x^3 - 4x^2 + 2x + 4$. Exactly one zero
is rational --- find it first, then solve the quadratic that is left. Give exact
answers, not decimals.}

\problem[7cm]{Factor $q(x) = 3x^3 - x^2 - 12x + 4$ completely over the
integers.}

\problem[7cm]{Solve $x^3 - x^2 - 13x - 3 = 0$. Give all real solutions in exact
form.}

\problem[7.5cm]{Find all zeros of $r(x) = x^4 - 2x^3 - 7x^2 + 8x + 12$. A
quartic needs the routine twice: after the first division you are left with a
cubic, so run the theorem again on it.}

\problem[7cm]{Find all zeros of $s(x) = x^3 - x^2 + 4x - 4$, including any
non-real zeros. State how many zeros the theorem itself was able to supply.}

\problem[7cm]{Factor $t(x) = 4x^3 - 8x^2 - x + 2$ completely over the integers.}

\problem[6cm]{\textbf{Synthesis.} For $u(x) = x^3 - 3x - 1$ the candidate list
is just $x = 1$ and $x = -1$, and $u(1) = -3$ and $u(-1) = 1$, so neither is a
zero. A classmate concludes: ``The rational root theorem found nothing, so
$u$ has no real zeros.'' Explain in two or three sentences why that conclusion
is wrong, and give evidence that $u$ does have a real zero between $x = -1$ and
$x = 1$. \noansline}
