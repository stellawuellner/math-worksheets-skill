\problem{$\log_2 8 + \log_2 4$ as one logarithm, then its exact value.}

\textbf{Solution:} Same base, so the product rule applies right to left:
$\log_2 8 + \log_2 4 = \log_2(8 \cdot 4) = \log_2 32$. Then ask which power of
$2$ gives $32$: $2^5 = 32$. \ans{\log_2 32 = 5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_3 54 - \log_3 2$ as one logarithm, then its exact value.}

\textbf{Solution:} A difference of logarithms with the same base condenses to the
logarithm of the quotient: $\log_3\!\dfrac{54}{2} = \log_3 27$, and $3^3 = 27$.
\ans{\log_3 27 = 3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Exact value of $2\log_4 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The power rule moves the coefficient inside:
$2\log_4 8 = \log_4 8^2 = \log_4 64$. Since $4^3 = 64$, the value is $3$.
(Equivalently $\log_4 8 = \tfrac{3}{2}$, and twice that is $3$.) \ans{3}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Expand $\log_b(5x)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The input is a product of two factors, so the product rule
splits it into a sum. \ans{\log_b 5 + \log_b x}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Expand $\log_b\!\left(\dfrac{x}{7}\right)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The input is a quotient, so the quotient rule splits it into a
difference --- numerator first. \ans{\log_b x - \log_b 7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Expand $\log_b\!\left(x^5\right)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} An exponent inside a logarithm comes out in front, by the
power rule. \ans{5\log_b x}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_2 3 + \log_2 8 - \log_2 6$ as one logarithm, then its exact value.}

\textbf{Solution:} Condense left to right, product first and then quotient:
\[ \log_2 3 + \log_2 8 - \log_2 6 = \log_2\!\frac{3 \cdot 8}{6}
   = \log_2\!\frac{24}{6} = \log_2 4 . \]
Since $2^2 = 4$, \ans{\log_2 4 = 2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Expand $\log_b\!\left(x^2 y^3\right)$ completely.}

\textbf{Solution:} Product rule first, then the power rule on each piece:
\[ \log_b\!\left(x^2 y^3\right) = \log_b\!\left(x^2\right) + \log_b\!\left(y^3\right) . \]
\ans{2\log_b x + 3\log_b y}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{$\log_b x = 1.7$, $\log_b y = 0.4$. (a) Expand
$\log_b\!\left(\dfrac{x^3}{y}\right)$. (b) Its value.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Quotient rule, then power rule on the numerator:
\[ \log_b\!\left(\frac{x^3}{y}\right) = \log_b\!\left(x^3\right) - \log_b y . \]
\ans{3\log_b x - \log_b y}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Substitute the given values into the expanded form:
$3(1.7) - 0.4 = 5.1 - 0.4$. \ans{4.7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Condense $3\log_b x + \dfrac{1}{2}\log_b y$.}

\textbf{Solution:} Move each coefficient inside as an exponent, then combine the
sum into one logarithm of a product:
\[ 3\log_b x + \tfrac{1}{2}\log_b y = \log_b\!\left(x^3\right) + \log_b\!\left(y^{1/2}\right) . \]
\ans{\log_b\!\left(x^3\sqrt{y}\right)}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{(a) Exact $\log_5\!\left(125^2\right)$. (b) $\log_5 9$ from
$\log_5 3 \approx 0.6826$.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Power rule: $\log_5\!\left(125^2\right) = 2\log_5 125$,
and $\log_5 125 = 3$ because $5^3 = 125$. So the value is $2 \times 3$.
\ans{6}

\textbf{Solution (b):} $9 = 3^2$, so $\log_5 9 = 2\log_5 3 \approx 2(0.6826)
= 1.3652$, which rounds to \ans{1.37}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.2cm}

\problem{Challenge: $\dfrac{1}{2}\log_2 36 + \log_2 10 - \log_2 15$.
(a) One logarithm and its exact value. (b) Why the $\tfrac12$ becomes a square
root.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Move the coefficient inside first, then condense:
\begin{align*}
  \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2 36 &= \log_2 36^{1/2} = \log_2 6 \\
  \log_2 6 + \log_2 10 - \log_2 15 &= \log_2\!\frac{6 \cdot 10}{15}
    = \log_2\!\frac{60}{15} = \log_2 4 .
\end{align*}
Since $2^2 = 4$, \ans{\log_2 4 = 2}

\textbf{Solution (b):} \emph{Instructor-judged.} The power rule
$\log_b(M^{\,p}) = p\log_b M$ is an identity, so it may be read right to left:
a coefficient in front of a logarithm is an exponent on its input. Here $p$ is
$\tfrac12$, and raising to the one-half power \emph{is} taking the square root,
so $\tfrac12\log_2 36 = \log_2 36^{1/2} = \log_2\sqrt{36} = \log_2 6$. Full credit
names the power rule and applies it in that direction, rather than asserting the
two expressions are equal.
