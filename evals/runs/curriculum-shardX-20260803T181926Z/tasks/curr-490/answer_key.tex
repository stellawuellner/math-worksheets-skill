\problem{\textbf{Average value of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0, 3]$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The question asks for an average value, so the setup is
$\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f$, with $a = 0$ and $b = 3$. Dividing by the interval
length is what turns an accumulated area into a height.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \frac{1}{3}\int_0^3 x^2\,dx
= \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 9 = 3$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Sanity check: $f$ runs from $0$ up to $9$ on this interval, so
an average of $3$ is plausible --- and it is below the midpoint value because
$x^2$ spends most of the interval small. \ans{3}}

\problem{\textbf{Water entering a tank at $r(t) = 4t + 3$ litres per minute.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The given function is a \emph{rate}, and the question asks how
much water arrives --- that is accumulated change, so integrate the rate over
$[0, 5]$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \int_0^5 (4t + 3)\,dt
= \left[2t^2 + 3t\right]_0^5 = 50 + 15 = 65$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The units check out: litres per minute times minutes gives
litres. \ans{65\ \text{L}}}

\problem{\textbf{Net displacement for $v(t) = 3t^2 - 12$ on $[0, 3]$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Net displacement'' means the signed change in position, so
integrate the velocity itself --- no absolute value.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \int_0^3 (3t^2 - 12)\,dt
= \left[t^3 - 12t\right]_0^3 = 27 - 36 = -9$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The answer is negative because the particle spends the first
two seconds moving backwards and does not fully recover. It ends up $9$ metres
behind where it started. \ans{-9\ \text{m}}}

\problem{\textbf{Total distance for $v(t) = 3t^2 - 12$ on $[0, 3]$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Total distance is the integral of \emph{speed}, so first find
where the velocity changes sign: $3t^2 - 12 = 0$ gives $t = 2$ in $[0, 3]$.

\textbf{Step 2.} On $[0, 2]$ the velocity is negative and on $[2, 3]$ it is
positive, so split there:
$\displaystyle \int_0^2 (3t^2 - 12)\,dt = 8 - 24 = -16$ and
$\displaystyle \int_2^3 (3t^2 - 12)\,dt = (27 - 36) - (8 - 24) = 7$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Add the absolute values: $|-16| + |7| = 23$. Compare with
problem $3$: the same velocity function, the same interval, and a completely
different number, because displacement lets the backward motion cancel and
distance does not. \ans{23\ \text{m}}}

\problem{\textbf{Average greenhouse temperature over $[0, 12]$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Average value again, this time of a sinusoidal model:
$\frac{1}{12}\int_0^{12} T(t)\,dt$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \int_0^{12}\!\left(18 + 6\sin\frac{\pi t}{12}\right)dt
= \left[18t - \frac{72}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi t}{12}\right]_0^{12}
= 216 + \frac{144}{\pi}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Divide by the interval length $12$: the average is
$18 + \frac{12}{\pi} \approx 21.8197$, which rounds to $21.82$. Note that it is
above the baseline $18$, as it must be: the sine term is non-negative across
this whole half period. \ans{21.82\ \text{degrees}}}

\problem{\textbf{Fuel remaining after four hours.}

\textbf{Step 1 (part a).} The consumption rate is $c(t) = 0.4 + 0.1t$, so the
fuel consumed is the accumulated change
$\displaystyle \int_0^4 (0.4 + 0.1t)\,dt
= \left[0.4t + 0.05t^2\right]_0^4 = 1.6 + 0.8 = 2.4$ litres.
\ans{2.4}

\textbf{Step 2 (part b).} An accumulated change is only half the story --- the
amount at the end is the starting amount plus (here minus) that change:
$14 - 2.4 = 11.6$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Sanity check: the tank started nearly full and the rate never
exceeds $0.8$ litres per hour, so losing under three litres in four hours is
reasonable. \ans{11.6\ \text{L}}}

\problem{\textbf{$v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3$ on $[0, 3]$: rest times, distance,
average speed.}

\textbf{Step 1 (part a).} At rest means $v = 0$: factor
$t^2 - 4t + 3 = (t - 1)(t - 3)$, so the particle is at rest at
$t = 1$ and $t = 3$ seconds. \ans{t = 1 \text{ and } t = 3}

\textbf{Step 2 (part b).} Only $t = 1$ is interior, so split there. The
antiderivative is $\frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t$. On $[0, 1]$ the integral is
$\frac{4}{3}$; on $[1, 3]$ it is $0 - \frac{4}{3} = -\frac{4}{3}$. Total
distance $= \frac{4}{3} + \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{3}$ metres.
\ans{\tfrac{8}{3} \approx 2.667}

\textbf{Step 3 (part c).} Average speed is total distance over elapsed time
--- an average value applied to speed, not to velocity:
$\frac{8/3}{3} = \frac{8}{9} \approx 0.889$ metres per second. (The average
\emph{velocity} would be $0$, since displacement over $[0, 3]$ is zero.)
\ans{0.889}}

\problem{\textbf{Average velocity of a cyclist with $v(t) = 3\sqrt{t}$ on
$[0, 4]$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Average value of the velocity function:
$\frac{1}{4}\int_0^4 3\sqrt{t}\,dt$. Rewrite the root as a power first.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \int_0^4 3t^{1/2}\,dt
= \left[2t^{3/2}\right]_0^4 = 2 \cdot 8 = 16$, so the average is
$\frac{16}{4} = 4$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Here the velocity never changes sign, so the displacement
$16$ metres equals the total distance and the average velocity equals the
average speed. That agreement is what fails in problem $7$.
\ans{4\ \text{m/s}}}

\problem{\textbf{Tank with water entering at $10 + 2t$ and draining at $3t$
litres per minute.}

\textbf{Step 1 (part a).} Two rates act at once, so the change in volume is the
integral of the \emph{net} rate, in minus out:
$(10 + 2t) - 3t = 10 - t$ litres per minute.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\displaystyle \int_0^6 \left(10 + 2t - 3t\right)dt
= \left[10t - \frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^6 = 60 - 18 = 42$ litres gained.
\ans{42}

\textbf{Step 3 (part b).} Amount at the end $=$ starting amount $+$ accumulated
net change $= 20 + 42 = 62$. The net rate $10 - t$ stays positive on the whole
interval, so the volume rises throughout --- consistent with a gain.
\ans{62\ \text{L}}}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: $v(t) = 4 - t^2$ on $[0, 3]$ with $s(0) = 2$.}

\textbf{Step 1 (part a).} Net displacement is the signed integral of velocity:
$\displaystyle \int_0^3 (4 - t^2)\,dt = \left[4t - \frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^3
= 12 - 9 = 3$ metres. \ans{3}

\textbf{Step 2 (part b).} Position is the initial position plus the
displacement, which is the Fundamental Theorem written for motion:
$s(3) = 2 + 3 = 5$ metres. \ans{5}

\textbf{Step 3 (part c).} The velocity is zero at $t = 2$ and changes sign
there, so split. On $[0, 2]$ the integral is $8 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{16}{3}$;
on $[2, 3]$ it is $3 - \frac{16}{3} = -\frac{7}{3}$. Total distance
$= \frac{16}{3} + \frac{7}{3} = \frac{23}{3}$ metres.
\ans{\tfrac{23}{3} \approx 7.667}

\textbf{Step 4 (part d).} Average velocity is the average value of $v$:
$\frac{1}{3}\int_0^3 (4 - t^2)\,dt = \frac{3}{3} = 1$ metre per second.
\ans{1}

\textit{Manual review (part e):} the average speed is
$\frac{23/3}{3} = \frac{23}{9} \approx 2.556$ metres per second, larger than the
average velocity of $1$. Accept any paragraph making the point that average
velocity divides the \emph{signed} displacement by the elapsed time, so the
backward motion after $t = 2$ cancels part of the forward motion, while average
speed divides total distance, where nothing cancels. A student who notes that
the two agree exactly when the velocity never changes sign has the idea.}
