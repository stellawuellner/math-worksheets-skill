\problem{\textbf{Sail $A(2,1)$, $B(10,1)$, $C(10,7)$ --- seam length and
perimeter.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $AB$ is horizontal and $BC$ is vertical, so those two lengths
are read straight off the coordinates: $AB = 10 - 2 = 8$ and
$BC = 7 - 1 = 6$. Only $AC$ needs the distance formula.

\textbf{Step 2.} $AC = \sqrt{(10-2)^2 + (7-1)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} =
\sqrt{100}$, so the slanted seam is \ans{10}

\textbf{Step 3.} The perimeter adds the three seams: $8 + 6 + 10$, so the sail
takes \ans{24 \text{ m}} of seaming.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $14$ for $AC$, from adding the two legs. A
slanted segment is always shorter than the two legs it spans --- that is the
triangle inequality, and it is a fast sanity check.}

\problem{\textbf{Slab $(-3,-2)$, $(5,-2)$, $(5,3)$, $(-3,3)$ --- area.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Opposite sides share an $x$- or a $y$-value, so the rectangle
is axis-aligned and its side lengths are coordinate differences.

\textbf{Step 2.} Width: from $x = -3$ to $x = 5$ is $8$. Height: from
$y = -2$ to $y = 3$ is $5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Area is width times height, $8 \times 5$, so the slab covers
\ans{40 \text{ m}^2}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $26$, which is the perimeter $2(8) + 2(5)$.
Perimeter is a length in metres; area is in square metres. Checking the unit
catches this before the number does.}

\problem{\textbf{Tile $P(0,0)$, $Q(6,0)$, $R(9,4)$, $S(3,4)$ --- perimeter.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $PQ$ lies on the $x$-axis, so $PQ = 6$. The slanted side $QR$
needs the distance formula, and because $PQRS$ is a parallelogram the opposite
sides repeat those two lengths.

\textbf{Step 2.} $QR = \sqrt{(9-6)^2 + (4-0)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25}$,
so $QR = \ans{5}$

\textbf{Step 3.} Perimeter $= 2(6) + 2(5)$, so the tile measures
\ans{22 \text{ units}} around.

\textit{Teaching note:} confirm the parallelogram before halving the work ---
$\overrightarrow{PQ}$ and $\overrightarrow{SR}$ are both $(6,0)$, which is what
makes the two long sides equal.}

\problem{\textbf{Flowerbed $(0,0)$, $(8,2)$, $(3,7)$ --- area.}

\textbf{Step 1.} No side is horizontal or vertical, so there is no base to read
off the grid. The shoelace formula takes the vertices in order and needs no
side lengths at all.

\textbf{Step 2.} Taking the vertices counter-clockwise:
\[
A = \tfrac{1}{2}\left| 0(2 - 7) + 8(7 - 0) + 3(0 - 2) \right|
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\left| 0 + 56 - 6 \right| = \tfrac{1}{2}(50)
\]

\textbf{Step 3.} The flowerbed covers \ans{25 \text{ square units}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $56$, the area of the smallest rectangle around
the triangle. A triangle inside a rectangle never fills more than half of it,
so an answer above $28$ here cannot be right.}

\problem{\textbf{Banner $A(-4,0)$, $B(0,3)$, $C(4,0)$, $D(0,-3)$ --- diagonals
and perimeter.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A midpoint is the average of the endpoints, taken one
coordinate at a time. Diagonal $AC$ runs from $(-4,0)$ to $(4,0)$ and diagonal
$BD$ from $(0,3)$ to $(0,-3)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Midpoint of $AC$: $\left(\frac{-4+4}{2}, \frac{0+0}{2}\right)
= \ans{(0,\ 0)}$

Midpoint of $BD$: $\left(\frac{0+0}{2}, \frac{3+(-3)}{2}\right)
= \ans{(0,\ 0)}$

The two diagonals share a midpoint, so each one cuts the other exactly in half.

\textbf{Step 3.} Side $AB = \sqrt{(0-(-4))^2 + (3-0)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} =
\sqrt{25}$, so $AB = \ans{5}$. With all four sides equal the perimeter is
$4(5)$, or \ans{20 \text{ units}}

\textit{Teaching note:} equal diagonal halves plus perpendicular diagonals is
the coordinate signature of a rhombus, and this banner is one --- which is
exactly what licenses multiplying a single side by four.}

\problem{\textbf{Park $L(1,2)$, $M(5,0)$, $N(9,8)$ --- slopes and area.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Slope compares rise to run, so it is the tool for deciding
whether the corner at $M$ is a right angle. Compute both slopes that meet
there.

\textbf{Step 2.} $\text{slope}(LM) = \dfrac{0-2}{5-1} = \ans{-\tfrac{1}{2}}$

$\text{slope}(MN) = \dfrac{8-0}{9-5} = \ans{2}$

Their product is $-1$, so $LM \perp MN$ and the corner at $M$ is a right angle.

\textbf{Step 3.} With a right angle at $M$ the two sides meeting there are the
base and the height: $LM = \sqrt{16+4} = 2\sqrt{5}$ and
$MN = \sqrt{16+64} = 4\sqrt{5}$, so
$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(2\sqrt{5})(4\sqrt{5}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(40)$. The park covers
\ans{20 \text{ square units}}

\textit{Check it the other way:} shoelace on the same three vertices gives
$\tfrac{1}{2}|1(0-8) + 5(8-2) + 9(2-0)| = \tfrac{1}{2}(40)$, the same number
without needing the right angle at all.}

\problem{\textbf{Building lot $(0,0)$, $(10,0)$, $(10,6)$, $(2,8)$ --- area.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two sides are axis-aligned and two are slanted, so there is no
single length-times-width shortcut. Shoelace handles the whole quadrilateral in
one pass, with the vertices taken in order around the boundary.

\textbf{Step 2.}
\[
A = \tfrac{1}{2}\left| 0(0-8) + 10(6-0) + 10(8-0) + 2(0-6) \right|
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\left| 60 + 80 - 12 \right| = \tfrac{1}{2}(128)
\]

\textbf{Step 3.} The lot covers \ans{64 \text{ m}^2}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $80$, the smallest rectangle that surrounds the
lot ($10$ wide by $8$ tall). The lot is strictly inside that rectangle, so the
bounding box is an upper bound, never the answer.}

\problem{\textbf{Is $W(-2,1)$, $X(3,3)$, $Y(5,-2)$, $Z(0,-4)$ a square?}

\textbf{Step 1.} A square needs two separate facts: four congruent sides, and
one right angle. Congruence alone leaves a rhombus, so distances settle half
the claim and slopes settle the other half.

\textbf{Step 2.} $WX = \sqrt{(3-(-2))^2 + (3-1)^2} = \sqrt{25+4} =
\ans{\sqrt{29}}$

The other three sides give the same radical: $XY = \sqrt{4+25}$,
$YZ = \sqrt{25+4}$, $ZW = \sqrt{4+25}$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Shoelace on the four vertices in order gives
$\tfrac{1}{2}|{-2}(3-(-4)) + 3(-2-1) + 5(-4-3) + 0(1-(-2))| =
\tfrac{1}{2}(58)$, so the area is \ans{29 \text{ square units}}

\textbf{Written justification (manual review).} A complete answer states both
halves: (i) all four sides measure $\sqrt{29}$, so the figure is at least a
rhombus; (ii) $\text{slope}(WX) = \tfrac{2}{5}$ and
$\text{slope}(XY) = -\tfrac{5}{2}$, whose product is $-1$, so the angle at $X$
is right --- and a rhombus with one right angle has four. The designer is
correct. Accept any write-up that establishes congruent sides AND
perpendicularity; a write-up that checks only the four lengths has proved a
rhombus, not a square, and should be sent back for the slope step.

\textit{Consistency note:} the area equals $\left(\sqrt{29}\right)^2 = 29$,
which is what a square's area must do. That agreement is itself evidence for
the claim.}
