\problem{\textbf{Dog-walking pay: plot the table and find the unit rate.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The three points are $(0,0)$, $(3,24)$ and $(5,40)$. Plotted,
they lie on one straight line, and that line starts at the origin --- the mark
of a proportional relationship.

\textbf{Step 2.} Unit rate from the origin to $(3,24)$:
$k = \dfrac{24 - 0}{3 - 0} = 8$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check between two points that are not the origin:
$k = \dfrac{40 - 24}{5 - 3} = \dfrac{16}{2} = 8$. The same rate everywhere is
what makes the graph a straight line through $(0,0)$.

\textit{Manual review:} the plotted points and the drawn line are the student's
own work --- check that all three points are placed correctly and the line is
ruled through the origin. Dev earns $8$ dollars per hour. \ans{k = 8}}

\problem{\textbf{Rice cost: plot the table and find the cost of one kilogram.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Points $(0,0)$, $(2,5)$ and $(6,15)$. All three lie on one
line through the origin, so cost is proportional to mass.

\textbf{Step 2.} From the origin to $(2,5)$: $k = \dfrac{5}{2} = 2.5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Between the two non-origin points:
$\dfrac{15 - 5}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$ --- the same constant.

\textit{Manual review:} inspect the student's plotted points and ruled line.
One kilogram of rice costs $2.5$ dollars. \ans{k = 2.5}}

\problem{\textbf{Cyclist graph: unit rate in kilometres per hour.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The line goes through the origin, so the unit rate is
$y \div x$ at any point on it. Use the marked point $(5,15)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $k = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$ kilometres per hour.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $0.33$, from dividing hours by kilometres. Always
put the quantity named by the $y$-axis on top. \ans{k = 3}}

\problem{\textbf{Paint graph: write the equation.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A line through the origin has the form $y = kx$, so the only
thing to find is $k$.

\textbf{Step 2.} The marked point is $(4,10)$, so
$k = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$ square metres per litre.

\textbf{Step 3.} Substituting the constant gives the equation
$y = 2.5x$. \ans{y = 2.5x}}

\problem{\textbf{Barrel: how long to collect 12 litres.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The equation is $y = 1.5x$, and the question fixes the output
at $y = 12$, so solve $1.5x = 12$ for $x$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $x = \dfrac{12}{1.5} = 8$ minutes.

\textbf{Step 3.} The graph agrees: reading across from $12$ litres meets the
line above $8$ minutes. \ans{x = 8}}

\problem{\textbf{Is the relationship proportional?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Divide $y$ by $x$ in every column. If the relationship is
proportional, all the quotients are the same number.

\textbf{Step 2.} $5 \div 1 = 5$, \quad $8 \div 2 = 4$, \quad
$14 \div 4 = 3.5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The three quotients are different, so there is no single
constant of proportionality. The graph settles it too: the line is straight but
it meets the $y$-axis at $2$, not at the origin, so it is not a proportional
relationship. \ans{5,\ 4,\ 3.5:\ \text{not proportional}}}

\problem{\textbf{Two runners: both unit rates and who is faster.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both lines pass through the origin, so each unit rate is
$y \div x$ at that line's marked point.

\textbf{Step 2.} Runner A through $(4,12)$: $\dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ metres per
second. Runner B through $(5,10)$: $\dfrac{10}{5} = 2$ metres per second.

\textbf{Step 3.} The steeper line has the larger unit rate, so runner A is
faster --- by $1$ metre per second. \ans{A = 3,\ B = 2}}

\problem{\textbf{Water tank: difference between two readings, and the unit
rate.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Read the marked points: at $1$ minute the tank holds $4$
litres, at $5$ minutes it holds $20$ litres.

\textbf{Step 2.} Difference: $20 - 4 = 16$ litres more.

\textbf{Step 3.} Unit rate: $\dfrac{20 - 4}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{16}{4} = 4$ litres
per minute, which matches $y \div x$ at either point. \ans{16,\ k = 4}}

\problem{\textbf{Bottling machine: unit rate, then a prediction off the graph.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The marked point is $(4,14)$ and the line goes through the
origin, so $k = \dfrac{14}{4} = 3.5$ hundreds of bottles per hour.

\textbf{Step 2.} The equation is $y = 3.5x$.

\textbf{Step 3.} At $x = 12$ hours: $y = 3.5 \times 12 = 42$ hundreds of
bottles. The drawn axis stops at $6$ hours, which is exactly why the equation
is worth having. \ans{k = 3.5,\ y = 42}}

\problem{\textbf{Printer: draw the graph, then find the time for 45 pages.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $5$ pages every $2$ minutes is a unit rate of
$\dfrac{5}{2} = 2.5$ pages per minute, so the equation is $y = 2.5x$ and the
graph is the line through $(0,0)$ and $(20,50)$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Set the output to $45$ pages: $2.5x = 45$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $x = \dfrac{45}{2.5} = 18$ minutes. On the graph, $45$ pages
sits between the gridlines at $40$ and $50$, above $18$ minutes.

\textit{Manual review:} the drawn line is the student's own work --- it must
start at the origin and pass through $(4,10)$ and $(20,50)$. \ans{x = 18}}
