# Making and qualifying predictions from scatter plots — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Study guide** (`ss_scatter_curr250.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections built on one small worked
  data set: reading a scatter plot (each dot is one individual; direction of the cloud), predicting
  with the trend line (substitute and get a *typical* value, not a guarantee), and deciding whether
  to trust the answer. The third rule box gives the three questions to ask of every number the line
  produces — is the input inside the measured range, is the answer possible, and is there really a
  pattern at all. Each section has a worked example opening with the strategy sentence and a try-it
  with the answer inverted inside the box.
- **Worksheet** (`ws_scatter_curr250.pdf`, 6 pages) — 12 problems, difficulty 1 → 5, all built on
  three labelled displays printed together on the first page so the student can keep it beside
  them: **A** study time vs quiz score with a trend line, **B** television vs sleep with a trend
  line, **C** shoe size vs spelling score with no pattern at all. After a short warm-up the three
  subskills interleave, so the student must decide each time whether to read the plot, use the line,
  or refuse the prediction. Deliberate traps: problem 5 asks for a prediction at 200 minutes (a
  score of 168 on a test out of 100), problem 11 for a prediction at 20 hours of television (a sleep
  time of −0.5 hours), and problem 9 for a prediction from a display with no association. The
  closing synthesis problem puts a causal newspaper headline next to Display A.
- **Answer key** (`ak_scatter_curr250.pdf`, 4 pages) — every problem worked with the reasoning shown,
  a Quick Answers bank, and a Curriculum block (8.SP.A.1, difficulty 1–5).

## What is verified, honestly

- **14 of the 19 printed answers are machine-checked** by SymPy: every value read from a display was
  checked against the same data array that plots the points, every trend-line prediction was
  recomputed, and the mean sleep time was recalculated independently of the key. The units (hours,
  minutes) are checked in both directions.
- **5 problems (5, 6, 9, 11, 12) carry an instructor-judged part.** Each also has a machine-checked
  part — the number the line produces — and the judged part is the qualification: *why* 168 or −0.5
  is not a prediction, what a negative association implies, whether Display C supports any
  prediction, and whether Display A justifies a causal headline. That reasoning is the point of the
  sheet and no program can grade it, so the key prints a rubric for each: what a full-credit
  response must name, and what earns half credit. The Quick Answers bank marks those `—`.
- No blanks are marked as working space; every printed blank is an answer.

The grade level prints on the answer key only, beside the standards code.
