\noindent\small Each solution names the formula, substitutes the givens, and keeps the unit
through every line. Answers marked $\approx$ are rounded to two decimal places; accept
equivalent exact forms such as $66\pi$.\normalsize

\problem{Closed carton, $\ell = 12$ cm, $w = 8$ cm, $h = 5$ cm. Total surface area?

\textbf{Solution:} A prism has three pairs of matching faces, so
$SA = 2(\ell w + \ell h + wh)$.
\begin{align*}
SA &= 2(12 \cdot 8 + 12 \cdot 5 + 8 \cdot 5) \\
   &= 2(96 + 60 + 40) = 2(196)
\end{align*}
$\ans{392 \text{ cm}^2}$ \quad (Getting 196 means the pairs were never doubled.)}

\problem{Scale 1 cm $=$ 4 m; room drawn 6.5 cm by 3.25 cm. Actual perimeter?

\textbf{Solution:} Convert each drawing length first, because perimeter is a length.
Actual sides: $6.5 \cdot 4 = 26$ m and $3.25 \cdot 4 = 13$ m.
Then $P = 2(26 + 13) = 2(39)$. \quad $\ans{78 \text{ m}}$}

\problem{Closed cylinder, $r = 3$ cm, $h = 8$ cm. Total surface area?

\textbf{Solution:} Curved side (a rolled-up rectangle) $+$ two circles:
$SA = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi r(r + h)$.
\begin{align*}
SA &= 2\pi (3)(3 + 8) = 66\pi \approx 207.3451\ldots
\end{align*}
$\ans{207.35 \text{ cm}^2}$ \quad (150.80 is the curved side alone --- the ends were dropped.)}

\problem{Workshop floor plan on a 1 m grid. Total floor area?

\textbf{Solution:} Cut the plan into two rectangles along the step. The lower rectangle is
8 by 5, giving $40$; the upper left rectangle is 3 wide and 4 tall, giving $12$.
Total: $40 + 12 = 52$. (A shoelace count of the six corners gives the same value.)
\quad $\ans{52 \text{ m}^2}$}

\problem{Shed 6 m by 4 m by 3 m; paint four walls and roof, not the floor.

\textbf{Solution:} Take the faces one kind at a time instead of using the closed-prism formula.
Two long walls: $2(6 \cdot 3) = 36$. Two short walls: $2(4 \cdot 3) = 24$.
Roof: $6 \cdot 4 = 24$. Floor: not painted.
Total $= 36 + 24 + 24$. \quad $\ans{84 \text{ m}^2}$
\quad (108 is the full closed prism --- the floor was painted by mistake.)}

\problem{Scale 1 cm $=$ 4 m; deck drawn 5 cm by 3 cm. Actual area?

\textbf{Solution:} Scale the two lengths, then multiply --- never scale the area once.
Actual sides: $5 \cdot 4 = 20$ m and $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ m, so $A = 20 \cdot 12$.
\quad $\ans{240 \text{ m}^2}$\\
Equivalently: drawing area $15$ cm$^2$ times $4^2 = 16$. The common error, $15 \cdot 4 = 60$,
uses the length factor on an area.}

\problem{Label wrapped on a can, $r = 4$ cm, $h = 11$ cm, with a 1 cm overlap.

\textbf{Solution:} Unrolled, the label is a rectangle. Its height is the can's height, 11 cm;
its length is the circumference plus the overlap: $2\pi(4) + 1 = 25.1327\ldots + 1$.
\[ A = (2\pi \cdot 4 + 1)(11) \approx 287.4601\ldots \]
$\ans{287.46 \text{ cm}^2}$ \quad (276.46 leaves out the glued overlap.)}

\problem{Two identical L-shaped panels: 10 m by 4 m with a 6 m by 3 m rectangle on top.

\textbf{Solution:} One panel splits into $10 \cdot 4 = 40$ and $6 \cdot 3 = 18$, so one panel is
$58$ m$^2$. The design needs two of them: $2(40 + 18) = 2(58)$.
\quad $\ans{116 \text{ m}^2}$ \quad (58 answers for one panel only.)}

\problem{Model at 1 : 5 has surface area 148 cm$^2$. Real crate's surface area?

\textbf{Solution:} Every length is multiplied by 5, and an area is a length times a length, so
area is multiplied by $5^2 = 25$: $148 \cdot 25$. \quad $\ans{3700 \text{ cm}^2}$\\
(740 uses the length factor 5 on an area. Volume would scale by $5^3$.)}

\problem{Closed box, square base 8 cm by 8 cm, total surface area 448 cm$^2$. Height?

\textbf{Solution:} Write the surface area in terms of $h$: two square ends of $8 \cdot 8$ and
four rectangular sides of $8h$.
\begin{align*}
2(8 \cdot 8) + 4(8h) &= 448 \\
128 + 32h &= 448 \\
32h &= 320
\end{align*}
$\ans{10 \text{ cm}}$ \quad Check: $128 + 4(8)(10) = 128 + 320 = 448$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{Design decision: cube 6 by 6 by 6 versus box 12 by 6 by 3, both of volume 216.

\textbf{Model answer (open reasoning --- review by hand):}
Box A (cube): $SA = 6(6 \cdot 6) = 216$ square centimetres.
Box B: $SA = 2(12 \cdot 6 + 12 \cdot 3 + 6 \cdot 3) = 2(72 + 36 + 18) = 252$ square centimetres.
Both hold 216 cubic centimetres, but B needs 36 square centimetres more cardboard, so
\ans{\text{Box A}} uses less material. The general reason: for a fixed volume, the closer a
box is to a cube, the smaller its surface area; a long flat box has more outside for the same
inside. A designer might still choose B --- flat boxes stack and ship better on a pallet, they
fit a wider product, and they give a larger printable front face for the label.
Award credit for any well-argued practical reason (shipping, shelf display, product shape,
ease of packing) that is stated alongside the correct numerical comparison.}

\problem{Tank drawing at 1 cm $=$ 2.5 m: 1.6 cm across, 3.2 cm tall. Painted area (curved wall
$+$ top only)?

\textbf{Solution:} Scale first, then apply the surface-area formulas.
Actual diameter $= 1.6 \cdot 2.5 = 4$ m, so $r = 2$ m; actual height $= 3.2 \cdot 2.5 = 8$ m.
\begin{align*}
\text{curved wall} &= 2\pi r h = 2\pi(2)(8) = 32\pi \\
\text{top} &= \pi r^2 = \pi(2)^2 = 4\pi \\
\text{painted} &= 36\pi \approx 113.0973\ldots
\end{align*}
$\ans{113.10 \text{ m}^2}$ \quad (125.66 paints both circles; the base is on the ground.)}
