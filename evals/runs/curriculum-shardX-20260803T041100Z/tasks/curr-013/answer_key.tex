\problem{\textbf{Apples on three plates.}
Count each plate one object at a time: Plate A has 5, Plate B has 8, Plate C has 3.
Now find the least. Three is said first when counting (3, 5, 8), so 3 is the least
and 8 is the greatest. Order: \ans{3, 5, 8}}

\problem{\textbf{Three number cards: 7, 4, 9.}
Count up and listen for which number is said first: 4, then 7, then 9.
The number said earliest in counting order is the least. Order: \ans{4, 7, 9}}

\problem{\textbf{Ten-frames.}
Frame A: the top row is full (5) and one more counter sits below, so $5+1=6$.
Frame B: both rows are full, so it is a full ten, 10. Frame C: 3 counters.
Counting order gives 3, then 6, then 10. Order: \ans{3, 6, 10}}

\problem{\textbf{Cube trains.}
Count the cubes in each train: Train A has 13 cubes, Train B has 7 cubes,
Train C has 11 cubes. Train B is the shortest train, so 7 is the least; Train A
is the longest, so 13 is the greatest. Order: \ans{7, 11, 13}}

\problem{\textbf{Books on three shelves.}
The three amounts are 20 (red), 12 (blue) and 21 (green).
Compare the tens first: 12 has 1 ten, while 20 and 21 each have 2 tens, so 12 is
the least. Then compare 20 and 21: same 2 tens, and 0 ones is fewer than 1 one,
so 20 comes before 21. Order: \ans{12, 20, 21}}

\problem{\textbf{Buttons in three jars.}
The three amounts are 41 (Mia), 14 (Sam) and 40 (Joy).
Look at the tens digit, not just the digits used: 14 has only 1 ten, while 41 and
40 each have 4 tens, so 14 is the least. Between 40 and 41, the ones digit decides:
0 ones is fewer than 1 one. Order: \ans{14, 40, 41}}

\problem{\textbf{Stickers.}
Step 1 --- find Ben's amount. Ben has 3 more than Ana's 8, so Ben has
$8+3=\ans{11}$ stickers.

Step 2 --- now all three amounts are known: Ana 8, Ben 11, Cal 6.
Counting order gives 6, then 8, then 11. Order: \ans{6, 8, 11}}

\problem{\textbf{Team points.}
Step 1 --- find each team's total. Team Red: $10+5=\ans{15}$ points.
Team Gold: $9+3=\ans{12}$ points. Team Blue already has its total, 20 points.

Step 2 --- order the three totals. Compare the tens: 12 and 15 each have 1 ten,
20 has 2 tens, so 20 is the greatest. Between 12 and 15, 2 ones is fewer than
5 ones, so 12 is the least. Order: \ans{12, 15, 20}}
