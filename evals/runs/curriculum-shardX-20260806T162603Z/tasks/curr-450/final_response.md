Three PDFs — study guide, worksheet, answer key.

**Study guide (`ss_models_curr450.pdf`, 2 pages).** Three sections, each a rule
box + worked example + try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.
Section 1 treats mean and median as two competing *models* of "typical" and says
when each is the honest choice. Section 2 is residuals: $y-\hat y$ per point,
squared and summed into one fit score, with the smaller score the closer fit.
Section 3 is the part students usually skip — three limits to state on every
conclusion: **who** (the group actually surveyed), **why not** (observational
data cannot show causation; name a confounder), and **how far** (a model is
trustworthy inside the range of its data, not outside).

**Worksheet (`ws_models_curr450.pdf`, 6 pages).** Twelve problems built on three
shared data sets printed once at the top: nine days of rainfall, six weekly
heights of one sunflower with two candidate lines (Model A and Model B), and a
bar chart of cars per household. A two-way travel survey table sits with problem
4 and is reused by problem 10. After a short warm-up the three subskills
interleave — summary choice, residuals, inference, summary, residuals,
inference — so from problem 3 onward the student has to decide what kind of
question they are looking at. Problem 12 is the synthesis: the two models'
week-8 predictions differ by 0.9 cm, and the student must write a two-sentence
conclusion whose second sentence is a genuine qualification. Difficulty ramps
1 → 5.

**Answer key (`ak_models_curr450.pdf`, 4 pages).** Full reasoning, including why
the IQR pairs with the median and why squaring residuals is what stops over- and
under-predictions cancelling. Every open item carries a written rubric with
explicit non-acceptances. Quick Answers bank at the top with each part labelled,
and a generated Curriculum block (HSS-ID.A, HSS-ID.B, HSS-IC.A, HSS-IC.B;
difficulty 1–5).

**What is verified — please read this part.** The sheet asks for 20 graded
responses. **Fourteen are machine-checked** with SymPy: the median, IQR and mean
of the rainfall data, both chart reads, all four probabilities, the residual, the
two sums of squared residuals, both week-8 predictions, and the gap between them.
**Six are instructor-judged** — problems 4(b), 5(b), 7, 9(b), 10(c) and 12(b).
That is not a shortfall; on a sheet about *qualified* inference the qualification
is the skill, and it is prose that no computer algebra system can grade. Each of
the six prints as `---` in the Quick Answers bank, and each has a rubric in the
key stating what full credit requires and what to refuse — for instance, "the
sample is too small" is not the right objection on problem 4 (200 is a fine
sample; coverage is the issue), and "correlation is not causation" with no named
confounder earns nothing on problem 7.

No misconception traps are declared here. The errors this topic produces are
errors of *claim* rather than of arithmetic — generalising past the sample,
extrapolating past the data — and those are caught by the rubrics, not by a
planted wrong number.
