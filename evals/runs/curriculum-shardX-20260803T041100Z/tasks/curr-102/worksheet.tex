\wstitleblock{Powers of Ten and Place-Value Relationships}{Grades 4--5 \quad Number and Operations in Base Ten}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Digit-value relationships, powers of ten in exponent form, shifting
digits by 10, 100 and 1000, and rounding to a place to estimate}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The one idea behind every problem.} Each place in our number
system is worth \textbf{ten times} the place to its right, and \textbf{one
tenth} of the place to its left. Everything below is that single fact used four
ways.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{hundred} & \textbf{ten} & & & & & & \\
\textbf{thousands} & \textbf{thousands} & \textbf{thousands} & \textbf{hundreds}
& \textbf{tens} & \textbf{ones} & \textbf{tenths} & \textbf{hundredths} \\
\hline
$10^5$ & $10^4$ & $10^3$ & $10^2$ & $10^1$ & $10^0$ & $\frac{1}{10}$ & $\frac{1}{100}$ \\
\hline
 & & & & & & & \\[0.6cm]
\hline
\end{tabular}

\small\textit{Reference chart. No values are filled in --- use the numbers given
in each problem.}\normalsize
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item Show the place-value thinking, not just the answer --- name the places you
compared or the number of places the digits moved.
\item A power of ten tells you \emph{how many places}: $10^2$ means two places,
$10^3$ means three places.
\item Write your final answer on the answer line.
\end{itemize}

\problem[5cm]{The number 4400 is written with two 4s.

\smallskip
The 4 in the thousands place is worth 4000. The 4 in the hundreds place is
worth 400.

\smallskip
How many times as much is the thousands 4 worth as the hundreds 4? Write the
division you used, then give the answer.}

\problem[5cm]{An exponent on 10 counts how many times 10 is used as a factor.

\smallskip
Write $10^3$ as a repeated multiplication, then give its value in standard
form.}

\problem[5cm]{Find $47 \times 100$.

\smallskip
Explain the move in place-value words: each digit of 47 shifts how many places,
and in which direction? Fill in a copy of the reference chart if it helps.}

\problem[5.5cm]{The number 0.88 is written with two 8s.

\smallskip
The 8 in the tenths place is worth 0.8. The 8 in the hundredths place is worth
0.08.

\smallskip
The hundredths 8 is worth what fraction of the tenths 8? Write the division
$0.08 \div 0.8$ as a decimal answer, and name the fraction it equals in words.}

\problem[5cm]{Estimate $4780 + 3260$ by rounding each number to the nearest
thousand --- that is, to the nearest $10^3$.

\smallskip
Write both rounded numbers, then the estimated sum.}

\problem[5cm]{Every whole number that ends in zeros can be written as a digit
times a power of ten.

\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} Fill in the exponent: \quad $60000 = 6 \times 10^{\square}$

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Multiply your $6 \times 10^{\square}$ back out to check it, and
write that product on the answer line.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Find $3.6 \times 1000$.

\smallskip
The digits of 3.6 shift three places to the left. Show where the 3 and the 6
land, and what fills the places behind them.}

\problem[5.5cm]{The number 55500 is written with three 5s.

\smallskip
The 5 in the ten-thousands place is worth 50000. The 5 in the hundreds place is
worth 500.

\smallskip
How many times as much is the ten-thousands 5 worth as the hundreds 5? Careful:
the two places are not next to each other --- count them.}

\problem[5cm]{Estimate $6238 - 2905$ by rounding each number to the nearest
hundred --- that is, to the nearest $10^2$.

\smallskip
Write both rounded numbers, then the estimated difference. Look closely at the
tens place of 2905 before you round it.}

\problem[5.5cm]{Find $4500 \div 100$.

\smallskip
Dividing by a power of ten shifts the digits the opposite way from multiplying.
Say how many places the digits move and in which direction, then give the
quotient.}

\problem[6cm]{A number is written in expanded form using powers of ten:
\[
3 \times 10^4 \;+\; 7 \times 10^2 \;+\; 5
\]
\textbf{(a)} What is the value of each term?

\smallskip
\textbf{(b)} Write the number in standard form. Two places have no term written
for them --- decide what digit belongs in each of those places and why.}

\problem[6cm]{A warehouse counts 8120 stickers and packs them into boxes of 39.

\smallskip
Estimate $8120 \div 39$ by first rounding the divisor to the nearest ten, so
that the division becomes a shift by a power of ten.

\smallskip
\textbf{(a)} What does 39 round to? \quad \textbf{(b)} Estimate the quotient.
\quad \textbf{(c)} Is your estimate larger or smaller than the exact quotient?
Explain in one sentence why rounding the divisor \emph{up} does that.}
