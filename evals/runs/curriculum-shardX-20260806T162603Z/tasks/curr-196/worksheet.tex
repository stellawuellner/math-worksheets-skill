\wstitleblock{Statistical Questions and Describing Variability}{Grade 6}{}

\begin{center}
\textit{Telling a statistical question from a single-answer question, measuring
spread, and describing what a data set looks like}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{statistical question} is one you answer by collecting data
        that \emph{varies}. ``How tall is the flagpole?'' has one answer.
        ``How tall are the trees in the park?'' has many, and they differ ---
        that variability is what makes it statistical.
  \item \textbf{Spread} says how far apart the values are:
        range $=$ largest $-$ smallest, and the interquartile range
        $=$ upper quartile $-$ lower quartile.
  \item To find quartiles, put the values in order, split them into a lower half
        and an upper half, and take the median of each half.
  \item When you are asked to \emph{describe} a data set, give a typical value
        \textbf{and} a measure of spread. One without the other is only half the
        story.
\end{itemize}
