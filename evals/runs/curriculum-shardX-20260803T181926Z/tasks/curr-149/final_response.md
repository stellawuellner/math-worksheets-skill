# Coordinates, line plots and patterns — find the mistake, then fix it (Grades 4–5)

Three PDFs: the student worksheet, a step-by-step answer key, and a two-page
study guide.

**What the worksheet does.** Eight problems, all on the same idea: a number read
in the wrong place. Two displays sit at the top of the sheet and are used by
name, so a young reader never has to guess which picture belongs to a question.

* A labelled grid (problems 1 and 2). Problem 1 shows that (5,4) and (4,5) are
  different points by asking how many grid steps separate them. Problem 2 is a
  find-and-fix: Nia counts 7 across and 2 up for the pair (2,7), lands on a
  point that is already labelled, and the sheet asks how far that is from where
  she meant — 10 steps, not a near miss.
* A line plot of bean-plant heights in half-inch steps (problems 4 to 6). Two
  find-and-fix items: Omar copies the height printed under the marks instead of
  counting them, and Kaya counts the five labelled heights instead of the
  fifteen plants. Problem 5 asks which height occurred most often, with the
  same confusion available and warned against.
* Two pattern problems (3 and 7) where the error is the order of the ordered
  pair, not the arithmetic: problem 7's Sam gets both numbers right and writes
  them the wrong way round.
* Problem 8 is open: plot (6,2) and (2,6), explain why they differ, and give a
  memory aid a classmate could use.

Difficulty runs 1 to 5.

**What was machine-verified.** Eight checks were recomputed with SymPy: the grid
step counts, both line-plot readings (which come from the same data that draws
the plot, so the picture and the key cannot disagree), the most common height,
the plant total, and both pattern terms. Eight misconception traps are declared
and each was proved to produce a value the problem's own check rejects — so an
answer of 0, 5, 12, 3 or 21 identifies a specific error rather than just being
marked wrong. They print in the answer key as a "Common wrong answers" list.

**What is flagged for manual review.** Problem 8 only, since it asks for a
drawing and a sentence. The answer key gives model wording, three usable memory
aids, and a checklist of what a full-credit response must contain.

**The study guide** covers the three skills the worksheet tests — across before
up, counting marks on a line plot (with its own small plot to read), and making
ordered pairs from two patterns — each with a rule box, a worked example, and a
try-it whose answer is printed upside down inside the box.
