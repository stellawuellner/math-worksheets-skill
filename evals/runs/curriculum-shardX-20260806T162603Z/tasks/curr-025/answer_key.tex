\problem{Warm-up. Just one more! Count on. \ $8+1$}

\textbf{Solution:} Adding 1 is one hop: say 8, hop 9. So $8+1=9$. \ans{9}

\problem{Warm-up. Count on from 6. \ $6+2$}

\textbf{Solution:} Say 6, hop 7, 8 --- two hops for the 2. So $6+2=8$.
\ans{8}

\problem{Warm-up. Start with the bigger number, then count on. \ $2+7$}

\textbf{Solution:} Addition works in either order, so start big: say 7, hop
8, 9 --- only two hops instead of seven. So $2+7=9$. \ans{9}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. \ $4+4$}

\textbf{Solution:} The numbers match --- it is a double. Doubles are facts
to know by heart: $4+4=8$. \ans{8}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. \ $3+7$}

\textbf{Solution:} 3 and 7 are tens partners --- they fit together to fill a
ten-frame. So $3+7=10$. \ans{10}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. \ $5+5$}

\textbf{Solution:} A double that is also a tens partner pair: $5+5=10$.
\ans{10}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. \ $6+4$}

\textbf{Solution:} 6 and 4 are tens partners: 6 needs exactly 4 to fill up
to ten. So $6+4=10$. \ans{10}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. Hint: it is almost a double! \ $3+4$}

\textbf{Solution:} Near double: $3+4$ is the double $3+3$ and 1 more.
$3+3=6$, and one more is 7. So $3+4=7$. \ans{7}

\problem{Pick your strategy, then add. \ $5+3$}

\textbf{Solution:} The 3 is small, so count on from 5: say 5, hop 6, 7, 8.
So $5+3=8$. \ans{8}

\problem{Challenge! Add $4+5$ with the strategy you like best. Then tell
which strategy you picked and why it is quick for these numbers.}

\textbf{Solution:} One quick way is the near double: $4+5$ is $4+4$ and 1
more, so $8+1=9$. Counting on from 5 also works: 6, 7, 8, 9. Either way,
$4+5=9$. \ans{9}

\emph{Tell which strategy and why (instructor judges):} any named strategy
that fits the numbers counts --- for example ``near double, because 4 and 5
are neighbors, so I did $4+4$ and one more'' --- as long as the reason
matches what the student actually did.
