\problem{Rope 9 cubes, scarf 4 cubes. How many cubes longer is the rope?}

\textbf{Solution:} Same unit on both, so subtract the counts: $9 - 4 = 5$.
The rope is longer by \ans{5~\text{cubes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Worm 6 clips, leaf 8 clips. Write $>$, $<$, or $=$: \ 6 \_\_ 8.}

\textbf{Solution:} 6 clips is fewer than 8 clips, so the worm is shorter:
\ans{6 < 8}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Pencil 7 cubes, marker 5 cubes, brush 8 cubes --- order shortest to
longest.}

\textbf{Solution:} The smallest count is 5, then 7, then 8:
\ans{5,\ 7,\ 8}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{How many stickers does Ben have?}

\textbf{Solution:} Count the dots in Ben's row of the chart: four dots.
\ans{4}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Who has the most stickers?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare the rows: Kim's row has the most dots (more than
Ana's and Ben's). \ans{\text{Kim}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{How many stickers do the three kids have all together?}

\textbf{Solution:} Add the three rows: $7 + 4 = 11$, and $11 + 9 = 20$.
\ans{20}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{How many more stickers does Kim have than Ben?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``How many more'' means find the difference: $9 - 4 = 5$.
\ans{5}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Teacher's ribbon 14 cubes, Jon's ribbon 8 cubes. How many cubes
shorter is Jon's?}

\textbf{Solution:} Subtract the counts: $14 - 8 = 6$. Jon's ribbon is shorter
by \ans{6~\text{cubes}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{How many fewer stickers does Ana have than Kim?}

\textbf{Solution:} ``How many fewer'' is also a difference: $9 - 7 = 2$.
\ans{2}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.1cm}

\problem{Challenge: Kim wants 12 stickers. (a) How many more does she need?
(b) Say how the chart helps.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The chart shows Kim has 9. Count on from 9 to 12: 10,
11, 12 --- three jumps. \ans{3}

(b) \emph{Instructor judges:} accept any wording that uses the chart as
evidence --- for example, ``the chart shows Kim already has 9, so I counted
on from 9 up to 12 and needed 3 more.''
