\sstitleblock{Equal Groups --- How Many Groups? How Many in Each?}

\skillheading{Skill 1 --- How many are in each group?}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{The three numbers in every sharing story:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{total} --- how many there are altogether (the biggest number)
  \item \textbf{groups} --- how many bags, plates, pots, rows
  \item \textbf{in each} --- how many go in one bag, one plate, one pot
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
\textbf{When the story gives the number of groups:}\quad
total $\div$ groups $=$ how many in each group.\par
\smallskip
Picture it: $(\bullet\,\bullet\,\bullet)\ (\bullet\,\bullet\,\bullet)\
(\bullet\,\bullet\,\bullet)$ --- three groups, and three dots \emph{in each}.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad $27$ seeds are shared into $3$ pots. How many seeds are
in each pot?
\step{The story gives how many pots there are, so $3$ is a group count and the
missing number is the size of one group --- share the total out.}
\step{$27 \div 3$: skip-count by threes to $27$, which takes nine of them.}
Each pot gets $\ans{9}$ seeds. Check: $3 \times 9 = 27$ $\checkmark$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad $21$ apples go into $3$ baskets, the same number in each.
How many apples are in each basket?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{7}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 2 --- How many groups?}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{When the story gives how many go in each group:}\quad
total $\div$ in each $=$ number of groups.\par
\smallskip
The words \textbf{``in each''} are the giveaway. ``$5$ cups'' counts groups;
``$5$ in each cup'' tells the size of a group.\par
\smallskip
\textbf{Never write back a number the story gave you.} If the story says $5$ go
in each cup, then $5$ is not the answer to ``how many cups?''
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad There are $15$ counters. Each cup holds $5$ counters. How
many cups are filled?
\step{The words in each tell us $5$ is the size of a group, so the missing
number is how many groups the total makes.}
\step{$15 \div 5$: take away five at a time --- fifteen, ten, five, none left,
so three cups were filled.}
There are $\ans{3}$ cups. Check: $3 \times 5 = 15$ $\checkmark$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad There are $28$ crayons and $7$ crayons go in each box. How
many boxes are filled?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{4}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{Skill 3 --- Check by multiplying back}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{The check:}\quad groups $\times$ in each group $=$ total.\par
\smallskip
If the multiplication does not land on the total, the two numbers are doing the
wrong jobs --- most often because a number from the story was written back as
the answer.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:}\quad There are $32$ marbles with $8$ in each jar. Someone says
there are $8$ jars. Check it, and give the right number of jars.
\step{Test the claim by multiplying back before doing anything else: eight jars
of eight would need sixty-four marbles, more than the total, so the claim is
wrong.}
\step{$32 \div 8$: count up by eights --- eight, sixteen, twenty-four,
thirty-two --- which is four jars.}
There are $\ans{4}$ jars. Check: $4 \times 8 = 32$ $\checkmark$
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:}\quad There are $45$ stickers with $9$ in each row. How many rows
are there? Check by multiplying back.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{5}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} the answer is never a number the story already gave you.
If the question repeats one of the numbers you were told, read it again --- you
have probably answered the other question.
\end{watchoutbox}
