\problem{$x + y = 5$ and $x - y = 3$. Find the pair that makes both true.}

\textbf{Solution:} Adding the two equations removes $y$: $2x = 8$, so $x = 4$.
Substituting back into $x + y = 5$ gives $y = 1$. Both equations check:
$4 + 1 = 5$ and $4 - 1 = 3$. \ans{(4,\, 1)}

\problem{$2x + y = 11$ and $x - y = 1$. Find the pair that makes both true.}

\textbf{Solution:} Adding removes $y$: $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$. Then
$4 - y = 1$ gives $y = 3$. Check the first equation too: $2(4) + 3 = 11$.
\ans{(4,\, 3)}

\problem{Lines $A$ ($y = x + 1$) and $B$ ($y = 7 - x$). (a) Crossing point.
(b) What it means.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} On the grid the two lines meet one gridline right of 2
and level with 4. Algebraically, $x + 1 = 7 - x$ gives $2x = 6$, so $x = 3$
and $y = 4$. \ans{(3,\, 4)}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} The point lies on line $A$ and on
line $B$ at the same time, so its coordinates satisfy $y = x + 1$ and
$y = 7 - x$ together --- and that is exactly what "solution of the system"
means. Substituting $(3, 4)$ into both equations is a complete explanation.
``It is where they cross'' with no link back to the equations is not.

\problem{Bike plans $y = 4h + 8$ and $y = 2h + 16$, with tables. (a) Plan A at
4 hours. (b) Plan B at 4 hours. (c) The shared pair.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Read the last column of plan A's table. \ans{24}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Read the last column of plan B's table. \ans{24}

\textbf{Solution (c):} The tables agree at 4 hours and \$24, and the algebra
agrees: $4h + 8 = 2h + 16$ gives $2h = 8$, so $h = 4$ and $y = 24$.
\ans{(4,\, 24)}

\problem{Gym P: \$25 plus \$8 a class. Gym Q: \$40 plus \$8 a class.
(a) When do they cost the same? (b) What that means.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Setting $8c + 25 = 8c + 40$ and subtracting $8c$ leaves
$25 = 40$, which is false, so no number of classes makes the costs equal.
\ans{\text{no solution}}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Both gyms charge the same \$8 per
class, so the \$15 difference in joining fees never closes: gym P is always
exactly \$15 cheaper. Graphically the two cost lines have equal slopes and
different intercepts, so they are parallel and never meet. ``No solution''
alone, with no reference to the gyms, is not a complete answer.

\problem{Lines $A$ ($y = x + 1$) and $C$ ($y = 2x - 4$). Crossing point.}

\textbf{Solution:} Reading the grid, the lines meet at the corner five right
and six up. Algebraically $x + 1 = 2x - 4$ gives $x = 5$, and then $y = 6$.
\ans{(5,\, 6)}

\problem{A student claims $(3, 2)$ solves $3x + y = 11$ and $x + 2y = 12$.
(a) The actual solution. (b) Why the claim fails.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} From the first equation $y = 11 - 3x$. Substituting
into the second: $x + 2(11 - 3x) = 12$, so $x + 22 - 6x = 12$, giving
$-5x = -10$ and $x = 2$; then $y = 5$. \ans{(2,\, 5)}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} Substituting $(3, 2)$ into the
first equation gives $3(3) + 2 = 11$, which is true --- and that is exactly
why the claim is tempting. The second equation gives $3 + 2(2) = 7$, not 12,
so the pair fails there. A solution of a system must satisfy every equation,
so one hit out of two is not enough. Full credit needs both substitutions
shown, or at least the failing one named.

\problem{Plan J: $y = 6x + 20$ with a table; plan K: $y = 2x + 32$.
(a) Plan J at 3 gigabytes. (b) The shared pair.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Read the last column of the table. \ans{38}

\textbf{Solution (b):} Set the two costs equal: $6x + 20 = 2x + 32$ gives
$4x = 12$, so $x = 3$, and then $y = 6(3) + 20 = 38$ --- the same 38 the table
shows. \ans{(3,\, 38)}

\problem{Lines $A$ ($y = x + 1$) and $D$ ($y = x + 4$). (a) Solution or none.
(b) How the graph shows it.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} Setting $x + 1 = x + 4$ and subtracting $x$ leaves
$1 = 4$, which is false. \ans{\text{no solution}}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} On the Graph Bank, lines $A$ and
$D$ climb at the same rate, so the vertical gap between them stays at 3 units
all the way across the grid and they never meet. A system whose graphs never
cross has no pair that lies on both, so it has no solution. Saying only ``they
are parallel'' does not say what that means for the system.

\problem{Pool: $y = 5v$ against $y = 2v + 30$. (a) Where they cost the same.
(b) What it means for a swimmer.}

\textbf{Solution (a):} $5v = 2v + 30$ gives $3v = 30$, so $v = 10$, and the
cost is $5(10) = 50$ dollars. \ans{(10,\, 50)}

\textbf{Solution (b) --- instructor-judged.} At exactly 10 visits the two
options cost the same, \$50. Below 10 visits the pay-per-visit line is the
lower of the two, so single visits are cheaper; above 10 visits the membership
line is lower, so the membership is cheaper. A correct answer names the
crossing and which option wins on each side of it; the coordinates repeated in
words are not enough.
