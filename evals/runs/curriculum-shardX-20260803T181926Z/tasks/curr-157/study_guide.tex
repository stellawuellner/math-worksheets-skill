\sstitleblock{Unit Rates with Fractions}

\begin{formulabox}
\textbf{What a unit rate is.} A unit rate says how much there is for exactly
\emph{one} --- one hour, one pound, one batch. Written as a division, it is
\emph{amount} $\div$ \emph{number of units}.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Dividing by a fraction:} $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b}
\times \frac{d}{c}$ --- keep the first fraction, flip the second, multiply.\\[3pt]
\textbf{Mixed numbers first.} Rewrite any mixed number as an improper fraction
\emph{before} you flip anything.
\end{formulabox}

\skillheading{1. Find a unit rate from two fractions}

\begin{formulabox}
Put the amount on top and the number of units underneath, then multiply by the
reciprocal. Dividing by a fraction below one makes the answer \emph{bigger} ---
that is not a mistake, it is what ``per one whole unit'' means.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A snail crawls $\frac{3}{4}$ of a metre in $\frac{1}{2}$ of
an hour. How many metres per hour?
\step{``Per hour'' names one hour as the unit, so divide the distance by the
time rather than comparing them any other way.}
\step{Flip the second fraction and multiply: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{1}
= \frac{3}{2}$.}

The unit rate is $\ans{\tfrac{3}{2}}$ metres per hour.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A pump moves $\frac{2}{5}$ of a tank in $\frac{1}{4}$ of an
hour. How much of a tank per hour?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{\tfrac{8}{5}}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{2. Compare two rates by their unit rates}

\begin{formulabox}
Two rates with different amounts \emph{and} different times cannot be compared
as they stand. Turn each into a unit rate --- the same ``per one'' for both ---
and then the two numbers can be compared directly.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} Ali cycles $\frac{3}{8}$ km in $\frac{1}{4}$ h; Bo cycles
$\frac{4}{5}$ km in $\frac{2}{5}$ h. Who is faster?
\step{Different distances over different times share no common footing, so
rewrite both as kilometres per one hour before comparing.}
\step{Ali: $\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{4}{1} = \frac{3}{2}$. Bo: $\frac{4}{5}
\times \frac{5}{2} = 2$.}

Ali $\ans{\tfrac{3}{2}}$, Bo $\ans{2}$, so Ali's rate $\ans{<}$ Bo's rate.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} Kai runs $\frac{2}{3}$ of a mile in $\frac{1}{2}$ of an hour;
Mia runs $\frac{3}{4}$ of a mile in $\frac{1}{2}$ of an hour. Find both unit
rates and write $>$ or $<$ between them.\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: Kai $\ans{\tfrac{4}{3}}$, Mia
$\ans{\tfrac{3}{2}}$, Kai $\ans{<}$ Mia}
\end{tryitbox}

\skillheading{3. Find a unit rate in a word problem}

\begin{formulabox}
Find the two quantities the story pairs up, and ask which one the question
wants \emph{one} of. That one goes underneath. Then divide as usual, and label
the answer with both units so you can hear whether it makes sense.
\end{formulabox}

\begin{examplebox}
\textbf{Example:} A pump empties $\frac{5}{6}$ of a tank in $\frac{2}{3}$ of
an hour. What fraction of a tank per hour?
\step{The question asks for one hour's worth, so the hours go underneath and
the tank amount on top.}
\step{$\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{4}$.}

That is $\ans{\tfrac{5}{4}}$ of a tank per hour.
\end{examplebox}

\begin{tryitbox}
\textbf{Try it:} A printer finishes $\frac{3}{5}$ of a job in $\frac{1}{4}$ of
an hour. How many jobs per hour?\\[2pt]
\rotatebox{180}{\footnotesize check: $\ans{\tfrac{12}{5}}$}
\end{tryitbox}

\begin{watchoutbox}
\textbf{(!) Watch out:} the costliest slip is dividing the wrong way round.
Before you flip, say the answer out loud with its units --- ``miles per hour''
puts miles on top and hours underneath. ``Hours per mile'' is a real quantity
too, just not the one the question asked for.
\end{watchoutbox}
