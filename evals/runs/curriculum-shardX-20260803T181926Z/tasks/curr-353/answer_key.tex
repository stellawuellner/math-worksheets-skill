\problem{\textbf{Multiply $7 + 2i$ by its conjugate.}
\par
The conjugate keeps the real part and flips the sign of the imaginary part, so
it is $7 - 2i$. Multiply the two:
\[ (7+2i)(7-2i) = 49 - 14i + 14i - 4i^2 = 49 - 4(-1) = 49 + 4 \]
The middle terms cancel and the $-4i^2$ turns into $+4$, which is why the
product is real --- this is the fact the whole worksheet runs on.
\par
$\ans{53}$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{6+4i}{2i}$.}
\par
The conjugate of $2i$ is $-2i$. Multiply the top and the bottom by it:
\[ \frac{(6+4i)(-2i)}{(2i)(-2i)} = \frac{-12i - 8i^2}{-4i^2}
   = \frac{-12i + 8}{4} = \frac{8 - 12i}{4} \]
Divide both parts of the numerator by $4$.
\par
$\ans{2-3i}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $-2 + 3i$ is the diagnostic wrong answer. It comes from
using $1/i = i$; the truth is $1/i = -i$.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{5+5i}{2+i}$.}
\par
Multiply the top and the bottom by the conjugate $2 - i$:
\[ \frac{(5+5i)(2-i)}{(2+i)(2-i)} = \frac{10 - 5i + 10i - 5i^2}{4 + 1}
   = \frac{10 + 5i + 5}{5} = \frac{15 + 5i}{5} \]
\par
$\ans{3+i}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $(3+i)(2+i) = 6 + 3i + 2i - 1 = 5 + 5i$ $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{11+10i}{4-i}$.}
\par
The denominator is $4 - i$, so its conjugate is $4 + i$:
\[ \frac{(11+10i)(4+i)}{(4-i)(4+i)} = \frac{44 + 11i + 40i + 10i^2}{16 + 1}
   = \frac{44 + 51i - 10}{17} = \frac{34 + 51i}{17} \]
Divide both parts by $17$: $34/17 = 2$ and $51/17 = 3$.
\par
$\ans{2+3i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Impedance from voltage and current.}
\par
Given $V = 2 + 23i$ volts and $I = 3 + 2i$ amps, impedance is $Z = V/I$, so the
computation is a division by the current.
\[ Z = \frac{2 + 23i}{3 + 2i} = \frac{(2+23i)(3-2i)}{(3+2i)(3-2i)}
   = \frac{6 - 4i + 69i - 46i^2}{9 + 4} = \frac{6 + 65i + 46}{13}
   = \frac{52 + 65i}{13} \]
Divide both parts by $13$.
\par
$\ans{4+5i\ \text{ohms}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $(4+5i)(3+2i) = 12 + 8i + 15i - 10 = 2 + 23i$ volts
$\checkmark$. The real part is the resistance and the imaginary part is the
reactance, both in ohms.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{11-10i}{3+2i}$, and answer Priya.}
\par
Dividing part by part is not a legal move: division is defined by
multiplication, and $\left(\frac{11}{3} - 5i\right)(3+2i)$ is nowhere near
$11 - 10i$. Only multiplying the top and the bottom by the conjugate keeps the
value of the quotient unchanged.
\[ \frac{(11-10i)(3-2i)}{(3+2i)(3-2i)} = \frac{33 - 22i - 30i + 20i^2}{9 + 4}
   = \frac{33 - 52i - 20}{13} = \frac{13 - 52i}{13} \]
\par
$\ans{1-4i}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $3.67 - 5i$ is the part-by-part answer, the error this
problem was built to expose.}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{9-6i}{3i}$.}
\par
The conjugate of $3i$ is $-3i$:
\[ \frac{(9-6i)(-3i)}{(3i)(-3i)} = \frac{-27i + 18i^2}{-9i^2}
   = \frac{-18 - 27i}{9} \]
Divide both parts by $9$. Both parts come out negative, so keep both signs.
\par
$\ans{-2-3i}$}

\problem{\textbf{Simplify $\dfrac{25}{3+4i}$.}
\par
First the requested statement: $(3+4i)(3-4i) = 9 + 16 = 25$. The denominator
times its conjugate is $25$, the same as the numerator.
\[ \frac{25(3-4i)}{(3+4i)(3-4i)} = \frac{25(3-4i)}{25} = 3 - 4i \]
\par
$\ans{3-4i}$
\par\smallskip
When the numerator is real, dividing by $a + bi$ just multiplies by the
conjugate and scales by $a^2 + b^2$ --- here the scaling was exactly $1$.}

\problem{\textbf{Current through the speaker.}
\par
From $V = I \cdot Z$, dividing both sides by $Z$ gives $I = V/Z$, with
$V = 1 + 8i$ volts and $Z = 2 + i$ ohms.
\[ I = \frac{1 + 8i}{2 + i} = \frac{(1+8i)(2-i)}{(2+i)(2-i)}
   = \frac{2 - i + 16i - 8i^2}{4 + 1} = \frac{2 + 15i + 8}{5}
   = \frac{10 + 15i}{5} \]
\par
$\ans{2+3i\ \text{amps}}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Check:} $(2+3i)(2+i) = 4 + 2i + 6i - 3 = 1 + 8i$ volts $\checkmark$}

\problem{\textbf{Synthesis: two divisions in disguise.}
\par
\textbf{(a)} Isolating $x$ means dividing by $3 + 2i$, so multiply by the
conjugate $3 - 2i$:
\[ x = \frac{4 + 7i}{3 + 2i} = \frac{(4+7i)(3-2i)}{9 + 4}
   = \frac{12 - 8i + 21i + 14}{13} = \frac{26 + 13i}{13} \]
\par
$\ans{x = 2+i}$
\par
\textbf{(b)} A zero is where $f(x) = 0$, so $(1-i)x = 6 + 2i$ and again the
unknown is freed by a division --- this time by $1 - i$, whose conjugate is
$1 + i$:
\[ x = \frac{6 + 2i}{1 - i} = \frac{(6+2i)(1+i)}{1 + 1}
   = \frac{6 + 6i + 2i - 2}{2} = \frac{4 + 8i}{2} \]
\par
$\ans{x = 2+4i}$
\par\smallskip
The conjugate used in (b) is $1 + i$, because the denominator was $1 - i$ and
the conjugate is what makes the denominator real: $(1-i)(1+i) = 1 + 1 = 2$.}
