# Compositions of Transformations and Symmetry — 10 problems

Three PDFs are ready:

## What the worksheet covers

A fluency ramp with no two problems sharing a skeleton, moving from one-step
compositions to naming the single motion a pair is equivalent to:

1. Two translations combined into one.
2. The smallest rotation that maps a regular decagon onto itself.
3. Two perpendicular reflections — find the image, then name the equivalent motion.
4. Two reflections in parallel lines, giving a translation of *twice* the gap.
5. A translation followed by a half turn.
6. Working backwards from a $24^\circ$ rotation to the number of sides.
7. Two reflections in intersecting lines, giving *twice* the angle.
8. A quarter turn then a reflection — again, name the single equivalent motion.
9. A hexagon's rotational symmetry, including why six turns close the loop.
10. The challenge: a reflection and a translation on a segment, with the length
    checked before and after and an argument for why it had to be preserved.

## What is verified, and what is not

The sheet asks for **21 separate responses** across the 10 problems.

- **17 are machine-checked** with SymPy — every combined component, every rotation
  angle, every image coordinate (an $x$ and a $y$ count as two answers), the
  number of sides, and both segment lengths in problem 10.
- **4 are instructor-judged**: 3(b), 8(b), 9(c) and 10(d) — naming the single
  equivalent motion in two places, and the two written arguments. Naming a
  transformation is prose, so it is flagged rather than dressed up as verified.
  Each is marked `---` in the Quick Answers bank, and the key gives a model
  response and a full / half / no-credit rubric. The key's own "What is verified"
  note says 17 of 21.

There are no `[unchecked]` marks.

## The study guide

Four sections: combining translations, symmetry of regular polygons, two
reflections in a row (the doubling rule for both parallel and intersecting
mirrors), and a pattern-matching section for naming the single equivalent motion
from what happened to the coordinates. Each has a worked example whose first step
is the reasoning move, and a try-it with the answer upside down inside the box.
