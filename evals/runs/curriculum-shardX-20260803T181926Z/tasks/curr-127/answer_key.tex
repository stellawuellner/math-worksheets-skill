\problem{Rewrite $\dfrac{1}{2}$ as an equivalent fraction with a denominator of $8$. What is the missing numerator?}

\textbf{Solution:} $8 \div 2 = 4$, so the denominator was multiplied by $4$. Multiply the numerator by the same factor: $1 \times 4$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{n = 4}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Fill in the missing numerator: \quad $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{?}{12}$}

\textbf{Solution:} $12 \div 4 = 3$, so multiply the numerator by $3$ as well: $3 \times 3$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{n = 9}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Rewrite $\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $\dfrac{3}{8}$ as equivalent fractions with the common denominator $24$.}

\textbf{Solution:} $24 \div 6 = 4$, so multiply the top and bottom of $\dfrac{5}{6}$ by $4$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{20}{24}}

Then $24 \div 8 = 3$, so multiply the top and bottom of $\dfrac{3}{8}$ by $3$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{9}{24}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Add. \quad $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4}$}

\textbf{Solution:} $4$ is already a multiple of $2$, so the common denominator is $4$: \ $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2}{4}$. Then $\dfrac{2}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4}$ adds the numerators over the common denominator.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{3}{4}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Add. \quad $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} $6$ is a multiple of $3$, so use $6$: \ $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{4}{6}$. Then $\dfrac{4}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$, which is already in lowest terms.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{5}{6}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Subtract. \quad $\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{1}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The least common denominator of $4$ and $6$ is $12$: \ $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{2}{12}$. Subtract the numerators: $9 - 2 = 7$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{7}{12}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Find the least common denominator of $\dfrac{3}{8}$ and $\dfrac{5}{12}$, then rewrite each fraction with it.}

\textbf{Solution:} Multiples of $8$: $8, 16, 24, \ldots$ \ Multiples of $12$: $12, 24, \ldots$ \ The least common denominator is $24$. Since $24 \div 8 = 3$, multiply $\dfrac{3}{8}$ top and bottom by $3$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{9}{24}}

Since $24 \div 12 = 2$, multiply $\dfrac{5}{12}$ top and bottom by $2$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{10}{24}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Add. \quad $\dfrac{5}{8} + \dfrac{1}{3}$}

\textbf{Solution:} $8$ and $3$ share no factor, so the least common denominator is $8 \times 3 = 24$: \ $\dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{15}{24}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{8}{24}$. Add the numerators: $15 + 8 = 23$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{23}{24}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Subtract. \quad $\dfrac{5}{6} - \dfrac{3}{10}$}

\textbf{Solution:} The least common denominator of $6$ and $10$ is $30$: \ $\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{25}{30}$ and $\dfrac{3}{10} = \dfrac{9}{30}$. Subtract: $25 - 9 = 16$, giving $\dfrac{16}{30}$. Both parts divide by $2$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{\tfrac{8}{15}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Add the mixed numbers. \quad $2\,\dfrac{1}{2} + 1\,\dfrac{1}{3}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Add the whole numbers: $2 + 1 = 3$. For the fractions use the common denominator $6$: \ $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{6}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{6}$, so the fraction part is $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{3\,\tfrac{5}{6}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Subtract the mixed numbers. \quad $4\,\dfrac{3}{4} - 1\,\dfrac{5}{6}$}

\textbf{Solution:} Common denominator $12$: \ $4\,\dfrac{3}{4} = 4\,\dfrac{9}{12}$ and $1\,\dfrac{5}{6} = 1\,\dfrac{10}{12}$. Since $\dfrac{9}{12}$ is smaller than $\dfrac{10}{12}$, regroup one whole: $4\,\dfrac{9}{12} = 3\,\dfrac{21}{12}$. Now subtract whole from whole and fraction from fraction: $3 - 1 = 2$ and $\dfrac{21}{12} - \dfrac{10}{12} = \dfrac{11}{12}$.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{2\,\tfrac{11}{12}}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{A muffin recipe uses $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a cup of flour and $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a cup of oats. How many cups of dry ingredients in all?}

\textbf{Solution:} Total means add: $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{3}{4}$. The least common denominator of $3$ and $4$ is $12$: \ $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$. Adding gives $\dfrac{17}{12}$, which is more than one whole, so rename it as a mixed number: $\dfrac{17}{12} = 1\,\dfrac{5}{12}$ cups.

$\Rightarrow$ \ans{1\,\tfrac{5}{12}}
