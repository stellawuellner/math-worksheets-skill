\wstitleblock{Transformations of Absolute-Value Graphs}{Algebra 1}{Shifting the V, reflecting and stretching it, and reading the vertex off the equation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item The parent graph $y = |x|$ is a V with its corner at the origin and arms of slope $1$ and $-1$.
  \item In $y = a|x-h| + k$ the corner sits at $(h, k)$. As always, $x - h$ moves the graph \textbf{right} by $h$ --- the bracket reads the opposite way round.
  \item $a$ controls the arms: negative flips the V upside down, $|a| > 1$ makes it steeper, $|a| < 1$ makes it wider.
  \item The $x$-intercepts come from setting $y = 0$ and remembering that an absolute value equation splits into \textbf{two} cases; the $y$-intercept comes from putting $x = 0$.
  \item Where a grid is provided, plot the corner first, then the intercepts, then draw two straight arms.
\end{itemize}
