# Find and Fix: Does the Data Really Show a Cause? — Grade 8 / Pre-Algebra

Three PDFs are ready:

- **Worksheet** (`ws_cause_curr249.pdf`, 5 pages) — 8 problems, each with its own data
  table, all aimed at the same confusion: two quantities moving together is an
  *association*, and calling it a *cause* is a much bigger claim. Three are full
  find-and-fix items where a named student's wrong work is printed (Ola on ice cream and
  beach rescues, Jack on a percentage denominator, Rita on firefighters and fire damage).
  The sheet covers the three standard escape routes — a third factor moving both
  quantities, the causation running backwards, and the difference between a survey and a
  randomised experiment.
- **Answer key** (`ak_cause_curr249.pdf`, 3 pages) — Quick Answers, a "What is verified"
  note, a **Common wrong answers** block, a curriculum block, and a worked solution per
  problem.
- **Study guide** (`ss_cause_curr249.pdf`, 2 pages) — three sections: computing the
  statistic the question actually asked for, why association is not a cause, and what
  evidence would settle it. Each has a rule box, a worked example and a try-it.

## Machine-checked misconception traps

Every wrong number printed on the sheet is declared in the verification data, recomputed,
and checked to be *distinguishably* wrong:

| Problem | Planted wrong answer | The error |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 48 | read across to the ice-cream row instead of the rescues row |
| 4 | 22.5 | Jack divided by all 200 students instead of the 60 instrument players |
| 7 | 24 | subtracted the firefighter counts instead of the damage figures |

## What is verified

**10 of the 17 answers are machine-checked** by SymPy. Only problem 1 is fully
machine-checked; the other seven problems are **partly instructor-judged**, because each
pairs a computed value with a written diagnosis:

| Problem | Machine-checked | Instructor-judged |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | the greatest number of rescues, 7 | what is wrong with Ola's conclusion |
| 3 | the difference, 5 | a third factor that moves both rows |
| 4 | the corrected percentage, 75 | the description of Jack's error |
| 5 | the mean, 84 | whether the headline is justified |
| 6 | the mean, 72 | what evidence would be needed |
| 7 | the difference, 170 | what is wrong with Rita's reasoning |
| 8 | means 85 and 77, difference 8 | which study can support a causal claim |

That matches the key's own "What is verified" note. The reasoning half of this topic
simply is not machine-checkable — a correct answer is an argument, not a number — so each
of those seven carries a written rubric in the key saying what a correct response must
contain, including which alternative arguments to accept.

## Notes for you

- Problem 7 is the reversed-direction case: more firefighters do not cause damage, bigger
  towns cause both. Either that argument or the town-size argument earns full credit.
- Problem 8 is the payoff. Two studies with similar-looking numbers, and only the one
  with random assignment can support a causal claim. If your student picks the survey,
  that is the conversation to have.
- Standards covered: 8.SP.A.1 (problems 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8) and 8.SP.A.4 (problem 4).
- The grade level appears on the answer key only.
