\problem{\textbf{Rice $250$ g and beans $300$ g on one scale --- total mass.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both bags sit on the same scale, so the two masses are being
\emph{put together}. Putting together is addition.

\textbf{Step 2.} $250 + 300$: add the hundreds, $200 + 300 = 500$, then the
$50$ that is left over.

\textbf{Step 3.} The unit stays grams, because grams were added to grams.
\ans{550\ \text{g}}}

\problem{\textbf{Pitcher of $800$ mL, $350$ mL poured out --- how much is left.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Water leaves the pitcher, so this is \emph{taking away}:
subtraction.

\textbf{Step 2.} $800 - 350$. Count back in friendly steps: $800 - 300 = 500$,
then $500 - 50 = 450$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The answer is a liquid volume, so it is written in millilitres.
\ans{450\ \text{mL}}}

\problem{\textbf{Measurement record: $350$ mL in the jug, $150$ mL poured in.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Read the two amounts out of the record table first --- that is
what the table is for. Juice is being added to juice, so add.

\textbf{Step 2.} $350 + 150$. Make a friendly number: $350 + 50 = 400$, and
there are $100$ left to add, so $400 + 100 = 500$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Both measurements were in millilitres, so the total is too.
\ans{500\ \text{mL}}}

\problem{\textbf{Four apples, each $150$ g --- total mass.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Four \emph{equal} groups of $150$ g is a multiplication, not
an addition of two different numbers.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4 \times 150 = 4 \times 100 + 4 \times 50 = 400 + 200$.

\textbf{Step 3.} A check by repeated addition gives the same total:
$150 + 150 + 150 + 150$.
\ans{600\ \text{g}}}

\problem{\textbf{Milk $450$ mL and $275$ mL emptied into one bowl.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two amounts are combined in one bowl, so add.

\textbf{Step 2.} $450 + 275$: $450 + 250 = 700$, and $25$ more is $725$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Millilitres added to millilitres stay millilitres.
\ans{725\ \text{mL}}}

\problem{\textbf{Mass table: how much heavier is the melon than the pineapple.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``How much heavier'' is a comparison of two masses, so
subtract the smaller from the larger. Read only the two rows the question names
--- the mango row is not part of this question.

\textbf{Step 2.} melon $900$ g, pineapple $650$ g, so $900 - 650$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $900 - 650 = 250$, and the difference of two masses is a mass.
\ans{250\ \text{g}}}

\problem{\textbf{Box of books $850$ g, one $275$ g book removed.}

\textbf{Step 1.} A book leaves the box, so the box gets lighter: subtract.

\textbf{Step 2.} $850 - 275$. Take away $200$ first: $850 - 200 = 650$. Then
take away $75$: $650 - 75 = 575$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Check by adding back: $575 + 275 = 850$ $\checkmark$
\ans{575\ \text{g}}}

\problem{\textbf{$600$ mL of soup shared equally into $4$ bowls.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``Shared equally'' means one amount is split into equal parts,
so divide.

\textbf{Step 2.} $600 \div 4$. Think $4 \times 15 = 60$, so $4 \times 150 = 600$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Each bowl gets the same liquid volume, in millilitres.
\ans{150\ \text{mL}}}

\problem{\textbf{Error analysis: $750$ mL in the bottle, $300$ mL poured out.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Noah's answer of $1050$ mL is larger than the $750$ mL he
started with, and pouring water out cannot leave more than there was. His
mistake was adding the poured-out amount instead of taking it away
($750 + 300 = 1050$).

\textbf{Step 2.} The correct one step is subtraction: $750 - 300$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $750 - 300 = 450$, which is less than $750$, as it must be.
\ans{450\ \text{mL}}

\textit{Manual review:} the written sentence is an open response. Accept any
wording that says he added instead of subtracting, or that the answer must be
smaller than the starting amount.}

\problem{\textbf{$950$ mL of soup poured equally into $5$ cups.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Equal shares of one amount, so divide: $950 \div 5$.

\textbf{Step 2.} Split $950$ into $500 + 450$. Then $500 \div 5 = 100$ and
$450 \div 5 = 90$.

\textbf{Step 3.} $100 + 90 = 190$, and a check gives $5 \times 190 = 950$
$\checkmark$
\ans{190\ \text{mL}}}
