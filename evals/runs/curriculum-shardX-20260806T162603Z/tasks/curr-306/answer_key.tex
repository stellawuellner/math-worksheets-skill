\problem{$j \parallel k$, eight numbered angles. (a) Name $\angle 3$/$\angle 6$ and $\angle 4$/$\angle 6$. (b) $m\angle 4 = 118^\circ$, find $m\angle 6$.}

\textbf{(a)} $\angle 3$ and $\angle 6$ are \textbf{alternate interior angles}: both lie between $j$ and $k$, and they sit on opposite sides of $t$. With $j \parallel k$ they are \emph{congruent}.

$\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$ are \textbf{same-side interior angles} (also called co-interior): both lie between $j$ and $k$, and both sit on the same side of $t$. With $j \parallel k$ they are \emph{supplementary}.

\textbf{(b)} Same-side interior angles are supplementary, so $m\angle 6 = 180 - 118$. \ans{62^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Corresponding angles $(3x + 20)^\circ$ and $(5x - 10)^\circ$; $j \parallel k$. Find $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} corresponding angles on parallel lines are congruent, so the two measures are equal.
\begin{align*}
  3x + 20 &= 5x - 10 \\
  30 &= 2x
\end{align*}
So $x = 15$. Check: both angles measure $3(15) + 20 = 65$ and $5(15) - 10 = 65$. \ans{x = 15}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$j \parallel k$, $m\angle 1 = 105^\circ$. Find $m\angle 6$.}

\textbf{Solution, two links:} $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$ are corresponding angles (both above their line, both left of $t$), so $m\angle 5 = 105$. Then $\angle 5$ and $\angle 6$ are a linear pair on line $k$, so their measures add to $180$: $m\angle 6 = 180 - 105$. \ans{75^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Same-side interior angles $(2x + 30)^\circ$ and $(4x)^\circ$; $j \parallel k$. Find $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} same-side interior angles on parallel lines are supplementary --- add, do not equate.
\begin{align*}
  (2x + 30) + 4x &= 180 \\
  6x + 30 &= 180 \\
  6x &= 150
\end{align*}
So $x = 25$. Check: $2(25) + 30 = 80$ and $4(25) = 100$, and $80 + 100 = 180$. \ans{x = 25}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{No parallel marks. (a) Which relationships survive? (b) $m\angle 2 = 74^\circ$, find $m\angle 4$.}

\textbf{(a)} The relationships that live at a \emph{single} vertex survive, because they follow from two lines crossing and nothing more: \textbf{vertical angles are still congruent} ($\angle 1 \cong \angle 4$, $\angle 2 \cong \angle 3$, and likewise at the lower vertex) and \textbf{a linear pair is still supplementary}.

The relationships that connect the \emph{two} vertices all require $j \parallel k$: corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, and same-side interior. Here they carry no guarantee --- the two lines meet somewhere off the page, and the angles differ by exactly that much.

\textbf{(b)} $\angle 2$ and $\angle 4$ share the ray of $j$ to the right of $t$, and their other sides are the two opposite rays of $t$, so they are a linear pair --- which needs no parallel assumption. $m\angle 4 = 180 - 74$. \ans{106^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Alternate interior: $m\angle 3 = (4x + 10)^\circ$, $m\angle 6 = (6x - 20)^\circ$. (a) $x$. (b) $m\angle 3$.}

\textbf{(a)} Alternate interior angles on parallel lines are congruent:
\begin{align*}
  4x + 10 &= 6x - 20 \\
  30 &= 2x
\end{align*}
So $x = 15$. \ans{x = 15}

\textbf{(b)} $m\angle 3 = 4(15) + 10 = 60 + 10$. Check: $m\angle 6 = 6(15) - 20 = 70$ too. \ans{70^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Alternate exterior: $m\angle 1 = (5x - 5)^\circ$, $m\angle 8 = (3x + 31)^\circ$. (a) $x$. (b) $m\angle 1$.}

\textbf{(a)} Alternate exterior angles on parallel lines are congruent --- both lie outside the two lines, on opposite sides of $t$:
\begin{align*}
  5x - 5 &= 3x + 31 \\
  2x &= 36
\end{align*}
So $x = 18$. \ans{x = 18}

\textbf{(b)} $m\angle 1 = 5(18) - 5 = 90 - 5$. Check: $m\angle 8 = 3(18) + 31 = 85$ too. \ans{85^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$m\angle 3 = (4x + 6)^\circ$, $m\angle 5 = (6x + 4)^\circ$, $j \parallel k$. (a) Name the pair. (b) $x$. (c) $m\angle 5$.}

\textbf{(a)} $\angle 3$ and $\angle 5$ are \textbf{same-side interior angles}: both lie between $j$ and $k$, and both are on the left of the transversal. That name means \emph{supplementary}, not congruent, so the two expressions have to be added and the sum set equal to $180$. Setting them equal would be the alternate-interior rule applied to a same-side pair --- the single most common error on this topic.

\textbf{(b)}
\begin{align*}
  (4x + 6) + (6x + 4) &= 180 \\
  10x + 10 &= 180 \\
  10x &= 170
\end{align*}
So $x = 17$. \ans{x = 17}

\textbf{(c)} $m\angle 5 = 6(17) + 4 = 102 + 4$. Check: $m\angle 3 = 4(17) + 6 = 74$, and $74 + 106 = 180$. \ans{106^\circ}
