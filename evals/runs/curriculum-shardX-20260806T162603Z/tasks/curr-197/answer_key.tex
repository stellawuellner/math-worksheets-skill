\problem{Mean of $4, 7, 5, 9, 5$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The five values total $4 + 7 + 5 + 9 + 5 = 30$, and
$30 \div 5 = 6$. \ans{6}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Range of $11, 17, 9, 20, 14$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The largest value is 20 and the smallest is 9, so the range
is $20 - 9 = 11$. \ans{11}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Median of $2, 9, 4, 11, 6, 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} In order: $2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11$. There are six values, so the
median is the mean of the third and fourth: $(6 + 8) \div 2 = 7$. \ans{7}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Mode of $3, 5, 3, 7, 3, 5, 8$.}

\textbf{Solution:} The value 3 appears three times, 5 appears twice, and the
others once each, so the mode is 3. \ans{3}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Quartiles of $2, 4, 8, 10, 14, 16, 20, 22$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Eight values split into a lower four and an upper four.
Lower half $2, 4, 8, 10$: median $(4 + 8) \div 2 = 6$. \ans{6}

(b) Upper half $14, 16, 20, 22$: median $(16 + 20) \div 2 = 18$. \ans{18}

(c) Interquartile range $= 18 - 6 = 12$. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Bicycle prices $12, 14, 13, 15, 46$ dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The prices total 100 dollars, and $100 \div 5 = 20$
dollars. \ans{20}

(b) In order: $12, 13, 14, 15, 46$. With five values the median is the third,
14 dollars. \ans{14}

(c) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The median. Four of the five bicycles are priced
between 12 and 15 dollars, and the single 46 dollar bicycle drags the mean up to
20 --- a price higher than four of the five actually on sale. Full credit needs
the choice of the median plus the effect of the one unusually large price on the
mean.

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Range and interquartile range of $1, 3, 5, \ldots, 23$.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) $23 - 1 = 22$. \ans{22}

(b) Twelve values split into a lower six and an upper six:
\[
  \underbrace{1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9,\ 11}_{\text{lower quartile } 6}
  \qquad
  \underbrace{13,\ 15,\ 17,\ 19,\ 21,\ 23}_{\text{upper quartile } 18}
\]
The interquartile range is $18 - 6 = 12$, far smaller than the range because it
ignores both ends. \ans{12}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Five numbers have mean 12; four of them are $8, 10, 15, 11$.}

\textbf{Solution:} A mean of 12 over five numbers means the total is
$12 \times 5 = 60$. The four known numbers total $8 + 10 + 15 + 11 = 44$, so the
fifth is $60 - 44 = 16$. Written as an equation,
$\dfrac{8 + 10 + 15 + 11 + x}{5} = 12$. \ans{x = 16}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Two bus routes, both with median 14 minutes.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) Route A in order is $10, 12, 14, 16, 18$. Leaving out the
middle value, the lower half is $10, 12$ and the upper half is $16, 18$, so the
quartiles are 11 and 17 and the interquartile range is $17 - 11 = 6$
minutes. \ans{6}

(b) Route B is $2, 8, 14, 20, 26$. The quartiles are $(2 + 8) \div 2 = 5$ and
$(20 + 26) \div 2 = 23$, so the interquartile range is $23 - 5 = 18$
minutes. \ans{18}

(c) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} Route A is more predictable. Both routes have
the same median, so the centre cannot separate them; the spread can. The middle
half of Route A's times covers only 6 minutes while Route B's covers 18. Full
credit needs the comparison of the two interquartile ranges, not just the
observation that the medians match.

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Delivery masses $12, 16, 20, 24, 28$ kilograms.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The masses total 100 kilograms, and $100 \div 5 = 20$
kilograms. \ans{20}

(b) The distances from the mean are $8, 4, 0, 4, 8$ kilograms. Their total is
24, and $24 \div 5 = 4.8$ kilograms. That is the typical distance a delivery
sits from the mean. \ans{4.8}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Groups of 4 at mean 80 and 6 at mean 90; find the combined mean.}

\textbf{Solution:} Means cannot simply be averaged when the groups differ in
size. Recover the totals: $4 \times 80 = 320$ and $6 \times 90 = 540$, so all
ten students total $860$ marks and the combined mean is
$860 \div 10 = 86$. Averaging 80 and 90 to get 85 would over-weight the smaller
group. \ans{86}

\vspace{0.22cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}\vspace{0.22cm}

\problem{Nine donations $2, 4, 4, 6, 6, 6, 8, 10, 44$ dollars.}

\textbf{Solution:} (a) The donations total 90 dollars, and $90 \div 9 = 10$
dollars. \ans{10}

(b) The values are already in order and there are nine, so the median is the
fifth: 6 dollars. \ans{6}

(c) Range $= 44 - 2 = 42$ dollars. \ans{42}

(d) Leaving out the median, the lower half is $2, 4, 4, 6$ with median 4, and
the upper half is $6, 8, 10, 44$ with median $(8 + 10) \div 2 = 9$. The
interquartile range is $9 - 4 = 5$ dollars. \ans{5}

(e) \textbf{Instructor-judged.} The median with the interquartile range. The
single 44 dollar donation lifts the mean to 10 dollars --- more than seven of
the nine donations --- and stretches the range to 42, while the median stays at
6 and the interquartile range at 5, which is what the bulk of the donations
actually look like. Full credit needs the chosen pair together with an
explanation naming the effect of the one large donation on both the mean and the
range.
