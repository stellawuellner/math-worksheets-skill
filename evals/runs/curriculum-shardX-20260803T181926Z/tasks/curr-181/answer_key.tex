\problem{\textbf{Ride height: at least $48$ inches.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Name the variable in words: $h$ is a rider's height in inches.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``At least'' means the boundary counts --- a rider who is
exactly $48$ inches tall may ride --- so the symbol is $\ge$, not $>$.

\ans{h \ge 48}

\textbf{Step 3.} On the number line: a \emph{closed} circle at $48$ with the
shading running to the right.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $h > 48$. That would turn away a rider who is
exactly at the line, which is not what the sign says.}

\problem{\textbf{Van capacity: no more than $14$ passengers.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $p$ is the number of passengers on a trip.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``No more than'' is the same as ``at most'': $14$ passengers
is allowed, $15$ is not, so the symbol is $\le$.

\ans{p \le 14}

\textbf{Step 3.} Closed circle at $14$, shading to the left.

\textit{Teaching note:} in context $p$ also cannot be negative, so a fussy
answer is $0 \le p \le 14$. Accept $p \le 14$ --- the sheet asks for the
constraint the sign states.}

\problem{\textbf{Maya's fair budget.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $s$ is the number of snacks. Entry is a one-time $\$16$; each
snack adds $\$4$, so the total spent is $16 + 4s$ dollars.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``Does not overspend'' means the total is at most her $\$60$:

$16 + 4s \le 60$

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract $16$ from both sides: $4s \le 44$. Divide by $4$
(a positive number, so the symbol does not flip).

\ans{s \le 11}

\textbf{Step 4.} Check the boundary: $16 + 4(11) = 60$ exactly $\checkmark$
Closed circle at $11$, shading left.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $4s + 16 \le 60$ solved as $s \le 15$ --- the
$\$16$ was subtracted from $4s$ instead of from $60$.}

\problem{\textbf{Gym membership within $\$150$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $m$ is the number of months. The joining fee is paid once, so
the total cost is $30 + 12m$ dollars --- the $30$ is not multiplied by $m$.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``At most $\$150$'' gives \ $30 + 12m \le 150$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract $30$: $12m \le 120$; divide by $12$.

\ans{m \le 10}

\textbf{Step 4.} Check: $30 + 12(10) = 150$ $\checkmark$ Closed circle at $10$,
shading left.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} writing $30m + 12 \le 150$ --- the one-time fee
and the repeating charge were swapped. Ask which number happens again every
month.}

\problem{\textbf{Fundraiser: at least $\$500$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $w$ is the number of wreaths sold. Money raised is
$180 + 16w$ dollars.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``At least $\$500$'' points the other way from the last two
problems --- they need to reach or pass the goal:

$180 + 16w \ge 500$

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract $180$: $16w \ge 320$; divide by $16$.

\ans{w \ge 20}

\textbf{Step 4.} Check the boundary: $180 + 16(20) = 500$ $\checkmark$ Closed
circle at $20$, shading to the \emph{right}.

\textit{Listen for:} shading the wrong way. Test one value: $25$ wreaths gives
$\$580$, which does reach the goal, so the shading covers $25$.}

\problem{\textbf{Elevator load limit.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $b$ is the number of boxes. Total weight is $165 + 45b$
pounds.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``No more than $2100$ pounds'' gives \ $165 + 45b \le 2100$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract $165$: $45b \le 1935$; divide by $45$.

\ans{b \le 43}

\textbf{Step 4.} Read it back. Boxes come in whole numbers, so the operator may
load any whole number of boxes up to and including $43$. Check:
$165 + 45(43) = 2100$ exactly, so $43$ boxes is right at the limit and is
allowed.

\textit{Teaching note:} this is the step students skip --- the algebra ends at
$b \le 43$, but the \emph{answer to the question asked} is ``at most $43$
boxes''.}

\problem{\textbf{Drone above $90$ metres.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $t$ is seconds since the descent began. Height after $t$
seconds is $240 - 15t$ metres --- descending, so the term is subtracted.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``Above $90$ metres'' is strict, so \ $240 - 15t > 90$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Subtract $240$ from both sides: $-15t > -150$.

\textbf{Step 4.} Divide both sides by $-15$. Dividing by a negative number
\emph{reverses} the symbol:

\ans{t < 10}

\textbf{Step 5.} Check with a value: at $t = 4$ the height is
$240 - 60 = 180$ metres, which is above $90$ $\checkmark$ At $t = 12$ it is
$60$ metres, which is not. Open circle at $10$ (at exactly $10$ seconds the
drone is \emph{at} $90$ metres, not above it), shading to the left.

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $t > 10$ --- the flip was forgotten. The check
value catches it instantly, which is why it is worth doing every time.}

\problem{\textbf{What Jonah got wrong.}

\textbf{Step 1.} ``No more than $12$'' means $12$ is the largest value still
allowed. Jonah's symbol, $<$, excludes $12$, so his inequality throws away a
value the sentence permits.

\textbf{Step 2.} The phrase calls for the symbol that includes the boundary,
$\le$:

\ans{n \le 12}

\textbf{Step 3.} On the number line the difference is visible: Jonah's version
has an \emph{open} circle at $12$; the correct version has a \emph{closed} one,
with the shading running left in both.

\textit{Full credit on the sentence:} it has to say that $12$ itself is allowed
(or that ``no more than'' includes the boundary). ``He used the wrong sign'' has
spotted the error without explaining it.}

\problem{\textbf{Rectangle with perimeter at most $46$ cm.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $l$ is the length in centimetres. Perimeter is two widths plus
two lengths: $2(8) + 2l$, which is $16 + 2l$ centimetres.

\textbf{Step 2.} ``At most $46$'' gives \ $2(8) + 2l \le 46$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Simplify and subtract $16$: $2l \le 30$; divide by $2$.

\ans{l \le 15}

\textbf{Step 4.} Check the boundary: a $8$ by $15$ rectangle has perimeter
$16 + 30 = 46$ $\checkmark$ Closed circle at $15$, shading left.

\textit{Teaching note:} a length also has to be positive, so the full set of
usable lengths is $0 < l \le 15$. Worth raising once the inequality is written
--- the context, not the algebra, supplies that second constraint.}

\problem{\textbf{Writing a story for $25 + 8n \ge 65$.} \emph{Open response ---
many correct answers, flagged for manual review. A model answer follows.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Read the structure first. There is a one-time $25$, a repeated
$8$ attached to $n$, a target of $65$, and ``at least'' pointing at $\ge$.

\textbf{Step 2.} A story that fits: Priya already has $\$25$ saved and adds
$\$8$ to her jar each week. She needs at least $\$65$ for a bike helmet. Here
$n$ counts the weeks she keeps saving, $25$ is what she starts with, $8$ is
what she adds per week, and $65$ is the goal.

\textbf{Step 3.} Solve it: $8n \ge 40$, so $n \ge 5$.

\textbf{Step 4.} Read it back into the story: after $5$ weeks she has exactly
$25 + 40 = \$65$, so $5$ weeks is enough, and every week after that is too.
The graph is a closed circle at $5$ shading right.

\textit{Full credit:} the one-time amount and the repeating amount are matched
to the right places, $n$ is named as a count, and the interpretation says
``$5$ or more'' rather than just restating the symbols. Any story with that
shape earns the marks.}
