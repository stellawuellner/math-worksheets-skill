\problem{Jon has 6 balloons. 2 balloons pop. How many balloons does Jon have
left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Popping is taking away: start with 6, take away 2. Count
back from 6: 5, 4. So $6-2=4$. \ans{4\ \text{balloons}}

\problem{There are 8 cookies on a plate. Max eats 3 cookies. Draw the
cookies and cross out the eaten ones if it helps. How many cookies are
left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Draw 8 cookies and cross out 3 for the ones Max ate.
Counting the plain cookies: 5. So $8-3=5$. \ans{5\ \text{cookies}}

\problem{9 kites are flying. 4 kites come down to land. Write the story
numbers in the equation, then write how many kites are still flying.}

\textbf{Solution:} Landing takes kites out of the sky: the start number is 9
and the taken-away number is 4, so the equation is $9 - 4 = {}$? Count back
from 9: 8, 7, 6, 5. So $9-4=5$. \ans{5}

\problem{Lena has 7 stickers. Ben has 5 stickers. How many more stickers
does Lena have than Ben?}

\textbf{Solution:} Compare by matching: pair each of Ben's 5 stickers with
one of Lena's. Lena's extras are $7-5=2$. \ans{2\ \text{stickers}}

\problem{Row A shows 6 counters. Row B shows 4 counters. Draw a line from
each counter in Row A to a partner in Row B. How many counters in Row A have
no partner?}

\textbf{Solution:} Each of Row B's 4 counters pairs with one counter in
Row A, so 4 counters get partners and the rest do not: $6-4=2$.
\ans{2\ \text{counters}}

\emph{Matching lines (instructor judges):} 4 pairing lines drawn, leaving
exactly 2 counters in Row A unmatched.

\problem{Zoe has 10 grapes. She eats 4 grapes, and then she eats 2 more. How
many grapes does Zoe have left?}

\textbf{Solution:} Take away twice: $10-4=6$ after the first snack, then
$6-2=4$ after the second. So $10-4-2=4$. \ans{4\ \text{grapes}}

\problem{Sam has 9 marbles. Tia has 6 marbles. How many fewer marbles does
Tia have than Sam?}

\textbf{Solution:} Match Tia's 6 marbles against Sam's 9. Sam has $9-6=3$
with no partner, so Tia has 3 fewer. \ans{3\ \text{marbles}}

\problem{Challenge! Ava picks 10 flowers. She gives 3 flowers to her mom.
Kim has 5 flowers. How many flowers does Ava have left? Who has more flowers
now? How many more?}

\textbf{Solution:} First the take-away step: Ava keeps $10-3=7$ flowers.
\ans{7}

Now compare: Ava's 7 against Kim's 5. Matching them up leaves $7-5=2$ extras
for Ava, so Ava has more --- by \ans{2}

\emph{Who has more (instructor judges):} the student writes ``Ava'', matching
the counts 7 and 5.
