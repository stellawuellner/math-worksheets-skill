\begin{center}
{\small\textit{Reading powers of the imaginary unit and points on the complex plane}}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\noindent\textbf{Two models to work from}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.7cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{The cycle.} Each new power multiplies the one before it by $i$,
    and after four steps you are back where you started. Because of that, a power
    of $i$ depends only on the remainder when the exponent is divided by $4$.
  \item \textbf{The plane.} A complex number $a + bi$ is the point $(a, b)$:
    $a$ along the horizontal \emph{real} axis, $b$ up the vertical
    \emph{imaginary} axis. Multiplying by $i$ moves a point a quarter turn
    counterclockwise around the origin, and its distance from the origin never
    changes.
  \item Show your reduction step (for example $i^{27} = i^{24} \cdot i^{3}$),
    not just the final value.
\end{itemize}
