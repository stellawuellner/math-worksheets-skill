\problem{\textbf{Mural: height $6$ ft, width $(2x+5)$ ft. Write the area with no
parentheses.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Area of a rectangle is height times width, so the area is
$6(2x + 5)$ square feet. The parentheses say ``the whole width'', so the $6$
multiplies \emph{both} pieces of that width.

\textbf{Step 2.} Distribute: $6 \cdot 2x = 12x$ and $6 \cdot 5 = 30$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The area is a sum of two rectangles, $12x$ and $30$ square
feet: \ans{12x + 30\ \text{ft}^2}

\textit{Check by area model:} a $6$ by $2x$ strip beside a $6$ by $5$ strip has
the same total area as one $6$ by $(2x+5)$ rectangle.}

\problem{\textbf{$9$ members, each billed $(4x + 15)$ dollars. Total billing?}

\textbf{Step 1.} Every member is billed the same expression, so the total is
$9(4x + 15)$ dollars --- a multiplier outside a sum, which is exactly the
distributive setup.

\textbf{Step 2.} Distribute across both terms: $9 \cdot 4x = 36x$ and
$9 \cdot 15 = 135$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Total billing in dollars: \ans{36x + 135}

\textit{Reading the answer:} $36x$ is the class charge for all nine members and
$135$ is the fixed part of the bill.}

\problem{\textbf{$6$ boards, each $(2x - 9)$ inches long. Total length?}

\textbf{Step 1.} The length repeated six times is $6(2x - 9)$ inches. The minus
sign belongs to the $9$, so the second product will be subtracted.

\textbf{Step 2.} $6 \cdot 2x = 12x$, and $6 \cdot (-9) = -54$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Total length: \ans{12x - 54\ \text{in}}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $12x - 9$ --- the $6$ was multiplied into the
first term only. Distribution reaches every term inside the parentheses.}

\problem{\textbf{Panel area $(18x + 24)$ cm$^2$, height $6$ cm. Write $6(\ \ )$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Factoring is distribution run backwards: we need the number
that multiplies into both $18x$ and $24$. The height $6$ is given, so test $6$
as the common factor.

\textbf{Step 2.} $18x \div 6 = 3x$ and $24 \div 6 = 4$, so the width is
$3x + 4$ centimetres.

\textbf{Step 3.} Factored form: \ans{6(3x + 4)}

\textit{Check:} distribute back --- $6 \cdot 3x + 6 \cdot 4 = 18x + 24$, the
area we started with.}

\problem{\textbf{Patio $(x+3)$ ft by $(x+8)$ ft. Write the area with no
parentheses.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both factors are sums now, so the distributive property is
applied twice: every term of the first factor multiplies every term of the
second.

\textbf{Step 2.} $x \cdot x = x^2$, \quad $x \cdot 8 = 8x$, \quad
$3 \cdot x = 3x$, \quad $3 \cdot 8 = 24$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Combine the two like terms $8x + 3x = 11x$:
\ans{x^2 + 11x + 24\ \text{ft}^2}

\textit{Area model:} the four products are the four rectangles of the patio ---
the original $x$ by $x$ square, two strips, and the $3$ by $8$ corner.}

\problem{\textbf{Is $4(3x + 2) + 2x$ the same as $14x + 8$?}

\textbf{Step 1.} A claimed equivalence is settled by rewriting one side into
simplest form, so expand the left side first.

\textbf{Step 2.} $4(3x + 2) = 12x + 8$. Now add the loose term:
$12x + 8 + 2x$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Combine like terms: $12x + 2x = 14x$, and the constant $8$
stays. \ans{14x + 8}

Maya is correct: the two expressions are equivalent, because the left side
simplifies to exactly $14x + 8$.}

\problem{\textbf{Area $(15x^2 + 10x)$ square units, width $5x$ units. Factor.}

\textbf{Step 1.} The width is given as $5x$, so $5x$ should come out of both
terms --- take the greatest common factor of the numbers ($5$) together with
the highest power of $x$ in every term ($x$).

\textbf{Step 2.} $15x^2 \div 5x = 3x$ and $10x \div 5x = 2$, so the length is
$3x + 2$ units.

\textbf{Step 3.} Factored completely: \ans{5x(3x + 2)}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $5(3x^2 + 2x)$ --- the numerical factor came out
but the shared $x$ did not, so the factoring is not complete.}

\problem{\textbf{Devin wrote $5(2x - 3) = 10x - 3$. Evaluate the original at
$x = 4$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two expressions are equivalent only if they agree at
\emph{every} value of $x$, so one value that disagrees settles it.

\textbf{Step 2.} Substitute into the original: $5(2 \cdot 4 - 3) = 5(8 - 3)
= 5 \cdot 5$.

\textbf{Step 3.} The original expression is worth \ans{25}

Devin's form gives $37$ at $x = 4$, so the forms are not equivalent. The
mistake: the $5$ was distributed to $2x$ only, leaving the $-3$ untouched.
Distributing to both terms gives $10x - 15$, and $10(4) - 15 = 25$ as it
should.}

\problem{\textbf{Rug $(2x + 3)$ ft by $(x + 6)$ ft. Write the area with no
parentheses.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Two binomial factors again, but the first one now has a
leading coefficient of $2$ --- every term still multiplies every term.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2x \cdot x = 2x^2$, \quad $2x \cdot 6 = 12x$, \quad
$3 \cdot x = 3x$, \quad $3 \cdot 6 = 18$.

\textbf{Step 3.} Combine $12x + 3x = 15x$:
\ans{2x^2 + 15x + 18\ \text{ft}^2}

\textit{Sanity check:} the leading term $2x^2$ has to come from the two leading
terms, and $2x \cdot x$ is the only way to get it.}

\problem{\textbf{$6$ kits at $(2x + 5)$ dollars and $3$ kits at $(4x - 1)$
dollars. (a) Total as $ax + b$. \ (b) Factor it.}

\textbf{Step 1 (a).} Two separate groups, so distribute each multiplier over
its own parentheses before anything is combined.

\textbf{Step 2 (a).} $6(2x + 5) = 12x + 30$ and $3(4x - 1) = 12x - 3$.

\textbf{Step 3 (a).} Add and collect like terms: $12x + 12x = 24x$ and
$30 - 3 = 27$. \ans{24x + 27}

\textbf{Step 4 (b).} Now run distribution backwards on $24x + 27$. The numbers
$24$ and $27$ share a factor of $3$, and there is no $x$ in the constant term,
so the greatest common factor is just $3$.

\textbf{Step 5 (b).} $24x \div 3 = 8x$ and $27 \div 3 = 9$:
\ans{3(8x + 9)}

\textit{Why $3$ and not $9$:} $9$ divides $27$ but not $24$, so it is not
common to both terms.}
