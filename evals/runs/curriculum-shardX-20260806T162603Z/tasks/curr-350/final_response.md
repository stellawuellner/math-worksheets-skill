# Planning Constructions and Justifying Them — geometry review set

Three PDFs are ready:

- **`ss_construct_curr350.pdf` — Skills Summary (2 pages).** Three sections with
  a rule box, a worked example and a try-it: writing a construction plan (what
  counts as a legal compass-and-straightedge move, and the three things every
  step must state), justifying with equal radii (the equal radii → congruent
  segments → congruent triangles → the claimed part template), and auditing a
  finished construction on coordinates. The watch-out box flags the trap that
  catches most students here: equal distances prove a point is on a
  perpendicular bisector, but not that it is a midpoint.
- **`ws_construct_curr350.pdf` — Student worksheet (6 pages), 10 problems.**
  After a short warm-up the three subskills are interleaved so that no two
  consecutive problems ask for the same kind of work — plan, check, justify,
  plan, check, justify, … and the student has to read what is being asked before
  writing. It closes with one synthesis challenge: construct a square on a given
  segment by composing earlier constructions, justify why it is a square and not
  a rhombus, and give the side length from coordinates. Three problems carry
  value-free diagrams to mark up; every written problem gets 7–12 cm of room.
- **`ak_construct_curr350.pdf` — Answer key (3 pages).** Every open item gets a
  model answer *and* a rubric — what full credit requires and what earns
  nothing. The Quick Answers bank at the top marks the instructor-judged items
  with `---` so no row looks complete when it is not, followed by the curriculum
  block (HSG-CO.D, with HSG-SRT.A.1 on the dilation problem).

## What is verified, and what is not

This is a construction and proof sheet, so the honest split is unusual and worth
stating plainly. The set carries **19 declared responses across 10 problems.
9 are machine checked** — every coordinate length was recomputed with SymPy from
the raw coordinates, so the numeric half of each check cannot be wrong.

**10 responses are instructor-judged**, and they are the heart of the sheet:
every construction plan and every justification. A plan is a written sequence of
compass moves and a justification is an argument; neither is something a
computer algebra system can grade, and pretending otherwise would be the one
thing this worksheet must not do. **Problems 3, 4, 6 and 7 carry no machine
check at all** — they are pure planning and justification — and the answer key
says so with a `---` in the Quick Answers row.

What the key gives you instead is a rubric for each: the moves that must appear,
what each arc must be said to guarantee, which congruence criterion must be
named, and, in several cases, an explicit statement of what does *not* earn
credit (re-telling the drawing instead of arguing from the equal radii; a plan
that omits the theorem; a square argument that only proves a rhombus).

The coordinate problems (2, 5, 8, 9, 10c) exist precisely to give this sheet a
verified spine: each one turns a construction claim — "this is the midpoint",
"this point is equidistant", "this dilation has factor 3", "all three sides are
congruent" — into an arithmetic statement that either holds or does not.

## Notes

- The grade level appears on the answer key only; the worksheet and study guide
  the student holds carry no level label.
- No misconception traps are declared: the verification types used here express
  a distance rather than a method choice, and the errors this topic produces are
  argumentative rather than numeric. They are addressed in the rubrics instead.
