\noindent\small\textit{Angle names refer to the shared diagram on the
worksheet: $\ell \parallel m$ cut by transversal $t$, with $a, b, c, d$ around
the upper intersection and $e, f, g, h$ around the lower one.}\normalsize

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle a = 118^\circ$. Find $m\angle d$ and $m\angle e$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle a$ and $\angle d$ sit on the same side of the
transversal on either side of line $\ell$, so together they make a straight
angle --- a linear pair.

\textbf{Step 2.} $180 - 118 = 62$, so $m\angle d$ is \ans{62^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} $\angle e$ occupies the same position at line $m$ that
$\angle a$ occupies at line $\ell$, so they are corresponding angles, and the
lines are parallel --- corresponding angles are therefore congruent.
\ans{118^\circ}

\textit{Listen for:} ``they look about the same''. The diagram is not to
scale; the reason is the parallel-line postulate, not the picture.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle c = 47^\circ$. Find $m\angle f$ and $m\angle g$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle c$ and $\angle f$ are both between the two parallel
lines and both on the same side of the transversal --- same-side interior
angles, which are supplementary.

\textbf{Step 2.} $180 - 47 = 133$. \ans{133^\circ}

\textbf{Step 3.} $\angle g$ corresponds to $\angle c$, so the two are
congruent. \ans{47^\circ}

\textit{Teaching note:} same-side interior is the one pair on this diagram
that adds to a straight angle rather than matching. Students who memorise
``parallel means equal'' get this one wrong every time.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle b = (3x + 10)^\circ$, $m\angle f = (5x - 24)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle b$ and $\angle f$ are corresponding angles, so with
$\ell \parallel m$ their measures are equal. Set the expressions equal.

\textbf{Step 2.} $3x + 10 = 5x - 24$ gives $34 = 2x$, so \ans{x = 17}

\textbf{Step 3.} Substitute back into either expression:
$3(17) + 10 = 51 + 10$. \ans{61^\circ}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $17$ given as the angle. Finding $x$ is only the
first half; the question asked for a measure in degrees. Checking the other
expression is worthwhile too: $5(17) - 24 = 61$ as well.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle h = 125^\circ$. Find $m\angle b$ and $m\angle e$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle b$ and $\angle h$ are both outside the parallel lines
and on opposite sides of the transversal --- alternate exterior angles, which
are congruent. \ans{125^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} $\angle h$ and $\angle e$ are adjacent along line $m$ on the
same side of it, so they form a linear pair.

\textbf{Step 3.} $180 - 125 = 55$. \ans{55^\circ}

\textit{Teaching note:} alternate exterior is the pair students meet least
often. Point out that it is the mirror image of alternate interior, taken
outside the parallel lines instead of between them.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle d = (7x - 15)^\circ$, $m\angle f = (4x + 21)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} Both angles lie between the parallel lines and on opposite
sides of the transversal --- alternate interior angles, so they are congruent.

\textbf{Step 2.} $7x - 15 = 4x + 21$ gives $3x = 36$, so \ans{x = 12}

\textbf{Step 3.} $7(12) - 15 = 84 - 15$. \ans{69^\circ}

\textit{Check it:} the other expression gives $4(12) + 21 = 69$ as well, which
is the fastest way for a student to catch an arithmetic slip.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle f = 108^\circ$. Find $m\angle d$ and $m\angle a$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle d$ and $\angle f$ are alternate interior angles, so
they are congruent. \ans{108^\circ}

\textbf{Step 2.} There is no single named pair joining $\angle f$ to
$\angle a$, so go through $\angle d$: $\angle a$ and $\angle d$ form a linear
pair at the upper intersection.

\textbf{Step 3.} $180 - 108 = 72$. \ans{72^\circ}

\textit{Teaching note:} this is the first problem that needs two relationships
chained together. Writing the middle result down before reaching for the
second rule is the habit worth building.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle c = (4x + 12)^\circ$, $m\angle f = (6x + 8)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle c$ and $\angle f$ are same-side interior angles, so
they are supplementary, not congruent. Their sum is $180$.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(4x + 12) + (6x + 8) = 180$ gives $10x + 20 = 180$, then
$10x = 160$, so \ans{x = 16}

\textbf{Step 3.} $4(16) + 12 = 64 + 12$. \ans{76^\circ}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $20$ --- from setting the two expressions equal,
which gives $x = 2$. That is the congruent rule applied to a supplementary
pair. Sanity check: $76 + 104 = 180$, as a same-side interior pair must.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle d = (5x - 8)^\circ$, $m\angle e = (3x + 12)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle d$ is interior at the upper line and $\angle e$ is
interior at the lower line, both on the same side of the transversal --- so
they are supplementary.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(5x - 8) + (3x + 12) = 180$ gives $8x + 4 = 180$, then
$8x = 176$, so \ans{x = 22}

\textbf{Step 3.} $5(22) - 8 = 110 - 8$. \ans{102^\circ}

\textit{Common wrong answer:} $22$ reported as the angle. Check the pair:
$m\angle e = 3(22) + 12 = 78$, and $102 + 78 = 180$.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle a = (2x + 34)^\circ$, $m\angle g = (5x - 11)^\circ$.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle a$ is outside the parallel lines at the upper
intersection and $\angle g$ is outside them at the lower one, on the other
side of the transversal --- alternate exterior angles, so congruent.

\textbf{Step 2.} $2x + 34 = 5x - 11$ gives $45 = 3x$, so \ans{x = 15}

\textbf{Step 3.} $2(15) + 34 = 30 + 34$. \ans{64^\circ}

\textit{Teaching note:} a student who cannot see the pair can still get there
in two steps --- $\angle a$ and $\angle c$ are vertical angles, and $\angle c$
and $\angle g$ correspond.}

\problem{\textbf{$m\angle b = (9x - 4)^\circ$, $m\angle e = (3x + 16)^\circ$
--- decide the relationship yourself.}

\textbf{Step 1.} $\angle b$ and $\angle e$ are not one of the named pairs, so
chain two that are: $\angle b$ and $\angle f$ correspond, hence are congruent,
and $\angle f$ and $\angle e$ form a linear pair. So $\angle b$ and $\angle e$
are supplementary --- they add to a straight angle, they are not equal.

\textbf{Step 2.} $(9x - 4) + (3x + 16) = 180$ gives $12x + 12 = 180$, then
$12x = 168$, so \ans{x = 14}

\textbf{Step 3.} $9(14) - 4 = 126 - 4$. \ans{122^\circ}

\textbf{Step 4.} Check: $m\angle e = 3(14) + 16 = 58$, and $122 + 58 = 180$.

\textit{Open response (manual review):} the written justification is the
student's own. Accept any answer that reaches supplementary through a valid
chain --- corresponding then linear pair, as above, or vertical angles then
same-side interior. Do not accept ``they looked supplementary''; the point of
the question is the chain of reasons. A student who assumed congruent gets
$6x = 20$ and a non-integer $x$, which is itself a signal that the wrong rule
was chosen.}
