Here are the three PDFs for the Grade 4–5 place-value review.

**Worksheet — `ws_placevalue_curr105.pdf` (6 pages, 12 problems).**
The sheet deliberately mixes the three decisions the unit is about — write it in
expanded form, say which is bigger, round it to a named place — so the student
has to work out what is being asked rather than repeating one procedure. After
two warm-ups the types alternate: expanded form, digit value, rounding,
comparing, and back again.

A few problems are doing specific work. Problem 5 gives an expanded form with no
hundreds term, so the standard form needs a placeholder zero — the single most
common multi-digit place-value error. Problem 7 quotes a classmate who compared
"the biggest digit" instead of the biggest place, and asks for the correction in
words. Problem 8 asks how many times greater one 5 is than another 5 in the same
number, which is the powers-of-ten idea in its most concrete form. Problem 11 is
a rounding boundary: rounding 8950 to the nearest hundred carries into the
thousands, and the student has to explain why. Problem 12 is the challenge, and
it is the one that ties the three skills together: expand a number, compare two,
round both, and then notice that the rounded values no longer answer the
question.

**Answer key — `ak_placevalue_curr105.pdf` (3 pages).**
Every solution names the place being used before it computes, which is the habit
the sheet is trying to build. Four problems carry a "common wrong answer" note —
the missing placeholder zero, reading the digit two places over when rounding,
giving the digit instead of its value. A quick-answer strip and a Curriculum
section (standard covered, difficulty range) are at the top.

**Study guide — `ss_placevalue_curr105.pdf` (2 pages).**
Four sections: expanded form (with a place-value chart), the value of one digit,
comparing by the biggest *place*, and rounding to a named place with the carry
case called out. Each has a worked example and a try-it whose answer is printed
upside down inside the box.

**What was verified.** 15 of the 16 checks behind the 12 problems were
recomputed independently — every expanded form was checked by adding the place
values back up to the original number, every rounding was recomputed with
half-up rounding, and every comparison was checked as a relation (so a key with
the symbol the wrong way round would have failed the build).

**One item is flagged for manual review, on purpose:** problem 12(d) asks the
student to *explain* why two attendances that round to the same thousand cannot
settle which day was busier. That is a written sentence, so it is marked for you
to read. The key gives a model answer and says what to look for.

**A note on how the numbers are printed.** The multi-digit numbers are written
without thousands separators (47308 rather than 47,308). That is deliberate: the
pipeline's prose checker, which cross-checks every number printed on the sheet
against the verified data, splits a comma'd numeral into two numbers and can no
longer see the real one — so with commas it would not have been able to catch a
mistyped given anywhere on this sheet. If you would rather the sheet showed the
commas, say so and I will put them back; the arithmetic is unchanged either way.
