\problem{Three pairs of sides tick-marked. Name the criterion and its three statements.}

\textbf{Criterion: SSS.} All three pairs of corresponding sides are marked congruent, and nothing about angles is needed.

\textbf{The three statements:} $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{DE}$ (single ticks), $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{EF}$ (double ticks), $\overline{CA} \cong \overline{FD}$ (triple ticks). Therefore $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ by SSS.

\textbf{Grading:} the criterion plus all three pairs, written in corresponding order. Listing the pairs out of correspondence (for example $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{EF}$) is the error to look for.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{SAS with $AB = 3x + 4$ and $DE = 5x - 10$ cm. Find $x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} congruent triangles have congruent corresponding sides, and $\overline{AB}$ corresponds to $\overline{DE}$, so the two lengths are equal.
\begin{align*}
  3x + 4 &= 5x - 10 \\
  14 &= 2x
\end{align*}
Check: $3(7) + 4 = 25$ and $5(7) - 10 = 25$. \ans{x = 7}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Two pairs of sides marked. (a) What completes SAS? (b) What completes SSS?}

\textbf{(a)} A pair of congruent \textbf{angles}, and it must be the \emph{included} angle --- the angle formed where the two already-marked sides meet, so $\angle B \cong \angle E$ for the marked pair here. Any other angle gives side-side-angle, which is not a criterion: the third side is then free to swing into two positions.

\textbf{(b)} The \textbf{third pair of sides} marked congruent. No angle information is needed at all; SSS uses sides only.

\textbf{Grading:} (a) must say \emph{included} (or identify the vertex between the two marked sides). Naming any angle without that condition is the common error.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{ASA with $m\angle A = 4x + 6$ and $m\angle D = 6x - 14$. (a) $x$. (b) $m\angle A$.}

\textbf{(a)} Corresponding angles of congruent triangles are congruent, so their measures are equal.
\begin{align*}
  4x + 6 &= 6x - 14 \\
  20 &= 2x
\end{align*}
So $x = 10$. \ans{x = 10}

\textbf{(b)} $m\angle A = 4(10) + 6 = 40 + 6$. Check: $m\angle D = 6(10) - 14 = 46$ too. \ans{46^\circ}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Right triangles, hypotenuse $15$ and leg $9$ cm each. (a) Criterion and why SSA fails otherwise. (b) The remaining leg.}

\textbf{(a) HL.} Both triangles have a right angle (at $C$ and at $Z$), and the matching parts are a hypotenuse and a leg --- exactly the hypotenuse-leg criterion, which applies to right triangles only.

Why the general pattern fails: two sides and an angle that is \emph{not} between them do not fix a triangle. Swinging the second side from the end of the first gives two different closing positions, so two non-congruent triangles can carry the same three measurements. In a right triangle that second position is unavailable --- the leg must meet the other leg at $90^\circ$ --- which is what rescues HL.

\textbf{(b)} By the Pythagorean theorem, the remaining leg is $\sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144}$. \ans{BC = 12 \, \text{cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Quadrilateral $ABCD$ split by diagonal $\overline{AC}$; $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AD}$, $\angle BAC \cong \angle DAC$. (a) The shared-side property. (b) The criterion.}

\textbf{(a)} The \textbf{reflexive property of congruence}: any segment is congruent to itself, so $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{AC}$. It is a legitimate line because both triangles literally contain the same segment --- there is nothing to prove, and it is the standard way a shared side enters a proof. No tick mark is needed or expected.

\textbf{(b)} The marked side $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AD}$, the marked angle $\angle BAC \cong \angle DAC$ \emph{between} them and the shared side, and $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{AC}$ give \textbf{SAS}, so $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ADC$.

\textbf{Grading:} (a) must name the reflexive property, not merely observe that the side is shared.

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{SSS with $AB = 2x + 1$, $DE = x + 9$, $BC = 3x - 4$ cm. (a) $x$. (b) $EF$.}

\textbf{(a)} $\overline{AB}$ corresponds to $\overline{DE}$:
\begin{align*}
  2x + 1 &= x + 9 \\
  x &= 8
\end{align*}
Check: $2(8) + 1 = 17$ and $8 + 9 = 17$. \ans{x = 8}

\textbf{(b)} $\overline{BC}$ corresponds to $\overline{EF}$, so $EF = BC = 3(8) - 4 = 24 - 4$. \ans{EF = 20 \, \text{cm}}

\vspace{0.2cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{$\overline{PR}$ and $\overline{QS}$ meet at $X$; $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{SR}$, $\angle QPX \cong \angle RSX$. (a) The hidden pair and the criterion. (b) Why the SSA variant fails.}

\textbf{(a)} The hidden pair is $\angle PXQ$ and $\angle SXR$, the two angles at the crossing point. They are \textbf{vertical angles}, so they are congruent by the \emph{Vertical Angles Theorem} --- no tick mark can show this, it has to be read off the figure.

Now list the three parts in order around each triangle: $\angle QPX \cong \angle RSX$, $\angle PXQ \cong \angle SXR$, and the side $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{SR}$. That side is \emph{opposite} one of the two angles rather than between them, so the criterion is \textbf{AAS}, and $\triangle PQX \cong \triangle SRX$.

\textbf{(b)} The classmate's version keeps $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{SR}$ and $\angle QPX \cong \angle RSX$ and adds $\overline{QX} \cong \overline{RX}$: two sides and an angle that is not between them. That pattern does not fix a triangle --- from the end of the first side the second can swing to two different closing points, so two triangles with those three measurements need not be congruent. Side-side-angle is not a criterion, and the vertical-angle pair the figure actually provides is what makes the real argument work.

\textbf{Grading:} (b) must give the reason (two positions are possible), not merely assert that SSA ``is not allowed''.
