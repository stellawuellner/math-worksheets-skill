\begin{center}
\textit{Reading a parabola from vertex form, from standard form, and from its
intercepts and opening direction}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Directions.} Each grid below is the same size and is blank on
purpose --- it is your plotting space. For every function, find the features
the problem asks for, then sketch the curve on its grid, marking the vertex,
drawing the axis of symmetry as a dashed vertical line, and plotting the
intercepts you found.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Vertex form} $y = a(x-h)^2 + k$: the vertex is $(h,k)$ and the
axis of symmetry is $x = h$. Watch the sign --- $(x+p)^2$ means $h$ is
\emph{negative}.
\item \textbf{Standard form} $y = ax^2 + bx + c$: the axis of symmetry is
$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$; put that $x$ back into the equation for the vertex
height. The $y$-intercept is $c$.
\item \textbf{Intercept form} $y = a(x-p)(x-q)$: the $x$-intercepts are $p$ and
$q$, and the axis of symmetry sits exactly midway between them.
\item \textbf{Opening direction:} $a > 0$ opens up and the vertex is the
lowest point; $a < 0$ opens down and the vertex is the highest point.
\end{itemize}
