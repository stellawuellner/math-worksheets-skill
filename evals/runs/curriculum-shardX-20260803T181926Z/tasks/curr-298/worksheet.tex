\wstitleblock{Piecewise Rules for Rates and Fees}{Algebra 1}{}

\noindent\small
Writing piecewise fee rules, reading a cost back to its input, finding a missing
rate, and comparing two schedules.
\par\smallskip
Every price list below charges one rate up to a breakpoint and a different rate
after it, so no single linear equation describes the whole cost. Work each one
this way:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Name the input variable and its unit (hours, pounds, kilowatt-hours,
        miles, gigabytes, pages) and name the output --- always a total cost in
        dollars.
  \item Write the rule branch by branch, each branch with the interval of inputs
        it governs. Use the form
        $C(x) = \text{flat fee} + (\text{rate})(x - \text{breakpoint})$ for any
        branch that starts charging only past the breakpoint.
  \item Decide which branch the question lands in \emph{before} you compute.
\end{itemize}
\normalsize
\vspace{0.25cm}

\problem[6cm]{A city garage charges \$4.00 for a stay of up to 2 hours, and
\$3.00 for each additional hour after the first 2, for stays up to 8 hours.
Let $t$ be the number of hours parked and $C(t)$ the charge in dollars.
Write the piecewise rule for $C(t)$, then find the charge for a 5-hour stay.}

\problem[6cm]{A shipper charges a flat \$6.00 for any package weighing up to
3 pounds, and \$6.00 plus \$1.25 for each pound over 3 pounds beyond that.
Let $w$ be the package weight in pounds and $C(w)$ the charge in dollars.
Write the piecewise rule for $C(w)$, then find the charge for a 7-pound package.}

\problem[7cm]{An electric utility bills the first 500 kilowatt-hours of a month
at \$0.11 per kilowatt-hour and every kilowatt-hour above 500 at \$0.16 per
kilowatt-hour. Let $n$ be the kilowatt-hours used and $C(n)$ the bill in dollars.
Priya used 800 kilowatt-hours and says her bill is \$128.00.
Write the piecewise rule for $C(n)$, explain in one sentence what Priya did
wrong, then compute the correct bill.}

\problem{A taxi charges \$3.50 for the first mile and \$2.25 for each additional
mile. Let $m$ be the trip length in miles and $C(m)$ the fare in dollars.
Write the piecewise rule for $C(m)$, then find the trip length that produces a
fare of exactly \$17.00.
\par\vspace*{6cm}\answerline{mi}}

\problem[6.5cm]{A community pool charges \$8.00 for the first 3 hours of a visit
and the same unknown amount $k$ dollars for each additional hour. A 7-hour visit
costs \$22.00. Write the piecewise rule for the cost of a visit of $t$ hours
using $k$, then find the value of $k$.}

\problem[6.5cm]{A phone plan costs \$30.00 per month and includes 5 gigabytes of
data. Each gigabyte above 5 costs \$12.00, but the monthly bill is never allowed
to exceed \$90.00. Let $g$ be the gigabytes used and $C(g)$ the bill in dollars.
Write the three-branch piecewise rule for $C(g)$, then find the bill for a month
in which 9 gigabytes were used.}

\problem{Using the same phone plan as problem 6 (\$30.00 base, 5 gigabytes
included, \$12.00 per gigabyte above 5, bill capped at \$90.00), find the data
usage at which the middle branch first reaches the \$90.00 cap --- that is, the
breakpoint where the flat capped branch takes over.
\par\vspace*{6cm}\answerline{GB}}

\problem{Look again at the phone-plan rule from problems 6 and 7. Explain why
the middle branch and the capped branch must give the same cost at the point
where they meet, and describe what a customer would see on the bill if the two
branches disagreed there by a few dollars. Write in complete sentences.
\par\vspace*{6cm}\noansline}

\problem[8cm]{Two garages sit on the same block. Garage A charges \$4.00 for a
stay of up to 2 hours and \$3.00 for each additional hour. Garage B charges
\$2.50 per hour from the moment you arrive, with no flat fee. For a stay longer
than 2 hours, let $t$ be the hours parked and $y$ the cost in dollars.
Write a two-equation system for the stay on which the two garages cost the same,
then solve it for $t$ and $y$.}

\problem[8cm]{A print shop charges a \$25.00 setup fee plus \$0.40 per page for
the first 200 pages of a job, and a reduced rate of $r$ dollars per page for
every page beyond 200. A 500-page job costs \$153.00. Write the piecewise rule
for the cost of a $p$-page job using $r$, then find $r$.}
