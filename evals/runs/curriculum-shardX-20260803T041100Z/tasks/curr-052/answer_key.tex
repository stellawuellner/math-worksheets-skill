\problem{\textbf{Write $546$ in expanded form.}
\par
Name the value of each digit by its place, then add those values:
the $5$ sits in the hundreds place and is worth $500$, the $4$ sits in the
tens place and is worth $40$, the $6$ sits in the ones place and is worth $6$.
\par
$\ans{546 = 500 + 40 + 6}$}

\problem{\textbf{Write $300 + 70 + 2$ in standard form.}
\par
Each part of an expanded form names one place. $300$ fills the hundreds place
with a $3$, $70$ fills the tens place with a $7$, and $2$ fills the ones place.
Reading the digits in order gives the standard form.
\par
$\ans{372}$}

\problem{\textbf{Write two hundred eighty-four in standard form.}
\par
Read the name in chunks. ``Two hundred'' is $200$, ``eighty'' is $80$, and
``four'' is $4$. Those are the three place values, so
$200 + 80 + 4$ is the expanded form and the digits are $2$, $8$, $4$.
\par
$\ans{284}$}

\problem{\textbf{Write $907$ in expanded form.}
\par
Hundreds: $900$. Tens: the digit is $0$, so the tens place is worth
nothing here and is left out. Ones: $7$. A place holding a zero still needs
its digit in standard form --- it just contributes nothing to the sum.
\par
$\ans{907 = 900 + 7}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} an answer of $90 + 7$ means the student read the $9$ as
$9$ tens. Ask which place the $9$ is sitting in.}

\problem{\textbf{Write $600 + 5$ in standard form.}
\par
There is no tens part in the expanded form, so the tens place is empty and
must be held open with a $0$; otherwise the $5$ would slide over into the tens
place and make $65$.
\par
$\ans{605}$}

\problem{\textbf{Which is greater, $400 + 30 + 9$ or four hundred
ninety-three?}
\par
Put both numbers in standard form first --- you cannot compare a sum with a
number name by looking at them.
$400 + 30 + 9 = 439$, and four hundred ninety-three is $493$.
Both have $4$ hundreds, so compare the next place: $3$ tens against $9$ tens.
Since $3 < 9$, the first number is smaller.
\par
$\ans{439 < 493}$}

\problem{\textbf{Maya wrote six hundred fifteen as $6015$. Fix it.}
\par
Maya wrote the $6$ for ``six hundred'' and then wrote ``fifteen'' as the
two digits $15$ beside it, giving four digits. But ``fifteen'' is not a new
place --- it is $1$ ten and $5$ ones, which fill the tens and ones places of
the same three-digit number.
\par
Hundreds $= 6$, tens $= 1$, ones $= 5$, so the expanded form is
$600 + 10 + 5$.
\par
$\ans{615}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Grading note:} $6015$ is the diagnostic wrong answer --- it means the
student is stacking chunks side by side instead of assigning each chunk to a
place. Have them build the number with base-ten blocks: $6$ flats, $1$ rod,
$5$ units.}

\problem{\textbf{Order $700 + 8$, seven hundred eighty, and $78$ from least
to greatest.}
\par
Convert everything to standard form first:
$700 + 8 = 708$, \ seven hundred eighty $= 780$, \ and $78$ is already in
standard form.
\par
Now compare. $78$ has only two digits, so it is the smallest. Both $708$ and
$780$ have $7$ hundreds, so compare tens: $0$ tens against $8$ tens, and
$0 < 8$.
\par
$\ans{78 < 708 < 780}$}

\problem{\textbf{Write $470$ in word form and in expanded form.}
\par
Word form (read the digits by place): \textbf{four hundred seventy}. There is
no ``and'' in a whole-number name, and the $0$ ones are not spoken.
\par
Expanded form: hundreds $400$, tens $70$, ones $0$ --- and a zero part is
dropped from the sum.
\par
$\ans{470 = 400 + 70}$
\par\smallskip
\emph{Manual review:} the spelling of the number name is read by a teacher;
only the expanded form is machine-checked.}

\problem{\textbf{Challenge: start with $300 + 60 + 5$, add ten more.}
\par
Step 1 --- standard form of the starting number: $300 + 60 + 5 = 365$.
\par
Step 2 --- ten more means one more ten, so the tens digit goes from $6$ to
$7$: $365 + 10 = 375$. Nothing else changes, because adding a ten only touches
the tens place (there is no regrouping here: $6 + 1 = 7$ still fits in one
digit).
\par
Step 3 --- expanded form of the new number: $300 + 70 + 5$.
\par
$\ans{375 = 300 + 70 + 5}$}
