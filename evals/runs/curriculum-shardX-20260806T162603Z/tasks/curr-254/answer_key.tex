\problem{Simplify $(4x + 7) - (x - 3)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad The minus sign applies to both terms of the second bracket, so
$-(x - 3)$ becomes $-x + 3$. Then $4x - x = 3x$ and $7 + 3 = 10$. \ans{3x + 10}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $5x^2 + 3x - 2x^2 + x$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Only terms with the same power may be combined.
Squares: $5x^2 - 2x^2 = 3x^2$. Linear: $3x + x = 4x$ (a bare $x$ has coefficient $1$).
The two groups stay separate. \ans{3x^2 + 4x}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Dev: $(5x^2 - 3x) - (2x^2 + 4x)$ written as $3x^2 + x$. Find the error, test it, correct it.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Dev distributed the minus sign to the first term of the
second bracket only. He used $-2x^2$ correctly but carried $+4x$ across unchanged; it should be
$-4x$. The correct middle step is $5x^2 - 3x - 2x^2 - 4x$. Full credit names the
distributing-the-minus error or writes that middle step; ``the answer is wrong'' does not.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad Substituting $x = 2$ into the original:
$(5\cdot 4 - 3\cdot 2) - (2\cdot 4 + 4\cdot 2) = (20 - 6) - (8 + 8) = 14 - 16$. \ans{-2}

\noindent Dev's expression gives $3\cdot 4 + 2 = 14$ at $x = 2$, so the test value exposes the error.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c):} \quad $5x^2 - 3x - 2x^2 - 4x = 3x^2 - 7x$. \ans{3x^2 - 7x}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $6x^3 - 2x^2 + x^3 + 5x^2$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Cubes together and squares together:
$6x^3 + x^3 = 7x^3$ and $-2x^2 + 5x^2 = 3x^2$. A cube and a square are never like terms, however
close they sit on the page. \ans{7x^3 + 3x^2}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Mara: $3x^2 + 4x + 2x^2$ written as $9x^3$. Find the error, test it, correct it.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Two things went wrong in one step. First, $4x$ was
treated as a like term with the two squared terms; it has a different power and cannot join them.
Second, the exponents were added along with the coefficients --- combining like terms adds
coefficients only and leaves the power alone. Full credit names both halves, or states the
like-term rule that rules the step out.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad At $x = 2$: $3\cdot 4 + 4\cdot 2 + 2\cdot 4 = 12 + 8 + 8$. \ans{28}

\noindent Mara's $9x^3$ gives $9\cdot 8 = 72$ at $x = 2$, nowhere near $28$.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c):} \quad Only the two squared terms combine:
$3x^2 + 2x^2 = 5x^2$, and $4x$ stays as it is. \ans{5x^2 + 4x}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Theo: $(2x - 5)(x - 3)$ expanded as $2x^2 - 11x - 15$. Find the error, test it, correct it.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} The two constants are both negative, so their product
is positive: $(-5)(-3) = +15$. Theo wrote $-15$. Everything else in his answer is right --- the
$2x^2$ and the $-11x$ from $-6x - 5x$ both check out. Full credit points at the product of the two
negative constants.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad At $x = 2$: $(2\cdot 2 - 5)(2 - 3) = (-1)(-1)$. \ans{1}

\noindent Theo's expression gives $8 - 22 - 15 = -29$ at $x = 2$, so one substitution settles it.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c):}
\begin{align*}
  (2x - 5)(x - 3) &= 2x^2 - 6x - 5x + 15 \;=\; 2x^2 - 11x + 15.
\end{align*}
\ans{2x^2 - 11x + 15}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Simplify $(3x^2 - 5x + 2) - (x^2 - 5x - 6) + (2x - 1)$.}

\textbf{Solution:} \quad Distribute the minus over all three terms of the middle bracket; the last
bracket is added, so it is unchanged:
\begin{align*}
  &3x^2 - 5x + 2 - x^2 + 5x + 6 + 2x - 1\\
  &= (3x^2 - x^2) + (-5x + 5x + 2x) + (2 + 6 - 1)\\
  &= 2x^2 + 2x + 7.
\end{align*}
Note that $-5x$ and $+5x$ cancel, so the surviving linear term comes only from the last bracket.
\ans{2x^2 + 2x + 7}

\vspace{0.15cm}\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.2pt}

\problem{Student line: $(x+4)^2 - (x-2)(x+2) = x^2 + 16 - x^2 - 4 = 12$. Find both errors, test, correct.}

\textbf{Solution (a) --- instructor-judged.} Error one: $(x + 4)^2$ was written as $x^2 + 16$. A
squared binomial always has a middle term, $2\cdot x\cdot 4 = 8x$, so it is $x^2 + 8x + 16$.
Error two: the minus in front of the second product reached only the $x^2$. Since
$(x-2)(x+2) = x^2 - 4$, subtracting it gives $-x^2 + 4$, not $-x^2 - 4$. Full credit names both;
naming one earns half credit.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (b):} \quad At $x = 3$:
$(3 + 4)^2 - (3 - 2)(3 + 2) = 49 - (1)(5) = 49 - 5$. \ans{44}

\noindent The student's line gives $12$, which is not even a function of $x$ --- a warning sign in
itself, since the original expression plainly still contains $x$.

\noindent\textbf{Solution (c):}
\begin{align*}
  (x+4)^2 - (x-2)(x+2) &= (x^2 + 8x + 16) - (x^2 - 4)\\
                       &= x^2 + 8x + 16 - x^2 + 4 \;=\; 8x + 20.
\end{align*}
Check at $x = 3$: $8\cdot 3 + 20 = 44$, matching part (b). \ans{8x + 20}
